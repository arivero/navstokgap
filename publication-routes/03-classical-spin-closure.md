# Classical spins and the dynamical meaning of a state

Publication assessment, 2026-09-13. The strongest foundations direction
identified in the general review; saved as a route for consideration, rather
than a change to the active research queue.

## The candidate

C125, C127 and C128 fit together into a coherent argument:

1. Restricted measurements on classical orientations reproduce the
   single-qubit Bloch ball.
2. Classical joint preparations supply a separable composite.
3. A reversible Hamiltonian interaction distinguishes preparations that
   initially represent the same retained operational state.
4. No finite enlargement of the retained observables repairs exact evolution
   for all admitted preparations on any nonzero time interval.

The [explicit two-spin example](../notes/hamiltonian-moment-descent.md) makes
the distinction tangible: reversibility of the underlying mechanics and
well-defined evolution of the reduced state are separate requirements.
The initial construction is in
[the orientation note](../notes/classical-orientation-closure.md).

## Journal route and remaining checks

P06 now supplies a standalone [LaTeX manuscript](../papers/classical-spins-operational-closure.tex),
[compiled PDF](../out/papers/classical-spins-operational-closure.pdf) and
[PDF-inclusive bundle](../out/publications/classical-spins-operational-closure.zip).
The [claim-to-source review](../reviews/spin-publication-P06.md) maps the
consolidated results to their existing audits. [B76](../reviews/spin-publication-B76.md)
completes bounded result-specific positioning: use a conceptual worked example,
with the explicit spin pair and finite-repair proof as its distinctive content.
Classical extensions and transformation-relative preparation equivalence have
close precedents. Author details and editorial review remain before submission.

This is the best candidate for *Foundations of Physics*, provided we establish
its contribution beyond familiar moment-closure problems. Restricted classical
representations and composition issues already have substantial coverage in
[Janotta–Hinrichsen](https://arxiv.org/html/1402.6562v3), while
[de la Torre and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5482) supply the relevant
reconstruction theorem. The repository's audits are
[B69](../references/batches/B69.md), [B70](../references/batches/B70.md) and
[B72](../references/batches/B72.md).

The [finite-enlargement proof](../notes/hamiltonian-finite-closure.md) is now
complete (C128/B74), including nonlinear updates of finite expectation vectors.
The standalone synthesis joins C125–C128 with a claim-to-source map.
Its contribution must be assessed against
restricted classical representations and exact finite Koopman closure; an
infinite moment hierarchy by itself is not a novelty claim. The B70 technical
proof remains unaudited, so the draft should use its stated premise boundary
without presenting a new reconstruction theorem.

*Foundations of Physics* welcomes substantive conceptual work as well as
research articles, making it a plausible destination for a sharply positioned
operational-state argument. See the
[journal scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/10701/aims-and-scope).

The general review recommends keeping this question as the research track
while preparing the [elastic-gas paper](01-elastic-gas-comparison.md) first.
The [orbit-action bound](02-closed-orbit-action-bound.md) remains the short-note
alternative. B76 records the result-specific search boundary; any stronger
priority claim would require broader coverage.
