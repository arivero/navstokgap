# Same collision law, different transport

Publication assessment, 2026-09-13. First publication choice from the general
review; saved as a route for consideration, rather than a change to the active
research queue.

## The candidate

C050–C051 compare two infinite elastic gases with the same masses, particle
speeds, density, energy density and mean collision frequency:

- Poisson preparation produces diffusive tagged motion and a positive
  long-window action coefficient.
- Ordered counterpropagating streams produce bounded, periodic tagged motion
  and a coefficient tending to zero.

The physical lesson is unusually clean: **collision frequency and kinetic
scales alone do not determine transport; the correlations in the preparation
matter.**

This could become a compact teaching paper, provisionally titled *Same
collisions, different diffusion*. It has an explicit mechanical construction,
elementary calculations and a memorable comparison. The central material
already exists in §§5–6 of
[the collision manuscript](../papers/collision-action-relaxation.tex).

The Poisson result has an established literature match. The specific ordered
comparison remains unmatched within B26's limited search. A targeted search
on correlated initial conditions and periodic hard-point gases is the
principal remaining originality check. See [B25](../references/batches/B25.md)
and [B26](../references/batches/B26.md).

## General assessment preserved from the review

The repository now contains material worth developing into a first
publication. The elastic-gas comparison is the first choice for AJP; the
classical-spin construction is the strongest foundations candidate. The
closed-orbit action bound is also a credible short-note candidate.

The main research achievement is becoming clear: we can identify precisely
which preparation, measurement or dynamical assumptions supply a positive
scale. The step that forces a universal $h(t)\to\hbar>0$ remains open.

The review also found a concrete prior-art match for the Ising spectral-gap
formula. Lubetzky–Sly, *Cutoff for the Ising model on the lattice*, p. 4,
explicitly state

$$\gamma=1-\tanh(2\beta),$$

independent of system size. Their rate-one clock convention restores our
factor $a$ immediately. This directly matches C124, beyond the magnetization
passages checked in B68. See the
[primary paper, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4320).

Our all-mode proof and local Hermitian construction remain useful exposition.
The review classifies that manuscript as a teaching synthesis, with lower
publication priority than the three candidates saved here. This records the
review's finding; the existing ledger and B68 audit retain their original
coverage statements.
