# Gauge-invariant Dobrushin mixing for four-dimensional \(SU(3)\) lattice gauge measures

**Manuscript C — full working draft.** Author and affiliation details are intentionally omitted pending approval. The estimate is a sufficient strong-coupling certificate. The Dobrushin comparison constants and gauge-invariant perturbation topology require independent specialist audit before submission.

## Abstract

We prove a volume-uniform connected-correlation estimate for bounded gauge-invariant Wilson-loop observables in a four-dimensional periodic \(SU(3)\) lattice gauge measure. A mixed oscillation of each plaquette interaction controls the total-variation influence between link-conditional distributions. Since a link has at most 18 distinct link neighbours in the plaquette adjacency graph, the Dobrushin condition is \(18(e^{J}-1)<1\). For the Wilson interaction with \(\beta=6/g^2\), the estimate applies when \(g^2>24/\log(19/18)\). It persists in an explicit open ball of gauge-invariant plaquette interactions. The result places a concrete Wilson-loop mixing certificate in a strong-coupling region and supplies an H2-type one-box estimate.

**Keywords:** lattice gauge theory; Dobrushin uniqueness; strong coupling; Wilson loops; exponential clustering.

## 1. Introduction

A useful mixing theorem for a lattice gauge measure should specify its observable algebra, be uniform in volume, and make its sufficient coupling regime explicit. Standard Dobrushin comparison provides a route from one-link conditional laws to decay of correlations. The present formulation keeps the final observables gauge invariant and gives a stability neighbourhood for local plaquette interactions.

Let the lattice be any periodic hypercubic four-torus. Each link variable lies in \(SU(3)\), with product Haar reference measure. The theorem concerns connected correlations of bounded Wilson-loop observables. It is formulated in the Wilson strong-coupling region selected by the explicit influence criterion below; the coupling inequality is sufficient, not a description of the phase boundary.

## 2. Gibbs measure and interaction seminorm

Let \(E\) and \(P\) be the unoriented link and plaquette sets, and write
\[
d\mu_\Phi(U)=Z_\Phi^{-1}
\exp\left\{\sum_{p\in P}\Phi_p(U_{\partial p})\right\}
\prod_{\ell\in E}dU_\ell.
\tag{1}
\]
Each \(\Phi_p\) is invariant under the vertex gauge transformations on its boundary. Let \(\mathcal W_\Lambda\) be the bounded algebra generated by real and imaginary parts of traces of closed Wilson loops. For a bounded observable set
\[
\delta_\ell F=\sup_{U,U':U_e=U'_e\;(e\ne\ell)}|F(U)-F(U')|.
\tag{2}
\]
For distinct boundary links \(r,s\in\partial p\), define
\[
J_{p;r,s}(\Phi)=\sup_{\omega,\omega':\,\omega_e=\omega'_e\;(e\ne s)}
\operatorname{osc}_{U_r}\left[\Phi_p(U_r,\omega)-\Phi_p(U_r,\omega')\right],
\tag{3}
\]
where \(\operatorname{osc}h=\sup h-\inf h\), and put
\[
J(\Phi)=\max_p\max_{r\ne s\in\partial p}J_{p;r,s}(\Phi).
\tag{4}
\]
Adding constants to plaquette potentials leaves (3) invariant; the norm is gauge invariant because its suprema range over the full boundary variables.

## 3. Conditional influence

Fix all links except \(r\). The conditional law is
\[
d\mu_r^\omega(V)=
\frac{e^{f_r^\omega(V)}}{\int_{SU(3)}e^{f_r^\omega(W)}\,dW}\,dV,
\qquad
f_r^\omega(V)=\sum_{p\ni r}\Phi_p((V,\omega)_{\partial p}).
\tag{5}
\]
If two boundary conditions differ only at \(s\), the conditional laws agree when \(r,s\) share no plaquette. When they do share a plaquette, use the density comparison
\[
\|p-q\|_{\rm TV}\le e^{\operatorname{osc}(f-f')}-1
\tag{6}
\]
for normalized densities relative to a common probability measure. This yields
\[
c_{rs}:=\sup_{\omega\sim_s\omega'}\|\mu_r^\omega-\mu_r^{\omega'}\|_{\rm TV}
\le e^{J(\Phi)}-1.
\tag{7}
\]
A link belongs to six plaquettes, with three other links per plaquette. The link adjacency graph therefore has degree at most 18, and the influence matrix \(C=(c_{rs})\) has row sum
\[
\alpha=\sup_r\sum_s c_{rs}\le18(e^{J(\Phi)}-1).
\tag{8}
\]

## 4. Main theorem

**Theorem 1 (Wilson-loop mixing certificate).** Suppose \(\alpha<1\). Then on every periodic volume, for all \(F,G\in\mathcal W_\Lambda\),
\[
|\langle F;G\rangle_{\mu_\Phi}|
\le\frac1{1-\alpha}
\sum_{r\in\operatorname{supp}F}\sum_{s\in\operatorname{supp}G}
\delta_rF\,\delta_sG\,e^{-\gamma d_\ell(r,s)},
\qquad \gamma=-\log\alpha>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(d_\ell\) is distance in the link adjacency graph. For fixed local loops, all constants are independent of the periodic volume.

*Proof.* The finite-volume Dobrushin comparison inequality bounds the covariance by the oscillations weighted by the resolvent \((1-C)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}C^n\). A length-\(n\) product of influences vanishes between links at graph distance greater than \(n\), while its row sum is at most \(\alpha^n\). Hence
\[
[(1-C)^{-1}]_{rs}\le\sum_{n\ge d_\ell(r,s)}\alpha^n
=\frac{\alpha^{d_\ell(r,s)}}{1-\alpha},
\]
which gives (9). Conditional distributions are defined on link representatives, but the theorem is applied to the gauge-invariant loop algebra. \(\square\)

The use of (9) for arbitrary bounded loop polynomials requires the stated oscillations and supports; for a fixed finite family these can be absorbed into a norm-dependent prefactor.

## 5. Wilson action and an open perturbation ball

In the convention
\[
\Phi_p^W(U_{\partial p})=\frac\beta3\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_{\partial p},
\qquad \beta=\frac6{g^2},
\tag{10}
\]
changing one of the other three links changes a staple \(S\) to \(S'\). Since \(\|S-S'\|_1\le6\),
\[
\left|\frac\beta3\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}[V(S-S')]\right|\le2\beta,
\]
so the oscillation is at most \(4\beta\). Therefore \(J(\Phi^W)\le4\beta\), and (8) is strictly below one whenever
\[
18(e^{4\beta}-1)<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta<\frac14\log\frac{19}{18}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g^2>\frac{24}{\log(19/18)}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(\Psi\) is a gauge-invariant plaquette-local perturbation with \(J(\Psi)<\rho\), subadditivity gives \(J(\Phi^W+\Psi)\le4\beta+\rho\). Thus the estimate holds throughout the open seminorm ball whenever
\[
4\beta+\rho<\log\frac{19}{18}.
\tag{12}
\]

## 6. Discussion

The theorem combines a standard influence-matrix strategy with a gauge-invariant loop-observable conclusion and an explicit local-interaction stability norm. Its volume uniformity follows from a degree bound independent of torus size. The interaction radius in (11) is conservative and serves as a sufficient certificate. Comparison with established character and polymer expansions should assess the relative constants, observable classes, and perturbation stability rather than compare threshold values in isolation.

At the level of the repository's proof interfaces, (9) supplies a one-box mixing estimate in the stated strong-coupling range. Scale-by-scale blocking, uniform renormalized observable normalization, and continuum reconstruction are separate interfaces with separate hypotheses.
