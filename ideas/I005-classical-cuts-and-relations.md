# I005: what survives when a process is cut?

The classical sources suggest relocating the main question: determine what
must be carried across a cut for a physical process to remain the same process.
An action-valued remainder could belong to this rule of composition, rather
than to a minimum speed or a minimum size of orbit. This is a research
interpretation prompted by the user on 2026-09-09, not an accepted theorem.

## 1. Cone: equality of a boundary and equality of neighbouring bodies

Plutarch, *De communibus notitiis* 39, reports Democritus's cone dilemma and
Chrysippus's reply through a hostile discussion. The alternatives are a stepped
cone with unequal sections and a cylinder with equal ones. Read the
[stored section](../docs/classics/Plutarch_DeCommunibusNotitiis_39_Goodwin1874_perseus.xml),
with [edition anchors](../docs/classics/Plutarch_DeCommunibusNotitiis_39_Goodwin1874_perseus.md).

The modern distinction worth borrowing is between the common boundary at a
cut, sections at separated heights, and the limiting relation between them.
Equality of a limiting boundary value leaves the transverse variation open.
There is no next distinct real height after a given height. A formalism that
retains only a limiting section can lose the information that distinguishes
a cone from a cylinder. This is a model-design lesson, rather than a claimed
translation of Chrysippus into differential geometry.

## 2. Arrow: location and transition have different logical jobs

Aristotle's *Physics* VI.9 discusses the inference from occupying a place at
an instant to rest; the [stored companion](../docs/classics/Aristotle_Physics_VI9_Zeno_Teubner1879_OCR.md)
records its damaged Greek OCR and reading boundary. Diogenes Laertius IX.72
[reports the locative dilemma under Zeno's name](../docs/classics/DiogenesLaertius_BookIX_Hicks1925_wikisource.md).
The related Diodorus tradition is a separate attribution question.

The productive modern question is what a cut-state contains. A position is
an occupancy statement; a velocity, momentum or transition rule relates that
state to neighbouring states. A completed classical phase state already carries
such relations. The research target therefore needs a physical reason why
that familiar completion is insufficient, or why its composition requires an
additional action-valued datum. Occupancy by itself is too weak a target.

## 3. Division everywhere: possible cuts and simultaneous independent parts

Aristotle, *Generation and Corruption* I.2, considers the disappearance of
extension if division leaves only points or nothing, then examines the
inference to indivisible magnitudes. The stored French translation's
[paragraphs 10–21](../docs/classics/Aristotle_GC_I2_BarthelemySaintHilaire1866_fr_wikisource.md)
are the relevant passage; its notes openly acknowledge interpretive difficulty.
Paragraph 19 introduces a proposed atomist inference that the following text
examines, so it should not be quoted alone as Aristotle's final conclusion.

The design distinction is between permitting any finite set of cuts and
declaring all resulting pieces independently specifiable at once. It asks
whether cutting preserves the compatibility conditions needed to reconstruct
the whole. This is directly relevant to inserting instants in a path integral:
one must specify the rule that combines the pieces, along with the values
assigned to the inserted instants.

**Provenance clarification, 2026-09-10:** [the anchored comparison](../notes/ancient-cuts-provenance.md)
locates this complication principally in Aristotle I.2, around 317a. The
further claim that inserting a node can move old vertices belongs to our modern
discrete variational interpretation. Preserve that distinction when reusing
the classical-source analogy.

## 4. Vacuum: separation and cohesion belong in the same experiment

Galileo's *Two New Sciences*, First Day, places indivisible vacua inside an
argument about material cohesion. The [stored OCR](../docs/classics/Galileo_TwoNewSciences_FirstDay_CrewDeSalvio1914_OCR.txt),
printed pp. 18–19, discusses small vacua as a proposed cement; his dialogue
presents the mechanism as a speculative thought. The wheel and bowl/cone
passages are routed by the [companion](../docs/classics/Galileo_TwoNewSciences_FirstDay_CrewDeSalvio1914_OCR.md).

This suggests treating physical separation as an interaction problem: removing
the material between two parts need not erase the relation holding them
together. Galileo's vacuum here is a historical account of cohesion, not the
modern quantum vacuum. Its useful contribution is the question of what physical
structure remains at an apparently empty interface.

The [al-Naẓẓām report](../docs/classics/Shahrastani_Nazzam_Leap_Haarbruecker1850_OCR.md)
adds a contrasting response to infinite subdivision: deny indivisible parts
and introduce the leap. The reporter then objects that a leap still traverses
a distance. In [Maimonides I.73](../docs/classics/Maimonides_Guide_PartI_Friedlander_wikisource.md),
the discussion of time-atoms, unequal speeds and the rotating millstone asks
whether a proposed microscopic rule preserves a body's coherence. These
traditions pose related tests without supplying one common doctrine.

## 5. Newton: preserve the relation while the magnitudes vanish

The Section I closing Scholium treats ultimate ratios and the limiting
velocity; [our anchored reading](../notes/principia-constant-force-action.md)
distinguishes Newton's language from later reconstructions. The useful lesson
is to ask what relation survives vanishing magnitudes. The local defect of a
smooth polygonal approximation may vanish while a properly specified ratio
remains meaningful. Our M05 work already separates path convergence from
action convergence under particular changing preparations.

## 6. Revised experiment: refinement, elimination and composition

R03 should define a finite-cut mechanical model with an explicit full state
and compare two procedures:

1. Compose the full segment dynamics, then eliminate the interface variables.
2. Eliminate those variables segment by segment, then attempt to compose the
   reduced descriptions using only the retained cut-state.

Determine what information or term is needed to make the reduced description
reproduce the first procedure as cuts are refined. Keep the Hamiltonian,
preparation and physical observations fixed. Distinguish observational cuts
from interventions. Start with one already available classical receiver;
the first deliverable is a diagram with exact maps and a specified observable,
not another assumed lower action floor.

Ordinary classical memory can already distinguish these two procedures.
If that is the result, record what restores composition. If an action-valued
term survives, test its dependence on preparation and whether an independent
physical principle forces it to stay positive. Any phase interpretation then
needs its own derivation: writing an exponential with a supplied action
constant would install the parameter being sought.

The working hypothesis is that the required scale governs relations between
pieces or alternatives. Proving a nonzero universal scale remains the main
goal. A19 and the force/excitation inequalities remain useful diagnostic
results, while R03 returns the next investigation to the original cut problem.
