# I001: from vanishing areas to a nonzero action scale

Status: user-originated heuristic, not a theorem. Recorded 2026-09-05.

The motivating proposal is that Newton's trajectory limits suppress an additional
action-like degree of freedom, perhaps connected to Jacobi variations, and that
Feynman's phase supplies a regularization leading to a positive bound.

## Candidate readings to test

1. A trajectory variation $\eta$ and its quadratic action cost. Already testable:
   unrestricted amplitudes give arbitrarily small costs. A positive Jacobi
   eigenvalue only bounds cost relative to a specified norm.
2. A genuine extra field $\chi$ with an extended action $S[q,\chi]$. No field
   space, coupling or measure is specified yet. These must be chosen before an
   existence or gap theorem has a statement. Test whether eliminating $\chi$
   simply inserts an assumed quantum scale.
3. An operational equivalence of unresolved path phases. Specify resources and
   confidence, then test whether the bound survives increasing resources. This
   may give a useful conditional bound even when a universal one fails.
4. A distributional constraint. Specify the variable, normalization, test-function
   space and limit parameter. The free quantum propagator provides a control:
   its short-time limit is a delta, not automatically a delta derivative.

## Historical conjecture (separate)

Newton's concern over vanishing magnitudes might illuminate the modern proposal.
The asserted delay of the first edition and any relation to the discarded
Classical Scholia need dated textual evidence. Neither the user's intuition nor
the existence of old drafts establishes causation or historical novelty.
