# A gauge-invariant Dobrushin certificate: an open $SU(3)$ Wilson-interaction ball in $3+1$ dimensions

This note discharges a deliberately limited, positive version of the
one-box mixing interface H2 in the strong-coupling region.  On every
periodic four-dimensional lattice it gives a volume-uniform exponential
connected-correlation bound for the *gauge-invariant Wilson-loop algebra*,
together with an explicit open ball of gauge-invariant plaquette
interactions on which the bound persists.  The certificate is at
$g^2>24/\log(19/18)$ for the $SU(3)$ Wilson normalization.  It is a
constructive strong-coupling H2 neighbourhood, not a statement that the
weak/intermediate-coupling scaling trajectory enters that neighbourhood.

The elementary Wilson calculation in
[the earlier Dobrushin note](dobrushin-uniqueness-wilson.md) is recovered
as a special case.  The point here is to state the conditional measures,
the gauge-invariant test algebra, and the interaction-norm openness in one
place, as required by workstream C.

## 1. Regulator, algebra, and a mixed-oscillation norm

Let $\Lambda$ be any periodic, hypercubic Euclidean lattice in four
dimensions, with unoriented link set $E$ and plaquette set $P$.  A
configuration is $U\in SU(3)^E$, with product Haar measure $dU$.  Consider
the plaquette-local, gauge-invariant Gibbs measure
\[
 d\mu_\Phi(U)=Z_\Phi^{-1}
 \exp\!\left\{\sum_{p\in P}\Phi_p(U_{\partial p})\right\}dU. \tag{1}
\]
The usual orientation and translation symmetries can be imposed, but are
not needed below.  Gauge invariance means that every $\Phi_p$ is invariant
under independent vertex conjugations of the links on $\partial p$.

Let $\mathcal W_\Lambda$ be the bounded algebra generated by real and
imaginary parts of traces of closed Wilson loops.  For a bounded function
$F$ define its link oscillation by
\[
 \delta_\ell F=
 \sup_{U,U':\,U_e=U'_e\ (e\ne\ell)}|F(U)-F(U')|.             \tag{2}
\]
For two different links $r,s$ on the boundary of a plaquette, define the
mixed oscillation of that plaquette interaction by
\[
 J_{p;r,s}(\Phi)=
 \sup_{\omega,\omega':\,\omega_e=\omega'_e\ (e\ne s)}
 \operatorname {osc}_{U_r}
 \bigl[\Phi_p(U_r,\omega)-\Phi_p(U_r,\omega')\bigr],       \tag{3}
\]
where $\operatorname {osc}h=\sup h-\inf h$ and the links other than $r$
are kept at the displayed boundary values.  Set
\[
 J(\Phi)=\max_{p\in P}\max_{r\ne s\in\partial p}J_{p;r,s}(\Phi).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
This is a local, gauge-invariant interaction seminorm: adding a constant
to a plaquette term changes neither (3) nor the measure, and (3) is
unchanged by a gauge transformation of the boundary variables.

The link graph joins two distinct links when they lie in one plaquette.
A four-dimensional link belongs to six plaquettes and has three other
links in each, hence has at most $18$ link-graph neighbours.  Write
$d_\ell(X,Y)$ for the corresponding distance between link supports.

## 2. Conditional distributions and their contraction

Fix all links except $r$.  The conditional distribution following from
(1) is
\[
 d\mu_r^{\omega}(V)=
 \frac{\exp f_r^\omega(V)}{\int_{SU(3)}\exp f_r^\omega(W)\,dW}\,dV,
 \qquad
 f_r^\omega(V)=\sum_{p\ni r}\Phi_p((V,\omega)_{\partial p}). \tag{5}
\]
It is a conditional distribution on a link representative, not a claim
that a link is a physical observable.  Gauge fixing is unnecessary: (5)
is covariant under boundary gauge transformations, while all observables
used in the theorem below lie in the invariant algebra $\mathcal W_\Lambda$.

We use the following direct density estimate.  If $p\propto e^f$ and
$q\propto e^{f'}$ relative to a common probability measure, then
\[
 \|p-q\|_{\rm TV}\le e^{\operatorname {osc}(f-f')}-1.       \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, $p/q\le e^{\operatorname {osc}(f-f')}$, and integrating the
positive part of $p-q$ proves (6).

Suppose two boundary configurations in (5) differ only at $s$.  If $r$
and $s$ do not share a plaquette, the two conditional laws agree.  If
they do share one, (3), (5), and (6) give the Dobrushin influence bound
\[
 c_{rs}:=\sup_{\omega\sim_s\omega'}
 \|\mu_r^\omega-\mu_r^{\omega'}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le e^{J(\Phi)}-1.                                         \tag{7}
\]
Consequently every row sum of the influence matrix $C=(c_{rs})$ obeys
\[
 \alpha:=\sup_r\sum_s c_{rs}
 \le18\bigl(e^{J(\Phi)}-1\bigr).                            \tag{8}
\]

## 3. Volume-uniform, gauge-invariant mixing theorem

> **Theorem 1 (Dobrushin certificate on the Wilson-loop algebra).**
> Let $\mu_\Phi$ be (1).  If
> \[
> 18\bigl(e^{J(\Phi)}-1\bigr)=\alpha<1,                    \tag{9}
> \]
> then, on every periodic volume and for all $F,G\in\mathcal W_\Lambda$,
> \[
> \big|\langle F;G\rangle_{\mu_\Phi}\big|
> \le\frac1{1-\alpha}
> \sum_{r\in\operatorname{supp}F}\sum_{s\in\operatorname{supp}G}
> \delta_rF\,\delta_sG\,e^{-\gamma' d_\ell(r,s)},
> \qquad\gamma'=-\log\alpha>0.                             \tag{10}
> \]
> In particular, for two fixed local Wilson-loop observables the
> constants in (10) are independent of the periodic volume.

*Proof.*  Replacing link variables one at a time and using (7) gives the
standard single-site oscillation comparison: a change at $s$ can alter a
conditional expectation at $r$ by at most $c_{rs}$ times the original
oscillation.  Iterating this comparison gives the resolvent
$(1-C)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}C^n$ and the covariance estimate
\[
 |\langle F;G\rangle|
 \le\sum_{r,s}\delta_rF\,[(1-C)^{-1}]_{rs}\,\delta_sG.       \tag{11}
\]
This step is finite-dimensional on each torus, so the iteration is just a
convergent geometric series when (9) holds.  A product $(C^n)_{rs}$
vanishes for $n<d_\ell(r,s)$, because one influence step travels only one
link-graph edge.  Its row sum is at most $\alpha^n$.  Hence
\[
 [(1-C)^{-1}]_{rs}
 \le\sum_{n\ge d_\ell(r,s)}\alpha^n
 =\frac{\alpha^{d_\ell(r,s)}}{1-\alpha},                   \tag{12}
\]
and (10) follows.  The derivation was performed on representatives of
link configurations, but (10) is applied only to gauge-invariant $F,G$;
it therefore gives a bound on the physical Wilson-loop algebra without
using a non-invariant one-link expectation. $\square$

The right side has the H2 form after the fixed finite support sizes and
oscillations of $F,G$ are included in $C'\|F\|\|G\|$.  It is also stronger
than needed for a selected finite local algebra, since it controls every
bounded Wilson-loop polynomial at once.

## 4. An explicit open $SU(3)$ Wilson neighbourhood

For the Wilson plaquette interaction in the conventional normalization,
\[
 \Phi_p^{W}(U_{\partial p})=
 \frac\beta3\operatorname {Re}\operatorname {tr}U_{\partial p},
 \qquad \beta=\frac6{g^2},                                  \tag{13}
\]
changing one of the other three links replaces a staple $S$ by $S'$.  The
changed term in the conditional log density is
\[
 \frac\beta3\operatorname {Re}\operatorname {tr}\bigl[V(S-S')\bigr].
\]
Its absolute value is at most $2\beta$, since
$\|S-S'\|_1\le6$.  Its oscillation is therefore at most $4\beta$, so
\[
 J(\Phi^W)\le4\beta.                                       \tag{14}
\]
The condition (9) holds whenever
\[
 18(e^{4\beta}-1)<1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \beta<\frac14\log\frac{19}{18}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g^2>\frac{24}{\log(19/18)}.                                \tag{15}
\]

More usefully, let $\Psi$ be any gauge-invariant plaquette-local
perturbation satisfying $J(\Psi)<\rho$.  Subadditivity of (3) gives
$J(\Phi^W+\Psi)\le4\beta+\rho$.  Thus for every reference coupling with
\[
 4\beta+\rho<\log\frac{19}{18},                             \tag{16}
\]
Theorem 1 applies throughout the open interaction ball
$\{\Psi:J(\Psi)<\rho\}$.  Equations (10), (15), and (16) are a positive,
fully specified H2 neighbourhood for a $3+1$-dimensional $SU(3)$ lattice
gauge regulator.

## 5. Scope at the C interface

This certificate has three useful features: it is gauge-invariant at the
observable level, uniform in periodic volume, and stable under an explicit
local interaction norm.  It does **not** identify its strong-coupling ball
with the continuum crossover, provide H1 blocking transport, or construct
an OS continuum limit.  Those are separate positive tasks in the
conditional assembly theorem.  The result here is precisely the C/H2
component that can be proved without importing an unverified correlation
length.
