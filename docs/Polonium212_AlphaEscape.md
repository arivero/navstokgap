# Polonium-212 emission and elastic scattering on lead-208

An absolute alpha energy, a beta–alpha delay, and an incident elastic
yield are available from three independent experiments. Lead-208 is the
daughter in the decay and the target in the scattering experiment.

**Outgoing energy.** H. Del Castillo, R. Roos, A. Tejera and F. Alba,
“Absolute measurement of ²¹²Po alpha particle energy,” *Revista
Mexicana de Física* **20**, 17–29 (1971). The authors measured the
orbit radius in a one-metre magnetic spectrograph and the field with
proton magnetic resonance. Their calibration uses the measured proton
gyromagnetic ratio, Faraday constant, alpha mass and speed of light.
Table I gives five runs and a mean alpha kinetic energy of
$8784.85\pm0.31$ keV. This is a measurement on the emitted particle.
Obtaining the two-body relative energy requires adding the daughter
recoil using its mass and the same momentum. [Original article, pp. 24–26](https://rmf.smf.mx/ojs/index.php/rmf/article/download/975/738).

**Incident particle.** G. M. Hudson and R. H. Davis, “²⁰⁸Pb(α,α)²⁰⁸Pb
scattering near the Coulomb barrier,” *Physical Review C* **9**,
1521–1524 (1974). EXFOR subentry C2798.002 records the digitization of
Fig. 1. At $170^\circ$ and $16.486$ MeV the elastic differential yield
relative to Rutherford is $0.997$ with digitized error $0.049$. Later
rows depart from Rutherford as incident energy increases. EXFOR gives
a 5–9% range of quoted cross-section errors across the measurements.
The table reports an angle and a yield. It gives neither a measured
turning radius nor a signed impact parameter for any one ion.
[Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevC.9.1521) and
[IAEA EXFOR, C2798.002, row 53](https://github.com/IAEA-NDS/exfor_master/blob/d3e392e3e86a1c20e9094451d1e80a95fff5eaac/exforall/C27/C2798.x4#L53).

**Clock.** P. Belli et al., “The half-life of ²¹²Po,” *European
Physical Journal A* **57**, 215 (2021), arXiv:2107.04233v1. In
thorium-loaded liquid scintillator the authors identified a beta pulse
from bismuth-212 and a subsequent alpha pulse from polonium-212.
Figure 9 shows the time differences for recognized pairs and the fit
including accidental coincidences. They report $T_{1/2}=295.1(4)$ ns
and test the effect of varying the bin width from the oscilloscope
sampling interval of $0.05$ ns. Pair selection and pulse overlap matter
at short delays. Section 2.2 gives 50,340,611 recorded events in
216.67 hours in the second stage. These include backgrounds and use
an oscilloscope threshold. For an absolute gain one would have to
identify launches by the initial pulse alone, bound contamination,
and count qualifying late pulses against that launch sample.
[Authors' paper, §§2.2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04233v1).

**Mechanical recoil.** J. Wang, T. W. Penny, J. Recoaro, B. Siegel,
Y.-H. Tseng and D. C. Moore, “Mechanical detection of nuclear decays,”
*Physical Review Letters* **133**, 023602 (2024), arXiv:2402.13257v2.
Electric impulses calibrated the motion of the levitated silica
spheres. The recoil analysis uses one momentum projection and 83 of
257 charge-change events. Figure 4 compares the distribution with
alpha and other decays in the chain. It does not locate the emerging
alpha on a nuclear length scale. [Figs. 3–4 and experimental passages](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13257v2).

## Reading coverage

The energy, delay and recoil passages above were read from text
extracted from the original PDFs on 18 November 2026. The elastic
numbers were read from the IAEA transcription of the authors' figure.
Original articles and figures are linked at their providers. The
[central-force test](../notes/balaban-newton-time-blocking.md#a-clock-on-escape-through-a-central-force-barrier)
uses these measurements to ask about the work on the escaping pair.
The shell dimensions and the maximum lowering proposed there are
independent calibration targets.
