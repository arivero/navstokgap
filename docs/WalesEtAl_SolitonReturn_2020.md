# Wales et al. — two encounters with one optical barrier

Oliver J. Wales, Ana Rakonjac, Thomas P. Billam, John L. Helm,
Simon A. Gardiner and Simon L. Cornish observed a rubidium-85
bright solitary wave split, turn in a trap, and meet the same barrier
again. The population on the right after the second encounter varies
widely near a centred narrow barrier. [Published article, *Communications
Physics* **3**, 51 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-020-0320-8)
and [authors' supporting data, Durham University (2019)](https://doi.org/10.15128/r1sf2685090).

## Measurement and reading coverage

The two encounters take place at constant laser power in a magnetic
harmonic trap. The preparation is an attractively interacting cloud of
about 2,500 atoms. The narrow-barrier image sequence uses a 75 µm
centre-of-mass amplitude. Images are destructive, with a 10 µs
probe pulse after optical-state preparation. The measured narrow
barrier waist is $3.6\pm0.4\,\mu{\rm m}$ after a resolution correction.
The harmonic centre fluctuates by $1.3\pm0.3\,\mu{\rm m}$ rms
between releases. The authors compare the observed output distributions
with Gross–Pitaevskii dynamics. Their conclusion concerns interference
in a solitary wave. [Figs. 4–5 and Methods in the published PDF](WalesEtAl_SolitonReturn_2020.pdf)
(passages and original published figures).

The [local data extract](WalesEtAl_SolitonReturn_2020.zip) contains the
unchanged CSV at “Data DOI/Figure 5/a/Data.csv” in the Durham deposit,
repacked as a single-member ZIP. Both column groups have the headings Offset, Rec,
Err. The ten entries in the second group at an offset of about
$-0.950\,\mu{\rm m}$ have right-side fractions ranging from 0.106 to
0.783, averaging about 0.400. The fit errors in the individual rows
are distinct from the scatter across releases. The published Fig. 5a
plots means and standard deviations at each offset. Its Fig. 5b–c
histograms combine several offsets. The 2019 deposit follows the
earlier manuscript's figure ordering. Its Fig. 4 readme describes
spliced images, and the preprint describes postselection of the last
five centred narrow-barrier images. The 2020 Fig. 4 caption specifies
random selection. We use the offset table without substituting either
edition's image selection for an ensemble bound. [Preprint and Fig. 4
discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06083) (passage) and
[Durham deposit](https://collections.durham.ac.uk/files/r1sf2685090)
(readme, offset table and archive metadata).

The reported ratios use the total detected on the two sides. They
leave open the matched, release-normalized early central and late
far-side fractions and the exterior transit-time limits in the
[trajectory test](../notes/balaban-newton-time-blocking.md#two-images-of-a-returning-orbit).
