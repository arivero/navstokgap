# Schmidt et al. — kinetic energies of hydrogen fragments

L. Ph. H. Schmidt, T. Jahnke, A. Czasch, M. Schöffler, H. Schmidt-Böcking
and R. Dörner measured the dissociation of vibrationally excited hydrogen
molecular ions at Goethe University Frankfurt. A 2.5 keV molecular-ion
beam captured electrons from a cold helium beam. The two neutral hydrogen
fragments and the helium recoil were detected in coincidence. Their
flight times and positions give the kinetic energy release of the pair
and the change in projectile energy. The group resolved different initial
excitation bands within an identified final electronic channel.
[Published paper, *Physical Review Letters* **108**, 073202 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.073202)
and [authors' version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3899v1).

## Reading coverage

Read the apparatus and analysis passages and the original figures in
the authors' version on 18 November 2026. Fig. 1(a) plots the measured
energy bands against an **inferred** radius. The row centres marked
2 and 3 are about 0.24 eV apart. The analysis selects transverse
recoil below about $3\,10^{-24}$ kg m/s. A dissociation structure
beginning at 7.286 eV calibrated the fragment energy scale. The
neutral-fragment detector was 80 mm across, and the paper gives a
typical flight time near 1.5 microseconds. Rotational angular momentum
was unresolved. [Figs. 1–3 and apparatus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.3899v1#page=1)
(passages and figures).

Fig. 4(d,e) gives experimental blue circles on a **kinetic-energy**
axis, with the squared calculated overlap in black. The following
readings use the marker centres from the figure embedded in the
authors' PDF, the labelled axes and the 0.10 eV marker spacing.

The article does not give the event list, exact bin edges or the
joint migration matrix for the fragment energy and the recoil-selected
band. The rows are conditional on capture into the chosen channel.
The manuscript's discussion of Fig. 3 compares two reflection
approximations and uses their average to assign radii. The authors
find a change in transfer probability with radius and attribute the
minima in the measured energy spectra to cancellation of contributions
from different radii in the quantum overlap. [Fig. 4 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.3899v1#page=5)
(energy plots and pp. 3–5).

The group's apparatus page also shows the directly recorded correlation
of fragment energy with the helium recoil momentum. The longitudinal
component determines energy transfer. The transverse recoil changes
the selected collision geometry. This detector does not measure the
probability of populating its selected electronic channel at fixed
radius separately for both vibrational bands.
[Frankfurt group, apparatus and recoil plots](https://www.atom.uni-frankfurt.de/research/40_ion_collisions/40_dissociative_collisions/)
(figures and passages).

A separate nitrogen measurement tabulates mean release energy, most
probable energy and full width at half maximum for the two fragment
charge states. Table II gives the $\mathrm{Bi}^{57+}$ column used in
the central-force comparison, for charge pairs $(2,1),(3,1),(2,2)$.
The reported widths are FWHM. They do not supply energy variances or
third moments. The authors attribute the large widths to a spread of
molecular potential curves. [Siegmann et al., Table II and Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.62.022718)
(passage and table).
