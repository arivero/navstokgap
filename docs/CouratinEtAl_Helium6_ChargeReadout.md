# Couratin et al. — electron loss in the decay of one-electron helium-6

C. Couratin and collaborators measured a bare lithium-6 daughter
fraction of $0.02339\pm0.00036$ following beta decay of trapped
$^{6}{\rm He}^{+}$. The one-electron parent removes the possibility of
an Auger cascade. A direct collision between the emitted beta and the
bound electron is still possible. The authors give a parent binding
energy of 54.4 eV, a beta endpoint of 3.5 MeV and a maximum nuclear
recoil energy of 1.4 keV. [Published paper, *Physical Review Letters*
**108**, 243201 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.243201)
and [authors' manuscript dated 14 May 2012](https://in2p3.hal.science/in2p3-00709325v1/document#page=2).

## What was counted

The trap uses radiofrequency electrodes and hydrogen buffer gas.
A beta telescope recorded energy and position. A spectrometer timed
recoiling ions in coincidence with the beta. The authors required a
deposit above 0.4 MeV in the scintillator and a well-defined recoil
impact. They selected decays 50–149 ms into each trap cycle and used
the emptied trap at 151–200 ms to estimate background.
Two fitted flight-time distributions give the relative populations of
$^{6}{\rm Li}^{2+}$ and $^{6}{\rm Li}^{3+}$. The fit accounts for a
measured $(0.38\pm0.05)\%$ relative detection efficiency advantage for
the bare ions. The statistical uncertainty in the fraction is 0.00035
and the systematic uncertainty is 0.000070. [Figs. 2–4 and Table I,
HAL manuscript](https://in2p3.hal.science/in2p3-00709325v1/document#page=3)
(apparatus and analysis passages).

The authors' sudden-approximation calculation projects the initial
$1s$ state onto the daughter bound levels after recoil. Their estimate
of the finite-duration correction uses an outgoing spherical beta
wave at speed $c$ and time-dependent quantum perturbation theory.
It changes the predicted probability by $-0.00020$. The prediction
for the selected events is 0.02322. These equations do not measure the
work done by an individual beta on a classical electron.
[Eqs. (1)–(5), HAL manuscript](https://in2p3.hal.science/in2p3-00709325v1/document#page=4)
(calculation passages).

The ionic binding threshold can also be probed with electron-impact
ionization. Döhring et al. report a crossed-beam scan near 54.418 eV
and discuss voltage precision, contact potentials and space charge.
They use the reference threshold to calibrate the energy axis. Their
scan is on a separate ion beam. An absolute energy calibration and
an upper bound on excited parents in the decay trap would be needed
to transfer it to individual decays. [Döhring et al., Fig. 8 and
energy-scale discussion](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjs/s11734-026-02488-4)
(passage).
