# Preiss et al. — site-resolved return of one rubidium atom

Fig. 1(b) of *Strongly Correlated Quantum Walks in Optical Lattices*
shows separate releases of a single $^{87}$Rb atom per horizontal
guide. A digital micromirror device selects the starting column of a
680 nm lattice. The authors apply a magnetic gradient and lower the
axial lattice depth. The transverse lattice and vertical confinement
remain on. They freeze the distribution at each chosen time and image
the occupied sites. They report about 98% single-site loading fidelity.
For the one-particle walk they retain only rows with one detected atom.
[Preiss et al., *Science* **347**, 1229–1233 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1260364)
and [authors' version, Fig. 1 and Methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3100v2)
(passages and figure).

## Reading coverage

The figure caption reports approximately 200 realizations for panel (b).
The text gives a revival probability of $0.88(2)$ at the single origin
site averaged over six adjacent guides. The source also gives a best
individual-guide value of $0.96(3)$. The authors fit the lattice
parameters to the dynamics and express the optical depths in recoil
units. Their energy shift to hopping ratio for Fig. 1(b) is 0.56.
The counts used here are position measurements.

The following readings use the density cells in the authors' original
Fig. 1(b) and the adjacent linear colour scale. At release the central
cell reads about 0.96. At 5 ms the cells at $-7,-6,-5$ read about
0.04, 0.13 and 0.09. The cells at $+5,+6,+7$ read about 0.10,
0.10 and 0.07. At 11 ms the cells at $-1,0,+1$ read about 0.05,
0.88 and 0.03. Allowing about one colour interval per site gives
the conservative thresholds in the [three-position test](../notes/balaban-newton-time-blocking.md#a-single-atom-returning-in-a-tilted-lattice).
The figure supplies neither attempted-launch denominators for these
stops nor simultaneous confidence bounds. Its cell counts refer to
different releases at each time. [Authors' original figure and colour
scale](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3100v2#page=1) (experimental histogram).

Preiss describes the 680 nm lattice, pinning and microscope in the
apparatus chapters of his thesis. He reports a physics-lattice lifetime
of 12(2) s, an imaging-lattice lifetime of about 30 s, a fluorescence
exposure of 500 ms and a measured point-spread width of about 600 nm
at half maximum. The high occupancy-identification fidelity applies
after pinning. A bound on the distance a moving atom travels during
transfer, and a timing law for the unpinned interval, require
separate measurements. The harmonic confinement used in preparing
the initial cloud does not determine the full axial potential during
the walk. [Preiss, *Atomic Bose-Hubbard Systems with Single-Particle
Control*, §§3.2–3.5 and ch. 5](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-0d85-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)
(apparatus and preparation passages).

Geiger et al. also imaged oscillations in position space, with lithium
and a much larger cloud. Geiger describes measuring the magnetic
curvature by bare trap oscillations, residual confinement around 13 Hz,
and an additional optical contribution in the lattice. This illustrates
how to measure a curvature in inverse seconds. Each rubidium guide
requires its own force calibration. [Geiger et al., *Physical Review
Letters* **120**, 213201 (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.213201)
(position measurements) and [Geiger's thesis, §§4.3.4, 4.3.8 and 5.2](https://web.physics.ucsb.edu/~weld/publications/Geiger.pdf)
(calibration passages).
