## Source digest

Cavalieri answers Galileo's objection from the bowl and the cone: Galileo had argued that if all the lines of two equal surfaces are equal the method fails, since in the bowl and cone the sections remain equal while one ends in a point and the other in a circumference. Cavalieri replies that his indivisibles are planes, that equal parts removed from cone and bowl leave equal remainders until the null plane is reached in both, and that he does not compose the continuum from indivisibles but only shows that continua have the ratio of their aggregates of indivisibles. Aproino writes on the indivisible and the infinite as Galileo teaches them; Santini discusses indivisibles at length. For this project, the 1634 letter is the direct exchange on Democritus's dilemma between the two founders of the modern method, and it states the position that the project's cut estimator formalizes: compare aggregates, never compose.
