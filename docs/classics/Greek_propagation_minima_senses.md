# Theophrastus, Sextus and Vitruvius: the differentiae indexed to the senses, Diodorus's partless bodies, and the voice as spreading waves

> Source: three works in one [excerpt file](Greek_propagation_minima_senses_excerpt.txt), each with its own edition recorded there; retrieved and verified 2026-09-18.
> Rights: all three editions are public domain. Stratton 1917 and Gwilt 1826 are translations out of copyright; Mutschmann 1912 is the Teubner Greek.
> **The Sextus text is archive.org OCR of Greek**, so accents and breathings are unreliable and the passage is kept as served; the sense is not in doubt but the orthography is a verification target.

## What each supplies

**Theophrastus, *De sensibus*, the Democritus section, c. 320 BCE.** This is
where Democritus's differentiae appear *indexed to sensory qualities*, and it
is the text from which any parallel with the Laozi triad would have to be
argued rather than from *Metaphysics* A.4:

> Democritus has no uniform account of all \<the sensory objects\>: some he
> distinguishes by the size \<of their atoms\>, others by the shape, and a
> few by the \<atomic\> order and position.

Stratton's introduction adds that Democritus "would in general explain the
effect of a sensory stimulus by the shape of its constituent atoms; but …
he is also found explaining the effect by their size, or their position",
which is Theophrastus's complaint against him. So the differentiae are not
a fixed triad mapped onto the senses; they are applied unevenly, and
Theophrastus's objection is precisely that the mapping is inconsistent. That
weakens rather than strengthens a term-by-term comparison with
[Laozi 14](Laozi_ch11_ch14_void_and_triad_wikisource.md), and it does so from
the Greek side, independently of the Chinese recension swap recorded there.

**Sextus Empiricus, *Pyrrhoneion Hypotyposeis* III.32, c. 200 CE.** In the
doxography of material principles: *Διόδωρος δὲ ὁ ἐπικληθεὶς Κρόνος ἐλάχιστα
καὶ ἀμερῆ σώματα*, "Diodorus surnamed Cronus \[posits\] least and partless
bodies", listed between the atoms of Democritus and Epicurus and the
unjointed masses of Heraclides and Asclepiades. Diodorus is the Greek author
whose denial of motion rests on minima, and he is the twin of
[Vasubandhu's](Abhidharmakosabhasya_motion_atoms_GRETIL.md) *na gatiḥ* three
centuries earlier. This passage certifies the minima; **the motion argument
itself, "a thing never is moving but it has moved", is at *Adversus
Mathematicos* X.85--120 and is not verified here**, volume II of Mutschmann
being unavailable through the routes tried.

**Vitruvius, *De architectura* V.3.6--7, c. 25 BCE.** The Roman statement of
the wave model, with a point the Greek and Indian versions do not make:

> The voice arises from flowing breath, sensible to the hearing through its
> percussion on the air. It is propelled by an infinite number of circles
> similar to those generated in standing water when a stone is cast therein
> … But, whereas the circles in water only spread horizontally, the voice,
> on the contrary, extends vertically as well as horizontally.

So the same water-ring image that carries the Stoic account in
[Diogenes Laertius VII.158](DiogenesLaertius_sound_and_minima_Hicks1925_wikisource.md)
and Praśastapāda's *vīcī-santāna* is here corrected into a spherical wave,
and the correction is argued from the disanalogy with water rather than
asserted. Vitruvius also notes that obstruction to the first undulation
disturbs all that follow, which is the propagation chain again.

## Coverage and limits

Reading level `passage` for the three locations; `metadata` for the rest of
each work. Three related items are cited but **not** verified here and should
not be treated as readings: Diodorus's motion argument at Sextus *AM*
X.85--120; Boethius, *De institutione musica* I.14, which repeats the
water-ring image at almost exactly the 500 CE boundary; and Lucretius,
*De rerum natura* IV, on the speed of the *simulacra*.
