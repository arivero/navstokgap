## Source digest

A dialogue written in October 1676, on the crossing from England to
Holland, on the nature of change and the continuum as they bear on motion.
Pacidius leads Charinus through the case against "quietulae interspersae",
the little rests that Empedocles and "docti quidam viri" among the moderns
interposed to explain how one motion can be faster than another: the
motions between the rests must be continuous or instantaneous leaps, and
once continuous motion is admitted a rotating radius gives unequal speeds
without any rest. He then states the leap doctrine as transcreation, the
body annihilated in one place and recreated in another, and refutes leaps
by proportion: size makes no difference, so a leap relegated to smaller
bodies is relegated again, "nisi atomos scilicet admittamus". For this
project it is the Newton-age statement of the scale-selection objection to
a floor, used in [the continuity note](../../notes/leibniz-continuity-records.md).

## Passage anchors

- Printed pp. 605–606: "ex veteribus Empedocles et ex recentioribus docti quidam Viri quietulas quasdam interspersas asseruêre"; the regress "tametsi indefinite progrederer subdividendo ac quietulas indefinite exiguas atque indesignabiles, motulis ejusdem naturae miscerem, opus tamen et tempusculis atque lineolis foret"; the authors "capere non potuerunt quomodo motus unus alio celerior esse possit, sine quiete interspersa", answered by the rotating radius.
- Printed p. 609: "quietes interspersas (quas inutiles supra ostendi) et saltus admittendos esse quibus fiat ut mobile pluribus momentis haereat in uno puncto, et vicissim plura puncta absolvat uno momento".
- Printed p. 617: "mobile E cum aliquandiu in loco A fuerit, extingui et annihilari, et in B momento post iterum emergere ac recreari; quod motus genus possimus dicere transcreationem"; Charinus: "cùm enim magnitudo aut parvitas nihil ad rem faciat", the animalcula as much smaller than us as a human head is than the earth, "omnia proportione sibi respondent", so the leap "tam in parvis quàm in magnis vitari debet".
- Printed pp. 617–618: "saltus istos semper ad minora ac minora propelli et nusquam consistere posse in natura rerum ... nulla autem ratio est, cur huic potius quàm illi corpusculorum gradui saltus illi miraculosi ascribantur, nisi atomos scilicet admittamus, seu corpora ita firma ut nullam subdivisionem nullumve flexum patiantur"; Leibniz denies such bodies by the same argument.
