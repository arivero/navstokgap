# Aristotle: cuts, contacts and consecutive points

Source: [On Generation and Corruption, Book I](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/gener_corr.1.i.html),
H. H. Joachim translation, Internet Classics Archive. Selected passages read
2026-09-10, chapter 2, especially the discussion around Bekker 317a.
This is a passage digest, not a transcription of the whole chapter.

## Passages read

Earlier in the division argument Aristotle asks what remains besides a contact,
division or point. Joachim renders the relational premise:

> every contact is always a contact of two somethings

Later Aristotle examines the inference from divisibility at any chosen point
to division at all points simultaneously. He raises the supposed division
immediately next to a division at the centre and rejects the assumption of
consecutive points. Joachim renders the conclusion:

> division is not ‘immediately-next’ to division, nor composition to composition.

Both excerpts occur in chapter 2. They locate the ancient question behind
the project's distinction between possible cuts and jointly realised divisions.
The chapter first presents an argument for indivisible magnitudes, then
examines its faulty inference; that proposed atomic conclusion belongs to the
argument under examination.

## Edition comparison and coverage

The stored [Barthélemy-Saint-Hilaire translation](Aristotle_GC_I2_BarthelemySaintHilaire1866_fr_wikisource.md)
has the corresponding discussion in paragraphs 20–21 and earlier contact
discussion in paragraph 14. Its editorial notes acknowledge substantial
interpretive difficulty. Its paragraph 21 uses “instant” where Joachim uses
“position”; a direct inference about inserted time instants requires Greek
collation rather than choosing the convenient translation.
