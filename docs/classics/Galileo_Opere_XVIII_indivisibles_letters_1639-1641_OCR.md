## Source digest

Cavalieri, having read the Discorsi, praises Galileo for navigating “the immense ocean of indivisibles, of vacua, of infinites, of light” and hopes his own indivisibles will be illuminated by Galileo's. The exchange with Liceti carries Galileo's late statements on the continuum and the infinite. The 1641 letters to Torricelli, Rocca and Mersenne show Cavalieri placing Torricelli's Archimedean results by indivisibles and defending the method after Guldin. For this project, these are the “other friends” of the correspondence on the cut, and the 1639 letter records the reception of the First Day's paradoxes by the author of the method.

## Passage anchors

- Archive lines 3037–3049: the ocean of indivisibles and the promise of the Geometria's indivisibles being illustrated by Galileo's.
- Archive lines 18580 and 18609: results of Archimedes obtained by way of indivisibles, in the letters to Torricelli and Rocca.
