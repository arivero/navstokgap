## Source digest

Plutarch reports Chrysippus's reply to Democritus's question whether the surfaces of adjacent sections of a cone cut parallel to the base are equal or unequal: the surfaces are “neither equal nor unequal”, while the bodies are unequal. Plutarch rejects this as a licence to write whatever comes to mind, insisting that unequal bodies have unequal surfaces.

For this project, this is the passage that H04 verified in Xylander's 1570 Latin; the English and Greek here supply searchable text for the reception task H05. The P01 review keeps the comparison with C032 as a modern analogy only.

## Passage anchors

- English: “if a cone is divided by a plane parallel with its base, what is to be thought of the superficies of its segments, whether they are equal or unequal”, followed by “the superficies are neither equal or unequal, but that the bodies are unequal”.
- Greek: “τὰς μὲν ἐπιφανείας … μήτ᾽ ἴσας εἶναι μήτ᾽ ἀνίσους, ἄνισα δὲ τὰ σώματα”.
