Motion of a projectile: the arrow's flight is many motions, carried by an impression (5.1.16-18)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  iṣāv ayugapat saṃyogaviśeṣāḥ karmānyatve hetuḥ  | kvs_5,1.16 |
  nodanād ādyam iṣoḥ karma karmakāritāc ca saṃskārād uttaraṃ tathottaram uttaraṃ ca  | kvs_5,1.17 |
  saṃskārābhāve gurutvāt patanam  | kvs_5,1.18 |

  Translation (working, for orientation only):
    The particular conjunctions in the arrow, being non-simultaneous, are the ground of the
    plurality of its motions. The first motion of the arrow is from impulsion; the next from
    the impression produced by that motion, and so the next, and the next. In the absence of
    the impression, falling results from gravity.

The same falling rule stated for an unsupported body (5.1.7, 5.1.13)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  saṃyogābhāve gurutvāt patanam  | kvs_5,1.7 |
  prayatnābhāve gurutvāt suptasya patanam  | kvs_5,1.13 |

  Translation (working, for orientation only):
    In the absence of conjunction, falling results from gravity. In the absence of effort,
    the sleeping man falls by gravity.

Sound is produced from sound, and is non-eternal (2.2.36-37)
------------------------------------------------------------

  saṃyogād vibhāgāc chabdāc ca śabdaniṣpatteḥ  | kvs_2,2.36 |
  liṅgāc cānityaḥ  | kvs_2,2.37 |

  Translation (working, for orientation only):
    Because sound is produced from conjunction, from disjunction, and from sound.
    And from the inferential mark, it is non-eternal.

========================================================================

The atom is imperceptible for want of magnitude (4.1.6-7)
---------------------------------------------------------

  mahaty anekadravyavattvād rūpāc copalabdhiḥ  | kvs_4,1.6 |
  adravyavattvāt paramāṇāv anupalabdhiḥ  | kvs_4,1.7 |

  Translation (working, for orientation only):
    Perception is of what is large, because it consists of many substances, and from colour.
    In the atom there is no perception, because it does not consist of [many] substances.
