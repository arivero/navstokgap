# Vaiśeṣikasūtra: the arrow's flight, sound from sound, and the atom

## What it supplies

The first Asian source in this collection that states a **positive theory of
propagation**, rather than a paradox about it. Three things the Greek and
Chinese material here does not give:

- **Motion as a succession carried by an impetus.** VS 5.1.16 makes the
  arrow's particular conjunctions *ayugapat*, non-simultaneous, and that is
  the ground of the plurality of its motions. VS 5.1.17 gives the mechanism:
  impulsion (*nodana*) causes only the **first** motion, and each later one is
  caused by the *saṃskāra* deposited by the motion immediately before it.
  VS 5.1.18 ends the series when the impression is spent and gravity takes
  over. Where Zeno's arrow argues from instants that motion is impossible,
  this argues from non-simultaneous conjunctions that motion is *many*, and
  supplies what carries it across the gaps.
- **Transmission by a generated chain.** VS 2.2.36 derives sound from
  conjunction, from disjunction, **and from sound**, so what reaches the ear
  is a later member of a chain rather than the original event; VS 2.2.37
  makes sound non-eternal. The ripple and kadamba-bud similes for that chain
  belong to the later commentators, not to this layer.
- **A stopping rule for division.** VS 4.1.6–7 make perception depend on
  magnitude and deny it of the atom. The Nyāya side of the corpus states the
  rule outright; see the leads below.

| 5.1.7, 5.1.13 | `saṃyogābhāve gurutvāt patanam` … `prayatnābhāve gurutvāt suptasya patanam` | The same falling rule, stated twice |
| 2.2.36–37 | `saṃyogād vibhāgāc chabdāc ca śabdaniṣpatteḥ` … `liṅgāc cānityaḥ` | Sound produced from sound; sound non-eternal |
| 4.1.6–7 | `mahaty anekadravyavattvād rūpāc copalabdhiḥ` … `adravyavattvāt paramāṇāv anupalabdhiḥ` | The atom below perception |

Two readings differ slightly from secondary reports: this edition has
`karmakāritāc` where some report `tat-karma-kāritāc`, and the genitive
`śabdaniṣpatteḥ` where some report `śabdaniṣpattiḥ`. The excerpt preserves
the edition as served.

## Coverage and limits

Reading level `passage` for the four groups above, verified character by
character against the GRETIL file; `metadata` for the rest of the work. No
commentary is stored here, and the sūtra layer is what this file certifies.

**Leads not verified here**, from the 2026-09-18 research round and to be
treated as citations rather than readings:

- **Vātsyāyana, *Nyāyabhāṣya*** (c. 400–450 CE), on NyS 2.2.13: the sound
  born of a contact generates further sounds, and the distant hearer
  apprehends the member that has come near the ear, argued from wood
  splitting, since the axe-wood contact has ceased while the sound is still
  heard at a distance. Reported as the strongest pre-500 CE Asian statement
  that transmission takes time.
- **Vātsyāyana on NyS 4.2.16 and 4.2.25**: division halts where nothing
  smaller exists, "that than which there is nothing smaller we call the
  *paramāṇu*", with a reductio from equality of magnitude under endless
  division.
- **Candrānanda's *vṛtti*** on 5.1.16–17 (dated before c. 1000 CE by
  Jambuvijaya, 8th–10th c. by Isaacson, so **outside the window**), which
  argues the discreteness rather than asserting it.
- **Praśastapāda, *Padārthadharmasaṃgraha*** (contested, c. 4th c. in older
  scholarship, commonly c. 530–600 CE in modern work, so **at or past the
  boundary**): *vega* as "cause of an uninterrupted series of motions in a
  fixed direction", destroyed by conjunction with a tangible substance, and
  sound as a *vīcī-santāna*, a series like water ripples.
