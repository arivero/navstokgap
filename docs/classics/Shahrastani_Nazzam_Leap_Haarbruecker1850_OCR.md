## Source digest

The section reports that al-Naẓẓām agreed with the philosophers in denying the indivisible part and first proposed the leap when confronted with the objection that an ant walking from one edge of a rock to the other traverses what has no end: it traverses partly by walking and partly by leaping, illustrated by a rope on a beam in a well. This is the Arabic kalām form of the cut question, where the atomists keep indivisible parts and time-atoms and al-Naẓẓām keeps infinite divisibility at the cost of a leap. For this project, it is the discrete counterpart of the finite-speed bridge, in which a body is refined by cuts yet crosses a divisible interval.
