# Laozi 11 and 14: the void that is used, and the triad that cannot be examined

> Source: Chinese Wikisource, [道德經 (王弼本)](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/道德經_(王弼本)) and [老子 (帛書校勘版)](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/老子_(帛書校勘版)); local verbatim excerpt [`.txt`](Laozi_ch11_ch14_void_and_triad_wikisource_excerpt.txt); retrieved and collated 2026-09-18.
> Metadata: the received *Laozi* is a layered compilation, strata conventionally 4th–3rd century BCE. The Mawangdui silk manuscripts come from a tomb sealed in 168 BCE and are the earliest witness consulted here. **The Guodian bamboo slips, c. 300 BCE, carry only part of the received text and were not consulted**, so whether either chapter is attested that early is open in this file.
> Extraction: both chapters copied from each recension, with Wang Bi's commentary omitted.

## What it supplies, and what it does not

Two passages that a reader of this collection will want compared with Greek
atomism, recorded here so the comparison can be settled instead of repeated.

**Chapter 11, the useful void.** Thirty spokes share one hub, and *當其無,
有車之用*, in its nothing lies the cart's usefulness; likewise the vessel and
the room, closing with *故有之以為利, 無之以為用* — what-is provides the
benefit, what-is-not provides the use. This is a fourth ancient answer to the
question of what emptiness does, beside three the collection already holds.
For the Greek atomists the void is what permits motion; for
[Vasubandhu](Abhidharmakosabhasya_motion_atoms_GRETIL.md) the existence of
gaps between atoms would leave motion through them unobstructed, which is an
objection rather than a licence; for
[Umāsvāti](Tattvarthasutra_ch5_wikisource.md) a positive substance does the
permitting and space merely accommodates. Here the void is neither a
permitter nor an obstacle but the seat of function.

**Chapter 14, the triad.** Looked at and not seen, call it *yí*; listened to
and not heard, call it *xī*; grasped at and not got, call it *wēi*; *此三者不
可致詰, 故混而為一*, these three cannot be interrogated further, so they merge
and become one. The structural resemblance to Democritus's three differentiae
— *rhysmós*, *diathigḗ*, *tropḗ*, reported by Aristotle at *Metaphysics* A.4
985b as shape, arrangement and position — is a triad of irreducible
differentiae resolving into one, and the two are roughly contemporaneous.

**The evidence here weighs against a term-by-term correspondence.** The two
recensions swap two of the three names:

| Sense | Wang Bi | Mawangdui |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sight | 夷 *yí* | 微 *wēi* |
| hearing | 希 *xī* | 希 *xī* |
| touch | 微 *wēi* | 夷 *yí* |

The middle term is stable and the outer two are transposed, so the specific
words were not stably tied to the modalities within the Chinese transmission
itself. Two further differences bear on it. Democritus's three are properties
of atoms that *generate* phenomenal difference; these three are sensory
failures that *deny* phenomenal access. And Democritus's three are not
indexed to sense modalities at all, whereas the indexing is the whole form
here. The honest label is **convergence of form, with no transmission
route shown**, and what would change it is a text placing the Greek triad in
a Chinese context or the reverse, which this file does not have.

## Passage anchors

| Chapter | Wang Bi | Mawangdui |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | `三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用` | `卅輻同一轂 當亓无 有車之用也` |
| 14 | `視之不見名曰夷…此三者，不可致詰，故混而為一` | `視之而弗見 名之曰微…三者不可至計 故混而為一` |

Mawangdui writes 卅 for 三十, 亓 for 其, 无 for 無, 捪 for 搏, and 不可至計 for
不可致詰.

## Coverage and limits

Reading level `passage` for the two chapters in both recensions;
`metadata` for the rest of the work. Wang Bi's commentary is present in the
source page and deliberately not stored. The Guodian slips, the Heshang Gong
commentary and the Beida Han slips are not covered, and the first of those is
what a dating argument would need.
