# The Renormalization Group According to Balaban - II. Large fields

This second exposition restores large-field configurations in the finite-volume three-dimensional scalar model. Its main representation includes the history of large/small-field regions, their boundaries, fluctuations and polymer terms (Theorem 3.1).

Interesting points from the selected passages:

- Introduction, equations (5)–(10): the block kernel preserves the integral, and a partition of unity sums over possible small-field regions. Large-field configurations remain in the integration.
- Theorem 3.1 requires sufficiently large block sizes and sufficiently small running coupling. It carries normalization factors and characteristic functions as well as the effective interaction.
- Section 3.13, Lemma 3.15, distinguishes locality in the fluctuation field from locality in independent integration variables. Boundary terms arise when that dependence is localized.
- Section 3.14 applies the cluster expansion after this localization; it does not simply assume independent local fluctuations.

Project connection (inference): our good-region estimates need compatible boundary and bad-region terms through successive refinements. Dropping rare regions would change the measure. This scalar theorem supplies an architecture to examine, rather than a proved non-Abelian substitution.
