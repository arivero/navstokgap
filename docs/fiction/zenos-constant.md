# Zeno's Constant

*An alternate history. Cambridge, 1685.*

Newton found the missing motion in a ring of darkness.

He had laid a curved glass upon a flat one and admitted a narrow thread of
red sunlight. Rings gathered around the point of contact. With the slightest
pressure of his thumb, he could make the darkness travel.

On the desk behind him lay the polygon: a body advancing straight, receiving
an impulse, advancing again. He had divided its journey into a thousand pieces.
Another division would cost him ink. Nature seemed able to afford infinitely
many.

Yet here was something curious about nature's arithmetic. Two contributions
of light could meet and leave a darkness.

When Halley arrived, Newton was drawing little arrows around a circle.

“Have you finished the orbit?”

“I have found a difficulty with addition.”

Halley put down his hat.

---

The apparatus occupied the next winter.

It began as an improvement to an air pump and became a brass labyrinth, with
two passages, a screw for lengthening one of them, and a plate on which the
smallest corpuscles left a momentary spark. Newton spent more upon it than
he cared to enter in his accounts.

With either passage open, sparks appeared at a certain place. With both open,
that place became dark. A turn of the screw restored it.

Halley counted the arrivals. Newton counted the turns.

In his first reckoning, Newton assigned each possible passage a chance. The
chances added, and the dark place remained inexplicable. Then he gave each
passage one of his little arrows. Length expressed its strength; direction
expressed its stage in a cycle. He added the arrows and squared the length
of their resultant.

Two equal arrows pointing opposite ways gave precisely the absence they
had observed.

“The probabilities cancel,” said Halley.

“The arrows cancel. We calculate the probability afterwards.”

---

For every possible journey Newton made a reckoning of motion and force,
accumulated through time:

$$
S=\int\left(\frac12m\dot q^{\,2}-V(q)\right)dt.
$$

He tried making the arrow turn in proportion to this quantity. A constant
was required to convert the reckoning into an angle. He wrote it as
$\zeta$, leaving its value to the brass instrument.

For the two equally weighted passages, at one chosen outlet, his rule became

$$
P=\left|\frac{e^{iS_1/\zeta}+e^{iS_2/\zeta}}{2}\right|^2.
$$

A difference of $\pi\zeta$ gave darkness. A difference of $2\pi\zeta$
restored the light.

Weeks of sparks and screw-turns supplied a value: minute, positive, and the
same when he changed the corpuscles. Dividing his calculation into shorter
intervals left that value intact. Each interval contributed a smaller turn;
successive turns still composed into the same arrow.

“Zeno's constant,” he said.

“The gentleman who stopped the arrow?”

“The gentleman who asked us to account for its passage.”

---

He returned to the orbit.

Let a small body move under an attraction with potential $-k/r$. At a fixed
energy, Newton assigned the returning wave a phase gained around the circuit
of $\oint p\,dq/\zeta$. A stationary wave must join itself with the same
direction of its arrow after a complete turn.

Thus the circuit must contain a whole number of turns:

$$
\oint p\,dq=2\pi n\zeta.
$$

For his first trial he chose a circle. Its momentum was constant in magnitude,
so the condition gave $mvr=n\zeta$. His familiar balance of attraction and
centripetal acceleration gave $mv^2/r=k/r^2$. Together they yielded

$$
r_n=\frac{n^2\zeta^2}{mk},\qquad
E_n=-\frac{mk^2}{2n^2\zeta^2},\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots.
$$

Halley leaned over the page.

“You have numbered the heavens.”

“These very small heavens.”

There was a first permitted circle. Above it the circles multiplied, their
relative separations narrowing. The great planetary orbits belonged to such
enormous numbers that an astronomer could spend his life among them and
mistake the sequence for an unbroken continuum.

Newton turned back to his thousand-sided polygon. He could keep cutting.
What the dark rings had taught him was how to carry something through every
cut: a direction of agreement, shared between possible journeys.

He sharpened his pen and wrote beneath the lemma:

*Let the parts diminish. Retain their manner of agreement.*

“Will you publish it?” Halley asked.

Newton examined the little Greek letter.

“First we must persuade the printer to keep this.”

---

*Author's note:* This Newton and his corpuscle interferometer belong to an
invented history. The equations use modern notation: $\zeta$ plays the role
of $\hbar$, with $h=2\pi\zeta$. His circular-orbit calculation is the
Bohr–Sommerfeld toy construction, rather than the full quantum Kepler state.
The name honours Zeno of Elea and the arrow paradox. The story imagines the
optical insight, probability rule and measurements that lead him there.
