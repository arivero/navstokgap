# B49 source companion: position preparation and observability

## Result and bounded coverage

The audit finds an established local observability precedent for the R18
question. Hermann and Krener define indistinguishability through equality of
all allowed output records and prove that the observability rank condition
implies local weak observability. This supports using a derivative/rank test
for the augmented final-record map after the incoming probe momenta are
revealed. It does not prove R18's rank for the Hamiltonian apparatus, global
injectivity on a positive position box, a uniform inverse estimate, or a
preparation-independent action bound.

## Primary passage

**Robert Hermann and Arthur J. Krener, “Nonlinear Controllability and
Observability,” IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control AC-22(5), 1977,
pp. 728–740, DOI 10.1109/TAC.1977.1101601.** [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~krener/1-25/10.IEEETAC77.pdf).
Theorem 3.1 begins on printed p. 733; its proof on p. 734 states that if the differentials of
observability functions generated by iterated Lie derivatives have full rank
at a point, a nonsingular local coordinate map is one-to-one in a
neighbourhood, and the system is locally weakly observable. The same page's
Lemma 3.2 proof says indistinguishable initial states give equal values for
these iterated derivatives by differentiating compositions of the flows of
constant-input vector fields. Evidence level: the worker read an indexed
passage; coordinator visual review covers printed pp. 733–734 of the scan.
This is a control-theoretic result for input-output systems; its use
here is an analogy and a local mathematical implication, not a claim that
the mechanical setup has been shown observable.

## Direct matches and consequences for R18

The direct literature match is the logical shape

`full rank of output-derived differentials -> local one-to-one observation`

and the flow-differentiation route that generates those differentials. A
derived consequence for R18 is that the augmented map from receiver-shell
coordinates and unknown initial probe positions to all revealed records can
be tested by its Jacobian: full rank at the nominal point would give only a
local inverse under a stated smoothness hypothesis. To obtain the desired
positive preparation-width conclusion, R18 must additionally establish a
uniform singular-value margin on a compact box, or construct a contraction
with explicit self-mapping margins. Rank failure is only evidence that this
sufficient route fails; it does not by itself prove an ambiguity fibre.

The closest position-uncertainty/backreaction precedent in the retained
material is R06/R18 itself: unknown initial positions enter final momenta at
order lambda squared through receiver and clock reaction. B47's smooth
variational-flow result justifies differentiating that response, while B48's
contraction result supplies the possible compensation mechanism. No audited
external source in this bounded batch gives the specific four-probe
position-to-momentum block or a preparation-width lower bound.

## Source-to-model suggestion for R19

After adding final probe positions and the initial clock phase, form the full
record Jacobian with respect to receiver shell coordinates and the unknown
initial probe positions. Use Hermann–Krener's flow-derivative idea to state
the rank test, then check a uniform lower singular-value bound on the fixed
position box. If the rank is full with a compact margin, apply a local inverse
estimate and quantify the surviving receiver uncertainty; if not, identify
the kernel and test whether it integrates to an admissible common-record
fibre. Keep the rank conclusion separate from global injectivity and from
any universal action-scale claim.
