# H04 — cone printing and reception

Xylander's 1570 Latin *Moralia* contains the cone-section dilemma on printed
pp. 823–824. This establishes early printed availability. The next historical
question is its use by particular authors writing on indivisibles and calculus.

## Verified witness

Plutarch, *Moralia*, translated by Wilhelm Xylander, Basel: Thomas Guarin, 1570.
UB Basel, UBH Bc II 109a, VD16 P 3681.
[Institutional record](https://www.e-rara.ch/bau_1/content/titleinfo/602308);
[stable DOI](https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-1852).

The paragraph beginning *Porro autem vide quomodo occurrerit Democrito* at the
bottom of p. 823 presents the cut parallel to a cone's base and Chrysippus's
neither-equal-nor-unequal answer. Page 824 continues Plutarch's criticism.
The coordinator visually checked both pages on 2026-09-07 after Luna's OCR
reading. See the [p. 823 companion](Xylander1570_p823.md) and
[p. 824 companion](Xylander1570_p824.md) for saved images and transcription.

The IIIF sequence labels are `[839]` and `[840]`, canvases 603151/603152;
these differ from the printed page numbers. The institutional record marks the
scans Public Domain Mark.

Auffret's pp. 271–273 compare the cone response with convergence; pp. 275–276
discuss Leibniz's *Theodicy* §§332–336 and structural affinity with Stoicism.
The coordinator independently read these passages. This establishes modern
historical attention to the cone, while leaving early-modern mathematical
reception as an author-specific question.
