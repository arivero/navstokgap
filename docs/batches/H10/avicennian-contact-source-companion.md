# Ibn Sina: contact and the formation of an extended body

A translated passage of *The Healing: Physics* III.4 gives the closest
Persianate match found in H10 to Vasubandhu's static contact dilemma.

## Text and access level

Jon McGinnis, *Avicenna*, Oxford University Press, 2010, pp. 75–76,
quotes *Physics* III.4, 189.14–190.3. Book metadata is verified at
[Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/3244), DOI
10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195331479.001.0001. The translation was exposed in the
search index of the author's university-hosted PDF:

[University PDF route](https://www.umsl.edu/~philo/files/McGinnis%20Works/Avicenna-GMT%20series/Avicenna-OUP.pdf).

## Passage paraphrase

When three purportedly indivisible constituents touch in a chain, the middle
one distinguishes its contacts with its two neighbours and is thereby
conceptually divisible. Complete interpenetration instead leaves the aggregate
without increased magnitude. The target includes minimal positive extensions
claimed to be conceptually indivisible; it is broader than zero-sized points.

## Source idea and next retrieval

Borrow the distinction between internal partlessness, spatially distinct
contacts, and aggregate extension. Compare it with Vasubandhu's verse 12 as
an argument structure. A transmission claim would require a separate textual
history. Ibn Sina is a Persianate author writing this work in Arabic.

For a direct witness use McGinnis's parallel Arabic/English *The Physics of
The Healing* (2009), whose two-volume edition is listed by the
[distributing publisher](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/distributed/P/bo10581412.html).
The remembered millstone/concentric-radius argument is a second retrieval
target: *Physics* III.4, provisionally §12, around p. 295 in that translation.
This locator remains to be checked against the book. Preserve the equal-time,
unequal-distance question; do not yet attribute exact wording to Ibn Sina.
