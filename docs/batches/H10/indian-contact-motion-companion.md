# H10 India: contact, composition and motion

## Result

Two primary Sanskrit routes were checked. Vasubandhu's *Viṃśatikā*
(kārikās 11–15 in the GRETIL/Lévi–Ui text) gives a direct static-composition
dilemma: six simultaneous directional contacts make a supposedly partless atom
six-part, while six atoms in one location make the aggregate no larger than one
atom. Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* II.1–3 gives the corresponding motion
dilemma: motion is not found in the traversed, untraversed, or a third
“being-traversed” path. These are arguments about the intelligibility of
composition and predication, not a derivation of a physical cone, speed limit,
or positive action scale.

## Vasubandhu, *Viṃśatikā vijñaptimātratāsiddhi*

**Source and version.** GRETIL, “Vasubandhu: Vimsatika
vijnaptimatratasiddhi,” based on S. Lévi, *Vijñaptimātratāsiddhiḥ: Deux
traités de Vasubandhu* (Paris, 1925), with Hakuju Ui's corrections (Tokyo,
1953, pp. 20–21); input by Takamichi Fukita. The electronic text marks the
prose commentary and kārikās separately, and says it is for reference use.
[GRETIL source and edition note](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/6_sastra/3_phil/buddh/vasvvmsu.htm)
**Reading level: passage** (kārikās and adjacent Sanskrit commentary read;
critical print not independently collated).

The preceding kārikā 11 challenges accounts of the perceptible external
object as a whole, atoms or their aggregate, within Vasubandhu's argument for
vijñaptimātra. The two halves of kārikā 12 read:

> ṣaṭkena yugapad yogāt paramāṇoḥ ṣaḍaṃśatā |
> ṣaṇṇāṃ samānadeśatvāt piṇḍaḥ syād aṇumātrakaḥ || 12 ||

Working gloss: simultaneous junction with six entails six parts; if the six
occupy the same place, the aggregate would be only atom-sized. GRETIL places
prose between the verse halves. That commentary specifies six directions and
distinct places of junction; it is distinct from the quoted stanza.

Kārikā 13 and commentary challenge the response, attributed to Kashmiri
Vaibhāṣikas, that aggregates join although atoms do not. Kārikā 14 tests
directional differentiation (*digbhāga*), shadow and obstruction; 15 turns to
succession and differentiated perception. These passages concern the adequacy
of an atomist account of perceptual objects. The six-contact dilemma supplies
our static composition question, rather than a general verdict on discrete
mechanical models.

**Text anchors.** Use GRETIL's explicit numbering: six contacts **12**,
aggregate response **13**, directional argument **14**. “Touch” is a convenient English
rendering of *yoga/saṃyoga* (union/junction); “sides” renders directional parts,
not an independently asserted Euclidean surface.

## Nāgārjuna, *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*, chapter II

**Source and version.** GRETIL text, input by Douglas Bachman and revised by
Richard Mahoney; the TEI transformation records the same provenance. [GRETIL
text and metadata](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/6_sastra/3_phil/buddh/nagmmk_u.htm)
**Reading level: passage** (II.1–3 and the continuation through II.25 checked).

The requested opening stanzas read:

> gataṃ na gamyate tāvad agataṃ naiva gamyate /
> gatāgatavinirmuktaṃ gamyamānaṃ na gamyate // MMK_2.1 //
>
> ceṣṭā yatra gatis tatra gamyamāne ca sā yataḥ /
> na gate nāgate ceṣṭā gamyamāne gatis tataḥ // MMK_2.2 //
>
> gamyamānasya gamanaṃ kathaṃ nāmopapatsyate /
> gamyamānaṃ vigamanaṃ yadā naivopapadyate // MMK_2.3 //

Working paraphrase of the exchange: II.1 challenges locating traversal in the
already traversed, the untraversed, or a separately established
being-traversed. II.2 puts forward the counterproposal that motion occurs
where activity is, in what is being traversed. II.3 replies that
being-traversed **without traversal** (*vigamanam*) is itself untenable:
how then assign traversal independently to it? II.4–5 press the resulting
double-traversal issue. Preserve this objection/reply structure when borrowing
the passage. The subject is the dependence of motion, path and agent
descriptions, rather than an empirical claim that ordinary bodies never move.
Interpreting *gamyamāna* as a mathematical instant would add a modern premise.

## Source idea for the project

The useful bridge is deliberately narrow: Vasubandhu supplies a primary
argument that a static aggregate cannot be both made from distinct, partless
contacts and perceptibly larger than one constituent; Nāgārjuna supplies a
primary argument that a motion predicate cannot be assigned cleanly to either
completed, future, or a third intermediate path. Together they motivate a
testable distinction in the project: **static distinct contacts** concern the
composition map and its spatial locality, whereas an **arrow/trajectory** adds
an ordered path and a temporal predicate. Neither passage supplies a universal
positive action lower bound. Any use in navstokgap must state the extra modern
premises (state space, contact rule, temporal sampling, and limit) rather than
attribute them to these texts.
