## Source digest

The statement is the release note for the three preprints and the primary record of their status.
Four things in it bear on how the mathematics may be cited.

**Scope of what is public.** Three results are released: forced blowup with smooth forcing for IPM,
Boussinesq and 3D incompressible Euler. A fourth, hypodissipative Navier–Stokes, is claimed but
withheld, with the Lean verification unfinished and no writeup. Buckmaster describes it as suggestive
of a route to unforced Euler.

**Attribution.** The programme is assigned to Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa, who had been
constructing forced blowups for several years with rough forcing. The stated contribution of Alpöge and
Buckmaster is pushing that programme to smooth forcing and to Euler, with heavy LLM assistance.

**Dates and tooling.** Slow progress for most of a year, up to forced IPM with smooth force. On
2026-08-15 the Boussinesq and Euler results were obtained; Lean verification followed on 2026-08-22.
Claude, Codex with GPT-5.6 Sol, and later Astra were used; Astra for writeups and auditing only.
Buckmaster records that the collaboration was personal, with no institutional agreement.

**Presentation.** Buckmaster states that the Boussinesq and Euler writeups are closer to machine output
under human direction than to a paper written by a person, and calls the Euler writeup AI slop. He
attributes the early release to outside pressure rather than to readiness.

## Source-facing notes

Pages 2–4 record a dispute over an unpublished claim: an internal OpenAI model is said to have produced
a roughly 100-page proof of finite-time blowup for the forced Navier–Stokes equations, stated to
Buckmaster as "Existence of forced blowup in $\mathbb R^3$ and $\mathbb T^3$" with a smooth forcing
function, "option c and d in Fefferman". Buckmaster states plainly that he has not seen that proof, does
not know what the model did, and is not accusing anyone; he reports what he was told and when.

For citation purposes the OpenAI claim has no public artifact: no preprint, no statement of hypotheses,
no formalisation, and no confirmation from OpenAI of its content. Sébastien Bubeck's public reply of
2026-09-08 calls the circulating allegations false and inflammatory and promises a fuller response, and
addresses the conduct rather than the mathematics. Treat the forced-Navier–Stokes claim as unverified
and outside the citable record until an artifact exists.

Review scope: full statement, pp. 1–4. Personal and conduct allegations are recorded here only where
they bear on the status, dating and attribution of the mathematics.
