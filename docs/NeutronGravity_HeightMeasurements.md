# Neutron heights above a horizontal mirror

Nesvizhevsky et al. recorded neutron tracks at the end of a horizontal
mirror. Ichikawa et al. later magnified the spatial modulation with a
cylindrical reflector. These experiments provide two different
position readouts for the [test of gravitational turning heights](../notes/dirichlet-wall-impulse.md#counting-turning-heights-from-neutron-positions).

**Direct tracks.** V. V. Nesvizhevsky et al., *European Physical
Journal C* **40**, 479–491 (2005), arXiv:hep-ph/0502081v2, §4,
Fig. 15. After a separate two-scatterer measurement, the group
removed the second scatterer and installed a uranium-coated track
detector. The plotted ordinate is the number of events in each height
interval. The marker centres in the original vector plot give the
following approximate readings, rounded to a count. The connected
theoretical curve is a separate plot element. [Authors' version, Fig. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0502081v2#page=37).

| Bin centre on the published scale ($\mu$m) | Approximate events |
|---:|---:|
| −2 | 47 |
| 0 | 161 |
| 2 | 324 |
| 4 | 346 |

The bin spacing is $2\,\mu{\rm m}$. The authors estimated the zero
height from their data, reported resolution near $2\,\mu{\rm m}$ and
excluded three points at the sharp edge in their comparison. They
describe this run as a detector-resolution measurement with several
populated states. These four numbers use the authors' fitted origin.
[Study of the neutron quantum states in the gravity field, §4](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s2005-02135-y).

**Magnified positions.** G. Ichikawa et al., *Physical Review Letters*
**112**, 071101 (2014), arXiv:1304.1660v4, Figs. 1–3. The group measured
the horizontal speed distribution by time of flight. Its mean and
standard deviation were $9.4$ and $2.8$ m/s. They fitted 400 detector
bins of $8\,\mu{\rm m}$ width after the neutron reflected from a
cylindrical rod. Their estimated object resolution was $0.7\,\mu{\rm m}$.
The classical model assigned loss probabilities at the ceiling and
floor and specular reflection to survivors. The reported fits give
$\chi^2=377.6$ for 394 degrees of freedom in the quantum model and
$439.2$ for 395 in the classical model. A magnified pixel depends on
trajectory and instrument alignment. An eventwise height response
and a clock distribution conditional on launch are needed to evaluate
the proposed score. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.071101)
and [authors' version, Fig. 3 and fit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.1660v4).

**Time snapshots.** Abele and Leeb distinguish the CR39 detector at
two positions, $x=0$ and $6$ cm in Fig. 6, from the series of times
in Fig. 7. The latter figure is a simulation. It cannot be used as
a series of independent experimental exposures. [Abele and Leeb,
Figs. 6–7 and captions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.2953).
