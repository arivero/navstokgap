Newton’s force comparison and the renormalization of a trajectory

Markdown source · PDF

6.1 Sol Max, 19 November 2026. Two ellipses can approach the same moving circle while their relative displacement still determines the force gradient in arbitrarily short times. There is a mechanical requirement that the ellipses can satisfy separately and fail together. At fixed positive angular momentum, let each have energy \(e>0\) above the circular minimum. Increase the orbital frequency of the linear central force from \(\omega\) to \(b\omega\), \(b>1\). Either ellipse can reach a final ellipse with the same excess \(e\). Under any one change of strength applied to both, at least one finishes with excess at least \(be\). The exact least possible worst error in preserving \(e\) is \((b-1)e\), even for smooth positive forces.

This answers two questions about Newton’s revision. His comparison of nascent displacements has a joint classical realization. Implementing an equal-energy identification by a common drive is a stronger demand with a sharp classical obstruction. Its absolute cost tends to zero as the orbits coalesce. For a convergent one-step discretization the classical evolution is a fixed point of blocking on the space of transfer maps. We give the scale law for time decimation and insertion of one point, an exact blocking rule on a physical momentum grid, and a convergent scheme whose single bare coupling fails to close after one block.

What the revision asks of two motions

Book I, Lemma X of the London Principia (1687) specifies a regular force. In Cambridge (1713) Newton specifies a finite force, either constant or continually increasing or decreasing. In Corollary 2 he compares departures generated by proportional forces applied at corresponding parts of similar figures. Corollaries 3 and 4 allow different forces and times and recover the force ratio from the nascent displacements divided by the squared times. Proposition VI expressly gives Corollary 4 as another way to obtain the orbital force formula. In the preceding Lemma IX he holds the comparison segment \(Ae\) fixed as the original points coalesce. The segment normalizes the geometry, and the squared-time ratio in Lemma X compares the intervals. Transporting a family between strengths at a prescribed energy would be a further operation. 1687, Lemma X and 1713, Lemma IX and 1713, Corollaries 2–4 and Proposition VI (diplomatic transcriptions, passages).

For equal masses, the modern vector form uses the departure \(q(t)-q(0)-t\dot q(0)\) from the inertial tangent. Its coefficient of \(t^2/2\) is acceleration. Acceleration times the common mass gives force. Requiring this comparison to work while the two starting states also approach each other strengthens the historical fixed-pair limit. The companion formalization derives Corollary 4’s ultimate ratio from the premises of Corollary 3 and a shared proportionality constant. The formal statement makes explicit where the force law enters. NewtonLean, Corollary IV at the cited revision (theorem statements and premises).

Two ellipses with a common clock

Use Newton’s Proposition X, with acceleration \(-\omega^2q\) in the plane. Fix the mass \(m>0\), angular frequency \(\omega>0\) and angular momentum \(J>0\). Choose \(A>B>0\) with \(m\omega AB=J\), and put \(d=A-B\). The same force acts on the following two test bodies, with the same origin, axes and time coordinate,

\[ q_1(t)=(A\cos\omega t,B\sin\omega t),\qquad q_2(t)=(B\cos\omega t,A\sin\omega t). \]

They start at opposite radial turning points on the same ray, with tangential velocities in the same direction. Their separation has constant length \(d\), and their velocity difference has length \(\omega d\). As \(d\) tends to zero they approach the same timed circle of radius \(\sqrt{J/(m\omega)}\). Both have energy \(E\), circular minimum \(E_c=\omega J\), and radial action \(I_r=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p_r\,dr\). The classical action formula at fixed \(J\) gives

\[ e=E-E_c=\tfrac12m\omega^2d^2,\qquad I_r=\frac{e}{2\omega}=\frac{m\omega d^2}{4}. \tag{1} \]

Here \(I_r\) labels a particular ellipse. The angular action \(J\) stays fixed in the limit. Newton, 1713, Proposition X (passage) and classical radial actions, equations (10)–(11).

Write \(\Delta q=q_1-q_2\) and take the component along their common launch ray \(x\). Lemma X’s normalized relative tangent departure is

\[ \frac{2[\Delta q_x(t)-\Delta q_x(0)-t\Delta\dot q_x(0)]}{d\,t^2} =\frac{2(\cos\omega t-1)}{t^2} \longrightarrow-\omega^2 . \tag{2} \]

The error is at most \(\omega^4t^2/12\), independently of \(d>0\). Thus any joint approach \(d,t\downarrow0\) gives the same force gradient per unit mass. At \(d=0\) the quotient refers to a limit of pairs. The mechanical input to its normalization is their actual launch separation.

What happens on Newton polygons. Give both bodies the same partition \(\pi\) of \([0,t]\). On a cell of length \(h_i\) apply the impulse before or after free flight, at time \(s_i\) equal to the left or right endpoint. Recompute both histories when a point is inserted. By linearity the relative \(x\) coordinate divided by \(d\) is a single polygon \(u\) starting at \(1\) with zero velocity. Summing the drift due to each impulse gives

\[ u(t)=1-\omega^2\sum_i h_i(t-s_i)u(s_i). \]

The formula with only past kicks also holds at any intermediate time. Writing \(U=\sup_{[0,t]}|u|\) gives \(U\le1+\omega^2t^2U\). For \(\omega t\le1/\sqrt2\) this gives \(|u(s)-1|\le2\omega^2t^2\). The endpoint quadrature for \(\int_0^t(t-s)\,ds\) has error at most \(\sum_i h_i^2/2\). With \(|\pi|=\max_i h_i\), the two estimates yield

\[ \left|\frac{2(u(t)-1)}{t^2}+\omega^2\right| \le \omega^2\frac{|\pi|}{t}+4\omega^4t^2. \tag{3} \]

It suffices to refine within the shrinking interval, \(|\pi|/t\to0\), with arbitrary \(d>0\). On a single cell of length \(t\), \(2(u(t)-1)/t^2\) is \(-2\omega^2\) for a kick before flight and zero for a kick after flight. Both meshes shrink in absolute time. The general fixed-duration polygon convergence theorem is used with its common-force Lipschitz hypothesis. Mixed impulse orders and finite-time convergence.

A requirement that holds separately and fails for the pair

At the final source frequency \(b\omega\), an ellipse with the same excess \(e\) would have radial action \(e/(2b\omega)\). Is there one mechanical change of the source that realizes this comparison for both launches? Allow any prescribed finite-duration, smooth, positive isotropic frequency \(\Omega(t)\) joining \(\omega\) to \(b\omega\), constant near its ends. It is common to both bodies and cannot depend on which launch it is driving. They exert no force on each other. At frequency \(\nu\) the Hamiltonian is \(H_\nu(q,p)=|p|^2/(2m)+m\nu^2|q|^2/2\). Define the final excess for launch \(i\) by \(e_{f,i}=H_{b\omega}(q_i,p_i)-b\omega J\).

Theorem. For the pair above and \(b>1\),

\[ \inf_{\Omega}\max_{i=1,2}|e_{f,i}-e|=(b-1)e. \tag{4} \]

One smooth positive drive attains the infimum for all these pairs at once. For either launch separately, another smooth positive drive can give \(e_{f,i}=e\).

Proof. At frequency \(\nu\) write \(X=\sqrt{m\nu}\,q\), \(Y=p/\sqrt{m\nu}\). These coordinates have units of square root of action and \(H_\nu=\nu(|X|^2+|Y|^2)/2\). The drive has the same two-by-two matrix \(S\) on \((X_j,Y_j)\) for each Cartesian component \(j\), using initial and final units at their respective ends. The Hamiltonian transfer and both changes of units have determinant one. Let \(l_1,l_2\) be the squared lengths of the columns of \(S\). The two launches exchange the weights \(m\omega A^2,m\omega B^2\) of those columns. Consequently their arithmetic mean obeys

\[ \frac{E_{f,1}+E_{f,2}}2=bE\,\frac{l_1+l_2}{2}\ \ge\ bE, \tag{5} \]

since \(l_1+l_2\ge2|\det S|=2\). Subtracting the common final circular energy gives \(\max_i e_{f,i}\ge be\). This is a comparison of two specified solutions. The established oscillator inequality for a uniform ensemble of initial phases uses the same transfer matrix. Equation (5) identifies two coalescing fixed-\(J\) launches which already realize its mean exactly. Robnik and Romanovski, equations (9)–(12) and the following mean-energy inequality (passage).

The lower bound holds even before the trajectory limit. Each free drift and each isotropic central kick has determinant one on a Cartesian coordinate and its momentum, and preserves \(J\) in the plane. Any common partition and common kick placement therefore give (5) exactly for the two discrete endpoint energies.

For attainment, the positive intermediate frequency \(\Omega_p=\sqrt b\,\omega\), run for \(\pi/(2\Omega_p)\), has the quarter-period map \((q,p)\mapsto(p/(m\Omega_p),-m\Omega_p q)\). Switching to \(b\omega\) gives \(E_f=bE\) for every input. There is also an exactly smooth realization. Take a smooth positive scale \(\rho\) constant at 1 initially and at \(b^{-1/2}\) finally. The Ermakov prescription \(\Omega^2=\omega^2/\rho^4-\ddot\rho/\rho\) preserves the endpoint oscillator action \(E/\nu\) for every phase. Stretching the duration makes \(\Omega^2>0\) throughout. Thus both excesses equal \(be\), which attains (4). Chen et al., scaling equation and endpoint conditions (passage, used for compression).

For launch 1 choose instead \(\Omega_p^2=b\omega^2 B/A\). At the end of its quarter period the position and momentum are perpendicular with \(|p_f|=mb\omega|q_f|\). The final orbit is the circle and \(e_{f,1}=0\). For launch 2 interchange \(A,B\). Varying \(\Omega_p\) continuously from the value for the chosen circle to \(\sqrt b\,\omega\) therefore brackets the desired excess strictly between 0 and \(be\). Smooth both switches over a sufficiently short positive time. Continuous dependence of the linear transfer on the ramps preserves the strict bracket for this fixed \(e>0\), and continuity in \(\Omega_p\) gives the target exactly. Time spent at the final strength can equalize the durations without changing either final energy. This proves the separate assertion. \(\square\)

At \(b=2\) the best common drive misses the equal-excess target on at least one orbit by \(e\), one full initial excitation. In final radial-action units the sharp error is \((1-1/b)I_r\). The fractional error survives arbitrarily fine comparison of neighbouring trajectories. The absolute error vanishes quadratically with \(d\). In particular the limiting moving circle itself admits a common drive to the final circle.

A Kepler bound from the whole radial orbit

For the potential \(-m\mu/r\), take \(m,J,\mu>0\). At fixed \(J\) the bound radial action \(I\) and energy above the circular minimum obey

\[ e_\mu(I)=\frac{m^3\mu^2}{2} \left(\frac1{J^2}-\frac1{(J+I)^2}\right). \]

The closed radial curve of excitation \(e\) bounds a disk of canonical area \(2\pi I\) in \((r,p_r)\). This is the action of the radial oscillation. Kepler action and its circular limit and Ramond and Perez, radial-action formula in the Kepler limit (passage following equation (2.7)).

Increase the attraction to \(\lambda\mu\), \(\lambda>1\). For any smooth rotationally invariant Hamiltonian drive with these endpoints whose reduced finite-time flow exists on a neighbourhood of the initial closed disk, at least one initial phase finishes with

\[ e_f\ \ge\ \lambda^2 e. \tag{6} \]

The disk argument uses conservation of phase volume, also used for the classical minimal-work principle. Here the observable is the maximum over initial phases at fixed radial action. Allahverdyan and Nieuwenhuizen, Phys. Rev. E 75, 051124 (abstract).

Indeed \(e_{\lambda\mu}(I)=\lambda^2 e_\mu(I)\). The target disk at that excitation has exactly the initial disk’s area. If the entire image of its boundary lay strictly inside the target disk, its enclosed image would have strictly smaller area. The reduced Hamiltonian flow preserves \(dr\,dp_r\). This proves (6), including the case in which a phase escapes to nonnegative energy. Take \(0<e<m^3\mu^2/(2J^2)\) so that the initial disk is compact. There are no walls or thermal preparations in this comparison. The proof finds a phase on the entire radial orbit. Identifying a prescribed two-phase witness for a nonlinear Kepler drive would require further information about its transfer.

Frequency doubling, decimation and point insertion

There are two senses in which Newton’s construction resembles renormalization. They admit a direct comparison in this example.

Comparing source strengths. At fixed \(J,e\), the map on the labels of ellipses is

\[ (\omega,I_r)\ \longmapsto\ (b\omega,I_r/b). \]

These maps compose by multiplication of \(b\) and preserve \(2\omega I_r=e\). For \(b=2\) the radial action halves. This resembles a Kadanoff change of scale with a specified quantity held fixed. It compares two different force strengths. Equation (4) gives a mechanical way to distinguish this map from a common time evolution of the two orbits. Kadanoff groups spins into cells, and Wilson and Kogut allow effective interactions to change as degrees of freedom are eliminated. Kadanoff, 1966, cell construction and Wilson and Kogut, 1974, pp. 82–83 and §12 (passages).

Blocking actual trajectory data. Hold the source and orbit fixed. At time spacing \(h\), every Cartesian position component satisfies \(q_{j+1}+q_{j-1}=2c(h)q_j\), where \(c(h)=\cos(\omega h)\). Keeping even sites gives the exact recurrence at spacing \(2h\),

\[ c(2h)=2c(h)^2-1. \tag{7} \]

The decimated data describe the same timed orbit with its original radial action. Doubling \(\omega\) at fixed \(h\) gives the same recurrence coefficient by changing the source. For instance the position response to initial momentum in time \(2h\) is \(\sin(2\omega h)/(m\omega)\), whereas the response at frequency \(2\omega\) in time \(h\) is \(\sin(2\omega h)/(2m\omega)\). The two operations agree on the trace of the transfer and can disagree on the transfer itself.

Inserting one point and changing the reference interval. Suppose \(0<\omega h<\pi\). Given the two endpoints, the unique intermediate position at time \(a h\), \(0<a<1\), is

\[ q(ah)=\frac{\sin((1-a)\omega h)\,q(0)+\sin(a\omega h)\,q(h)} {\sin(\omega h)}. \tag{8} \]

The subinterval transfers compose to the old endpoint transfer. This is the classical oscillator instance of the established perfect discretization construction. The Gell-Mann–Low aspect appears when one expresses the same physics using a coefficient fixed at a new reference interval. The change of coefficient and its differential form here are

\[ \Sigma_s(c)=\cos(s\arccos c),\qquad h\,\frac{dc}{dh}=-(\arccos c)\sqrt{1-c^2}. \tag{9} \]

The invariant is \(\arccos(c(h))/h=\omega\). Inserting one point uses \(\Sigma_a(c)\) and \(\Sigma_{1-a}(c)\) for the two new coefficients. One has \(\Sigma_s\Sigma_v=\Sigma_{sv}\) wherever the initial, intermediate and final angles lie in \((0,\pi)\). On that branch \(\Sigma_2\) is precisely (7). Outside it different frequencies can give the same sampled coefficient, and at \(\omega h=\pi\) the endpoints do not determine the inserted point. This supplies an exact scale-composition example of the Gell-Mann–Low functional equation idea. The running quantity in (9) is a coefficient of a classical recurrence. Gell-Mann and Low, 1954 (abstract on scale functional equations), Bahr, Dittrich and Steinhaus, §3 (harmonic refinement passage) and Rivero, 1998, equations (5)–(10) (passages on scale control and insertion).

How generally does a discretization define a renormalization flow?

The continuum evolution as a fixed point. Let \(A_{t,s}\) be any continuous one-step rule on the full state for \(s<t\). A blocking transformation on such rules is

\[ (\mathcal B A)_{t,s}=A_{t,r}\circ A_{r,s},\qquad r=(s+t)/2. \]

Assume the products on all partitions of each finite interval converge, as their mesh tends to zero, locally uniformly in the launch state. Assume the intermediate states stay in compact subsets of the common domain for compact sets of launches. Write \(\Phi_{t,s}\) for this limit. Then \((\mathcal B^n A)_{t,s}\to\Phi_{t,s}\), since these are the products on the dyadic refinements. More strongly, refine \([s,u]\) and \([u,t]\) separately for any \(s<u<t\). Their union is another vanishing-mesh partition. Local uniform convergence on the intermediate compact set and continuity of the limiting transfer give

\[ \Phi_{t,s}=\Phi_{t,u}\circ\Phi_{u,s}. \]

Thus the continuum law is fixed by blocking at any inserted point. When the method converges to Newton’s initial-value problem this fixed law is the classical flow. This argument takes convergence as a hypothesis. Concrete estimates establishing it for the two Newton impulse orders are available. All-partition construction.

What a change of variables preserves. Consider any method with specified observations at each resolution. Let \(\pi'\) refine \(\pi\). Let \(S_\pi x\) denote what the exact curve \(x\) would give at resolution \(\pi\), and \(R_{\pi\pi'}\) the restriction of fine data to coarse data. Require \(R_{\pi\pi'}S_{\pi'}=S_\pi\) and require successive restrictions to compose. Taking the image of a set of fine histories under \(R\) then gives exact, associative blocking on sets of histories. An invertible change of finite data \(C_\pi\) at each resolution gives the conjugate restriction \(C_\pi R_{\pi\pi'}C_{\pi'}^{-1}\). It has the same composition law, including in a full discrete Fourier basis. Retaining selected Fourier modes introduces a further projection whose closure has to be tested. Information discarded by restriction can prevent its inversion.

For an approximate method let \(x_\pi\) have error at most \(\eta_\pi\) relative to \(S_\pi x\) in a chosen norm. If the restriction is \(L\)-Lipschitz, coarse predictions satisfy

\[ \|R_{\pi\pi'}x_{\pi'}-x_\pi\| \le L\eta_{\pi'}+\eta_\pi . \tag{10} \]

This triangle inequality gives asymptotic agreement whenever the right side tends to zero. Sampling a common timed curve in the sup norm has \(L=1\). Equation (3) illustrates why a normalized observable with shrinking denominator needs its own estimate. Exact closure on a proposed set of retained variables \(Pz\) has a further condition. For a fine block transfer \(F\), all fine states with the same \(Pz\) must have the same \(PFz\). This condition is necessary and sufficient to define the coarse deterministic map on the image of \(P\). Closure in a prescribed family of couplings also requires the resulting map to belong to that family.

For a countertest take a convergent kick-then-drift discretization of the same oscillator. In \((q,\dot q)\) its time-\(h\) step at stiffness per mass \(k=\omega^2\) is

\[ M_h(k)=\begin{pmatrix}1-kh^2&h\\-kh&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

The lower right entry of \(M_h(k)^2\) is \(1-kh^2\). Every \(M_{2h}(k')\) has lower right entry one. Thus even arbitrary renormalization of the single stiffness cannot give the blocked full-state step at finite \(h>0\), \(k>0\). In the space of determinant-one transfer matrices the exact block is \(M_h(k)^2\). This example gives both the flow on enlarged data and the failure of a particular one-coupling description. Convergence for arbitrary harmonic partitions.

A grid in physical momentum. One can use momentum itself as the mesh coordinate. For one Cartesian harmonic mode of energy \(E_x>0\), put \(P_*=\sqrt{2mE_x}\). On a monotone arc retain the sign \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}q\). From the energy law \(q(p)=\sigma\sqrt{P_*^2-p^2}/(m\omega)\). For a cell of momenta \(p_0,p_1\) traversed in the direction of motion its elapsed time is

\[ \tau_\sigma(p_0,p_1)=-\frac{\sigma}{\omega} \big[\arcsin(p_1/P_*)-\arcsin(p_0/P_*)\big]. \tag{11} \]

Inserting any intermediate momentum composes by addition of cell times. In particular, take midpoint approximations to \(1/(\omega\sqrt{P_*^2-p^2})\) on a nonuniform momentum grid. Blocking adjacent cells replaces their inverse speeds in momentum space, \(dt/|dp|\), by their momentum-width-weighted mean. The rule is associative and preserves elapsed time. On compact subintervals of \((-P_*,P_*)\) the midpoint sums converge uniformly for cumulative time. This extends to the turning points. The integrand is convex and integrable, so every midpoint sum over a terminal layer is bounded by the exact integral over that layer enlarged by one mesh cell. This bound tends to zero with the layer width and mesh. Linear interpolation and inversion of the strictly monotone cumulative time recover the timed arc. At a turn one joins signed arcs and keeps their order. Forget the sign and the same momentum \(p=0\) allows \(q=\pm P_* /(m\omega)\), giving opposite \(\dot p\). A deterministic rule on that one retained momentum fails the criterion above. The momentum mesh width has units of momentum. Neither a time lattice nor a quantum phase was used in obtaining (11).

Consequence for STATE

The bare comparison in Lemma X survives joint refinement of time and of the two launches. Requiring a single change of force to preserve their common excess energy would add a condition that already fails in classical mechanics by (4). Its sharp relative error stays finite as the circle is approached, while its absolute action cost tends to zero. In the thermal problem the equilibrium work identity gives the ratio an entropy consequence and the Nernst heat theorem independently constrains that entropy. Planck’s December 1900 paper introduced the energy element \(h_P\nu\), with \(\nu\) in cycles per unit time, when counting distributions of energy among resonators in equilibrium. Thermal central-force proof and Planck, 1900, pp. 82–83 in translation (passage on energy elements).

The mechanical inputs are \(m,J,\omega,d\), the timed source profile and its duration. The inverse-square comparison adds \(\mu\), of dimension length cubed per time squared, and its chosen excitation. The time grid uses a spacing \(h\) in seconds. The momentum grid also fixes the component energy \(E_x\), hence the momentum amplitude \(P_*\), and uses cell widths in momentum units. The cut fraction \(a\) and the scale factors \(b,\lambda\) and \(s\) in (9) are dimensionless. The physical reduced phase constant \(\hbar\), with \(h_P=2\pi\hbar\), never enters. Even at fixed dimensionful reference \(J\) the entire construction admits classical dynamics with zero phase scale. An exclusion from the trajectory limit would require another principle about which comparisons the mechanics must realize.
