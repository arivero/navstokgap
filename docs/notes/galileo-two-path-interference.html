Galileo’s comparison as two-path interference

Markdown source · PDF

A coherent, endpoint-matched comparison of Newton’s impulsive chord with Galileo’s constant-force parabola has action difference

\[ K_\tau:=\Delta S=\frac{F^2\tau^3}{24m} =\frac{F}{4v}A_{\rm inertial,fall}=\frac{\tau\Delta E}{12}. \]

At supplied reduced phase parameter \(\hbar>0\), its optimal equal-prior error against the zero-phase reference, using \(n\) independent controlled passes and an arbitrary joint measurement, is exactly

\[ p_{\rm err}^{(n)}=\frac12\left(1- \sqrt{1-\cos^{2n}\!\left(\frac{K_\tau}{2\hbar}\right)}\right). \]

For fixed \(n,\hbar\), refinement sends this error to \(1/2\). At fixed \(\tau>0\), the coherent error oscillates as \(\hbar\downarrow0\) and has no limit. A specified averaging of the inverse phase parameter gives a genuine noncommutation theorem for coherence; a specified tunable comparison gives one for distinguishability (Theorem 3). These prescriptions make precise the extra operation needed in the proposed classical-first limit.

The resulting action threshold is conditional on the phase rule, the Born rule and the resources of the comparison. It re-expresses the polygon-lift phase bound through the halved functional. Theorems C and E of the fifth-postulate note constrain different records with the same supplied quantum action constant. Positivity of that constant remains an independent physical obligation. All results below are proved under their displayed hypotheses; the scale-selection step remains open.

The symbol \(\hbar\) is the supplied reduced quantum phase constant, with physical Planck action \(h_P=2\pi\hbar\). The time mesh and instrument resolution remain separate. Varying or averaging inverse values in the limiting theorems below is a formal family-of-models prescription, not an experiment that varies nature’s constant or an automatic record-resolution law.

The comparison uses controlled unitaries and product copies; Proposition 2 is not used in the limiting Theorem 3. Its relative averaging prescription is part of the hypothesis. Model checks are internal, not independent review.

A second experiment records the electron’s position after opening either slit or both. Three calibrated count distributions can give an action band. Moving a window of known width across the beam can determine a single reduced action magnitude in the scalar model, even with unknown illumination. The force the window exerts on the transmitted beam limits what published fringes establish about zero action. A classical shutter with one random-impulse scale reproduces the entire averaged pattern, even when the electron and shutter positions are read together. Two slits and a position detector.

1. The Galileo area and the two histories

Let \(m,F,v,\tau>0\), with downward coordinate \(y\) and common horizontal motion \(x=vt\). The initial tangent and falling curve are

\[ y_I(t)=0,\qquad y_F(t)=\frac{Ft^2}{2m},\qquad A_{\rm inertial,fall}=\frac{vF\tau^3}{6m},\qquad \Delta E=\frac{F^2\tau^2}{2m}. \tag{1} \]

Thus \((3F/v)A_{\rm inertial,fall}=\tau\Delta E\). The common-endpoint chord is \(y_C(t)=F\tau t/(2m)\). For the transverse Lagrangian \(L=m\dot y^2/2+Fy\), direct integration gives

\[ S[y_I]=0,\qquad S[y_F]=\frac{F^2\tau^3}{3m},\qquad S[y_C]=\frac{3F^2\tau^3}{8m},\qquad S[y_C]-S[y_F]=K_\tau. \tag{2} \]

The horizontal action cancels in every difference. Equation (2) also exposes the endpoint issue: the bare tangent and parabola finish at different heights. Adding \(d\chi(y,t)/dt\) to \(L\) changes their action difference by their unequal final \(\chi\) values. Their measurable relative phase therefore requires endpoint matching and specified control phases. The conversion coefficient for \(K_\tau\) is \(F/(4v)\); the area conversion \((F/v)A_{\rm inertial,fall}\) equals \(4K_\tau\).

Here is the exact matching used throughout. Supply canonical quantum kinematics \([y,p]=i\hbar\) in the Schrödinger representation, and set

\[ U_\tau=\exp\left[-\frac{i\tau}{\hbar} \left(\frac{p^2}{2m}-Fy\right)\right],\qquad Q_\tau=e^{iF\tau y/(2\hbar)} e^{-i\tau p^2/(2m\hbar)}e^{iF\tau y/(2\hbar)}. \]

Lemma 1 (matched comparison). With these scalar-potential conventions,

\[Q_\tau=e^{iK_\tau/\hbar}U_\tau.\tag{3}\]

Proof. The kernel of \(Q_\tau\) has the free normalization and phase \(L_\tau(x,y)=m(y-x)^2/(2\tau)+F\tau(x+y)/2\). The classical solution joining \(x\) to \(y\) is \(q_*(t)=x+(y-x)t/\tau+F(t^2-\tau t)/(2m)\). Substitution into the action gives \(L_\tau-K_\tau\). The fluctuation action about \(q_*\) is exactly \(\int m\dot\xi^2/2\), with zero endpoint fluctuations, hence the same free Gaussian normalization. This proves (3), equivalently the exact quadratic kernel identity in the insertion note, §§2–3 (passage). \(\square\)

The impulses in \(Q_\tau\) reproduce the chord and match the parabola’s final momentum after the last half-kick. Equation (3) holds for every initial body state. A branch-dependent scalar potential would add its own phase, so its absence or calibration is part of the experiment. The polygon-lift note, Theorem 1 (full-read) proves the corresponding identity for every partition.

For a literal tangent arm, the explicit reunion in the Galileo note, §§3–4 (full-read) uses signal forces \(F,-F,F\) over durations \(\tau,2\tau,\tau\) against a free reference. Its closed action difference is \(\Delta S_{\rm loop}=-2F^2\tau^3/(3m)=-16K_\tau\). Every phase formula below applies with that signed difference when that four-cell experiment is chosen. The coefficient belongs to the reunion protocol; the original area (1) remains the geometric anchor.

For fixed endpoints the constant-force action has one stationary path. The chord is an off-shell path for the continuous-force action and a classical history for the impulsive control. Thus (3) compares two controlled histories, each with an exact quadratic path integral. Treating the tangent and parabola as two stationary points of one fixed-endpoint constant-force functional would change the variational problem.

2. What the halved functional measures

Use \(\hbar\) for reduced phase parameter and \(\eta\in[0,1/2)\) for a target decision error. Identification with quantum mechanics means \(\hbar=|\hbar|\), with ordinary Planck constant \(h_P=2\pi|\hbar|\).

Proposition 2 (two contributions and their remainder). Suppose a halved functional, with its normalization and observable fixed, has two retained contributions

\[ \mathcal A_\hbar =a_0e^{iS_0/\hbar+i\mu_0} +a_1e^{iS_1/\hbar+i\mu_1}+R_\hbar, \quad a_j\ge0,\quad |R_\hbar|\le r. \]

Put \(\phi=(S_1-S_0)/\hbar\) and \(\beta=\mu_1-\mu_0\). Then

\[ |\mathcal A_\hbar|^2 =a_0^2+a_1^2+2a_0a_1\cos(\phi+\beta)+E, \qquad |E|\le2(a_0+a_1)r+r^2.\tag{4} \]

Proof. Expand the square, bound the leading amplitude by \(a_0+a_1\), and bound its cross term with \(R_\hbar\) by \(2(a_0+a_1)r\). \(\square\)

For two nondegenerate stationary points at a fixed partition, \(a_j=|O(z_j)|/\sqrt{|\det S''(z_j)|}\) and \(\mu_j\) contains the observable phase and Hessian signature. Stationary phase supplies \(r\le C_\pi\hbar\) for sufficiently small \(\hbar\), with cutoffs and smoothness as in round 1, §1 (full-read). Equation (4) retains that partition-dependent constant; uniform control as the partition changes needs a separate estimate. For the exactly matched quadratic propagators in (3), the relative phase has zero remainder and zero relative Gaussian signature.

At a fixed phase, a large \(|S_1-S_0|/\hbar\) still leaves the cross term in (4) at full amplitude. Averaging, selecting a branch, or retaining an orthogonal branch record supplies the missing diagonalization. Equal-action contributions survive phase averaging, as round 1’s pair of quartic saddles demonstrates.

An operational record requires a preparation and measurement rule in addition to (4). For example, tag the arms by normalized pointer states \(|r_0\rangle,|r_1\rangle\), and put \(\gamma=\langle r_0|r_1\rangle\). Recombining the arms gives the cross term \(2a_0a_1\operatorname{Re}(\gamma e^{i(\phi+\beta)})\) and visibility \(V=2a_0a_1|\gamma|/(a_0^2+a_1^2)\). For balanced arms and pure tags, optimal inference of the arm from the pointer has

\[ D_{\rm path}=\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^2},\qquad p_{\rm path}=\frac{1-D_{\rm path}}2,\qquad V^2+D_{\rm path}^2=1.\tag{5} \]

The eigenvalues of the difference of the two rank-one tag states are \(\pm\sqrt{1-|\gamma|^2}\), proving (5) by the binary measurement optimization used below. This is the pure balanced case of established interferometric duality; see Schwindt, Kwiat and Englert 1999 (abstract). Path identification depends on the tags, while phase detection depends on coherent recombination. Orthogonal tags identify the arm even at \(\Delta S=0\) and remove its interference. A bare halved functional supplies neither those tags nor their physical cost.

3. Exact phase record, error target and resources

Prepare a balanced control qubit and a common arbitrary body density operator \(\rho\). Under hypothesis \(H_0\), apply \(U_\tau\) in both arms; under \(H_1\), apply \(U_\tau\) in arm 0 and \(Q_\tau\) in arm 1. Supply coherent control with calibrated relative scalar phases, no extra environmental tags, the Born rule, and equal hypothesis priors. Equation (3) factorizes the output into the same body state under both hypotheses and the control states

\[ |+\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad |+_\phi\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \phi=K_\tau/\hbar.\tag{6} \]

A recombiner of phase \(\alpha\) has exact port probabilities \(P_\pm(\alpha|\phi)=[1\pm\cos(\phi-\alpha)]/2\). Scanning \(\alpha\) reveals the fringe shift; its visibility remains one for every \(\phi\). The optimal distinction of the two hypotheses is

\[ D_n=\sqrt{1-\cos^{2n}(\phi/2)},\qquad p_{\rm err}^{(n)}=\frac{1-D_n}{2}.\tag{7} \]

Proof. For normalized vectors with overlap \(c\), the difference of their rank-one density operators has eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt{1-|c|^2}\). For any equal-prior binary test \(0\le M\le I\), the success probability is \([1+\operatorname{tr}M(\rho_1-\rho_0)]/2\); the positive eigenspace attains the maximum. Here \(|\langle+|+_\phi\rangle|=|\cos(\phi/2)|\) and \(n\) independent copies raise the overlap to its \(n\)th power. This proves (7) including attainability. \(\square\)

Thus (7) detects a history-dependent phase against a reference. The uncontrolled body channels of \(Q_\tau\) and \(U_\tau\) coincide for every \(\rho\), giving error \(1/2\) even with arbitrarily many copies. The coherence resource in (6) is essential to exposing the scalar.

For the original unclosed free/falling pair, the final displacement and impulse themselves supply a further signal. A pure body’s overlap then contains the expectation of a Weyl displacement in its prepared state, so its magnitude depends on that preparation. For each \(\tau>0\) one can choose a wavefunction with position support narrower than the nonzero interaction-picture displacement \(F\tau^2/(2m)\); its translated support is disjoint and the two outputs are orthogonal. Shrinking that support costs increasing momentum spread. Thus the phase-only theorem applies to the matched, coherently controlled record (6), with its specified resources; an unrestricted detector of the raw endpoint separation requires a separate preparation or disturbance budget.

Write \(\delta=\operatorname{dist}(\phi,2\pi\mathbb Z)\in[0,\pi]\). For a fixed positive integer \(n\), (7) reaches error at most \(\eta\) iff

\[ \delta\ge d_{n,\eta},\qquad d_{n,\eta}=2\arccos\left([4\eta(1-\eta)]^{1/(2n)}\right). \tag{8} \]

On the first lobe \(0\le K_\tau/\hbar\le\pi\), this is equivalent to

\[ K_\tau\ge\hbar d_{n,\eta},\qquad \tau\ge\left(\frac{24m\hbar d_{n,\eta}}{F^2}\right)^{1/3}, \qquad A_{\rm inertial,fall}\ge\frac{4v\hbar}{F}d_{n,\eta}. \tag{9} \]

For \(n=1\), \(d_{1,\eta}=2\arcsin(1-2\eta)\), exactly the polygon-lift record bound, §4 (passage). For \(0<\eta<1/2\), \(d_{n,\eta}\to0\) as \(n\to\infty\); for \(\eta=0\) it equals \(\pi\) at every finite \(n\). With \(k\) coherent passes replace \(\phi\) by \(k\phi\). These resource choices change the threshold.

The dependence on errors is explicit. If actual hypothesis states \(\widetilde\rho_i\) satisfy \(\tfrac12\|\widetilde\rho_i-\rho_i\|_1\le e_i\), the optimum error changes by at most \((e_0+e_1)/2\), by the triangle inequality in (7). A common phase calibration error \(u\) on each of \(n\) independent signal copies gives distance at most \(\sqrt{1-\cos^{2n}(u/2)}\le\sqrt n\,|u|/2\), and hence changes the optimal error by at most \(\sqrt n\,|u|/4\) when the reference is exact. An action uncertainty \(|\delta S|\) contributes \(|u|=|\delta S|/\hbar\). These bounds state how accurately the control must be known to use (9).

4. The two orders of limits

Put \(C=F^2/(24m)>0\), so \(K_\tau=C\tau^3\).

Theorem 3 (refinement, classical resolution and the missing prescription).

  1. For the fixed coherent protocol (6), with fixed finite \(n\),

\[ D_n\le\min\left(1,\frac{\sqrt n\,C\tau^3}{2\hbar}\right), \qquad \lim_{\tau\downarrow0}p_{\rm err}^{(n)}=\frac12 \quad(\hbar>0).\tag{10} \]

At every fixed \(\tau>0\), \(\lim_{\hbar\downarrow0}D_n\) fails to exist: its liminf is zero and limsup is one. Consequently the coherent classical-first iterated limit is undefined.

  1. Fix \(b\in(0,1)\) and average the inverse phase parameter by replacing \(\phi\) with \(u\phi\), uniformly over \(u\in[1-b,1+b]\), forgetting \(u\). For this explicitly averaged two-arm state, its normalized coherence is

\[ \Gamma_b(\tau,\hbar) =\frac1{2b}\int_{1-b}^{1+b}e^{iuK_\tau/\hbar}\,du =e^{i\phi}\operatorname{sinc}(b\phi),\qquad |\Gamma_b|\le\min\left(1,\frac{\hbar}{bK_\tau}\right). \tag{11} \]

With \(\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1\), the iterated limits are

\[ \lim_{\hbar\downarrow0}\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\Gamma_b=1, \qquad \lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\lim_{\hbar\downarrow0}\Gamma_b=0. \tag{12} \]

(Refinement after a Fable review, 2026-09-27.) The non-commuting limits (12) depend on averaging over a relative window of the inverse resolution. With an absolute window of half-width \(w_0\) in \(1/\hbar\) the average is \(e^{i\phi}\operatorname{sinc}(K_\tau w_0)\), independent of \(\hbar\), and both iterated limits equal one. Theorem 3(b) therefore states a property of the relative-averaging prescription, which is the prescription (12) uses.

For fixed weights and signature difference in (4), this prescription leaves a diagonal-intensity error bounded by \(2a_0a_1\min(1,\hbar/(b|\Delta S|))\) in addition to the averaged remainder bound of (4), with bound \(2a_0a_1\) when \(\Delta S=0\). If the stationary-phase remainder obeys \(r\le C_\pi\hbar\) uniformly for small resolution, averaging uses resolutions \(\hbar/u\) and one may take \(r\le C_\pi\hbar/(1-b)\).

  1. Alternatively, assume a calibrated comparison can be attenuated to any action \(rK_\tau\), \(0\le r\le1\), under both hypotheses, with no additional relative phase. For example, use duration \(r^{1/3}\tau\) in (6), keeping \(F,m\) fixed. Let \(D_{\rm tun}\) be the best single-copy distance over these choices. Then

\[ D_{\rm tun}(\tau,\hbar) =\sin\left(\min\left\{\frac{K_\tau}{2\hbar},\frac\pi2\right\}\right), \tag{13} \] \[ \lim_{\hbar\downarrow0}\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}D_{\rm tun}=0, \qquad \lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\lim_{\hbar\downarrow0}D_{\rm tun}=1. \tag{14} \]

Proof. The inequality \(1-(1-x)^n\le nx\) for \(x\in[0,1]\), with \(x=\sin^2(\phi/2)\), proves (10). For fixed \(K_\tau>0\), the sequences \(\hbar_j=K_\tau/(2\pi j)\) and \(\hbar'_j=K_\tau/((2j+1)\pi)\) give respectively \(D_n=0\) and \(D_n=1\). Integration gives (11); dominated continuity at \(\phi=0\) and the displayed reciprocal bound at \(\phi\to\infty\) prove (12). In (c), maximize \(|\sin(r\phi/2)|\) over \([0,1]\). The first maximum is reached at \(r=\pi/\phi\) when \(\phi\ge\pi\); otherwise \(r=1\) maximizes it. This proves (13)–(14). \(\square\)

Part (b) formalizes the loss of off-diagonal terms needed to recover the two selected critical-point weights. Its averaged balanced state approaches \(I/2\) classically first, and \(|+\rangle\langle+|\) when refined first. The trace distance between these two limiting states is \(1/2\); dephasing by itself supplies no accessible which-path tag. Part (c) instead supplies an actual discrimination experiment with errors tending respectively to \(1/2\) and zero. Its extra tunable control is a physical hypothesis; changing the output recombiner alone would leave (7) unchanged. A classical branch-selection prescription can also retain distinct histories at every positive cell length, but their paths converge together as that length vanishes. A limiting Dirac measure alone expresses no retained record of the shrinking difference.

These conclusions sharpen the proposed classical-first statement. For any fixed \(b\) the condition \(b|\Delta S|/\hbar\gg1\) controls the averaged cross term, with the explicit error in (11). For coherent discrimination the exact condition is (8), with its phase aliases. The two conditions express different measurements. Along a joint limit the dimensionless ratio \(C\tau^3/\hbar\) governs both: tending to zero erases the phase signal; a finite ratio gives the corresponding signal, including the aliases in (8); a diverging ratio requires the prescription in (b) or (c) for a definite limit.

For a partition of fixed total duration \(T\), the matched polygon action is \(K_\pi=C\sum_j\tau_j^3\le CT|\pi|^2\). Its coherent distance from the exact curve is at most \(\sqrt n\,CT|\pi|^2/(2\hbar)\). After \(k\) uniform halvings a cell’s action falls by \(8^{-k}\) and the whole polygon defect by \(4^{-k}\). Hence the whole fixed-duration comparison converges as well. Resolving ever smaller cells can require increasing resources: for a fixed small-error target the small-phase independent-copy scaling is \(n\) of order \((\hbar/K_\tau)^2\), whereas coherent repetition uses \(k\) of order \(\hbar/K_\tau\). Such budgets were held fixed in (10).

5. Relation to the disturbance and concentration floors

The fifth-postulate note, Theorems C, E and §7 (passage) separates observable algebra, states and records. With \(\hbar=|\hbar|\), the three bounds here have the following scopes.

Bound Quantity constrained Extra hypotheses beyond Newtonian geometry
Phase record (9), one copy \(K_\tau\ge2|\hbar|\arcsin(1-2\eta)\) on the first lobe Coherent controlled comparison, calibrated reunion, Born rule and one pass
Theorem C(b) \((s/8)\sum_j\Delta D_j+(J/2)\sum_j\Delta X_j\ge|\hbar|\arcsin(1-2\eta)\) Regular quantum joint states, unitary instrument, test valid for every initial body state; the theorem’s interpolating-state suprema
Theorem E \(\lambda_0(ab/|\hbar|)\ge(1-2\eta)^2\) Quantum state concentrated in both position and momentum windows with probability at least \(1-\eta\) each

Here \(s=F\tau^2/(2m)\), \(J=F\tau\), \(a,b\) are window half-widths, and \(\lambda_0\) is the largest eigenvalue of the time-and-band-limiting operator with the fifth-postulate note’s convention. For Theorem E’s finite threshold take \(0<\eta<1/2\); at \(\eta=0\) simultaneous compact concentration is impossible for finite \(a,b\). A phase difference of \(\pi\) already gives perfect discrimination in (7).

The geometry relates \(sJ=12K_\tau\). Converting Theorem C into a bound on \(K_\tau\) additionally needs bounds on the allowed disturbances \(\Delta D_j,\Delta X_j\) relative to \(J,s\). Converting Theorem E needs a physical identification of the concentration widths \(a,b\) with trajectory resolution. Choosing, for example, \(a=\alpha s\) and \(b=\beta J\) would give \(K_\tau\ge|\hbar|c_*(\eta)/(12\alpha\beta)\), with \(c_*(\eta)=\lambda_0^{-1}((1-2\eta)^2)\) and the positive dimensionless apertures \(\alpha,\beta\) supplied. Geometry alone fixes neither choice.

The phase experiment has a two-dimensional record space. Its Fubini–Study distance from the reference is \(\delta/2\), and the equal-prior error target requires that distance to be at least \(\arcsin(1-2\eta)\). This is the same statistical-angle step used in Theorem C’s disturbance proof. Theorem E uses the overlap norm of two spectral projections in an infinite-dimensional canonical state space. Its concentration function and error dependence carry different information. Their common right-hand scale comes from the supplied canonical phase constant; the equality of the scale alone establishes no equivalence of the three experimental tasks.

There is a stronger exact bridge within the restricted Gaussian mark model. The polygon-lift note, Theorem 7 (passage) uses the same functional \(\mathcal K_\tau[f]=(2m)^{-1}\iint G_\tau(s,u)f(s)f(u)\,ds\,du\) for the polygon phase and the optimal mark deflection. For constant force, \(\mathcal K_\tau=K_\tau\); with mark trade-off \(\kappa=|\hbar|/2\), its supremal squared deflection is \(d_{\rm mark}^2=2K_\tau/|\hbar|\). That equality retains the Gaussian, uncorrelated error/recoil and dense-protocol hypotheses. It leaves the general C and E bounds with their own quantities and quantifiers.

Thus the two-path calculation gives the polygon theorem’s existing phase bound in the halved-functional representation, plus its explicit limit prescriptions. It adds no independent exclusion of the common zero branch allowed by round 2, §4 (passage). Every \(\hbar>0\) supports (3)–(14); their thresholds scale with that freely supplied value. At zero, the exponential representation is singular, while classical mechanics still supplies ordinary trajectories and, with state completeness, sharp pointer records as in Theorem C(c). A singular coordinate for that classical theory creates no inconsistency in the theory itself.

Two slits and a position detector

Newton’s 1713 Corollary 4 to Lemma X makes displacement a measure of force. Here the mechanical calibration is the displacement produced by a known transverse impulse \(J\). Denote it by \(BJ\). For free flight from a mask to a detector over time \(T\), \(B=T/m\), where \(m\) is the inertial mass. Fix the ruler, screen pixel pitch \(b\), flight-time gate \(T_0\pm\Delta T\) with \(T_0>\Delta T\), and a nonzero screen frequency \(k\) in radians per unit length. The gain can also be calibrated with a reference impulse and a ruler in a focusing apparatus. A mass referred to a mechanical standard gives action in absolute units. Free flight with just a ruler and clock gives action per unit mass. Newton’s revision, passages from 1687 and 1713.

What to count. Randomize the setting on fresh trials from one source. Pass both beams, only the left, or only the right. For each setting \(s\) let \(z_s(k)=\int e^{ikx}\,d\mu_s(x)\), with \(\mu_s\) the screen count measure per incident trial. A loss contributes zero. A separately recorded common background cancels in \(C=z_{LR}-z_L-z_R+z_{\varnothing}\). Both quadratures are sums over the same observed positions. Fix an error allowance \(e(k)\) such that \(\delta=(|C_{\rm obs}|-e(k))_+\) is a lower bound on the ideal \(|C|\). It includes counting error, any change in incident flux, unmatched transmission and unobserved screen tails. An upper bound on a missed count probability bounds its whole Fourier contribution. Rounding to pixels introduces at most \(|k|b/2\) per counted particle. A common translation-invariant detector blur multiplies \(C\) by a characteristic function of modulus at most one. Three histograms normalized to their own transmitted counts discard the relative weights. This statistic uses only the counted positions. Phase retrieval from propagated intensities is established. For example, Teague uses irradiance and perimeter phase data under the wave propagation equation (abstract). Teague, 1983.

What a positive excess measures. At the plane of selection let \(Q,P\) be transverse position and mechanical momentum. First take two intervals with widths \(w_L,w_R\), centre separation \(D\), projections \(\Pi_L,\Pi_R\) and \(d_\pm=D\pm(w_L+w_R)/2\), with \(d_->0\). The both-open state \(\rho\) is supported on their union and has trace at most one. It can include arbitrary unobserved degrees of freedom. Each single-open operation must preserve its corresponding block \(\Pi_j\rho\Pi_j\) to the accuracy charged to \(e(k)\). For classical trajectories this just means passing the same particles with the same downstream response on the open side. Any such classical source, including one with memory, gives \(C=0\) by additivity.

For operator models suppose the common detector coordinate is \(X=AQ+BP+c\), with \(A\) dimensionless and \(c\) a length. Take the real linear combinations self-adjoint on closure on a common invariant core permitting unitary commutator differentiation, and let \(K=\|[Q,P]\|<\infty\). This class includes commuting motion. With \(E=e^{ikX}\) the only contributions to \(C\) are the two off-diagonal blocks. The separated-spectrum Sylvester estimate gives \(d_-\|\Pi_L E\Pi_R\|\le\|\Pi_L[Q,E]\Pi_R\|\), and likewise in reverse. Unitary commutator differentiation gives \(\|[Q,E]\|\le|kB|K\). With \(p_j=\operatorname{tr}(\rho\Pi_j)\) the trace norm of each coherence block is at most \(\sqrt{p_Lp_R}\) by Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz. Consequently a measured \(\delta>0\) requires

\[ K\ \ge\ \frac{d_-\,\delta}{2\sqrt{p_Lp_R}\,|k|B_{\max}}, \qquad B_{\max}=\sup |B|. \]

The same bound holds for a common mixture of flight times in the gate, using Cauchy–Schwarz on the trial probabilities. A positive excess requires both arms to transmit. Using \(2\sqrt{p_Lp_R}\le1\) gives a conservative bound without measuring the arm weights separately. It rules out wholly commuting motion in the stated apparatus. A lower bound on the norm leaves open commuting sectors of other preparations. No scalar commutator or calibrated relative phase enters. The ordered-interval estimate uses Theorem 9.2 with the compressed position operators shifted by the midpoint of the gap (passage). Bhatia and Rosenthal. For the unitary integral under the stated domain hypotheses see §3.1 (passage). Iserles and Kropielnicka.

In a regular Weyl representation with trial reduced action \(a\), \(e^{ikX}\) translates position support by a signed distance of magnitude \(|akB|\). Modular translations as probes of separated wave packets are established (passage, §II). Tollaksen et al.. An off-diagonal block can survive only if a translated interval meets the other. Thus for \(B_{\min}=\inf|B|>0\) a positive excess requires

\[ \frac{d_-}{|k|B_{\max}}\ \le\ |a|\ \le\ \frac{d_+}{|k|B_{\min}} . \]

Under the action dilation \(x=sX',\,t=rT'\), the slit widths, centres, offset \(c\) and screen pitch divide by \(s\). The gate times divide by \(r\), momenta and impulses multiply by \(r/s\), and \(k'=sk\), \(B'=B/r\), \(a'=ra/s^2\) at fixed mass. Both the prepared slit labels and \(kx\) at detection are invariant with these transformed settings. Holding the physical ruler and clock fixed makes the band independent of mathematical mesh.

Two positive scales can give identical records. Even with the same mass, slits, clock and pixels the band need not pick a unique \(a\). Take free flight at fixed \(T\) inside the gate, \(a>0\), \(\beta=aT/m\), slits of width \(D/4\) about \(\pm D/2\), and a normalized coherent wave \(\psi\) with a nonzero packet within \(D/64\) of each centre and zero elsewhere. Put \(s=21/20\), \(\tilde a=sa\) and choose the other incident wave by

\[ \tilde\psi(sq)=s^{-1/2} \exp\!\big[i(1-s)q^2/(2\beta)\big]\,\psi(q). \]

Both supports stay inside their original slits. In the free Fresnel kernel for \(\tilde a\), substituting \(q'=sq\) cancels the change in the input quadratic phase and leaves the same Fourier integral in \(x\). The output acquires only the factor \(\exp[i(1/s-1)x^2/(2\beta)]\). This works for each slit separately, their coherent sum and the rejected outcome. The full screen laws thus agree even before binning. The preparations obey the same measured slit transmissions while differing in their unresolved illumination within each slit. The only length in the chosen input envelope can be taken proportional to \(D\).

A mask displaced from the slits. One way to use three settings is to put complementary edges at the same coordinate \(q_*\) in the actual mask plane and a both-open run. For a distance \(d>0\) measure the incident probabilities \(p_-(d)\) in \([q_*-d,q_*)\) and \(p_+(d)\) in \([q_*,q_*+d]\). A translation of the same edge to \(q_*-d,q_*,q_*+d\) measures these by differences of total transmission with full collection and calibrated position resolution (for a continuous beam the endpoint convention has no effect). When \(|akB|\le d\), translation in one direction allows only one of the two off-diagonal blocks to contribute. Its initial and final points both belong to these strips. Cauchy–Schwarz on that matrix element, and on any common time mixture, gives the observable implication

\[ \delta>\sqrt{p_-^{\,\rm upper}(d)\,p_+^{\,\rm upper}(d)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |a|>\frac{d}{|k|B_{\max}} . \]

This control includes diffraction before the selector.

What a full mask scan adds. Translate one opaque screen with a clear window of fixed width \(W\). In the actual selector plane its ideal transmission at setting \(y\) is \(M_y=\mathbf{1}_{[y,y+W]}(Q)\), acting as the identity on transmitted amplitudes. Let \(z_o\) denote the mask-out harmonic and \(z_y\) the transmitted harmonic, background subtracted and on the same incident count scale. Hold \(B\ne0\) fixed for the moment. For a trial scalar action set \(u=akB\). The two factors in \(M_y E M_y\) test membership of positions a distance \(|u|\) apart. The set of \(y\) that pass both has length \((W-|u|)_+\), independent of either position. Integrating the matrix element gives

\[ \int_{\mathbb R} z_y(k)\,dy=(W-|akB|)_+ z_o(k). \]

This uses the familiar aperture autocorrelation (passage, Hopkins on the pupil function and frequency response). Hopkins, 1955. A stable common detector blur multiplies both sides by the same factor. Under the dilation above \(y'=y/s\), \(W'=W/s\). Thus integration over the physical mask settings scales by the same factor as \(W\). If \(z_o\ne0\) and the observed ratio \(\int z_y\,dy/(Wz_o)\) is a real number \(R\) strictly between zero and one, the sole compatible magnitude is \(W(1-R)/|kB|\). An imaginary part outside the errors tests the model itself. Thus this scan can distinguish the explicit pair above on the same screen. A positive classical ensemble with a passive mask and any fixed downstream response contributes for exactly \(W\) of mask travel per trajectory. It gives \(R=1\), including for rays with an unobserved history. This prediction assumes free passage through the clear window.

A classical shutter can also transmit with a random transverse impulse. At fixed \(B\) choose a classical incident ensemble giving the measured mask-out position law, then add an independent kick \(J\) on transmission. After integration over \(y\), every incoming ray has weight \(W\). For the mechanical momentum scale \(j_0>0\) choose the density and its characteristic function

\[ g(J)=\frac{1}{2\pi j_0}\operatorname{sinc}^2\! \left(\frac{J}{2j_0}\right),\qquad \mathbb E e^{ivJ}=(1-j_0|v|)_+ . \]

Here \(\operatorname{sinc}x=\sin(x)/x\) with value one at zero. This is the same rectangular-aperture Fourier pair. Setting \(j_0=|a|/W\) gives the scalar model’s full averaged screen measure at every \(k\), including after common detector blur. In the classical model \(j_0\) is an apparatus impulse. The ideal density has long tails. For the specified single harmonic and \(0\le R\le1\), two equally likely finite kicks \(J=\pm\arccos(R)/|kB|\) give exactly the same ratio. Even the location of the integrated diffraction cutoff can therefore be reproduced by a force distribution. Keeping the individual mask positions supplies further observations. Classical random kicks as an explanation of certain which-way measurements have prior art (abstract). Wiseman and colleagues, 1996.

With the original finite time gate one can still use \(D_{\rm scan}=|W z_o-\int z_y\,dy|\). For each common delay the loss of the overlap length is at most \(|ak|B_{\max}\) and the absolute harmonic is at most the incident probability at the mask. Averaging gives \(D_{\rm scan}\le p|ak|B_{\max}\), where \(p\) is the total mask-plane probability per incident trial. For measured value \(D_{\rm obs}\) and total error \(e_D\) in units of length, this gives \(|a|\ge(D_{\rm obs}-e_D)_+/(p|k|B_{\max})\) without resolving the two slits. For a finite range \(I\) choose \(y\) uniformly there and multiply the trial mean by \(|I|\). With full transmission counting, the missing integral has modulus at most \(Wp-\int_I p_y\,dy\), since every position is passed for \(W\) of travel and \(|z_y|\le p_y\). Finite position accuracy of the selector can be included by calibrating its actual translation distribution. These operations survive arbitrary mathematical subdivision of \(T\).

How a mechanical disturbance could imitate the signal. In free classical flight an extra transverse impulse \(J_j\) at the selector changes the Fourier record of arm \(j\) by at most \(p_j |k|B_{\max}\mathbb E_j|J_j|\), since \(|e^{iku}-1|\le |ku|\). Here \(J_j\) is the change between shutter settings on a transmitted trajectory. For a distributed force difference use the endpoint displacement bound \(m^{-1}\int_0^T(T-t)|\Delta F_j(t)|\,dt\) in place of \(B_{\max}|J_j|\). These are independent mechanical controls on a systematic error. In an operator realization a common additive self-adjoint error \(R\) in \(X\) with \(\|R\|\le r_x\) changes each Fourier effect by at most \(|k|r_x\) by the bounded-perturbation formula.

Newtonian recoil gives one way to measure such a displacement independently of the electron pattern. For an isolated free shutter of mass \(M\), let \(D_M\) be its final displacement relative to the continuation of its incoming inertial line. Conservation of the transverse centre of mass gives the particle’s extra displacement as \(-(M/m)D_M\), even for two opposite forces whose net impulse vanishes. Ruler readings on the shutter at \(-T_c,0,T\) with absolute error \(\rho_M\) bound the extrapolation error by \(2(1+T/T_c)\rho_M\). A force on the support or momentum carried away by another body or field belongs in this balance. An independent recoil bound needs the absolute displacement on transmitted trials, including readout disturbance. A signed mean can vanish for the two opposite kicks above.

The scan allows a force test even if scattering directly at an edge is strong. Suppose independent controls localize unrestricted deflection to strips of width \(\eta\) on the inside of each window edge, certify an opaque exterior, and bound extra displacement elsewhere in the transmitted beam by \(r_x\). For each classical ray at most \(2\eta\) of window travel lies in the strips. There the Fourier readout can change by at most two. The resulting classical allowance in \(D_{\rm scan}\) is at most \(p(4\eta+W|k|r_x)\) in units of length. Edge transmission and deflection require their own calibration before using this allowance.

There is an explicit classical \(a=0\) alternative if the shutter can change that force freely. Fix classical incoming momentum zero and a continuous incoming position law on each slit with the measured slit weights. In each transmitted arm let \(u(q)\) be its conditional cumulative probability, uniform on \([0,1]\). For shutter setting \(s\) let \(H_s\) be the target cumulative distribution of the detected coordinate, conditional on transmission. Apply the transverse impulse \(J_s(q)=[H_s^{-1}(u(q))-Aq-c]/B\). The same downstream flight now gives the prescribed \(H_s\) for every setting and leaves the position at the selector unchanged. For a finite screen and \(B\ne0\) the impulses are bounded. With additive transmitted weights this reproduces all three count measures, including a fringe pattern. Finite forces suffice even with the same flight time. In free flight keep the incident ray label \(q\), exert the constant force \(m[H_s^{-1}(u(q))-q]/[h(T-h/2)]\) for any chosen duration \(0<h<T\), then let the particle coast. Its endpoint is exactly \(H_s^{-1}(u(q))\). Newton’s finite-force hypothesis alone permits this change of apparatus. The same force works under arbitrarily fine subdivision of the flight. Additional observations during the pulse, or an independent bound on its deflection, can distinguish it.

Can measuring both bodies remove the recoil alternative? Let the shutter move freely, with transverse position \(Y\) and momentum \(P_M\). An offset \(y\) within it selects the relative position \(S=Q-Y\). For fixed \(T\) after the selection, read both endpoints \(X_e=Q+TP/m\) and \(X_M=Y+TP_M/M\). Their joint harmonic has independent screen frequencies \(k_e,k_M\). In regular commuting constituent Weyl algebras with trial actions \(a_e,a_M\), its unitary translates the support of \(S\) by a distance of magnitude \(T|a_e k_e/m-a_M k_M/M|\). The same window overlap therefore gives

\[ \frac1W\int_{\mathbb R}z_y(k_e,k_M)\,dy =\left(1-\frac{T}{W} \left|\frac{a_e k_e}{m}-\frac{a_M k_M}{M}\right|\right)_+ z_o(k_e,k_M). \]

For a common action \(a\), the classical construction above now gives the two bodies opposite impulses \((J,-J)\) with \(j_0=|a|/W\). The joint harmonic acquires the same factor for every \((k_e,k_M)\). A classical incident ensemble can have any prescribed mask-plane position marginal and any prescribed mask-out endpoint pair at this fixed time: draw those two pairs and set each incoming momentum to its mass times its sampled displacement divided by \(T\). Thus both endpoint records and any common subsequent binning agree for the full averaged scan. Total transverse momentum is conserved in the classical construction.

An explicit pair uses the same source at every time in the clock gate. Prepare independent centred Gaussian position and momentum marginals for each body \(i=e,M\), with zero cross covariances and independently fixed rms widths \(\ell_i\) and \(\pi_i=m_i\sigma_{v,i}\). Set \(a_*=\min_i\ell_i\pi_i>0\). The product Gaussian is an admitted Wigner state at \(a_*\) since \(\ell_i\pi_i\ge a_*/2\). Its free-flight marginals equal those of classical Gaussian particles with those widths (Gaussian characterization and linear canonical propagation, abstracts). Simon, Sudarshan and Mukunda and their Gaussian propagation paper. Use the passive window at \(a_e=a_M=a_*\) and the window with opposite kicks of scale \(a_*/W\) at \(a_e=a_M=0\). Their averaged joint position laws agree at each delay, hence with any common finite clock resolution. This preparation specifies lengths, velocity widths, masses, window, pixel pitches and clock gate. The positive trial scale is constructed from those inputs. It is independent of a value for physical \(\hbar\).

A finite version uses just three mask offsets and three bounded kicks. Give the mask real amplitude transmission \(t(S-y)=[1+\cos(2\pi(S-y)/\Lambda)]/2\) with ruler-calibrated pitch \(\Lambda\). Choose \(y=0,\Lambda/3,2\Lambda/3\) equally often. Retain transmission, clock and both positions and average over the offset. The three spatial Fourier coefficients of \(t\) are \(1/4,1/2,1/4\) for orders \(-1,0,1\). Averaging the three offsets cancels every product of distinct orders. The pass probability is \(3/8\). Conditional on passing, the two-body output at \(a_*\) is a mixture of opposite momentum shifts. In the classical model use the same incident Gaussian law and the measured acceptance \(t(S-y)^2\). On transmission apply opposite kicks \((J,-J)\) drawn independently from

\[ \Pr(J=0)=\tfrac23,\qquad \Pr(J=+2\pi a_*/\Lambda)=\Pr(J=-2\pi a_*/\Lambda)=\tfrac16 . \]

The averaged acceptance for each incoming relative position is \(3/8\), so the admitted classical Gaussian ensemble has the original weights. This gives exactly the same rejection probability and joint endpoint law at each delay in the gate with finite mask travel. Equal translation errors with the same distribution at the three offsets still cancel the cross terms. The mechanical parameter to check independently at this finite grating is the impulse \(2\pi a_*/\Lambda\).

Retaining the offset separates this explicit pair. Take \(\ell_e=\ell_M=\Lambda\) and \(\pi_e=\pi_M=\pi_0\). Set \(\kappa=2\pi/\Lambda\), \(\mu=mM/(m+M)\) and choose the clock centre \(T_0=\mu\Lambda/(a_*\kappa)\) using the specified preparation widths. At offset zero count \(\cos(\kappa(X_e-X_M))\) per incident particle. Write \(L^2=\ell_e^2+\ell_M^2\) and \(\sigma_V^2=\sigma_{v,e}^2+\sigma_{v,M}^2\). At \(T_0\) the relative Weyl translation spans one pitch. The centred positions in the two mask factors differ by one pitch, so each is a half-pitch from the midpoint. Their product changes the sign of the first Fourier order of \(t^2\). Every classical kick in the displayed pair contributes phase one. The Gaussian characteristic function gives the signed difference of the two predicted counts

\[ z_{\rm quantum}-z_{\rm classical} =-\tfrac12 e^{-(\kappa T_0\sigma_V)^2/2} (1+e^{-2\kappa^2L^2}) . \]

The difference stays observable with independently fixed resolutions. For \(s=T/T_0\in[0.95,1.05]\) the first and second cosine coefficients of the mask-product difference are \((\cos(\pi s)-c_s)/2\) and \((1-c_s)/8\), where \(c_s=(2+\cos(2\pi s))/3\). The constant coefficient cancels. The Gaussian position averages of these cosines against the readout are at least \(1/2\) for the first and between zero and one for the second. Since \(\cos(\pi s)<-3/4\), \(c_s>5/6\) and \(\kappa T_0\sigma_V\le1\), the negative difference has magnitude greater than \(3/16\) throughout the gate. Pixel pitches \(b_e,b_M\) and an offset error \(|\Delta y|\) change the difference by at most \(\kappa(b_e+b_M+2|\Delta y|)\), using \(|t'|\le\kappa/2\). Taking each at most \(\Lambda/256\) leaves a gap greater than \(1/16\) per incident trial before counting error. The prediction compares the two specified instruments. A force law allowed to vary with the setting still needs independent control.

The long tails of \(g\) are dispensable for any fixed finite detector. For \(n\) exhaustive joint pixel and clock outcomes, including overflow, let \(v(J)\) be the vector of their probabilities at fixed kick. Its components are measurable, bounded and sum to one. Positive cubature for these \(n\) functions gives at most \(n\) finite kicks and positive weights reproducing \(\int v(J)g(J)\,dJ\) exactly. The weights sum to one because the pixels exhaust the outcomes (Corollary 2, passage). Bayer and Teichmann. For a fixed \(h\) shorter than the earliest flight time, the opposite forces \(\pm(J/h)(4-6t/h)\) on \(0<t<h\) deliver exactly \(\pm J\). Their first time moments vanish, so at every \(T>h\) the endpoint shifts are exactly \(TJ/m\) and \(-TJ/M\). The force remains finite under any mathematical refinement of the pulse. A prescribed independent ceiling \(J_{\max}\) is a different condition. Truncating \(g\) at this ceiling and renormalizing changes any pixel-event probability by at most the removed probability, bounded by \(4j_0/(\pi J_{\max})\) using the displayed density. Distinguishing the alternatives requires a sufficiently small measured ceiling.

6. Consequence for STATE

Item 2 gains an exact two-path error law on the Galileo cubic action, with the endpoint conversion, phase aliases, resources and limiting prescriptions explicit. Fixed-resolution refinement erases this phase record; classical-first averaging or calibrated attenuation gives the precise contrasting limits. The result locates a conditional record threshold at \(K_\tau=\tau\Delta E/12\) for the matched polygon protocol, and at the corresponding calibrated action for a literal inertial reunion. Position counts after complementary apertures offer a different test. Three settings admit an explicit pair of positive action scales. The integrated movable-window harmonic distinguishes their magnitudes under calibrated passive transmission. Its outstanding physical input is an observed deviation from the classical sum larger than independently bounded shutter and detector effects. Opposite classical impulses reproduce the averaged electron and shutter positions jointly. The record that cancels internal force exactly also cancels a common quantum action. The necessity of such a common scale remains open.
