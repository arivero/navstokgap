Constructing Newton motion: refinement, continuation and regularity

Markdown source · PDF

Maintained result. For a globally Lipschitz acceleration, arbitrary positive partitions and independently mixed kick–drift orders construct the same unique initial-value motion, with an explicit error proportional to the mesh. A conservative logarithmic force family shows the exact limit of that sufficient hypothesis: an unbounded slope still permits unique motion when \(0<\beta\le2\); finite-time arrival and waiting branches occur for \(\beta>2\). Local action tends to zero in both regimes. These are classical existence and uniqueness statements, with no positive action premise.

For a continuous outward central force, the departure of one moving orbit from its inertial tangent at successive half-times determines whether a body initially at rest at the centre can leave. The short-time force coefficient converges uniformly over bounded-speed orbits in both cases. Allowing the force to change sign gives two explicit laws with the same moving positions, velocities and accelerations at all those times and opposite answers about rest.

At a point of nonzero speed and curvature, one finite inward force gives opposite continuum orbits for the two orders of an impulse and a free flight. A nonnegative spherical gravitating source restores unique nonradial motion. For the branching force, uniform agreement of the canonical products over a set of trial actions is equivalent to a positive infimum of that set. A positive lower width for classical ensembles gives the same refinement property. For the shrinking Gaussian preparations the two theories also give the same probabilities for each of the two limiting radii. The odds change when the impulse interval is comparable to the position width times its squared logarithm.

The polygon proof uses standard stability, consistency and Gronwall arguments. The arrival test is a direct energy calculation, not the general first-order Osgood criterion. Teschl [@TeschlODE2012, §2.7, pp.54–56 and Problem 2.25, p.58; passages read] supplies the Euler compactness background; López Pouso [@LopezPouso2005; metadata and abstract read] treats a broader second-order problem whose full theorem is not used here. See the source companion. Internal Terra/Sol checks of the written arguments were model reviews, not independent peer review or novelty evidence.

1. Start with a finite Newton polygon

Let \(m>0\) be the mass and \(f(q)=F(q)/m\) the acceleration. The desired motion would satisfy \[\dot q=v,\qquad\dot v=f(q),\qquad(q(0),v(0))=(q_0,v_0).\tag{1}\]

For now we do not assume that a solution exists. Given a positive time step \(\tau\), define a drift and a force kick by \[ D_\tau(q,v)=(q+\tau v,v),\qquad K_\tau(q,v)=(q,v+\tau f(q)). \tag{2} \]

A step can kick first, \(D_\tau K_\tau\), or drift first, \(K_\tau D_\tau\). The first choice uses the force at the old position; the second uses the force at the new position. Both give a finite piecewise straight trajectory with impulses. Neither is assumed to agree exactly with the unknown continuous motion.

Fix \(T>0\), take any finite positive partition of \([0,T]\), choose either ordering in each cell, and compose the maps chronologically. There is no equal-step or nesting requirement. When a time is inserted, recompute the later states from the fixed initial state and the prescribed force rule.

Existence of the limit requires two kinds of estimates. Stability bounds how differences grow. Consistency bounds the error in the finite force balance. The two together will construct the ODE solution.

2. Lipschitz control supplies stability

Assume for this construction that \[|f(q)-f(Q)|\le\Lambda|q-Q|\quad(q,Q\in\mathbb R),\qquad\Lambda\ge0. \tag{3}\]

The quantity \(\Lambda\) has units of inverse time squared. Choose a reference frequency \(\rho>0\) and use the length-valued phase-space norm \[ \|(q,v)\|_\rho=|q|+\frac{|v|}{\rho},\qquad \alpha=\max(\rho,\Lambda/\rho),\quad \beta=\rho+\Lambda/\rho,\quad b=|f(0)|/\rho. \tag{4} \]

There is no action constant in these definitions. Directly from (2), \[ \operatorname{Lip}(D_\tau)\le1+\rho\tau,\qquad \operatorname{Lip}(K_\tau)\le1+\Lambda\tau/\rho. \tag{5} \]

Thus either cell map has Lipschitz constant at most \(e^{\beta\tau}\). The constants multiply under composition, and their exponents add. Every finite polygon therefore has the same upper amplification \(e^{\beta T}\), independently of the number and placement of vertices.

We also need a bound on the states themselves, because consistency involves the force along the polygon. For either cell map \(M_\tau\), \[ \|M_\tau z\|_\rho\le e^{\beta\tau}\|z\|_\rho +\tau e^{\beta\tau}b. \tag{6} \]

For a kick-first cell, the inhomogeneous term is actually at most \(\tau(1+\rho\tau)b\); for a drift-first cell it is \(\tau b\). Iterating (6) gives the common vertex bound \[R=e^{\beta T}(\|z_0\|_\rho+Tb).\tag{7}\]

In particular \(|q_j|\le R\), \(|v_j|\le\rho R\), and \(|f(q_j)|\le A:=|f(0)|+\Lambda R\). A very irregular partition has not created a new force or stability constant.

3. The polygons construct the ODE solution

Linearly interpolate both vertex coordinates to obtain \(z_\pi(t)=(q_\pi(t),v_\pi(t))\). The displayed velocity is continuous; it differs from the physical constant velocity along each straight polygon segment by at most that cell’s kick. This distinction disappears with the mesh.

Let \(\delta=|\pi|\) be the maximum cell duration. In a kick-first cell, with elapsed time \(s\in[0,\tau]\), \[ \dot q_\pi-v_\pi=(\tau-s)f(q_j),\qquad \dot v_\pi-f(q_\pi)=f(q_j)-f(q_\pi). \tag{8} \]

In a drift-first cell the analogous expressions are \(-sf(q_{j+1})\) and \(f(q_{j+1})-f(q_\pi)\). The bounds in (7) give, for almost every time, \[ \|\dot z_\pi-\mathcal F(z_\pi)\|_\rho\le C\delta, \quad\mathcal F(q,v)=(v,f(q)),\quad C=|f(0)|+2\Lambda R. \tag{9} \]

The vector field \(\mathcal F\) is Lipschitz with constant \(\alpha\) in the norm (4). Two polygons with the same initial state consequently satisfy \[ \sup_{t\le T}\|z_\pi(t)-z_\sigma(t)\|_\rho \le C\frac{e^{\alpha T}-1}{\alpha} (|\pi|+|\sigma|). \tag{10} \]

To verify this estimate, integrate the two residual equations (9), subtract, and apply the elementary integral Gronwall inequality. That inequality follows by integrating the differential inequality for an upper bound after multiplying by \(e^{-\alpha t}\).

Completeness of continuous path space now constructs a uniform limit \(z(t)\). The residual integral tends to zero; the Lipschitz bound passes \(\mathcal F(z_\pi)\) to \(\mathcal F(z)\) under the time integral. Hence \[z(t)=z_0+\int_0^t\mathcal F(z(s))\,ds.\tag{11}\]

The limit is differentiable and solves (1). Applying the same difference estimate with zero residual proves uniqueness. Letting one mesh tend to zero in (10) gives a quantitative error for any other partition. Both kick orders, mixed arbitrarily, lead to the same solution. Since \(T\) was arbitrary, the global Lipschitz hypothesis constructs forward motion for every finite time.

This is the first analogy with Balaban: one proves bounds for each finite description before appealing to a limiting object. The limit follows from estimates uniform in the refinement. Nothing in this argument requires choosing \(\hbar>0\).

4. Local continuation and velocity-dependent forces

For locally Lipschitz acceleration, extend it from a bounded region and apply the construction until the trajectory leaves that region. Continuation then requires bounds preventing escape from every compact phase-space region.

For \(V\in C^1\) with locally Lipschitz \(\nabla V\), (1) has a unique local solution, by the usual Cauchy–Lipschitz theorem. Along it, \[E(q,v)=\tfrac12m|v|^2+V(q),\qquad \frac d{dt}E=0.\] If \(V\) is bounded below, (2) bounds \(|v|\). On any finite interval \(|q(t)|\le |q_0|+|t|\sup|v|\), so \((q,v)\) remains in a compact set. The local continuation theorem then gives a unique solution for all positive and negative times. Coercivity of \(V\) is a stronger condition than is needed for this finite-time argument.

Smoothness alone does not guarantee global existence. For \(m=1\), \(V(q)=-q^4/4\), \(q_0>0\) and \(v_0=q_0^2/\sqrt2\), the zero-energy solution \[q(t)=\frac{q_0}{1-q_0t/\sqrt2}\] escapes to infinity at \(t=\sqrt2/q_0\). A force singularity can also stop a classical solution: for example, a radial attractive Kepler trajectory can reach \(r=0\) in finite time. A rule for continuing through the singular point is further data.

In three dimensions the Lorentz force, with a static scalar potential and a static magnetic field, gives \[ \dot q=v,\qquad m\dot v=-\nabla V(q)+e\,v\times B(q). \] It is not a force depending on position alone. If \(\nabla V\) and \(B\) are defined and locally Lipschitz on all of \(\mathbb R^3\), the full phase-space vector field is locally Lipschitz, so local existence and uniqueness still hold. Moreover \(v\cdot(v\times B)=0\), hence the conservative energy is conserved. If \(V\) is bounded below, the same speed and position bounds as above prove global existence and uniqueness, even if \(B\) is not globally bounded.

For instance, if \(V\) is constant and \(B=B_0\hat z\), the field rotates the transverse velocity with cyclotron frequency \(eB_0/m\) and leaves the longitudinal velocity constant. A velocity-dependent force is thus not an exception to the ODE theory; its dependence must be included in the phase-space vector field being estimated.

If \(B=\nabla\times A\) on \(\mathbb R^3\), the quantum Hamiltonian is \[H_{\hbar,A}=\frac1{2m}(-i\hbar\nabla-eA(q))^2+V(q).\] The kinetic momenta generally do not commute: \([\pi_i,\pi_j]=i\hbar e(\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i)\). A positive scalar Brownian-bridge density does not directly cover this magnetic problem: a magnetic Euclidean representation contains a phase. The coherent-packet theorem treats the scalar Hamiltonian; it does not prove this magnetic extension.

An endpoint problem fixes \(q(0)\) and \(q(T)\) rather than initial velocity. It has a separate possible singularity. For the harmonic oscillator the full phase-space derivative is \[ \begin{pmatrix} \cos\omega T&\sin(\omega T)/\omega\\ -\omega\sin(\omega T)&\cos\omega T \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det=1. \]

The position response to initial velocity is \(\sin(\omega T)/\omega\). It vanishes at a conjugate time while the full phase-space map stays invertible. The corresponding endpoint action Hessian can fail to invert even though the initial-value motion remains unique. The time-blocking note gives a finite-polygon determinant that approaches this singularity under refinement.

Therefore a failure of one position-only generating function is not, by itself, a failure of Newton’s dynamics. A physical completion argument has to establish which extra information the complete theory needs and why it cannot be retained classically.

5. The conservative arrival criterion

Near zero let \(a\) be continuous, odd, positive on \((0,r_0)\), zero at zero and smooth away from zero. Consider \[ \ddot q=a(q),\qquad W(q)=\int_0^q a(s)\,ds\quad(q\ge0), \qquad V(q)=-mW(|q|),\quad m>0. \tag{L1} \]

Assume a bounded continuous global extension of the force, smooth away from zero, by cutting off the potential outside a neighborhood of zero. The bounded-cusp argument, section 1, then gives global existence and bounds preventing escape. The issue remaining here is uniqueness, not existence.

Conservation of \(E=m\dot q^2/2+V(q)\) follows by the chain rule since \(V\in C^1\). Any solution starting at rest at zero has \(E=0\). On a positive departing segment its velocity must be \[ \dot q=\sqrt{2W(q)},\qquad \mathcal T(q)=\int_0^q\frac{ds}{\sqrt{2W(s)}}. \tag{L2} \]

Arrival criterion. If \(\mathcal T(q)=\infty\) for one, hence every small \(q>0\), rest is the unique local solution from \((0,0)\). If \(\mathcal T(q)<\infty\), the inverse \(Q=\mathcal T^{-1}\) constructs departures \(q(t)=\pm Q(t-t_0)\) after every waiting time \(t_0\).

For the first implication, a nonconstant solution has an excursion of one sign starting from zero. Its velocity cannot vanish inside that excursion because \(W>0\), and separation of variables would give a finite value for the divergent integral (L2). For the converse, \(Q'=\sqrt{2W(Q)}\) and \(Q''=a(Q)\). Both tend to zero at departure, so adjoining rest gives a \(C^2\) Newton trajectory. Reversing the two departures supplies two different incoming full states that merge at zero in finite time, exactly as in the cusp note.

For \(a(q)=c q^\alpha\) this test reproduces the familiar \(\alpha<1\) nonuniqueness threshold: its integrand is proportional to \(q^{-(1+\alpha)/2}\). The logarithmic family below tests the more delicate border where no power-law Lipschitz constant exists.

Exact logarithmic threshold

Fix \(\Omega,\ell>0\), \(\beta>0\) and put \(r=\beta/2\). For sufficiently small \(q>0\), with \(L=\log(\ell/q)>\max\{1,r\}\), set \[ W(q)=\frac{\Omega^2}{2}q^2 L^\beta,\qquad a(q)=\Omega^2q\bigl(L^\beta-rL^{\beta-1}\bigr). \tag{L3} \]

Extend \(a\) oddly near zero and the potential with a smooth cutoff farther out. This gives a bounded \(C^1\) potential and continuous bounded force; \(a(0)=0\) since \(q L^\beta\to0\). Its derivative on the positive side is \[ a'(q)=\Omega^2\left[L^\beta-\frac{3\beta}{2}L^{\beta-1} +\frac{\beta(\beta-1)}2L^{\beta-2}\right], \tag{L4} \]

which diverges for every \(\beta>0\). Thus none of these forces is locally Lipschitz at zero.

Nevertheless the energy-zero speed and arrival integral are exact: \[ \dot q=\Omega qL^r,\qquad \mathcal T(q)=\frac1\Omega\int_L^\infty u^{-r}\,du. \tag{L5} \]

Consequently rest is unique for \(\beta\le2\). For \(\beta>2\), \[ \mathcal T(q)=\frac{L^{1-r}}{\Omega(r-1)},\qquad Q(t)=\ell\exp\left(-[\Omega(r-1)t]^{-1/(r-1)}\right). \tag{L6} \]

The formula for \(Q\) is valid until the departure leaves the region where (L3) is used. It is flat to every order at \(t=0\), so an exactly resting body and one that leaves after a delay satisfy the same Newton equation and initial data.

For \(\beta\le2\) the whole extended IVP is unique. Away from zero this is ordinary local Lipschitz uniqueness. At zero with nonzero initial velocity, the conserved-energy first-order equation has a nonzero square root and a \(C^1\) right-hand side near the crossing, so it gives unique passage. At zero velocity the arrival criterion applies. Bounded force then gives a global reversible point flow. Its dependence on initial state is continuous: common finite-time bounds give compactness for any converging initial sequence; every subsequential limit solves the same unique IVP. Thus all subsequences have the same limit. A divergent local slope has not destroyed reversible mechanics.

At the borderline \(\beta=2\), the outgoing energy-zero solution from \(q_0>0\) is particularly informative: \[ q(t)=\ell\left(\frac{q_0}{\ell}\right)^{e^{-\Omega t}}, \qquad \dot q(0)=\Omega q_0\log(\ell/q_0). \tag{L7} \]

While it remains in the logarithmic region, it stays continuous as \(q_0\downarrow0\) at every fixed finite time, although its position sensitivity diverges. This is a genuine example of unique stable motion without a Lipschitz dependence bound at rest.

The general first-order Osgood sufficient test applied directly to a modulus of size \(s[\log(1/s)]^\beta\) reaches only \(\beta\le1\). The stronger range \(1<\beta\le2\) above uses the conservative Newton structure and energy; failure of that first-order sufficient test is not a proof of nonuniqueness.

What one moving orbit determines about rest

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-18. In the 1713 Principia, Lemma X, Corollary 4 relates initial displacement divided by time squared to force. Proposition VI invokes it for the departure from an orbital tangent. The following is a modern question about the same quadratic coefficient. Can it converge uniformly under refinement even though a body at rest has several continuations? We use a diameter orbit. Proposition VI’s geometric ratios are defined at positive radius. Newton, 1713, Book I (Lemma X and Proposition VI, passages).

Write the force as \(m\mathbf a(q)\), with \(m>0\). On a compact region \(K\) where \(\mathbf a\) is continuous, let \(\omega_K\) be its modulus of continuity. Every exact orbit staying in \(K\) at speed at most \(V\) obeys

\[ \left|\frac{2[q(t+s)-q(t)-s\dot q(t)]}{s^2} -\mathbf a(q(t))\right|\le \omega_K(Vs). \tag{D1} \]

The quotient averages the acceleration with the unit-mass weight \(2(s-u)/s^2\) on \(0\le u\le s\). This is the integral form of Newton’s equation in (1). Thus even for a family of trajectories the coefficient converges uniformly, with the same bound on every inserted cell of duration \(s\).

Now take \(\mathbf a(q)=a(r)q/r\) for \(0<r\le r_0\) and \(\mathbf a(0)=0\), where \(a\) is continuous, \(a(0)=0\), \(a\ge0\), and \(W(r)=\int_0^r a(u)\,du>0\) for \(r>0\). The magnitude \(a\) may oscillate. Start a radial solution \(x_v\) at the centre with any speed \(v>0\). Its radius increases uniquely by \(\dot x_v=\sqrt{v^2+2W(x_v)}\). Take \(H>0\) small enough that \(x_v(H)\le r_0\) and \(\dot x_v\le 2v\). Define the dimensionless excess over the inertial tangent and a sum with units of time by

\[ d(s)=\frac{x_v(s)-vs}{vs},\qquad t_n=2^{-n}H,\qquad S_v(H)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{t_n}{\sqrt{d(t_n)}}. \tag{D2} \]

Criterion. Rest at the centre is the unique forward motion locally if \(S_v(H)=\infty\). If \(S_v(H)<\infty\), a body can wait for any time and then leave along any ray. More precisely, the zero-energy travel time \(\mathcal T\) in (L2) obeys

\[ \frac{S_v(H)}8\le \mathcal T(x_v(H)) \le\frac{2}{\sqrt3} S_v(H). \tag{D3} \]

The rest criterion in (L2) is the usual conservative quadrature. Angular momentum from rest is zero, so every departing excursion lies on a ray. Comparison (D3) uses positions of the moving orbit alone, with no differentiation of its tangent excess. López Pouso, 2005 (abstract, general scalar existence criterion).

To prove (D3), put \(z(s)=\dot x_v(s)/v-1\). Outward acceleration gives nondecreasing \(0\le z\le1\). Its average \(d(s)\) satisfies \(z(s/2)/2\le d(s)\le z(s)\) and is nondecreasing. Changing variables \(r=x_v(s)\) in (L2) gives

\[ \mathcal T(x_v(H)) =\int_0^H\frac{1+z(s)}{\sqrt{z(s)[2+z(s)]}}\,ds . \]

For \(0\le z\le1\) the factor \((1+z)/\sqrt{2+z}\) lies between \(1/\sqrt2\) and \(2/\sqrt3\). If \(I_d=\int_0^H ds/\sqrt{d(s)}\) and \(I_z=\int_0^H ds/\sqrt{z(s)}\), the average bounds give \(I_z\le I_d\le2\sqrt2 I_z\). On \([t_{n+1},t_n]\), the endpoint values of \(1/\sqrt d\) bracket the integrand. Summing the two bounds gives \(S_v(H)/2\le I_d\le S_v(H)\) and proves (D3), including the divergent case. The improper integral also equals the supremum of lower sums on compact subintervals of \((0,H]\). Its finiteness is independent of choosing dyadic subdivisions and of the nonzero speed chosen in (D2).

For the force in (L3) let \(L_s=\log(\ell/(vs))\). Since \(x_v(s)/(vs)\to1\), energy gives \(z(s)\sim\Omega^2s^2 L_s^\beta/2\). Averaging gives \(d(s)\sim\Omega^2s^2 L_s^\beta/6\), using \(\int_0^s u^2 L_u^\beta du\sim s^3 L_s^\beta/3\). The terms of (D2) are therefore asymptotic to \(\sqrt6/(\Omega[L_H+n\log2]^{\beta/2})\). Every member of the family has the uniform finite-force limit (D1). The sum diverges for \(0<\beta\le2\) and converges at every \(\beta>2\), where even an arbitrarily delayed departure obeys that same limit. Here \(r_0,\ell\) are lengths, \(H\) is a time, \(v,V\) are speeds and \(\Omega\) is an inverse time. Both outcomes occur in classical mechanics with no action parameter.

Two central forces with the same positions at successive half-times

Keep the work primitive \(W(r)>0\) for \(r>0\) and allow the radial acceleration to reverse sign. The positions, velocities and accelerations of a moving solution at the whole sequence \(t_n=2^{-n}H\) can then agree for two force laws whose solutions from rest have different uniqueness properties. This pair uses autonomous potentials fixed across all \(n\).

Fix \(H,v,m>0\). Prescribe two increasing radius functions by \(x_i(t)=v\int_0^t(1+z_i(s))\,ds\) on \([0,H]\), starting with \(z_1(t)=(t/H)^{3/2}\). In each \(I_n=[t_n/2,t_n]\) set \(u=2t/t_n-1\). Use the smooth step \(g(s)=\rho(s)/(\rho(s)+\rho(1-s))\), where \(\rho(s)=e^{-1/s}\) for \(s>0\) and is zero otherwise. Put \(\chi(u)=g(8u-1)g(5-8u)\) and \(\psi(u)=8g'(8u-6)\). Then \(0\le\chi\le1\) is one on \([1/4,1/2]\), while the nonnegative \(\psi\) has disjoint support and integrates to one. Set

\[ \begin{split} b_n&=\frac{2}{t_n}\int_{I_n}\chi(2s/t_n-1) [z_1(s)-(s/H)^2]\,ds,\\ z_2(t)&=(1-\chi(u))z_1(t)+\chi(u)(t/H)^2+b_n\psi(u). \end{split} \tag{D4} \]

The subtracted and added integrals are equal on each \(I_n\). Hence \(x_1(t_n)=x_2(t_n)\) by summing the intervals down to zero. Both \(z_i\) coincide in a neighbourhood of each node, so the speeds and accelerations there also agree. Inside the interval, \(0\le b_n\le(t_n/H)^{3/2}\) and \(z_2>0\). Differentiation gives \(|z_2'|\le C(t_n/H)^{1/2}/H\) with \(C=2(1+\|\chi'\|_\infty+\|\psi'\|_\infty)\). Both \(z_i\) thus extend to \(C^1\) functions with value and derivative zero at \(t=0\).

Since \(x_i'\ge v\), the assignments

\[ a_i(x_i(t))=v z_i'(t),\qquad W_i(x_i(t))=v^2 z_i(t)(1+z_i(t)/2) \]

define continuous accelerations with \(a_i(0)=0\) on the same radial interval. The second identity follows from \(W_i'=a_i\) and \(W_i(0)=0\). The potentials \(-mW_i(|q|)\) are \(C^1\) at the origin. They admit bounded-force extensions past the common outer radius. The first force points outward. The second must turn inward on each sufficiently small cell to reach \((t/H)^2\) from the larger \(z_1(t_n/2)\), then turns outward again.

For the first force (L2) is finite since \(\int_0^H(t/H)^{-3/4}dt\) is finite. For the second, on the plateau \(1/4\le u\le1/2\) one has \(1/\sqrt{z_2(t)}=H/t\). Its integral contributes at least \(H/8\) on each cell. The travel-time integrand in the proof of (D3) is at least \(1/\sqrt{2z_2}\), so (L2) diverges. Thus the first force admits arbitrary waiting followed by departure, while the second has unique rest. Inserting a time inside a modified cell can distinguish the two moving curves. Knowing the complete moving curve even determines \(W(x_v(t))=(\dot x_v(t)^2-v^2)/2\). Equality here concerns one particular nested sequence accumulating at the initial instant.

Crossing times and arbitrary-mesh polygons

Let \(q_n=\ell e^{-n}\) with \(n\) sufficiently large. Along the zero-energy trajectory, the time for the annulus \(q_{n+1}<q<q_n\) is \[ \tau_n=\frac1\Omega\int_n^{n+1}u^{-r}\,du, \qquad \tau_n\sim\frac1{\Omega n^r}. \tag{L8} \]

On this annulus the local acceleration slope bound is of order \(\Lambda_n=\Omega^2n^\beta\). The short-cell scale \(\Lambda_n^{-1/2}\) is therefore of the same order as \(\tau_n\). One can subdivide an annulus crossing into a fixed number of shorter steps to obtain a small local Lipschitz amplification parameter. There is no uniform slope bound across all annuli.

The issue is whether infinitely many controlled annulus crossings can fit into finite physical time: \[ \sum_{n\ge N}\tau_n \begin{cases} =\infty,&\beta\le2,\\ <\infty,&\beta>2. \end{cases} \tag{L9} \]

For \(\beta\le2\), a zero-energy trajectory cannot reach the infinite-depth boundary at zero in finite time. For \(\beta>2\), perfectly regular motion on each finite collection of annuli reaches that boundary in finite time, and the branch datum is missing there. Local estimates at every finite scale do not by themselves control their infinite composition. This is the useful multiscale connection with the Balaban lesson; it is not a transfer of his field-theory proof.

For the unique regime, arbitrary-mesh Euler polygons, and the lesson’s mixed kick–drift polygons, converge uniformly on every fixed finite time interval. Bounded force gives common position and momentum bounds; uniform continuity gives a vanishing integral residual; compactness supplies solution subsequences; uniqueness makes the whole family converge. No \(O(\text{mesh})\) rate is asserted here.

In the nonunique regime, exact zero initial data make these deterministic polygons remain at rest on every partition. However, preparations \(q_0\downarrow0\), \(\dot q_0=\Omega q_0L_0^r\downarrow0\) have \[ L(t)=\left[L_0^{1-r}+\Omega(r-1)t\right]^{-1/(r-1)} \longrightarrow [\Omega(r-1)t]^{-1/(r-1)}. \tag{L10} \]

Thus they converge to the nonzero departure (L6) at fixed small \(t>0\). For each positive \(q_0\), choose a fine enough polygon to approximate that regular trajectory; a diagonal sequence then has vanishing mesh and vanishing initial error but a departing limit. Refinement is not jointly stable with preparation refinement at zero. This is a stronger failure than an unbounded numerical error estimate, while keeping the exact-zero polygon’s resting limit explicit.

Vanishing local action and a quantum residual

The positive outgoing momentum is \(p(q)=m\Omega qL^r\). Its canonical action from zero to \(q\) is \[ \mathcal J(q)=\int_0^q p(s)\,ds =m\Omega\ell^2\int_L^\infty e^{-2u}u^r\,du. \tag{L11} \]

For \(L>r/2\), elementary bounds give \[ \frac{m\Omega}2q^2L^r \le\mathcal J(q) \le\frac{m\Omega q^2L^r}{2-r/L}. \tag{L12} \]

The lower bound uses \(u^r\ge L^r\); the upper bound uses \((L+v)^r\le L^r e^{rv/L}\) after writing \(u=L+v\). Therefore \(\mathcal J(q)\sim(m\Omega/2)q^2L^r\to0\) for every \(\beta>0\). The temporal threshold (L9) creates no positive action threshold.

There is still a concrete quantum comparison. In the local region \(V=-p(q)^2/(2m)\), take the stationary energy-zero test functions \[ \psi_\pm(q)=p(q)^{-1/2} \exp\left(\pm\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathcal J(q)\right), \qquad H_\hbar=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dq^2}+V(q). \tag{L13} \]

Their eikonal and first-derivative transport terms cancel exactly. Writing \(b(q)=p(q)^{-1/2}\) gives \(H_\hbar\psi_\pm =-(\hbar^2/2m)b''e^{\pm i\mathcal J/\hbar}\) and the exact local ratio \[ \frac{|H_\hbar\psi_\pm|}{|V\psi_\pm|} =\left[\frac{\hbar}{m\Omega q^2L^r}\right]^2 \left|\frac34-\frac rL+\frac{r(r+2)}{4L^2}\right| \sim\frac{3\hbar^2}{16\mathcal J(q)^2}. \tag{L14} \]

For example, the differentiation behind this identity is \(p'/p=(1-r/L)/q\) and \(p''/p=-r[1-(r-1)/L]/(q^2L)\); then \(b''/b=3(p'/p)^2/4-p''/(2p)\). Equation (L14) is a local residual identity, not an error bound for a normalized state or the full propagator.

At fixed \(\hbar>0\), action comparable with \(\hbar\) makes the local residual of this trajectory ansatz order one even in the classically unique regime. Every sufficiently small positive \(\hbar\) defines a radius \(q_\hbar\) by \(\mathcal J(q_\hbar)=\hbar\), and that radius tends to zero as \(\hbar\to0\). The bounded-potential Hamiltonian has global unitary dynamics at each positive \(\hbar\) by the cusp note’s held Kato–Rellich input. A circle with two tangent departures

Use length \(R>0\), inverse time \(\omega>0\) and inertial mass \(m>0\). In this section positions are in units \(R\), times in \(1/\omega\), momenta in \(mR\omega\) and energies in \(mR^2\omega^2\). Write \(r=|q|\). Launch in the plane at \(q_0=(1,0)\) with \(v_0=(0,1)\). Specific angular momentum in these units is one. The acceleration is \(-g(r)q/r\). Fix \(b=e^{-20}\) and on \(|y|=|r-1|\le b\) set

\[ g(r)=r^{-3}-\alpha(y),\qquad \alpha(y)=y(L^4-2L^3),\quad L=\log(1/|y|),\qquad \alpha(0)=0. \tag{C1} \]

The odd \(\alpha\) is strictly increasing there since its derivative off zero is \(L^2(L^2-6L+6)>0\). Also \(\alpha(b)<1/4\). Hence \(g\) is positive and strictly decreasing on the annulus. Extend \(g\) linearly from zero to \(g(1-b)\) on the inside and by \(g(1+b)(1+b)^2/r^2\) outside. This is one continuous inward force, bounded by \(3\) in acceleration units, with a bounded \(C^1\) potential \(U\), \(U'=g\). Its magnitude on the departing outward branch decreases continuously near the join.

The same force occurs as a reciprocal interaction in a closed Newtonian two-body Hamiltonian. Take positive inertial masses \(m_1,m_2\) with reduced mass \(m\) and pair potential \(mR^2\omega^2 U(|q_1-q_2|/R)\) in dimensional positions. The centre of mass moves freely and the relative motion is exactly (C1).

The radial equation and angular velocity are \(y''=\alpha(y)\) and \(\theta'=r^{-2}\). At the common energy, \(|y'|=|y|L^2\) once \(y\ne0\). Besides the circle, either radial branch can leave after any waiting time \(s\). Their radii for \(t>s\) are

\[ r(t)=1\pm Y(t-s),\qquad Y(u)=e^{-1/u}\quad(u>0), \tag{C2} \]

until \(Y=b\). This is the logarithmic example (L3) translated to a circular orbit by the centrifugal term. Integrating \(r^{-2}\) gives the angle with the common value at \(s\). All time derivatives of the two motions agree with the circle at the join. In physical units the speed is \(R\omega\), the acceleration magnitude is \(R\omega^2\) and the curvature is \(1/R\) there. The same entire incoming arc, angular momentum, energy and short-time force coefficient admit both futures.

Which orbit the polygons select

\[ \frac{r_{n+1}-2r_n+r_{n-1}}{h^2} =-g(r_n)+\frac{B(r_{n+1},z)+B(r_{n-1},z)}{r_n^2} =\alpha(r_n-1)+e_n,\qquad |e_n|\le350h^2 . \tag{C3} \]

For the constant, each neighbour differs in radius by at most \(2h\). Taylor’s formula for \(|q|\), with Hessian norm at most \(2\) in the ball, bounds the radial second difference by \((3+8)h^2\). For \(w\ge1/2\), \(|B(w,z)-1/(2w)|\le z/(2w^3)\le16h^2\). The first two derivatives of \(1/(2w)\) have magnitude at most \(2\) and \(8\). Summing its two Taylor expansions about \(r_n\) costs at most \((22+32)h^2\). Multiplication by \(r_n^{-2}\le4\) gives (C3).

Since \(\alpha(h^2/4)/h^2\to\infty\), induction in (C3) gives positive increments of \(s_n\) and second difference at least \(h^2\alpha(s_n)/2\). \[ 2\sqrt2\sum_{k\ge1}[\log(1/d)+k\log2]^{-2} \le \frac{2\sqrt2}{\log2\,\log(1/d)} . \tag{C4} \]

For every \(t>0\) first choose \(d\) so the latter bound is less than \(t\), then take \(h\) small. The signed radius has reached \(d\) by \(t\). Every limit therefore departs immediately on its prescribed side. The energy integral gives precisely (C2) with \(s=0\), and the angular equation fixes the full curve. What a nonnegative spherical source supplies

Newton’s shell theorem gives a physically independent restriction on the force law. Return to dimensional variables. Let \(G>0\) and let \(M(r)\) be the enclosed mass of a finite nonnegative spherical source, continuous at \(r>0\), with \(0<M(\infty)<\infty\). A point mass at zero is allowed. The inward acceleration is \(g(r)=GM(r)/r^2\). For every nonzero specific angular momentum \(j=|q\wedge\dot q|\), this force has a unique global trajectory from each nonradial point state, including when the radial speed vanishes. No derivative bound on the density is needed. Newton, 1713, Book I, Propositions LXX–LXXI (spherical shell theorems, passages).

Write \(\Phi'=g\), \(\Phi(\infty)=0\) and \(U_j(r)=j^2/(2r^2)+\Phi(r)\) for the energy per unit mass at a radial turn. Its unique stationary radius \(R_c\) satisfies \(GR_c M(R_c)=j^2\): \(rM(r)\) increases strictly wherever \(M(r)>0\). For every \(r>0\), monotonicity of the enclosed mass gives \(\Phi(r)-\Phi(R_c)\ge GM(R_c)(1/R_c-1/r)\) on either side of \(R_c\). Consequently

\[ U_j(r)-U_j(R_c)\ \ge\ \frac{j^2}{2} \left(\frac1r-\frac1{R_c}\right)^2 . \tag{C5} \]

A point source attains equality. Starting at the circular energy forces \(r=R_c\). Elsewhere a radial turning point is simple, and its nonzero radial acceleration fixes the continuation by the energy quadrature. With nonzero radial velocity the same quadrature determines the motion directly. Finally \(U_j(r)\ge j^2/(2r^2)-GM(\infty)/r\) keeps every finite-energy orbit away from zero. The speed is bounded on that exterior region, giving global continuation.

For (C1), \(r^2g(r)<1\) just outside the unit circle while \(g(1)=1\). Its dimensionless Poisson density \((r^2g)'/(4\pi r^2)\) is signed. It is constant in the core, zero beyond \(1+b\) and integrable to every finite power in the shell. Indeed the sole unbounded factor there grows as \(\log^4(1/|r-1|)\) and the integral for its \(p\)th power is bounded by a constant times \(\int_{20}^{\infty} L^{4p}e^{-L}dL\). Continuity of \(r^2g\) excludes a surface delta at the joins. The squared field integral \(4\pi\int_0^\infty r^2g(r)^2dr\) is finite. Thus integrability of the signed source and finiteness of its field integral allow the order dependence in (C3).

This uniqueness survives source and time refinement together. Let \(M_k\to M\) uniformly on compact radial intervals in \((0,\infty)\) for continuous enclosed masses as above. Fix the initial nonradial state and a finite time interval. Choose an annulus containing the whole limiting orbit strictly in its interior. Cut off all forces beyond a slightly larger annulus with the same cutoff, equal to one on the first. They now converge uniformly to a continuous bounded field. The §3 compactness argument, with the uniform force difference added to the residual, applies to arbitrary positive time partitions and arbitrary choices of impulse order. Every limit follows the unique orbit up to any first exit, which the orbit never makes. The original polygons thus have the same limit along every joint refinement. For a point source the fixed-time Coulomb estimate, §5a also takes a supplied reduced action to zero at fixed noncollisional initial position and momentum. Holding the nonzero angular momentum of the orbit fixed is compatible with this limit.

\[ \lim_{\delta\downarrow0} \sup_{\substack{a\in A,\ |\pi|\le\delta\\\sigma,\sigma'}} |p_{a,\pi,\sigma}-p_{a,\pi,\sigma'}| =\begin{cases}0,&\inf A>0,\\1,&\inf A=0.\end{cases} \tag{C6} \]

Each later \(J\) is at least half that value and \(\sin(\sqrt{10}\,t)/\sqrt{10}\ge t/2\) there. The sum of \(h\) times the remaining time over the kicks is at least one quarter of the squared block length, since \(h\ell^2\to0\). This again more than doubles \(J\). Each of the intervals from \(u_*/\ell^2\) to \(t_1\) and from \(t_1\) to \(t_2\) contains \(20\log\ell-O(1)\) blocks. Subsequently the signed nodal increments stay positive. During a flight the radius differs from linear interpolation by \(O(h^2)\), by bounded centrifugal acceleration. This proves (C11).

For clarity about diffraction, put \(E_2=d|H|/J^2\) and \(E_3=d^2|I|/J^3\). Let \(F_j\) denote the \(j\)th radial derivative of the acceleration during flight or of the velocity increment per unit \(h\) at a kick, as appropriate, and use the sum of continuous and impulse integrals \(d\nu=dt+h\sum_n\delta_{t_n}\). The explicit modified force bounds on the rays are

\[ |F_2|\le C(1+\ell^3/\max(s,|y|)),\qquad |F_3|\le C(1+\ell^3/\max(s,|y|)^2). \]

The Jacobi Green function is \(J(t)J(v')\int_{v'}^t J(u)^{-2}du\le4(t-v')J(t)/J(v')\). This follows from the Wronskian, which is preserved across every velocity jump. Differentiating the ray equation twice and three times gives sources \(F_2J^2\) and \(F_3J^3+3F_2JH\). Put \(B_2=d\,|F_2|\), \(B_3=d^2|F_3|\) and \(D=J(v')/J(t)\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} E_2(t)&\le4\int_0^t(t-v')D B_2\,d\nu(v'),\\ E_3(t)&\le4\int_0^t(t-v')D^2 \bigl(B_3+3B_2 E_2(v')\bigr)d\nu(v'). \end{aligned} \tag{C12} \]

Up to \(t_2\), \(D\le2\), \(B_2=O(\ell^4)\), \(B_3=O(\ell^5)\) and the time integral of \(t-v'\) is at most \(2t(t+h)\). Hence \(E_2=O((\log\ell)^2)\) and \(E_3=O(\ell(\log\ell)^2+(\log\ell)^4)\). After \(t_2\), split each integral at \(t_1\). On its first part \(D=O(\ell^{-8})\). On its second part (C11) gives \(B_2+B_3=O(d+\ell^{-5})\). First applying the \(E_2\) bound and then the \(E_3\) bound gives \(E_2+E_3=o(1)\) uniformly for \(t_2\le t\le T\).

Transport the half-density along these rays, so \(A(t,R_t(v))=A_0(v)/\sqrt{J(t,v)}\). Use the classical phase for each flight and subtract \(h\widetilde U\) at each kick. The standard caustic-free WKB residual bounds the norm error from the flights by \(\tfrac12 d^2\int_0^T\|\partial_r^2A(t)\|dt\). Schubert, Vallejos and Toscano, §2, (20)–(22) (transport and Duhamel estimate, passage). The amplitudes have compact support away from the radial endpoint, and kicks preserve the norm. Twice differentiating the transported half-density gives

\[ d^2\|\partial_r^2 A(t)\|\le C n_k \sup_v\bigl(J^{-2}+E_2/J+E_2^2+E_3\bigr). \]

On \([0,t_2]\) the integral of this bound is \(O(n_k(\log\ell)^3/\ell)=o(n_k)\). On \([t_2,T]\) it is \(o(n_k)\) by (C11)–(C12). Using the original quantum kicks on the approximate wave adds at most \(C n_k(t_1+h)s^2\ell^3/d^2=o(n_k)\) by (C10). After \(t_1\) its support is outside the modified strip. Unitarity propagates these residual bounds through every factor. All errors are uniform over retained modes, so their squared norms sum to \(o(1)\).

For either cutoff the final approximate wave is supported at radii converging uniformly to its selected radius. The two supports are disjoint at \(T\). Their masses equal their initial cutoff masses. The full initial radial position law gives the same limiting \(x\) density \(e^{-x^2}/\sqrt\pi\) as in the classical calculation. Letting the angular and radial cutoffs increase and \(\eta\) decrease to zero removes an arbitrarily small initial norm. This proves (C7) for every bounded continuous radial score, including \(f\).

Complete separation and the continuum dynamics

Suppose now \(h/(d\ell^2)\to\infty\). Define \(e\) by \(eL_e^2=h\), \(L_e=\log(1/e)>2\). Then \(d/e\to0\) and \(L_e\sim\log(1/h)\). In (C8) rescale the radius by \(e\) and time by \(L_e^{-2}\). For every bounded initial cutoff the rescaled displacement starts at zero in the limit and its initial difference quotient tends to \(\sigma/2\). Formula (C9) now reads \((\sigma/2)\sinh u\). The same level estimate yields the immediate \(\sigma\) branch for the classical ensemble.

For the quantum state first consider subsequences with \(\ell/L_e\) bounded. Keep the preparation width \(d\) and smoothing width \(s=d/\ell\). The smoothed force has the same inner limit since its change is \(O(s\ell^3)=o(e L_e^4)\). Use \(L_e\) in \(t_1,t_2\). All rays on a bounded initial cutoff now lie on the selected side after \(u_*\). The early upper bound is \(Ce\exp(C L_e^2 t_2)<\sqrt e\). Equation (C11) then gives \(|R_t-1|\ge eL_e^8\) and \(D=O(L_e^{-8})\) on its respective intervals. The early bounds for \(B_2,B_3\) are \(O(L_e^4),O(L_e^5)\), and their late sum is \(O(d+L_e^{-5})\) since \(d\le e\). Thus the same transport proof gives vanishing norm error, including the smoothing cost \(O((t_1+h)\ell)\). There is just one outgoing radius.

If \(\ell/L_e\to\infty\), \(d\) tends to zero faster than every fixed power of \(h\). Compare the packet directly with its point polygon \((q_n,p_n)\) in (C3). For \(r_n=|q_n|=1+y_n\), \(n\ge1\), put \(\Lambda_n=1+\log^2(1/|y_n|)\). The acceleration field \(F\) satisfies

\[ |F(x)-F(q_n)|\le C\Lambda_n^2 |x-q_n|. \]

Within \(|y_n|/2\) of \(q_n\) this is the derivative bound from (C1). For larger distances use its global short-distance modulus \(Cv(1+|\log v|^4)\) at distance \(v\), obtained by integrating \(1+\log^4(1/|y|)\) across a radial interval of length \(v\). The direction factor is Lipschitz in the annulus, the core is linear and the exterior has bounded derivative. In particular \(F\) is \(1/2\)-Hölder globally. This anchored estimate uses the same moment argument as the power-cusp propagation.

At the first cell Hölder continuity and the exact drift and kick shears bound both root-mean-square deviations from the point polygon by \(C\sqrt d\). For subsequent cells let \(E_n=\Lambda_n\|(Q-q_n)\psi_n\|+\|(P-p_n)\psi_n\|\). The signed displacement grows along this polygon, so \(\Lambda_n\) decreases. The exact shears and the anchored bound give \(E_{n+1}\le(1+C h\Lambda_n)^2 E_n\) in either order. The initial \(|y_1|\) is at least \(h^2/4\). By (C4) each level \(v\le |y_n|<2v\) costs at most \(C/\log^2(1/v)+O(h)\) in time. Summing over at most \(C\log(1/h)\) dyadic levels gives \(\sum_{n\ge1}^{N-1}h\Lambda_n =O(\log(1/h)+h\log^3(1/h))\). The amplification is at most a fixed power of \(1/h\), whereas \(E_1=O(\Lambda_1\sqrt d)\) decays faster than every such power. The terminal packet therefore selects the same radius as the point polygon. Every subsequence has a further subsequence in one of the two regimes for \(\ell/L_e\). This completes the joint limit in (C7), including \(\lambda=\infty\).

For the exact continuum evolution, at each fixed positive \(d\) the products converge for the quantum state by the product formula and for the classical ensemble by its almost-everywhere uniqueness. For any sequence \(d\downarrow0\) and any fixed bounded continuous radial score, choose equal cells with \(h<d^2\) and a score error less than \(d\) from that continuum evolution. Formula (C7) at \(\lambda=0\) gives the equal mixture for the exact dynamics as well.

In dimensional terms the inputs are \(m,R,\omega\), the time \(T/\omega\), the cell duration \(h/\omega\) and the specified preparation. They fix \(S_0=mR^2\omega\), the potential energy unit \(mR^2\omega^2\), the initial position and momentum deviations \(Rd/\sqrt2\) and \(mR\omega d/\sqrt2\), and the readout width \(R Y(T)\). The fixed constants \(b,T\) and the function of \(|q_{\rm phys}|/R\) used for the score are dimensionless. For a quantum sequence \(a\) with \(0<\lambda<\infty\), the sequence \(c a\) at the same cells, \(c>0\), has parameter \(\lambda/\sqrt c\). Distinct \(c\) therefore give different limiting scores within the prescribed canonical family. The model with zero quantum action and classical widths \(w=a/S_0\) reproduces the entire limiting radial law of the first sequence, including its order bias. Equality is asserted for these limits and this observable family.

6. Consequence for STATE

Initial-value construction, continuation, endpoint caustics and finite-time mergers have different hypotheses. Unbounded force slope alone establishes no merger and no action floor. Uniform convergence of the Lemma X coefficient holds even at a centre that admits delayed departures. For outward radial forces (D2) decides uniqueness from one moving orbit. The sign-changing counterexample (D4) delimits that inference. The cusp preparation theorem uses an actual full-state merger and additional preparation laws. The separate endpoint blocking argument retains inverse Hessians, normalization and source response; the nonlinear cell controls a supplied positive weight. Neither operation follows solely from constructing a classical orbit. The moving-circle example now distinguishes uniform agreement over trial actions from agreement at each fixed positive action. A nonnegative spherical source admits order-independent classical refinement, including at a circular orbit. Classical ensembles of positive lower width also admit uniform refinement. Even the two branch probabilities at the transition scale agree for matched preparations. In the scaled radial variables the momentum unit is \(mR\omega\,d\log^2(1/d)\). Its product with \(Rd\) exceeds the Gaussian action \(a\) by a factor \(\log^2(1/d)\). Whether the central-force argument imposes any independent relation between these two resolutions remains open.
