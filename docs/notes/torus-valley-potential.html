One-loop torus valleys: local confinement, periodicity and spectral limits

Markdown source · PDF

For a constant abelian \(SU(2)\) background on \(T^3_L\), the one-loop zero-point energy defined by discarding the formal \(m=0\) Fourier term in the Poisson-summed expression (distinct from the physical \(k=0\) oscillator) is \[U(a)=\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\Phi(La),\qquad \Phi(b)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}} \frac{1-\cos(m\cdot b)}{|m|^4}.\] Here \([a]=L^{-1}\). This nonnegative bounded periodic function has the local expansion \[U(a)=2\hbar c|a|+\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\hbar cL|a|^2 +O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3).\] The first term is the four transverse zero-mode oscillators’ energy; the quadratic correction is useful input to a small-volume comparison. The formula is exact for this specified one-loop prescription, not for the interacting ground-state energy. A local Taylor expansion supplies neither the global operator bound nor the nonzero-mode estimates in the Feshbach reduction.

The derivation below retains the frequency count, Fourier normalization and symmetry argument. Weiss (Phys. Rev. D 24 (1981) 475, metadata) and Lüscher (1983, abstract) are historical source leads, not independent checks of this calculation. No novelty or full field-theory gap is claimed.

1. Frequencies in a constant abelian background

Let \(a_i=\alpha_iT^3\), \(i=1,2,3\), with \(\vec\alpha\in\mathbb R^3\), and write \(a\) for \(\vec\alpha\) when no confusion arises. Decompose the adjoint fluctuation \(\tilde A_i=\tilde A_i^3T^3+\tilde A_i^+T^++\tilde A_i^-T^-\). The covariant derivative \(D_j=\partial_j+[a_j,\cdot]\) acts on the neutral component as \(\partial_j\) and on the charged components as \(\partial_j\pm i\alpha_j\), so on the Fourier mode \(e^{ik\cdot x}\), \(k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3\), the charged components have covariant momentum \(k\pm a\). The quadratic form \(\frac1{2g^2}\int|D_i\tilde A_j-D_j\tilde A_i|^2\) is, for a constant abelian background, the free transverse form with \(k\) replaced by \(k\pm a\) on the charged components, so after the usual transverse gauge choice the frequencies are \[\hbar\omega^{(0)}_k=\hbar c|k|\quad(\text{neutral, two polarizations}),\] \[\hbar\omega^{(\pm)}_k=\hbar c|k\pm a|\quad(\text{charged, two polarizations each}),\] away from shifted zero frequencies. At \(k=0\) and \(a\ne0\), the background-covariant transverse condition \(a\cdot\tilde A^{\pm}=0\) leaves two charged spatial polarizations; the neutral components are the valley directions themselves: with \(a=\alpha_1T^3\) along \(x_1\) only, these are the charged parts of \(\tilde A_2,\tilde A_3\), four real oscillators of angular frequency \(c|a|\), while the charged parts of \(\tilde A_1\) are gauge directions removed by Gauss’s law. Enhanced zero modes at \(a=0\) or shifted momenta require their own treatment; this local oscillator count is not a uniform projection across them.

The \(k=0\) term is C133. The SU(2) matrix model of C133 with \(\vec x_1=\lambda\hat n\), \(\vec x_2=\vec x_3=0\) has potential \(\tfrac12g^2(|\vec x_1\times\vec x_2|^2+|\vec x_1\times\vec x_3|^2+\cdots)\), whose transverse Hessian at that point gives the components of \(\vec x_2,\vec x_3\) orthogonal to \(\hat n\), four oscillators of frequency \(g|\lambda|/\sqrt m\); in the field-theory variables of G07 Proposition 7 (\(m=\hbar L^3/(g^2c)\), \(g_B^2=\hbar cL^3/g^2\)) this frequency is \(c|a|\). The zero-point energy \(4\cdot\tfrac12\hbar c|a|=2\hbar c|a|\) is the confining term of G07 Theorem 6(1) and the floor of G08 Theorem 2 along the valley.

2. Poisson summation

The zero-point energy of the fluctuations relative to \(a=0\), summing two polarizations and both charges, is \[U(a)=\frac{\hbar c}2\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}\sum_{\rm pol=1,2}\big(|k+a|+|k-a|-2|k|\big) =\hbar c\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}\big(|k+a|+|k-a|-2|k|\big),\] where the \(k=0\) term is \(2\hbar c|a|\) and the neutral modes cancel. The raw summands are nonnegative and of order \(|a|^2/|k|\); their unregularized sum diverges. Periodicity of a regulated sum alone does not justify exchanging this sum and subtraction. We use the specific Fourier finite-part prescription that discards the \(m=0\) term below. It restores periodicity by momentum reindexing and fixes the displayed one-loop potential; a different prescription needs its own matching. For \(f(k)=|k|\) the distributional Fourier transform is \(\hat f(x)=\int|k|e^{-ik\cdot x}d^3k=-8\pi/|x|^4\) for \(x\ne0\) (the formula \(\widehat{|k|^\alpha}=2^{\alpha+3}\pi^{3/2}\frac{\Gamma((3+\alpha)/2)}{\Gamma(-\alpha/2)}|x|^{-3-\alpha}\) at \(\alpha=1\), with \(\Gamma(-\tfrac12)=-2\sqrt\pi\)), and \[\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}f(k+a)=\Big(\frac L{2\pi}\Big)^3\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^3}\hat f(mL)\,e^{im\cdot La}\] with the \(m=0\) symbol a prescription-dependent subtraction, not a separately evaluable distributional Fourier value. Hence \[U(a)=\hbar c\Big(\frac L{2\pi}\Big)^3\sum_{m\ne0}\Big(-\frac{8\pi}{L^4|m|^4}\Big)\big(e^{im\cdot La}+e^{-im\cdot La}-2\big) =\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\sum_{m\ne0}\frac{1-\cos(m\cdot La)}{|m|^4}=\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\Phi(La).\] Matching a lattice dispersion to this continuum one-loop expression requires a separate cutoff estimate. No uniform relative \(O(a_{\rm lat}^2/L^2)\) error follows merely from the nonzero Fourier indices.

Properties of \(\Phi\): it is nonnegative, periodic with period \(2\pi\) in each component, invariant under the cubic group, bounded by \(2\sum'|m|^{-4}\), and zero exactly on \(2\pi\mathbb Z^3\).

3. Expansion at the origin and the sign of the correction

\(\Phi\) is not smooth at \(0\). Its Laplacian is the periodic Green’s function \(G(b)=\sum'_me^{im\cdot b}|m|^{-2}\), which has the Coulomb singularity \(2\pi^2/|b|\) at the origin (from \(\int d^3m\,e^{im\cdot b}|m|^{-2}\)) and a finite remainder: \(G(b)=2\pi^2/|b|+C_M+O(|b|^2)\), where \(C_M\) is the constant term of the periodic Coulomb Green’s function of the cubic lattice, the analytically continued Epstein sum \(\sum'_m|m|^{-2}\). Since \(\Delta(\pi^2|b|)=2\pi^2/|b|\), the function \(\Phi-\pi^2|b|\) has Laplacian \(C_M+O(|b|^2)\) near \(0\), and the only quadratic form with cubic symmetry and Laplacian \(C_M\) is \(\tfrac16C_M|b|^2\); so \[\Phi(b)=\pi^2|b|+\tfrac16C_M|b|^2+O(|b|^3),\qquad U(a)=2\hbar c|a|+\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\,\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3).\] The linear term \(2\hbar c|a|\) is the \(k=0\) contribution of Section 1, so the nonzero modes contribute \[U_\perp(a)=U(a)-2\hbar c|a|=\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3),\] with \(C_M<0\) for the cubic lattice (lattice-sum tables; the sign is not re-derived here and affects only the direction of a subleading correction). In the rescaled variables \(a=g^{2/3}\xi/L\) this is \(\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}g^{4/3}|\xi|^2\,\hbar c/L\), of relative order \(g^{2/3}\) to the zero-mode Hamiltonian \(g^{2/3}h_3\,\hbar c/L\): the size that (H3) of the formal local power counting suggests for the Feshbach reduction. The coefficient is identified for this one-loop prescription, not for the full compressed-operator gap hypothesis (H3).

3a. General constant backgrounds at quadratic order

The following extension is conditional: suppose a specified gauge-fixed nonzero-mode determinant, including its longitudinal and ghost contributions, defines a smooth even gauge-invariant one-loop function near the trivial background and is matched to the same Fourier prescription as the abelian calculation. An unfixed Hessian at a general noncommuting off-shell background does not automatically have these properties. Under that assumption its quadratic term is constrained by symmetry. It is invariant under the constant gauge transformations, which rotate the three colour vectors \(\vec a_i\) simultaneously, under the cubic group acting on the spatial index, and under \(a\to-a\). The quadratic invariants of \((\vec a_1,\vec a_2,\vec a_3)\) under \(SO(3)_{\rm colour}\) are the bilinears \(\vec a_i\cdot\vec a_j\), and the only cubic-invariant symmetric form in the spatial indices is \(\delta_{ij}\), so \[U_\perp(a)=c\,\hbar cL\sum_{i=1}^3|\vec a_i|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3),\] with a single pure number \(c\), which the abelian valley (\(\vec a_i=\alpha_i\hat e_3\), \(\sum_i|\vec a_i|^2=|\alpha|^2\)) determines: \(c=C_M/(3\pi^2)\). The Landau-level structure of non-commuting backgrounds cannot change this quadratic invariant within the same smooth one-loop prescription. The remainder is at least cubic in this local estimate. This identifies a candidate coefficient for (H3); symmetry does not establish a cutoff-uniform expansion, a global relative bound or the gap of the compressed operator.

3b. The abelian theory in the same expansion

For compact \(U(1)\) the constant modes commute, the quartic potential vanishes, and the photons are neutral, so the one-loop potential of the holonomy is identically zero: the constant-mode Hamiltonian is the free Laplacian on the torus of holonomies, \(H_0=\frac{e^2c}{2\hbar L^3}|p|^2\) with \(a\) periodic of period \(2\pi/L\), spectrum \(e^2\hbar c|n|^2/(2L)\), \(n\in\mathbb Z^3\), gap \(e^2\hbar c/(2L)\). The abelian small-volume gap is linear in \(e^2\), not \(e^{2/3}\), because there is no commutator to confine the holonomy and no charged mode to give it a potential; with \(e\) fixed by the absence of running, the \(1/L\) decay is uncompensated and the infinite-volume theory is gapless, in agreement with the Coulomb phase of Guth and Fröhlich–Spencer. In the nonabelian matrix model, the \(g^{2/3}\) scale comes from the kinetic/quartic commutator balance. Running of the field-theory coupling requires a general-background renormalization and matching calculation; it cannot be read from the commuting-valley function \(U\) alone.

4. Saturation and central holonomies

The full one-loop potential satisfies \(0\le U\le2\hbar c\max\Phi/(\pi^2L)=O(\hbar c/L)\), and its minima are at \(La\in2\pi\mathbb Z^3\). For \(SU(2)\) with adjoint charges \(\pm1\), \(La=2\pi e_i\) gives fundamental holonomy \(\exp(2\pi iT^3)=-1\). With strictly periodic gauge transformations these are distinct central holonomy configurations. Strictly periodic \(SU(2)\) gauge transformations identify covering shifts by \(4\pi/L\) (and Weyl reflection), so the infinitely many covering minima represent \(2^3\) central holonomy triples. If transformations periodic only up to the centre are instead gauged, the identification and allowed sectors change. A configuration is not itself a quantum state.

When centre transformations are global symmetries, their characters on three torus cycles label electric-flux sectors: \(\mathbb Z_N^3\) gives \(8\) labels for \(SU(2)\) and \(27\) for \(SU(3)\). The latter has three central holonomies per direction. These labels and the one-loop barrier scale \(O(\hbar c/L)\) determine neither spectral splittings nor spectral weights. Tunnelling and electric-flux interpretations require the corresponding Hamiltonian, boundary conventions and estimates; they are not derived by counting minima.

In the isolated matrix model, balancing kinetic and quartic terms gives \(La\sim g^{2/3}\) and the energy scale \(g^{2/3}\hbar c/L\). Its linear valley term is the local limit of \(U\), whereas \(U\) itself saturates and is periodic. Crucially, \(U_\perp=U-2\hbar c|a|\) is not bounded or periodic on the covering space: the subtracted linear term grows. The boundedness of \(U\) therefore cannot establish a global perturbation bound for \(U_\perp\) relative to the confining matrix operator. Linear confinement may control \(|a|\) and allow some form bound; the small relative bound needed for a gap comparison has not been supplied. Nor does a local quadratic estimate control arbitrarily large \(|\xi|\) after \(a=g^{2/3}\xi/L\).

Localization near one minimum and an expansion of the full field-theory gap require the Feshbach hypotheses and a global treatment of the holonomy domain. The informal comparison \(g(L)^{2/3}\sim1\) marks the loss of a small parameter; it proves no delocalization, sector coalescence or crossover at a specified value of \(z=\Delta L/(\hbar c)\).

5. Consequence for STATE

The explicit one-loop input is a periodic bounded \(U\) with central minima and a local correction \(U_\perp=(C_M/(3\pi^2))\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3)\). The symmetry argument identifies its quadratic form under the stated smooth-prescription assumption. Global (H3), nonzero-mode comparison, \(SU(3)\) matrix control, cutoff removal and physical spectral transport remain separate. The abelian zero-mode benchmark has no such one-loop valley potential; that contrast alone proves no nonabelian continuum gap.
