Time blocking, Newton polygons and explicit quantum action

Markdown source · PDF

A bounded inward central force admits a unique classical passage through the centre at nonzero speed. At a zero-speed encounter with a bounded repulsive force, two continuations of the same incoming orbit obey the same force balance after every time insertion and have equal action on every cell. A detector at a fixed time distinguishes them. Two shrinking source profiles select the opposite returns, yet the fraction of incoming classical flux that reaches either core tends to zero as an exact power of its radius. Central-force passage.

For repulsive inverse-square scattering, three incident energies make a different test possible. A sum of measured angular yields must vanish for every common classical distribution of impact parameters, including a sharply concentrated beam. An observed excess over the calibration error gives a lower bound on a trial action in the spin-zero Coulomb exchange model. Three-energy test.

At the reported preparation’s nominal widths, at least ten percentage points of the incident flux need another explanation if the beam presents the same axial potential to every atom. A second image would locate the returning atoms. The same bound applies to outward motion through a spherical shell. Two barrier powers.

The time lattice has one dimension. Sections 1–6 place one mechanical coordinate at each time. The central-force calculation places a particle in three-dimensional space at each time. The force polygons are modern constructions. The Galileo observable remains the displacement between the inertial line and the falling parabola.

We assemble standard Green-function, contraction and Gaussian-elimination arguments. No literature novelty is claimed. The source ingredient is Balaban’s controlled effective density under blocking, described in his ICM overview, printed pp. 1260–1262 (text read), and Dimock’s small-field exposition, sections 2.1, 2.4, 4.5 and Theorem 14 (selected passages read). 1. Actions and the constant to be investigated

Let \(m,T>0\), \(x,y\in\mathbb R\), and \(\pi=(0=t_0<\cdots<t_N=T)\). Write \(\tau_j=t_{j+1}-t_j\) and \(w_j=(\tau_{j-1}+\tau_j)/2\) for interior vertices. For a potential \(V\) define

\[ S_\pi^\sigma(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \frac{m(q_{j+1}-q_j)^2}{2\tau_j} +\sigma\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{\tau_j}{2} [V(q_j)+V(q_{j+1})],\qquad q_0=x,\ q_N=y. \tag{1} \]

The real-time Newton action is \(S_\pi^R=S_\pi^{-1}\), with weight \(e^{iS_\pi^R/\hbar}\) when \(\hbar>0\). The Euclidean action is \(S_\pi^E=S_\pi^{+1}\), with weight \(e^{-S_\pi^E/\hbar}\). Their stationary equations have opposite potential signs. They must not be interchanged when interpreting a background as a Newton trajectory. At \(\hbar=0\) these exponential expressions are not defined; the classical stationary equation is defined directly. No relativistic premise or \(c\) is used in this calculation. Ordinary Planck action is \(h_P=2\pi\hbar\).

For real time put \(f=-V'/m\), the acceleration. Section 2 needs only a Lipschitz function \(f\), even without first specifying \(V\). A Lipschitz condition is a sufficient uniqueness and stability condition; continuity alone can give local existence. Neither statement alone guarantees continuation for all times or uniqueness of a two-endpoint problem.

2. Arbitrary intermediate times

Let \(\ell(t)=x+(y-x)t/T\) and

\[ G(t,s)=\min(t,s)-\frac{ts}{T}. \tag{2} \]

This is the positive Dirichlet Green function of \(-d^2/dt^2\). Define the polygon map, with piecewise-linear output, by

\[ (\mathcal T_\pi q)(t)=\ell(t) -\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}w_jG(t,t_j)f(q(t_j)). \tag{3} \]

Lemma 1 (the exact row sum). At every vertex,

\[ \sum_{j=1}^{N-1}w_jG(t_i,t_j)=\frac{t_i(T-t_i)}2. \tag{4} \]

At every \(t\in[0,T]\) the sum is at most \(T^2/8\).

Proof. For fixed \(t_i\), the function \(s\mapsto G(t_i,s)\) is linear on each partition interval and vanishes at the endpoints. Its trapezoidal integral is therefore exact, and its integral is \(t_i(T-t_i)/2\). As a function of \(t\), the sum in (4) is the linear interpolant of that concave quadratic. It lies below the quadratic, whose maximum is \(T^2/8\). This proof uses no ratio between consecutive time steps. \(\square\)

Assume \(|f(u)-f(v)|\le\Lambda|u-v|\) for all \(u,v\), and put

\[\theta=\frac{\Lambda T^2}{8}<1.\tag{5}\]

Proposition 2 (one bound for every polygon). The map (3) has a unique fixed polygon. Its slopes \(v_j=(q_{j+1}-q_j)/\tau_j\) satisfy

\[v_j-v_{j-1}=w_jf(q_j).\tag{6}\]

The continuous map

\[ (\mathcal Tq)(t)=\ell(t)-\int_0^T G(t,s)f(q(s))\,ds \tag{7} \]

has a unique fixed point, which is \(C^2\) and solves \(\ddot q=f(q)\) with the two prescribed endpoints. Every sequence of partitions with \(|\pi|\to0\) converges to this solution. Neither nesting nor equal intervals is required.

Proof. Lemma 1 bounds the Lipschitz constants of (3) and (7) in the supremum norm by \(\theta\). Repeated application converges because the successive differences form a geometric series. This proves existence and uniqueness in the complete polygon space or in \(C([0,T])\). The derivative of \(G(\cdot,t_j)\) jumps by \(-1\) at \(t_j\), giving (6). Differentiating (7) gives the continuous equation.

Inserting a vertex changes its neighbours’ weights \(w_j\) as well as the new solution. It does not add one kick while freezing the old force history. Retained vertices need not remain fixed when the trapezoidal action itself is refined.

For a quantitative limit, write \(B=\|f(q)\|_\infty\) and \(U=\|\dot q\|_\infty\) for the continuous solution. For every fixed \(t\), \(s\mapsto G(t,s)f(q(s))\) is Lipschitz with constant at most \(B+T\Lambda U/4\). On an interval of length \(\tau\), replacing its integral by the endpoint trapezoid has error at most half that constant times \(\tau^2\). Hence

\[ \|q_\pi-q\|_\infty\le \frac{T|\pi|}{2(1-\theta)} \left(B+\frac{T\Lambda U}{4}\right). \tag{8} \]

Indeed, subtract the two fixed-point equations and absorb the \(\theta\|q_\pi-q\|_\infty\) term. The constants in (8) belong to the fixed solution and interval, not to the partition. \(\square\)

For a force Lipschitz only in a tube \(|q(t)-\ell(t)|\le r\), the same proof holds within that tube if \(\theta<1\) and

\[ \frac{T^2}{8}\big(B_0+\Lambda r\big)\le r, \qquad B_0=\sup_t|f(\ell(t))|. \tag{9} \]

This makes the closed tube invariant. Uniqueness is then asserted only inside it. Equation (9) is a finite version of a small-field domain that survives the construction; it cannot justify discarding paths outside it from a statistical or oscillatory integral.

3. The action Hessian and elimination

Let \(\mathsf K_\pi\) be the Dirichlet stiffness matrix, with diagonal \(1/\tau_{j-1}+1/\tau_j\) and adjacent entries \(-1/\tau_j\), and let \(\mathsf W_\pi=\operatorname{diag}(w_j)\). The tent function (2) gives

\[ (\mathsf K_\pi^{-1})_{ij}=G(t_i,t_j),\qquad \|\mathsf K_\pi^{-1}\mathsf W_\pi\|_{\infty\to\infty} \le\frac{T^2}{8}. \tag{10} \]

The first identity follows by taking the slope jump of that tent at each node; the second is Lemma 1. In particular, for \(V\in C^2\) with \(|V''|\le m\Lambda\), the Hessian of (1) obeys

\[ \mathsf H_\pi^\sigma =m\mathsf K_\pi+\sigma\mathsf W_\pi \operatorname{diag}(V''(q_j)) \ \ge\ m(1-\theta)\mathsf K_\pi. \tag{11} \]

To see the quadratic-form inequality, the nonnegative matrix \(\mathsf K_\pi^{-1}\mathsf W_\pi\) has spectral radius at most its maximum row sum. It is similar to the positive symmetric matrix \(\mathsf W_\pi^{1/2}\mathsf K_\pi^{-1}\mathsf W_\pi^{1/2}\). Thus \(\mathsf W_\pi\le(T^2/8)\mathsf K_\pi\), which proves (11). The factor \(1-\theta\) is uniform over arbitrary partitions.

For a physical force source \(J_j\), inserted as a linear action term \(\sum_jw_jJ_jq_j\), the stationary response therefore has the bound

\[ \|\delta q\|_\infty\le \frac{T^2}{8m(1-\theta)}\|\delta J\|_\infty. \tag{12} \]

Here the bound is for the differential response. It follows directly by factoring \(\mathsf H_\pi^\sigma\) as \(m\mathsf K_\pi(I+\mathsf K_\pi^{-1}\mathsf W_\pi \sigma\operatorname{diag}(V'')/m)\) and using a Neumann series.

With the global curvature bound, (11) makes the finite action coercive and strictly convex in its interior vertices. Eliminating some vertices by minimization is then unambiguous, and its Hessian is the Schur complement

\[ \mathsf H_{BB}-\mathsf H_{BA}\mathsf H_{AA}^{-1}\mathsf H_{AB}. \tag{13} \]

Successive eliminations associate. With only a local tube, this is a statement on the chosen nondegenerate branch. In particular, for the real-time positive quartic potential the unrestricted action is unbounded below, as the Rivero note already records; local control is not a global minimization theorem. Nor does the eliminated action generally retain only the original kinetic and potential terms.

4. What the same inverse says when \(\hbar\) is present

At a Euclidean background, its Gaussian fluctuation reference has density proportional to \(\exp[-\eta^T\mathsf H_\pi^E\eta/(2\hbar)]\) and covariance

\[ \mathsf C_{\pi,\hbar}^E=\hbar(\mathsf H_\pi^E)^{-1},\qquad (\mathsf C_{\pi,\hbar}^E)_{jj} \le\frac{\hbar}{m(1-\theta)}\frac{t_j(T-t_j)}{T} \le\frac{\hbar T}{4m(1-\theta)}. \tag{14} \]

These are exact Gaussian statements. For a nonlinear action they do not assert the covariance bound for its full measure without a further argument. In real time, normalized quadratic Fresnel integration gives the algebraic centered second moment \(i\hbar(\mathsf H_\pi^R)^{-1}\), which is not a probability covariance.

For one block of Gaussian variables, integration retains both the Schur complement (13) and a determinant factor: respectively \((2\pi\hbar)^{|A|/2}(\det\mathsf H_{AA}^E)^{-1/2}\) in Euclidean time and \((2\pi i\hbar)^{|A|/2}(\det\mathsf H_{AA}^R)^{-1/2}\) when the real Hessian is positive. Determinants, normalization and any higher interactions must accompany the stationary action. These are the extra data that Balaban’s effective-density viewpoint instructs us to retain.

Equations (11)–(14) identify the connection to ODE control: the curvature constant that makes the background construction stable also controls the Gaussian fluctuations for every positive supplied \(\hbar\). They do not create the multiplier \(\hbar\) in (14).

5. Written controls: Galileo and the oscillator

Constant force. For \(V(q)=-Fq\), \(\Lambda=0\). Equation (7) is exactly

\[q(t)=\ell(t)+\frac{F}{2m}t(t-T).\tag{15}\]

By (4), every polygon has these same vertex positions. The fluctuation Hessian is the free one for either action sign, and the Euclidean covariance is \((\hbar/m)G(t_i,t_j)\). For a single inserted midpoint of a cell of length \(T\), its variance is \(\hbar T/(4m)\); for an unequal cut at \(u\) it is \(\hbar u(T-u)/(mT)\). The deterministic vertex survives at \(\hbar=0\); the Gaussian width tends to zero. This uses precisely the Galileo comparison already normalized in the refinement note.

One arbitrary cut (Terra’s connection). Split \(T=u+v\) and let \(H_u\) be the piecewise-linear hat of height one at \(u\), zero at the cell endpoints. Then

\[ \int_0^T(H_u')^2dt=\frac{T}{uv},\qquad a_F=-\frac{Fuv}{2m},\qquad \frac m2\int_0^T(a_FH_u')^2dt =\frac{F^2uvT}{8m}=K_T-K_u-K_v. \tag{15a} \]

where \(K_s=F^2s^3/(24m)\). The free bridge’s conditional inserted-vertex innovation has variance \(s_u^2=\hbar uv/(mT)\). For the two equal-variance Gaussian vertex laws, with means separated by \(a_F\), direct integration of their log-density ratio gives

\[ D\big(N(a_F,s_u^2)\,\|\,N(0,s_u^2)\big) =\frac{a_F^2}{2s_u^2} =\frac{K_T-K_u-K_v}{\hbar}. \tag{15b} \]

Here \(D\) is relative entropy in nats. Successive bridge innovations factor into independent Gaussian hat coefficients: conditioned on a cell’s endpoints, its inserted value has the variance above, and the two residual bridges are independent. The hats are also orthogonal in the Dirichlet energy because each finer hat has zero derivative integral inside a cell where an older hat has constant derivative.

Thus classical vertex elimination, conditional Gaussian normalization and the cut energy are related by one Schur factorization. The cut-measure note already proves the additive action budget; the useful connection here is to the blocking variables. Equation (15b) assumes these Gaussian laws. It does not establish a physical bound on the information in complete apparatus records.

Oscillator and caustics. Let \(V=m\omega^2q^2/2\), \(\omega>0\). Its initial-value equation has the unique explicit solution \(q(t)=q(0)\cos\omega t+\dot q(0)\sin\omega t/\omega\) for all times. Nevertheless its two-endpoint problem loses uniqueness at \(T=\pi/\omega\) when \(y=-x\); for other \(y\) it has no solution at that time. A singular boundary Hessian therefore need not mean a failure of initial-value mechanics.

On a uniform \(N\)-cell partition, \(\tau=T/N\), \(N\ge2\), the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the difference operator is \(4\tau^{-2}\sin^2(\pi/(2N))\). Its Hessian first becomes singular at

\[ T_N=\frac{2N}{\omega}\sin\frac{\pi}{2N} \longrightarrow\frac\pi\omega. \tag{16} \]

This checks the partition-independent sufficient window \(\omega^2T^2<8\): \(T_2=\sqrt8/\omega\), while the continuum caustic is later. An ODE Lipschitz constant cannot exclude those later caustics.

For an exact Gaussian control, suppose \(T<\sqrt8/\omega\) and set \(\alpha=2\arcsin(\omega\tau/2)\). The determinant ratio is

\[ R_N=\frac{\det\mathsf H_\pi^R}{\det(m\mathsf K_\pi)} =\frac{\sin(N\alpha)}{N\sin\alpha} \longrightarrow\frac{\sin(\omega T)}{\omega T}. \tag{17} \]

Indeed the determinant of the tridiagonal matrix with diagonal \(2-\omega^2\tau^2=2\cos\alpha\) and off-diagonal \(-1\) satisfies \(D_n=2\cos\alpha D_{n-1}-D_{n-2}\), with \(D_0=1,D_1=2\cos\alpha\). Thus \(D_{N-1}=\sin(N\alpha)/\sin\alpha\); the free determinant is \(N\). For \(N=2\), (17) gives \(R_2=1-\omega^2\tau^2/2\), also obtained by completing the single midpoint square.

With the usual free normalization for each cell, finite Gaussian integration gives the real-time kernel

\[ K^\pi_\hbar(y,x)= \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar T}}\,R_N^{-1/2} e^{iS_\pi^R(q_\pi)/\hbar}. \tag{18} \]

The prefactor follows from \(\det\mathsf K_\pi=T/\prod_j\tau_j\) and completing the quadratic form. The determinant identity follows by the same tridiagonal recurrence, or by successive free one-vertex eliminations. Equation (18) retains the same \(\hbar\) at every depth. Reversing the sign of the oscillator term would put \(1+\omega^2\tau^2/2\) in the two-cell determinant and remove the real-time conjugate point. This sign test distinguishes the Euclidean and Newton constructions.

6. Large fields, continuation and the next scale question

A local Lipschitz bound controls a local construction. To continue an initial-value solution, one also needs control preventing escape from every bounded phase-space region. In the confining example \(V(q)=\lambda q^4/4\), \(\lambda>0\), conservation of a fixed classical energy \(E>0\) gives

\[ |q|\le R_E=(4E/\lambda)^{1/4},\qquad |p|\le\sqrt{2mE},\qquad |f'|\le\frac{3\lambda R_E^2}{m}. \tag{19} \]

These bounds permit continuation of the initial-value solution. Applying the local polygon lemma to a whole tube may require a larger radius than \(R_E\) and the invariance condition (9). For the inverted quartic, \(q(t)=q_0/[1-q_0\sqrt{\lambda/(2m)}t]\), \(q_0>0\), is instead a finite-time escape solution. Its force is locally Lipschitz everywhere. This is an explicit reason to distinguish local control from continuation. Balaban’s large-field estimates address weighted integrals over all field regions; that is a statistical obligation distinct from trajectory continuation.

Keeping \(\hbar\) explicit in the positive quartic Euclidean action, set \(t=Ts\) and \(q=\sqrt{\hbar T/m}\,z\). Then

\[ \frac{S^E}{\hbar} =\int_0^1\left[\frac{(z')^2}{2}+\frac g4z^4\right]ds, \qquad g=\frac{\lambda\hbar T^3}{m^2}. \tag{20} \]

For the zero-endpoint free reference, the Brownian-bridge reflection bound is

\[ \mathbb P\!\left(\sup|q|>b\sqrt{\hbar T/m}\right) \le2e^{-2b^2},\qquad b>0. \tag{21} \]

It follows by applying the positive and negative one-sided reflection bounds and a union bound; the Rivero note, section 3, supplies the same calculation. On this small-field tube the real quartic curvature gives \(\theta\le3b^2g/8\). Thus the natural perturbation parameter combines force curvature, the observation interval and the explicitly supplied quantum width. For the interacting normalized measure, its denominator and its own large-field contribution still require control; (21) alone is not that result.

The next useful test is to retain the normalized fluctuation and large-field terms through repeated time insertion, and determine which physical premise would fix their action multiplier independently of the weight \(e^{-S^E/\hbar}\) or \(e^{iS^R/\hbar}\). The existing quartic zero-limit theorem prevents treating refinement consistency alone as that premise. A loss of boundary invertibility also needs the oscillator countertest above before it is used to argue for completion of Newton’s dynamics.

A complementary construction suggested by Terra is to control the initial-value polygon flow and its tangent map under arbitrary insertion. Their composition is causal and remains meaningful through the oscillator’s boundary caustics. Comparing that description with the endpoint action would separate loss of one coordinate description from an actual need for additional physical dynamics.

Central-force passage under point insertion

In the 1713 Principia, Book I, Proposition I, Corollary 4 compares forces by the inward sagittas of arcs traversed in equal times as the arcs shrink. In Proposition VI Newton explicitly points to the new Corollary 4 of Lemma X as another proof of the orbital force rule. Does this local force information determine where the body emerges from a small neighbourhood of the centre? Use one spatial position at a fixed time as the readout. 1687, Book I, Lemma X and Proposition I and 1713, Book I, Lemma X and Propositions I and VI (diplomatic transcriptions, passages).

Finite inward force. Take \(m>0\) and \(F(q)=-f(r)q/r\), \(r=|q|\), with \(f\ge0\), continuous force at zero, locally Lipschitz away from zero and \(|F|\le F_*\). The potential \(V\) has its minimum at the centre. Nonzero angular momentum prevents arrival there at finite energy. A radial arrival from a point with \(E>V(0)\) crosses with the nonzero velocity fixed by its incoming direction and energy. The radial travel integral \(\int dr/\sqrt{2(E-V(r))/m}\) fixes its clock on either side. At the minimum energy a body at rest at the centre stays there by energy conservation. These statements give uniqueness through the centre without bounding \(f'\). With a globally bounded force, compactness and uniqueness then give convergence of the usual kick–drift polygons on each finite time interval, including for changing positive time partitions. Classical polygon construction and compactness argument (passage).

The impulse estimate is uniform over these force profiles. Suppose a ray enters a ball of radius \(b\) at speed at least \(v_0>0\) and \(bF_*/m\le v_0^2/2\). Attraction gives \(|\dot q|\ge v_0\) inside. Hence \((r^2)''=2|\dot q|^2+2q\cdot F/m\ge v_0^2\). Integrating on each side of closest approach bounds the time inside by \(2\sqrt2\,b/v_0\) and the change in momentum by \(2\sqrt2\,F_*b/v_0\). In particular no shrinking ball with this fixed force bound can reverse a finite incoming momentum. This is a classical restriction on trajectories.

A bounded central force at a turning point. Fix a ruler distance \(R\), inertial mass \(m\) and \(A>0\) with units energy/length\(^{3/2}\). In three spatial dimensions take \(V_0(r)=-Ar^{3/2}\) up to \(2R\). The force points outward and tends continuously to zero at the origin. For \(r>2R\) continue its magnitude by the inverse square law matched at \(2R\). For each \(0<b<R\), keep this exterior potential for \(r\ge b\) and use \(V_{b,\pm}(r)=-A b^{3/2}v_\pm(r/b)\) inside, with

\[ v_+(u)=\tfrac14+\tfrac34u^2,\qquad v_-(u)=1-\tfrac32(1-u)+\tfrac38(1-u)^4,\qquad 0\le u\le1. \]

Both profiles have \(v(1)=1\) and \(v'(1)=3/2\). Both derivatives increase from zero, with \(v_-'(u)=\tfrac32[1-(1-u)^3]\). Thus every core has a finite, outward, radially nondecreasing force and a Lipschitz gradient of the potential. A spherical distribution of like charges can source each profile because its enclosed charge is proportional to the nondecreasing function \(r^2|F(r)|\). The maximum force difference is at most \(3A\sqrt b/2\) and the maximum potential difference is \(3A b^{3/2}/8\). Each force converges uniformly to \(F_0\), since all three magnitudes inside \(b\) are at most \(3A\sqrt b/2\) and they agree outside. At the centre \(v_+(0)=1/4\) and \(v_-(0)=-1/8\). A diameter orbit of energy zero passes the first centre and turns before the second. Here zero energy is referred to \(V_0(0)\) in both runs. The incoming state at \(q=R e_1\) has the same inward momentum \(p_R=\sqrt{2mA}R^{3/4}\) for every \(b\).

Write \(p_b=\sqrt{2mA}b^{3/4}\), \(t_b=mb/p_b\) and \(J_b=bp_b\). Let \(u_+=0\) and let \(u_-\) be the simple zero of \(v_-\). The time and ordinary mechanical action between entering the core and leaving it are respectively

\[ 2t_b\int_{u_\pm}^1\frac{du}{\sqrt{v_\pm(u)}}, \qquad 2J_b\int_{u_\pm}^1\sqrt{v_\pm(u)}\,du . \]

At \(E=0\), \(\int p\cdot dq\) equals the time action. All four integrals are finite. Inserting intermediate points on the trajectory adds the corresponding integrals exactly. Halving \(b\) scales the time by \(2^{-1/4}\) and the action by \(2^{-7/4}\) in either core. The exterior inward travel time to \(b\) is \(4(t_R-t_b)\), where \(t_R=mR/p_R\). Thus at the same clock time \(T=8t_R\) the two positions tend to \(-Re_1\) and \(+Re_1\). The trajectories are unique and reversible for each fixed \(b\).

Point insertion with the source held fixed. Now use the single potential \(V_0\) throughout. Put \(t_*=4t_R\), so \(T=2t_*\). Through the common arrival the signed diameter coordinate is \(x(t)=R(1-t/t_*)^4\). After arrival the two continuations are \(x_\pm(t)=\pm R(t/t_*-1)^4\), with \(p=m\dot x\). Both have zero velocity and acceleration at the join. From rest there, the signed force estimate \(2m x_\pm(t_*+\delta)/\delta^2\) tends to zero on both branches. Newton’s short-time measurement returns the correct force even where it cannot determine the later side. For any outgoing time cell their actions coincide since \(m\dot x^2/2-V_0(x)\) is the same for the two signs. On the first duration \(\delta\) after arrival it is the integral of \(m\dot x^2\), namely \(16mR^2\delta^7/(7t_*^8)\). The finite-force cusp, its waiting solutions and reversible classical dynamics on entire histories are established in the arrival argument (passage).

One can test this on time nodes using only the given force law. On a cell of duration \(\tau\), interpolate \(F_0\) at its initial, midpoint and final positions by a quadratic \(B(u)\) in elapsed time. At \(s=\tau/2,\tau\) impose the impulse and displacement balances

\[ p(s)=p(0)+\int_0^s B(u)\,du,\qquad x(s)=x(0)+s p(0)/m+\frac1m\int_0^s(s-u)B(u)\,du . \]

Start with a partition containing \(t_*\). On each branch the force in time is quadratic on each side of the join, so its interpolant is exact. Both sets of nodes solve the same implicit cell equations on every subdivision, however fine. Selecting a root would supply a branch rule. Grant a detector independent position and clock error bounds \(\delta_x\le R/4\) and \(\delta_t\le t_R/4\), held fixed as the mesh and \(b\) shrink. Throughout that clock window the limiting distance is at least \(R(15/16)^4>R/2\). The explicit exterior travel time gives uniform convergence there for the two finite-core returns as well. Recording the side therefore separates them at these fixed resolutions. Lagrange interpolation, DLMF §3.3(i) (passage).

What the incoming flux sees. At the incoming sphere \(r=R\) on the same energy shell, write \(p_\perp\) for the two tangential momentum components and \(L=R|p_\perp|\). The normalized inward Liouville flux is uniform on \(|p_\perp|<p_R\) for each entry point: the normal speed cancels the Jacobian of the energy delta function. A ray reaches \(b\) precisely when \(L<J_b\), up to tangency. Outside that disk both motions agree at every time. Consequently even the joint incoming and outgoing record has total-variation distance bounded by

A three-energy test of central-force trajectories

Newton’s force measurement and an outgoing orbit direction give an action difference in mechanical units. Consider two equal masses in a nonrelativistic elastic collision. Write \(\mu\) for their reduced mass, \(v\) for their incident relative speed, \(E=\mu v^2/2\), and \(\kappa>0\) for the coefficient of the repulsive potential \(\kappa/r\) (energy times length). With \(d=\kappa/(\mu v^2)\) the Rutherford orbit gives the impact parameter \(b=d\cot(\theta/2)\) for the labelled centre-of-mass deflection \(0<\theta<\pi\). The turning radius is \(d+\sqrt{d^2+b^2}\ge 2d\). Any change of force confined strictly inside \(2d\) leaves every such incoming classical orbit intact. Deflections outside that region, including atomic screening or multiple collisions, require an independent error bound. Rutherford, 1911, §2, pp. 672–674 (passage, applied to relative motion).

If the outgoing particles are unlabelled, the two possible angles are \(\theta\) and \(\pi-\theta\). Their relative angular characteristic action, set to zero at \(\pi/2\), has magnitude

\[ |\Delta W|=\frac{2\kappa}{v}u,\qquad u=|\log\cot(\theta/2)|. \]

Indeed Hamilton’s endpoint variation gives the angular momentum as the magnitude of the angular derivative. The two alternative derivatives enter the difference with the same sign and their magnitudes sum to \(\mu v d[\cot(\theta/2)+\tan(\theta/2)]=2\kappa/(v\sin\theta)\). The difference uses a common large-radius reference, so the common radial action constant drops out. This supplies a mechanical action coordinate for the detector, before specifying any rule for phases. Hamilton, 1834, law of varying action (passage).

Three settings on one incoming ensemble. Count each collision once and accept the full azimuth. With a target at rest the smaller of the two laboratory fork angles is \(\alpha\le\pi/4\) and \(u=\log\cot\alpha\). Choose a dimensionless angular displacement \(U\) and a fixed width \(0<w<U\). Choose incident energies so that \(d_\pm=d_0 e^{\pm U}\) (\(E_\pm=E_0e^{\mp U}\)). For any reference length \(b_{\rm ref}>0\) set \(X=\log(b/b_{\rm ref})\) and \(s_j=\log(d_j/b_{\rm ref})\). The classical observation at setting \(j\) is exactly \(u_j=|X-s_j|\), including both possible assignments of the outgoing particles. A head-on collision lies outside the finite angular windows below.

Let \(H_w(y)=(1-|y|/w)_+\) and let \(Y_j[h]\) be the sum of the event weights \(h(u)\) divided by the integrated luminosity in that run. For a thin uniform target the latter is the incident particle count times its areal number density, so \(Y_j\) has units of area. The incoming impact measure per unit exposure may be arbitrary, provided it is common across the three settings. Define

\[ C=Y_+[H_w]+Y_-[H_w]-Y_0[H_w(\,\cdot-U)] . \]

Then \(C=0\) for Newton trajectories. For each individual \(X\), the two disjoint triangular windows centered at \(s_0\pm U\) sum exactly to \(H_w(|X-s_0|-U)\). Integration proves the identity even for point masses in the incoming law. The reference length cancels. It is enough that \((X,\zeta)\) have one joint law when the actual Coulomb length is \(d_je^\zeta\) in run \(j\). The same identity then holds pointwise in \(X-\zeta\). Thus even a common fractional energy spread correlated with impact parameter is allowed. A common error in the absolute force strength also cancels. Time insertion leaves the continuum deflection unchanged on these collision-free orbits.

The detector weight is Lipschitz with constant \(1/w\). If the measured \(u_j\) differs from the ideal value by at most \(e_j\), the classical residual obeys \(|C|\le M(e_++e_-+e_0)/w\). Here \(M\) is an upper bound on the common impact measure of the union of the three preimages, enlarged by their error margins. A discrepancy \(\epsilon_j\) between the actual and common input measures, measured by the largest absolute difference on weights between zero and one on that union, adds \(\sum_j\epsilon_j\) to the bound. The \(\epsilon_j\) and \(M\) have units of area. Uncertainties in efficiency, backgrounds, luminosity and sampling enter in the units of \(C\). A detector distance and pixel width set the angular error \(\delta\alpha\). On an acceptance enlarged to \(\alpha\ge\alpha_{\min}>0\) within the folded half, its contribution to \(e_j\) is at most \(2\delta\alpha/\sin(2\alpha_{\min})\). A flight length \(\ell\) and an independent clock interval \(t\) give \(s_j=\log(\kappa t^2/(\mu b_{\rm ref}\ell^2))\). Thus calibrated errors in \(\ell,t\) propagate directly to \(s_j\). The clock and position resolutions stay fixed while the mathematical time mesh shrinks.

Run normalization can also be eliminated for the classical test. At three widths \(w_k<U\), let \(N_{jk}\) be the background- and efficiency-corrected weighted counts with the above windows. If only the three positive run exposures are unknown, the middle-setting row \((N_{0k})_k\) must be a positive linear combination of the other two rows with the same coefficients at all widths in the mean. The matrix of mean counts therefore has determinant zero. This tests relative angular shapes alone when the aperture response is known.

How accurately must the force be known? Write the actual radial force on a scattering orbit as \(\kappa[1+g(r)]/r^2\), positive outwards. Measure the polar angle \(\phi\) from the incoming asymptote and let \(\Phi=\pi-\theta\) at exit. For \(b>0\) and a repulsive scattering orbit with \(0<\Phi<\pi\), Binet’s equation for \(y=1/r\) is \(y''+y=-(d/b^2)[1+g(r)]\), with \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(0)=1/b\). At the exit \(y(\Phi)=0\). The sine Green function gives

\[ \frac{b}{d}\tan(\theta/2)=1+\bar g,\qquad \bar g=\frac{\int_0^\Phi\sin(\Phi-\phi)\,g(r(\phi))\,d\phi} {1-\cos\Phi}. \]

This is a positive weighted average of the actual force error along the orbit. It requires no bound on a force gradient. An independent bound \(|\bar g|\le\gamma<1\) adds at most \(-\log(1-\gamma)\) to the error \(e_j\) above. A bound on \(|g|\) from short-time force measurements on the traversed annulus suffices if the tails are also controlled. An outer portion of total polar angle \(h\) with \(|g|\le D\) contributes at most \(Dh/(1-\cos\Phi)\) to the average. This allows a screened tail to be treated separately. There is a direct threshold for an ideal counter accepting \(0\le u\le w\) with full azimuth, uniform incident fluence and one fixed speed. \[ \frac{Y_{\rm actual}}{Y_{\rm Coulomb}} \le 1+\gamma^2+2\gamma\coth(2w). \]

For a twofold excess one therefore needs \(\gamma\ge 1/(\sqrt{1+\coth^2(2w)}+\coth(2w))\). For \(w=\log\cot40^\circ\) this is about \(0.16\). Three work readings and the low-energy limit

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-18. For an equilibrated spherical atom and a distinguishable negative test charge, the electrostatic binding energy is convex in inverse distance. This allows three work readings to bound the turn of an entire annulus without reconstructing a radial force or truncating a multipole series. If the equilibrium potential has the measured dipole coefficient in its asymptotic tail, the annulus can move outward as the collision energy falls. The resulting low-energy observable is the rate coefficient \(vQ_{\rm mt}\), where \(Q_{\rm mt}\) weights each elastic collision by the fraction of incident longitudinal momentum it transfers.

Why charge penetration has a sign. Fix a point nucleus of charge \(+Ze\), a projectile of charge \(-e\) with \(e>0\), and \(\kappa=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)\). A trial state \(a\) of the target has internal energy \(H_a\), mobile electron number measure \(n_a\ge0\) of mass \(Z\), and energy at clamped probe position \(\mathbf R\) \[ H_a-E_0-\frac{Z\kappa}{|\mathbf R|} +\kappa\int\frac{dn_a(x)}{|\mathbf R-x|}. \] Take the infimum over the same rotationally invariant family of states at every \(\mathbf R\), with \(E_0\) the isolated minimum. Assume this equilibrium work \(W(r)\) is finite and continuous for \(r>0\) and tends to zero at infinity. An unpolarized spherical minimizing state gives \(W(r)\le0\) by the shell theorem. For a sphere of radius \(d<|\mathbf R|\) centred at \(\mathbf R\), the nuclear term has its centre value as its average. Each repulsive electron kernel averages to \(1/\max(d,|\mathbf R-x|)\), at most its centre value, including for electrons inside the averaging sphere. Every trial energy at the centre therefore dominates the average of \(W\) over that sphere. Taking the infimum at the centre gives \(\Delta W\le0\) in distributions. In radial coordinates this is precisely convexity of \(-W(1/u)\) in \(u>0\). Its value at \(u=0\) is zero and it is nonnegative, so it is nondecreasing. The sign uses a negative probe and negative mobile charges. Exchange with the probe, magnetic forces and delayed relaxation are separate physical assumptions. Newton’s shell theorem applies to each frozen state even when the minimum responds nonlinearly. Motte 1729, Book I, Section XII, Propositions LXX–LXXI (passages). The variational treatment of an external potential also applies to electronic ground-state energies. The sign argument itself requires no quantum state law. Lieb (1983) (abstract, variational background).

What the discrepancy measures about a trial action

Convergence to the classical gas at these three temperatures would exclude a sequence of trial actions tending to zero that fits the separated data. A quantitative version allows a steep core. In the spin-independent two-body Schrödinger family let \(h\) denote the trial reduced action, \(m\) the atomic mass, \(\mu=m/2\) the reduced mass and \(\sigma=h\sqrt{\beta/\mu}\). Suppose \(V\ge-E\), with \(E\ge0\), and choose a radius \(a\) beyond the minimum. Assume the truncated potential \(V_a\) (zero outside \(a\)) decreases to its minimum and then increases, allowing flat pieces and a hard core. Let \(I_a=\int_{|\mathbf r|>a}|V|\,d^3\mathbf r\) be finite. For the direct and exchange terms together, \[ \begin{split} |B_h(T)-B_0(T;V_a)|&\le \varepsilon_T(h),\\ \varepsilon_T(h)&=N_A e^{\beta E} \left[8\pi\left(a^2c_1\sigma+\frac{c_3\sigma^3}{3}\right) +\frac{\beta I_a}{2}+\frac{\lambda^3}{2^{5/2}}\right],\\ \lambda&=h\sqrt{2\pi\beta/m},\qquad c_j=6(3/2)^{j/2}\Gamma(1+j/2). \end{split} \] The direct thermal trace uses the relative Brownian-bridge average of \(\exp[-\beta\int_0^1 V(|\mathbf r+\sigma b_t|)\,dt]\) for a standard three-dimensional unit bridge \(b\). Replacing \(V\) by \(V_a\) costs at most \(N_A\beta e^{\beta E} I_a/2\) by translation in \(\mathbf r\) and the exponential’s slope on \([-E,\infty)\). For a hard core, cap the common repulsion first and then pass to the hard-core limit. The absolute exchange contribution is at most its free spin-polarized magnitude times \(e^{\beta E}\), giving the last term. Unpolarized spin one-half reduces the spin weight further. Garberoglio and Harvey, direct and exchange traces, Eqs. (9)–(18) (formula passages).

To bound the remaining direct term, fix a bridge whose displacement never exceeds \(d=\sigma\sup_t|b_t|\). Its path weight lies between the smallest and largest values of \(f(r)=e^{-\beta V_a(r)}\) on the sampled radii. At each level strictly between \(0\) and \(e^{\beta E}\), except for plateau levels of measure zero, the region above that level has at most two radial boundaries of radii \(u\le a\). This use of the canonical energy variance is standard. Gibbs, 1902, Ch. VII (canonical averages and fluctuations).

One can test this alternative even when higher density terms matter. At fixed \(T\), let \(G(\rho,T)=\int_0^\rho b(T,s)\,ds\), the excess molar Helmholtz free energy divided by \(R_{\rm g}T\) relative to the same ideal gas. This follows from \(p=-(\partial F/\partial V)_T\) at fixed atom number. Allow a temperature-independent microscopic energy with arbitrary internal variables and many-body forces, ordinary separable translational kinetic energy, canonical equilibrium and a single gas phase on the integration path. If \(q(T)\) is the free atom’s internal partition function, \(G-\log q\) differs by a temperature-independent constant from minus the log of the many-atom partition integral per atom after integrating translational momenta. It is concave in \(x=1/T\) by Hölder’s inequality, also in a thermodynamic limit in which these free energies exist. Hence \(\mathcal D[G]\le\mathcal D[\log q]\). If independent calorimetry bounds the free atom’s internal heat capacity by \(c\,k_B\) throughout the interval, then \((\log q)''(x)\le c/x^2\). Subtracting \(\log q\) from \(-c\log(x/x_m)\) gives a convex function, so \[ \mathcal D[G]\le c\left(\frac35\log 3-\frac25\log 2\right). \] This test uses pressure isotherms from zero to a common density and an independent thermal bound. It accommodates rare internal states and requires no cutoff radius or force fit. For example, if isotherms up to \(250\ {\rm mol/m^3}\) establish that \(\mathcal D[b]\) stays within \(1\ {\rm cm^3/mol}\) of the dilute contrast, the lower value \(5.364\) above would imply \(\mathcal D[G]\ge0.001091\) and require \(c>0.0028\). This tolerance specifies the experimental precision needed for that inference. The three tabulated coefficients alone give neither that pressure integral nor the internal heat capacity. They exclude the equilibrated point-atom pair potential class at the stated accuracy. Independent necessity of a positive physical action remains open.

How many atomic motions can the measured heat support?

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-18. Warming normal liquid helium-3 from 30 to 50 mK at fixed molar volume \(32\,{\rm cm^3/mol}\) takes less than one fifth of the minimum heat for equilibrated Newton atoms with independently mobile momenta. Adding internal states, correlations or many-body positional forces cannot close that difference. In a finite vessel the same measurement excludes an interval of small positive trial actions for each fixed scalar fermion Hamiltonian described below. A mechanical measurement suggested by Lemma X would test the assumption that the atomic motions themselves remain accessible in the liquid.

The preparation and readout. The classical exclusion. Take a closed, fixed-volume Gibbs preparation with temperature-independent Hamiltonian and phase-space measure. Conditional on the configurations and internal variables \(z\), allow \(d\) unrestricted momenta with energy \(\tfrac12(p-a(z))^\top M(z)^{-1}(p-a(z))+W(z)\), \(M(z)>0\), and the ordinary momentum volume element. Equipartition and the canonical energy variance give \[ C_V(T)\ \ge\ \tfrac12 k_B\,\langle d\rangle_T,\qquad \frac{1}{N\Delta T}\int_{T_c}^{T_w}\langle d\rangle_T\,dT \ \le\ 2\bar c\ \le\ 0.60 . \] Indeed the conditional quadratic energy has variance \(d(k_BT)^2/2\) even if \(d\) depends on the sector. The variance of the conditional mean adds a nonnegative term. This use of the Gibbs identities allows arbitrary correlations, annealed labels and interactions in \(W\). Ordinary point atoms have \(d=3N\). Fixing the total momentum removes at most three such directions from the entire sample. Their minimum heat per mole over the interval is \(0.030 R_{\rm g}\,{\rm K}\) up to that finite-size correction. Every mathematical subdivision of the heating interval gives the same finite-heat inequality. Gibbs, 1902, Ch. VII (passage, canonical fluctuations and quadratic momenta).

An action bound for the same finite heat. Put \(\beta_i=1/(k_BT_i)\) for \(i=c,w\), \(\beta_m=(\beta_c+\beta_w)/2\) and \(J[f]=(f(\beta_c)+f(\beta_w))/2-f(\beta_m)\). For \(f=N^{-1}\log Z\), the change in its slope between the endpoints is \(Q/N\). Convexity bounds the midpoint difference by this slope change times \((\beta_c-\beta_w)/4\) (the maximum of the triangular kernel for the midpoint difference). For \(3N\) classical momenta, integrating the Gaussians leaves a log-convex positional partition integral. Hence \[ J[f_{\rm obs}]\le\frac{(\beta_c-\beta_w)Q}{4N}\le0.020,\qquad J[f_{\rm cl}]\ge\frac32\log \frac{\beta_m}{\sqrt{\beta_c\beta_w}} =\frac32\log\frac4{\sqrt{15}}>0.048 . \] The first inequality applies to any equilibrium model reproducing the heat. Additive energy offsets and constant normalization of the partition function cancel. The positive difference \(\delta=0.028\) per atom uses only the two temperatures and the heat allowance.

Consider \(N\) identical spin-\(\tfrac12\) atoms of mass \(m\) with Dirichlet walls in a bounded vessel \(\Omega\) with piecewise smooth boundary. Let \(V\) be a fixed spin-independent permutation-symmetric scalar potential, bounded below, with \(e^{-\beta V}\) Riemann integrable and positive in integral. This includes hard cores with null contact boundaries, interpreted as Dirichlet exclusions on the allowed open configurations. Write \(Z_F^h\) for the fermionic trace with kinetic operator \(-h^2\sum_i\Delta_i/(2m)\), where \(h\) is a trial reduced action, and \(\lambda_\beta=h\sqrt{2\pi\beta/m}\). In zero field sum over all spin states. The matching classical reference is \[ Z_{\rm cl}^h=\frac{2^N}{N!}\lambda_\beta^{-3N} \int_{\Omega^N}e^{-\beta V(x)}\,dx . \] Take all grid cubes of side \(\ell\) lying wholly in the vessel. In the sum below \(D\) ranges over ordered products of \(N\) distinct such cubes. Define the dimensionless loss of classical positional weight by \[ L_\beta(\ell)= \frac{\sum_D \ell^{3N}e^{-\beta\sup_D V}} {\int_{\Omega^N}e^{-\beta V(x)}\,dx},\qquad A_\beta(\ell)=-N^{-1}\log L_\beta(\ell). \] For \(0<\lambda_\beta<\ell\) and positive \(L_\beta\), \[ 0\le\frac1N\log\frac{Z_{\rm cl}^h}{Z_F^h} \le A_\beta(\ell)-3\log(1-\lambda_\beta/\ell). \] For the left inequality apply Golden–Thompson on the antisymmetric subspace. On its diagonal the free heat kernel is a determinant divided by \(N!\) on labelled coordinates including spin. Hadamard’s inequality bounds it by the product of diagonal one-particle kernels, each at most \(\lambda_\beta^{-3}\) by Dirichlet domination. Additional Dirichlet hard-core exclusions can only reduce the trace, also relative to a finite repulsive barrier filling each excluded region. Sending that barrier to infinity gives the stated upper bound. Golden (abstract, thermal trace inequality) and Ceperley (passage, free-particle density matrix, Eq. 32).

For the right inequality confine one atom to each of \(N\) distinct cubes, use Dirichlet walls, and replace \(V\) there by its supremum. Antisymmetrizing the disjoint cell products leaves one copy per unordered cell set and \(2^N\) spin choices. For fixed spin populations the number of choices is \(\binom{N}{N_\uparrow}\) on both sides in place of \(2^N\), so the comparison is unchanged. The variational principle bounds their summed traces above by the full fermionic trace. The one-dimensional cell trace is at least \(\ell/\lambda_\beta-1\) by the integral bound for the decreasing Dirichlet Gaussian sum. Taking three factors per atom gives the displayed inequality. This argument uses the sign of a free determinant only on its diagonal.

Write \(A_*=(A_{\beta_c}+A_{\beta_w})/2\) at the same \(\ell\). The midpoint correction \(N^{-1}\log(Z_{\rm cl}^h/Z_F^h)\) is nonnegative. Thus a fermion model fitting the observed heat must satisfy, whenever \(A_*<\delta\), \[ h\ \ge\ \ell\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi\beta_c}}\, \big[1-e^{-(\delta-A_*)/3}\big]. \] If \(\lambda_{\beta_c}\ge\ell\) this already holds. Otherwise average the error at the two endpoints and use \(\lambda_{\beta_w}\le \lambda_{\beta_c}\). At fixed \(N,\Omega,V\), \(L_\beta(\ell)\to1\) on successively halved grids by lower Riemann approximation. Walls and particle coincidences have zero configuration volume. A positive \(\ell\) with \(A_*<\delta\) therefore exists for each such model. Obtaining a number in action units requires \(m\), a physical length \(\ell\) and control of the classical weight on that length from the vessel geometry, sample density and interaction energies. The grid estimates that weight without imposing a minimum length. Uniformity over changing potentials or increasing sample sizes remains open. Under \(h\mapsto a h\) with all preparation and calorimeter settings fixed, \(\lambda_\beta/\ell\) is multiplied by \(a\) while the heat allowance and \(A_\beta\) are unchanged. Rescaling the bath temperature, atomic mass or vessel dimensions to undo this would change measured inputs.

What would a Lemma X measurement add? For smooth, time-independent ideal holonomic constraints and the atomic mass metric, the response to a small constant force applied to atom \(i\), read in one unit direction \(e\), is the corresponding diagonal entry of the mass-weighted orthogonal projector \(P\) onto allowed velocities. Operationally that entry is the limit of \(2m\, e\cdot(\langle x_i(\tau)\rangle_F-\langle x_i(\tau)\rangle_0)/ (F\tau^2)\) for a force \(Fe\) and matched initial ensembles, averaged over their configurations. Smooth finite forces give the short-time expansion away from impacts. The trace of \(P\) counts the mobile directions at each configuration. Thus for a uniformly sampled atom and an isotropically sampled axis, the temperature-averaged mean response is at most \(2\bar c/3\le0.20\) in this constrained equilibrium class. Unconstrained atoms give one. The required independent readout measures mass, local applied force, elapsed time and displacement, with control of the finite-time remainder and atom selection. A uniform force tests only one collective profile per axis. Udwadia and Kalaba (passage, constrained equations for forces doing zero virtual work).

Both the force and its readout can have finite resolution. In Cartesian coordinates for equal atomic masses let \(u\) be the desired unit vector at the chosen atom. A force \(F b\) and displacement sensor along \(c\) give the initial response \(c^\top P b\). Map the two profiles at their physical spot widths and bound \(\|b-u\|\le\zeta_F\) and \(\|c-u\|\le\zeta_x\). The spatial error in the normalized response is at most \(\zeta_F+\zeta_x+\zeta_F\zeta_x\) since \(\|P\|\le1\). For a timed exposure \(\tau\), let \(L\) bound, in inverse seconds, the rate of change of the mean acceleration difference along \(c\) in units of \(|F|/m\). The displacement averages the acceleration with weight \(2(\tau-s)/\tau^2\). If the error on the paired displacement is at most \(\sigma_x\) in metres, the additional error is bounded by \[ L\tau/3+2m\sigma_x/(|F|\tau^2). \] Both terms use apparatus calibrations independent of a time mesh. With positive \(L\) and \(\sigma_x\) the best duration for this estimate is \(\tau=(12m\sigma_x/(|F|L))^{1/3}\) if the acceleration bound holds that long. Shrinking the plotted time step cannot improve this physical measurement.

Heat and a recoil spectrum have both been measured at the same nominal temperature and pressure. At 500 mK and 10 bar the thermodynamic table gives \(C_P/(\nu R_{\rm g}T)=0.6964\,{\rm K}^{-1}\). Stability gives \(C_V\le C_P\), hence a pointwise classical ceiling of \(0.2322\) on the mean projected response using the tabulated value. Allowing \(C_V/(\nu R_{\rm g})\le0.40\) raises the ceiling to \(0.267\). Bryan et al. selected a nominal 800 meV incident neutron energy and a mean scattering angle of \(125^\circ\) at this temperature and pressure. They compared the recoil line to a single-atom impulse profile with instrumental resolution and final-state corrections. Kollar and Vollhardt (Table IV, 10 bar) and Bryan et al. (experimental approach and extraction of kinetic energy).

For a mechanical comparison, deliver opposite impulses of magnitude \(I\) on matched preparations to a uniformly chosen atom along a random axis, and read the work \(W_\pm\) done on the sample. In the impulsive limit the coordinates stay fixed, the momentum change is projected onto allowed velocities, and \(m\langle W_++W_-\rangle/I^2\) equals the mean projector entry. The opposite signs cancel the initial velocity. Suppose an instrument determines the mean entry \(r\) only on a fraction \(\eta\) of these uniformly assigned paired trials, with selection independent of the sign and with the two works matched at fixed initial state or in conditional mean. Nonnegativity of each projected squared impulse makes \(\eta r\) a lower bound for the unconditional entry. Thus \(\eta r>0.267\) would exclude even the constrained classical model at the warmer state under the stated heat allowance. A calibrated impulse, delivered work, mass and a lower bound on the represented fraction suffice. Conditioning on detected scattering events alone loses this last factor. For example, a fraction \(s\) of freely mobile atoms among fixed atoms has true mean entry \(s\), yet gives unit response on the mobile subset for every \(s>0\). The published profile measures scattering intensity in a selected energy and angle range. It supplies neither the opposite-work measurement nor that uniform sampling calibration. This is the remaining physical step between the heat deficit and Newton’s individual force readout.

What inertial mass can change. Consider an equilibrium mixture in which either isotope may occupy any admissible atomic position. Allow hidden internal coordinates, many-body forces and smooth ideal holonomic constraints. For the mass-only comparison the constraint surface, its statistical weights apart from the kinetic integral, and the positional energy are invariant under changing the isotope at a site. An isotope-specific constant energy or spin multiplicity can be included in its activity. Let \(z\) be the common ratio of isotope activities in two coexisting phases, with these constants included. An observation region and its sampling rule must be defined without reference to the isotope of the atom to be counted.

At fixed configuration the induced kinetic mass matrix has the form \(G(m)=G_{\rm rest}+mB^\top B>0\), where \(B\) maps generalized velocities to the three Cartesian velocity components of the chosen atom and \(G_{\rm rest}\ge0\). Integrating the canonical momenta gives a weight proportional to \(\sqrt{\det G(m)}\). In the mechanical realization of Lemma X above, the initial acceleration response of this atom to an applied force at the same atom is \(BG^{-1}B^\top\). Consequently \[ \frac{d\log\sqrt{\det G(m)}}{d\log m} =\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(mBG^{-1}B^\top)\ \in[0,3/2]. \] The matrix in the trace is a positive contraction because \(mB^\top B\le G\). Integrating between the measured atomic masses gives \[ 1\le\sqrt{\det G(m_4)/\det G(m_3)}\le K,\qquad K=(m_4/m_3)^{3/2}<1.54 . \] The conditional light/heavy odds at every position with the other labels held fixed therefore lie between \(z/K\) and \(z\). Integrating either inequality over a phase, with its other atoms and constraints, preserves it. Thus \(K^{-1}\le\alpha\le K\). Comparing these ratios uses equilibration at a common temperature. Its absolute value is needed only to express the work in energy units. Unconstrained Cartesian atoms give exactly \(\alpha=1\), the classical equilibrium isotope result. The determinant bound covers partially frozen motions as well. Bigeleisen and Mayer (abstract, equilibrium isotope separation), Fixman (abstract, constrained kinetic determinant) and NIST isotope masses (table).

How much extra force is required? Draw a tagged helium-3 environment in \(A\) and a tagged helium-4 environment in \(B\) from the equilibrium number-weighted samples. Take independent draws so that the two composition measurements enter through their product. At fixed coordinates interchange the isotopes. Let \(W\) be the change in positional energy before the surroundings relax, and \(D\) the ratio of the final product of kinetic weights to the initial product. The first substitution multiplies the kinetic weight by a factor in \([1,K]\) and the reverse one by a factor in \([1/K,1]\). Interchanging the labels again restores the original pair. Integrating the changed weights cancels both activity factors and gives the equilibrium perturbation identity \[ \frac1\alpha=\big\langle D e^{-\beta W}\big\rangle_{3A,4B}, \qquad K^{-1}\le D\le K,\qquad \beta=1/(k_BT). \] This form uses phases at common isotope chemical potentials. In a finite closed cell the same change of variables swaps two uniformly chosen atoms in different regions within one configuration. The left side then uses the ratio of the two joint probabilities of opposite labellings. No product approximation is needed for that version. For a mutual pair interaction the pair between the two chosen atoms stays the same. Zwanzig, 1954 (equilibrium free-energy perturbation identity).

One can also test the two reported marginal concentrations in a closed cell without inferring any joint probability. Fix positive isotope counts \(N_3,N_4\) and condition on the unlabelled geometry. If \(p_i\) is the probability that site \(i\) is light, choose \(j\) uniformly among the other sites. The ratio of probabilities of \((3_i,4_j)\) and \((4_i,3_j)\) is precisely \(p_iN_4/[(1-p_i)N_3]\). The mass-only swap bounds this by \(K\) and its reciprocal by \(K\), because the simultaneous mass changes have opposite signs. Thus every site’s odds lie between \(N_3/(K N_4)\) and \(K N_3/N_4\). The same interval contains the ratio of mean counts in any isotope-blind window, even with fluctuating window occupancy. In particular \(\alpha\le K^2<2.38\) at fixed counts, already excluded by the measured isotherm. With a uniform bound \(|W|\le w\) on all potential-energy changes, the same argument gives \(\alpha\le K^2e^{2\beta w}\). The observed enrichment therefore requires some swaps costing more than \(k_B(1.99\,{\rm K})\) even in the closed-cell comparison. The stronger mean bound below uses the common activities of coexisting phases.

Jensen’s inequality and restriction of the positive integral to any subset of swaps of cost at most \(w\) give, respectively, \[ \langle W\rangle_{3A,4B}\ge k_BT\log(\alpha/K),\qquad \Pr_{3A,4B}(W\le w)\le K e^{\beta w}/\alpha . \] For the Leiden isotherm the first lower bound exceeds \(k_B(4.68\,{\rm K})\). The second says that fewer than one in ten of the represented pairs can have cost at most \(k_B(1\,{\rm K})\). The probability bound continues to hold on the reversible subset if some substitutions are forbidden and assigned infinite cost, provided each counted swap has the same base-measure mapping and kinetic bound. Arbitrary isotope-dependent state multiplicities or constraints require a separate calibration of those weights. The gravitational contribution is at most \((m_4-m_3)gH\) for a vertical separation \(H\). One metre gives less than \(k_B(0.0012\,{\rm K})\) at terrestrial gravity.

For pairwise central forces define \(F_{st}(r)=-v'_{st}(r)\) positive outwards and normalize \(v_{st}(\infty)=0\). Let \(n^A_{3t}(r)\) count, in mean, neighbours of isotope \(t\) within \(r\) of a helium-3 atom in \(A\), and define \(n^B_{4t}\) in the other phase in the same way. The isotope-dependent part of the pair work is exactly \[ \langle W_{\rm pair}\rangle = \sum_{t=3,4}\int_0^\infty [F_{4t}(r)-F_{3t}(r)]\,[n^A_{3t}(r)-n^B_{4t}(r)]\,dr . \] Indeed, substitution at one site costs \(\sum_j(v_{4t_j}-v_{3t_j})(r_j)\). Using \(v_{st}(r_j)=\int_{r_j}^\infty F_{st}(r)\,dr\) and subtracting the reverse substitution gives the formula when the absolute integrals are finite. The counts are dimensionless. The two-body force can be calibrated as reduced mass times the radial projection of the relative vector acceleration, using lengths, elapsed times and a finite-time error allowance as above. An upper bound for this integral plus wall, field and isotope-specific many-body work below the measured threshold would reject the classical explanation. Even exact forces in isolated atom pairs leave a possible many-body interaction. Take a smooth radial function \(\phi\) of finite range \(a\), with \(0\le\phi\le1\) and zero slope at the origin. Put \(n_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\phi(r_{ij})\) and choose a smooth \(s\) that is zero for \(n\le1\) and one for \(n\ge2\), with \(0\le s\le1\). Add \(u\sum_i b_i s(n_i)\) to the host energy, where \(b_i=1\) for helium-3 and zero for helium-4. Substituting a light atom for a heavy one at fixed positions costs exactly \(u s(n_i)\). The conditional isotope odds therefore have the same two plateaux as in the one-body example. The added force on atom \(i\) from the \(ij\) separation is \[ -u[b_i s'(n_i)+b_j s'(n_j)]\phi'(r_{ij})\, (x_i-x_j)/r_{ij}. \] These forces act along centre lines and satisfy action and reaction. 7. Consequence for STATE

Newton action necessity is the active priority. This note gives the first \(0+1\) background construction: arbitrary unequal polygons converge on a stated Lipschitz-controlled interval; the same Green function controls source response and the Gaussian width proportional to \(\hbar\). Its oscillator example separates boundary caustics from initial-value failure. The return-side detector gives a common observable for the two orders of the core and action limits. The incoming Liouville flux already controls the sensitive trajectories classically. The open physical question is whether observed return laws exclude zero action. The three-energy collision identity removes arbitrary common impact concentration from a classical comparison. Its use on real data requires control of the accessible force, of how the ensemble changes with the setting, and of the angular response. A lower bound from Mott exchange also supplies that dynamical law. The physical premise that would force a positive action for central motion in general remains open. Three equilibrium work readings control nonlinear static screening and charge penetration. The two-pulse test covers delayed motion in its stated model. The thermal shutter test bypasses the absolute microwave boost calibration. Its remaining premises are the exterior binding work during passage and the driven velocity moment. The field-free, finite-time position measurement dispenses with the latter and has yet to be matched to data at the stated preparation. The liquid-helium heat measurement excludes mobile classical Gibbs atoms even with internal states. Isotope enrichment bounds the extra work needed even with constrained atoms. An independent bound on the isotope-dependent force during passage between the two environments would test the remaining classical alternative. The two-power barrier comparison needs guarded return counts and independent control of the optical work across the occupied beam. Returning through the same static barrier removes its interior force profile from the two-image bound. Four launch energies bound the effect of a nonunique barrier-top orbit. Applying this to rubidium requires matched guarded counts and an independent bound on single-atom work. With one atom per guide the remaining conditions are the work across several cells, freeze and acceptance errors, and the time allowed for a distant return. For a central orbit the centrifugal drop between equal shell phases supplies the corresponding work in ruler and clock units.
