A bounded Alpoge–Sussman peek: what returns to Newton in 0+1

Markdown source · PDF

For an exact noncircular Kepler orbit, requiring a local, reversible Euclidean transfer for its first radial harmonic gives an apparent action equal to twice the classical radial action. Its second harmonic is incompatible with that transfer. Keeping just the two orbital speed bounds already forces a relative discrepancy of at least \(9/80\) in a real-time squared displacement. A positive jump process reproduces both frequency laws, and a third radial reading tests positivity across an inserted cut. All the orbital data come from one Newtonian ellipse. 2. A connected fourth response in the exact Newton cell

Use the convex quartic cell at zero source, with

\[ d\nu(z)=Z^{-1}\exp\left[-\frac1\hbar \left(\frac a2z^2+\frac b4z^4\right)\right]dz, \qquad a,b,\hbar>0, \qquad m_j=\mathbb E_\nu Z^j. \tag{1} \]

Here \(a=mT/(uv)\) and \(b=w\lambda\), with \(T=u+v\) and \(w=T/2\). The coefficient \(b\) has units action/length\(^4\). Symmetry gives zero odd moments, and the quartic tail justifies all differentiations and integrations by parts below.

Integration by parts against \(z\) and \(z^3\) gives

\[ a m_2+b m_4=\hbar,\qquad a m_4+b m_6=3\hbar m_2. \tag{2} \]

Multiply the second identity by \(m_2\), the first by \(m_4\), and subtract:

\[ \boxed{\quad m_4-3m_2^2 =-\frac b\hbar\left(m_2m_6-m_4^2\right)<0.\quad} \tag{3} \]

Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(m_4^2\le m_2m_6\) by comparing \(Z\) and \(Z^3\). Equality would require \(Z^3\) proportional to \(Z\) almost surely, which is impossible for this everywhere positive density. Thus (3) is a strict finite test of its departure from a Gaussian law. No small-\(b\) or small-\(\hbar\) expansion was used.

For a source \(j\) of units action/length, let \(N(j)=\mathbb E e^{jZ/\hbar}\). Then

\[ (\log N)^{(4)}(0)=\frac{m_4-3m_2^2}{\hbar^4},\qquad N^{(4)}(0)=\frac{m_4}{\hbar^4}. \tag{4} \]

The subtraction matters: an ordinary fourth moment is nonzero even for a free Gaussian. A connected response removes the three Gaussian pairings. For the retained action of the insertion cell,

\[ A_\hbar(\mu)-A_\hbar(0) =\frac a2\mu^2-\hbar\log N(a\mu),\qquad A_\hbar^{(4)}(0) =-\frac{a^4}{\hbar^3}(m_4-3m_2^2)>0. \tag{5} \]

This uses the same normalization and source convention as the nonlinear-cell note. It is an additional full-law response identity, not an inference from the background trajectory alone.

3. Refinement can hide an interaction in an unscaled probe

Put \(g=b\hbar/a^2\) and \(Y=\sqrt{a/\hbar}\,Z\). The normalized law of \(Y\) is proportional to \(e^{-Y^2/2-gY^4/4}\). As \(g\downarrow0\), dominated convergence gives its second, fourth and sixth moments \(1,3,15\). Equation (3) therefore gives

\[ m_4-3m_2^2\sim-\frac{6b\hbar^3}{a^4},\qquad A_\hbar^{(4)}(0)\sim6b. \tag{6} \]

For equal time pieces \(u=v=T/2\), \(a=4m/T\), \(b=\lambda T/2\) and \(g=\lambda\hbar T^3/(32m^2)\). At fixed \(m,\lambda,\hbar>0\), shrinking the cell consequently gives

\[ m_4-3m_2^2\sim-\frac{3\lambda\hbar^3}{256m^4}T^5, \qquad \frac{A_\hbar^{(4)}(0)}w\longrightarrow6\lambda. \tag{7} \]

Thus the raw local cumulant tends to zero even though the retained potential’s fourth derivative tends to the nonzero derivative of \(\lambda\mu^4/4\). Neither a finite nonzero cell cumulant nor its raw vanishing establishes the status of an interacting continuum theory. A fixed macroscopic observable and an estimate uniform in refinement are required. That is the useful parallel with PR1’s cutoff and volume comparison, without importing its unreviewed field-theory proof.

4. The 0+1 reflection-positivity control, including zero action

RP1’s rotation theorem needs \(d\ge2\). Its time-positivity ingredient has a direct \(0+1\) control. Fix \(m,\omega>0\) and consider a centered stationary Gaussian process with covariance

\[ C_\hbar(t-s)=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}e^{-\omega|t-s|}, \qquad \hbar\ge0. \tag{8} \]

At zero multiplier this is the deterministic zero process. For real smooth compactly supported positive-time tests \(f_i\), write \(Q(f)=\iint f(t)C_\hbar(t-s)f(s)dt\,ds\) and \(u_i=\int_0^\infty f_i(t)e^{-\omega t}dt\). Time reflection is \((\theta f)(t)=f(-t)\). Its characteristic functional obeys

\[ \Phi(f_i-\theta f_j) =e^{-Q(f_i)/2}e^{-Q(f_j)/2} \exp\left(\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}u_i u_j\right). \tag{9} \]

For any finite collection, the last kernel is positive semidefinite: expand its exponential into the sum of rank-one kernels \([\hbar/(2m\omega)]^n u_i^n u_j^n/n!\). Multiplication by the displayed positive diagonal factors preserves positivity. This proves the full Gaussian exponential-observable reflection inequality, including at \(\hbar=0\), where every matrix entry is one.

Hence reflection positivity and a time generator bounded below do not exclude a trivial zero-action member. Positive \(\hbar\) in (8) is a supplied covariance scale; the quartic witness (3) is nonzero for each positive value but collapses at zero. No universal positive action value or relativistic \(c\) follows from these tests.

Kepler motion and reflection at a cut

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-19. Newton’s revised Lemma X uses initial displacement to determine the force, and Proposition XXXI asks for an elliptic position at an assigned time. Both use the time of the motion. To test whether positivity adds a physical premise, keep that time \(t\) distinct from an auxiliary transfer time \(\tau\). Lemma X, 1687 and 1713 and Proposition XXXI, 1713 (edition passages).

Take a classical bound orbit of mass \(m\) in the potential \(-m\mu/r\), with \(\mu=GM>0\), energy \(E<0\) and angular momentum \(J>0\). Write \(A=-m\mu/(2E)\), \(I=m\sqrt{\mu A}\), \(e=\sqrt{1-(J/I)^2}\in(0,1)\) and \(\nu=\sqrt{\mu/A^3}\). These fix the length and time scales. Under the stationary preparation the mean anomaly \(\phi\) is uniform on the circle and advances by \(\nu t\). The radius is \(r=A(1-e\cos u)\), where \(u-e\sin u=\phi\). On \(0\le u\le\pi\), \(r\) goes from periapsis to apoapsis. Thus \(X=\cos\phi\) and \(Y=\cos(2\phi)=2X^2-1\) are bounded functions of radius alone, with the same values on the return leg. The law of \(X\) is \(\pi_0(dx)=dx/(\pi\sqrt{1-x^2})\) on \((-1,1)\). Stationary Kepler preparation and source for the orbit clock (Kepler equations, passage).

The Newtonian trajectory obeys the exact second-difference identity \(X_{-t}-2X_0+X_t=2(\cos(\nu t)-1)X_0\). Its normalized limit is \(-\nu^2X_0\). The actual inward force \(m\mu/r^2\) is finite throughout the orbit. Yet time reflection \(\Theta:t\mapsto-t\) gives, for \(F=X_t-X_0\),

\[ \mathbb E[(\Theta F)F]=\cos(\nu t)\,[\cos(\nu t)-1]<0, \qquad 0<\nu t<\pi/2 . \tag{10} \]

The sign follows from \(\mathbb E[X_0X_t]=\tfrac12\cos(\nu t)\). Moving the time-zero reading to a positive time tending to zero gives the same strict sign by continuity. Newtonian time reversal and finite force therefore admit a negative reflected square at every sufficiently fine cut.

There is also a bound that uses just one physical time lag. For any stationary candidate let \(D_f(t)=\mathbb E[(f_t-f_0)^2]\). Positivity of the reflected square of \(f_t-f_0\) requires \(D_f(2t)\le2D_f(t)\). With \(T=\pi/(2\nu)\) the ellipse supplies \(D_X(T)=1\). A candidate preserving this single number and cut positivity at each dyadic step must have \(D_X(t_n)/t_n\ge1/T\) at \(t_n=T/2^n\). On the orbital interval \(|dX/dr|\le K\) with \(K=(1+e)^2/(Ae)\), since \(|\sin(u-e\sin u)-\sin u|\le e\sin u\). Extend \(X\) by its endpoint values outside that interval. It follows that

\[ m\,\frac{D_r(t_n)}{t_n}\ \ge\ \frac{m}{K^2T} =\frac{2m\nu(Ae)^2}{\pi(1+e)^4}>0 . \]

The right side has action units. On the actual orbit \(|\dot X|\le\nu\) and \(D_X(t_n)/t_n\to0\). Thus applying cut positivity in mechanical time would contradict finite-speed motion already for a single orbit. Conditionally joining two independent legs at the observed radius would discard the sign of the radial velocity that Newton carries through the cut. Position and momentum at a classical cut.

What follows from keeping the orbital frequencies

Use a specified additional rule for the auxiliary time. Keep the positive frequencies in the real cosine correlations and give frequency \(j\nu\) the transfer factor \(e^{-j\nu\tau}\) for \(\tau\ge0\). This rule acts on the spectral weights. Substitution of \(i\tau\) for \(t\) in the real cosine would give a hyperbolic cosine. For \(f_j=\cos(j\phi)\), the prescribed correlation is \(C_j(\tau)=\tfrac12e^{-j\nu\tau}\), \(j\ge1\).

There is a classical reflection-positive realization at every subdivision. On the circle take the Poisson semigroup, whose multiplier on \(e^{ij\phi}\) is \(b^{|j|}\) with \(b=e^{-\nu\tau}\). Its density relative to uniform angle is \((1-b^2)/(1-2b\cos\phi+b^2)\). This is a conservative symmetric Markov transfer. Given the middle phase, its reflected past and its future are independent with the same conditional law, so reflected cylinder squares are nonnegative. Its phase makes jumps. The construction uses the orbital frequency and uniform phase, with no phase constant. Stein and Shakarchi, Chapter 2, §5.4 (Poisson kernel and Abel multipliers, passage).

Now suppose the auxiliary transfer is a conservative symmetric Markov semigroup \(P_\tau=e^{-\tau H}\) on a stationary probability space, with a regular, strongly local Dirichlet form \(\mathcal E\). This is the diffusion hypothesis used here. Additional state variables are allowed. Suppose \(X\) has the stated radial law and its first mode has the two correlations \(C_1(s),C_1(2s)\) above at one fixed \(s>0\). Then \(\|P_sX-e^{-\nu s}X\|^2=0\) and the spectral theorem gives \(HX=\nu X\). For bounded \(X\) the energy measure \(d\Gamma(X)\) is normalized by \(\int d\Gamma(X)=\mathcal E(X,X)\). The strongly local chain rule gives

\[ \int h'(X)\,d\Gamma(X) =\mathcal E(X,h(X)) =\nu\int xh(x)\,d\pi_0 =\nu\int(1-x^2)h'(x)\,d\pi_0 . \tag{11} \]

The last equality is integration by parts with the stated arcsine density. Taking primitives of continuous tests identifies the pushforward of the energy measure. Hence for every \(C^1\) function \(g\) on \([-1,1]\), \(\mathcal E(g(X),g(X))=\nu\int(1-x^2)g'(x)^2\,d\pi_0\). Boutet de Monvel, Lenz and Stollmann, pp. 192–193 (regular strongly local forms and energy-measure chain rule, passage).

For \(r=g(X)\) the derivative is finite at both turning points, since \(dr/d\phi=Ae\sin u/(1-e\cos u)\) vanishes linearly there. Writing \(I_r=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p_r\,dr\), (11) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} m\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\frac{\mathbb E[(r_\tau-r_0)^2]}{\tau} &=2m\mathcal E(r,r) =2m\nu\int_0^{2\pi}\left(\frac{dr}{d\phi}\right)^2\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\\ &=\frac{2m}{\nu}\,\overline{\dot r^{\,2}}=2I_r=2(I-J). \end{aligned} \tag{12} \]

The dot and overbar in the last line use the mechanical time and its orbital average. The local circle heat semigroup with generator \(\nu\partial_\phi^2\) realizes the first mode and (12). At \(I=3J/2\) the apparent action is \(J\). It vanishes in the circular limit at fixed \(J\). The local energy density in the physical radius is \(\nu(dr/d\phi)^2\) times the radial probability. It varies around the orbit and tends to zero at the turning points. A homogeneous kinetic coefficient \(a/(2m)\) cannot match it for any constant \(a\). Kepler radial action, equation (11).

There is an exact test with a second radial function. Equation (11) gives \(\mathcal E(Y,Y)=16\nu\int(x^2-x^4)d\pi_0=2\nu\). The two prescribed readings \(C_2(s),C_2(2s)\) instead force \(HY=2\nu Y\), hence \(\mathcal E(Y,Y)=\nu\), since \(\|Y\|^2=1/2\). Thus four exact correlations at two fixed positive lags rule out every such strongly local transfer in the same clock, including hidden variables. The positive Poisson transfer has the prescribed second mode. The local circle heat transfer gives \(e^{-4\nu\tau}\) on it.

A finite physical-time gap from the orbital speeds

The same conflict has a quantitative test in mechanical time if the transfer is to reconstruct the real, even correlation as \(\langle f,\cos(tH)f\rangle\). This is a spectral matching premise for this particular stationary preparation. A Hamiltonian bounded below can still have transitions to lower energies from an excited state.

On the ellipse \(\overline{\dot X^2}=\nu^2/2\) and \(\overline{\dot Y^2}=2\nu^2\). For a reconstructed correlation its negative second derivative at zero is \(\|Hf\|^2\). Impose only the corresponding upper bounds \(\|HX\|\le\nu/\sqrt2\), \(\|HY\|\le\sqrt2\,\nu\), with the same equal-time radial law. These are frequency-moment bounds from physical time, in inverse seconds squared when squared. They allow rough paths in auxiliary time.

Strong locality alone gives \(\mathcal E(Y,Y)=(16/3)\mathcal E(X,X^3)\) by the chain rule, without assuming either mode is an eigenfunction. The second speed bound gives \(\mathcal E(Y,Y)\le\|Y\|\|HY\|\le\nu\). Since \(\int X^4=3/8\), \(\int X^6=5/16\) in the stationary probability,

\[ \|(H-\nu)X\|\,\|X^3\|\ge \nu\int X^4-\langle HX,X^3\rangle\ge 3\nu/16 . \]

Squaring and using the first speed bound gives \(9\nu^2/80\le\|(H-\nu)X\|^2\le\nu^2-2\nu\mathcal E(X,X)\). The mean frequency in the normalized spectral measure of \(X\) is therefore \(2\mathcal E(X,X)\le71\nu/80\). In particular \(2\langle X,P_sX\rangle\ge e^{-71\nu s/80}\) by Jensen’s inequality.

For an entirely real-time reading take \(u_*\) maximizing \((1-\cos u)/u\) on \(u>0\), and put \(t_*=u_*/\nu\). The maximizer is the root of \(\tan(u_*/2)=u_*\) in \((\pi/2,\pi)\). Integrating the inequality \(1-\cos z\le z(1-\cos u_*)/u_*\) against the nonnegative frequency measure gives

\[ 2\langle X,(1-\cos(t_*H))X\rangle \le \frac{71}{80}(1-\cos u_*),\qquad D_X^{\rm Newton}(t_*)=1-\cos u_* . \tag{13} \]

This discrepancy needs neither exact exponential readings nor a limit of readout precision. Its hypotheses are stationarity with the specified radial marginal, the two mechanical speed bounds, strong locality, and the proposed spectral relation between the clocks.

What insertion between two readings tests

Smooth paths can reproduce even the complete two-time laws of the Poisson clock. Draw a uniform phase \(\Phi\) and an independent angular speed \(V\) with Cauchy density \(\nu/[\pi(v^2+\nu^2)]\). Set \(\phi_\tau=\Phi+V\tau\) modulo \(2\pi\), using the same \(V\) throughout. The characteristic function \(\mathbb E e^{ijV\tau}=e^{-|j|\nu|\tau|}\) gives exactly the same pair transition densities at every lag. This is a random parametrization in auxiliary time. Its mean squared speed diverges, whereas the physical Kepler clock has speed \(\nu\). McMullen, Probability Theory, Theorem II.6 (Cauchy characteristic function, passage).

Insert \(Y_0\) between \(X_{-s}\) and \(X_s\). In the smooth process \(X_{-s}X_s=(\cos 2\Phi+\cos 2Vs)/2\), so the three-time mean is \(1/4\). In the Poisson process it is \(e^{-2\nu s}/4\), by conditioning on \(\Phi\) at the cut. In fact full reflection positivity forces the second value given the Poisson two-time laws. Put \(\lambda=e^{-\nu s}\) and \(U=X_s-\lambda X_0\). Its reflected square is zero. Cauchy–Schwarz for the reflected form gives \(\mathbb E[(\Theta U)Y_0X_s]=0\). The pair law gives \(\mathbb E[X_0Y_0X_s]=\lambda/4\). Together they fix the three-time mean. All functions are bounded, and the time-zero variable is also available as a mean-square limit from positive times. Thus retaining the same hidden speed on both legs fails positivity on histories despite matching every pair. This is a test of the composition at a cut in addition to positivity of the two-point kernel.

The separation persists with finite radius and time resolution. Choose the two lags \(u,v\) independently in \([s,2s]\) with \(s=(\log2)/\nu\). For the three marks at \(-u,0,v\), the respective means are \(e^{-\nu|u-v|}/4\ge1/8\) and \(e^{-\nu(u+v)}/4\le1/16\), by the same trigonometric identity and conditional expectation. These bounds hold for any distribution of the lags in the windows. Round each radius in bins of width \(1/(512K)\) before evaluating \(X,Y\). Since \(|X|,|Y|\le1\) and \(|2x^2-2z^2|\le4|x-z|\) there, each three-time mean moves by at most \(6/512\). The resulting intervals remain disjoint. The equality of all pair laws persists under the identical rounding and timing.

Finite accuracy and a local bulk degree of freedom

The exact two-mode obstruction has no positive tolerance uniform over hidden local rates. To see this, add a dimensionless coordinate \(0\le y\le L\) to the angle. Give the cylinder the probability and form

\[ d\pi_\eta=\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\frac{\delta_0(dy)+(\eta/L)\,dy}{1+\eta}, \qquad \mathcal E_\eta(u,u)=\frac{\nu}{1+\eta} \int_0^L\int_0^{2\pi}|\nabla u|^2\,\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,dy . \tag{14} \]

Here \(L,\eta>0\) are dimensionless. Use the periodic \(H^1\) form domain with its Sobolev trace at \(y=0\). For each fixed \(L,\eta\) the form norm is equivalent to the \(H^1\) norm by the trace bound. Smooth functions are dense both in the form domain and uniformly in the continuous functions on the closed cylinder. This gives a conservative regular strongly local form, with exactly uniform angle. The construction uses the harmonic-extension representation of the square root of a Laplacian. Caffarelli and Silvestre, §1 (half-space Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, passage).

For \(j\ge1\) set \(h_j=f_j\cosh(j(L-y))/\cosh(jL)\) and \(\alpha_j=j\nu\tanh(jL)\). The function is harmonic inside and has zero normal derivative at the top. Integration by parts in (14) gives \(H_\eta h_j=0\) in the bulk and \(H_\eta h_j=\alpha_j f_j\) at the bottom. With \(b_\eta=\sqrt{\eta/[2(1+\eta)]}\) this yields \(\|h_j-f_j\|\le b_\eta\) and \(\|(H_\eta-\alpha_j)h_j\|\le\alpha_j b_\eta\) in \(L^2(\pi_\eta)\). The contraction semigroup and Duhamel’s formula then give \(\|P_\tau f_j-e^{-\alpha_j\tau}f_j\|\le3b_\eta\) for all \(\tau\ge0\). Since \(\|f_j\|=1/\sqrt2\) and \(|e^{-\alpha_j\tau}-e^{-j\nu\tau}|\le j\nu/\alpha_j-1\),

\[ \sup_{\tau\ge0,\ j\ge1} \left|2\langle f_j,P_\tau f_j\rangle-e^{-j\nu\tau}\right| \le 3\sqrt{\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}}+\coth L-1 . \tag{15} \]

Thus local continuous processes approach the target two-time correlations to arbitrary accuracy as \(\eta\downarrow0\), \(L\uparrow \infty\). The three-time Poisson mean is approached too, since the cylinder mean is \(\int Y(P_sX)^2\,d\pi_\eta\) and \(P_sX\) approaches \(e^{-\nu s}X\) in norm. The bulk has angular and transverse diffusion coefficient \(\nu L/\eta\). Also \(\mathcal E_\eta(f_j,f_j)=\nu Lj^2/[2(1+\eta)]\). The diverging initial slopes explain how uniform agreement of correlations can coexist with a different limit after division by the time step. In particular this construction cannot keep the mechanical speed bounds used in (13).

5. Consequence for STATE

The authorized Alpoge–Sussman peek is recorded at a fixed commit with selected source coverage. It contributes a concrete fourth-response test and a warning to preserve complete source-dependent densities. The noncircular ellipse supplies a finite force and nonconstant physical-time motion at every refinement. The apparent action (12) belongs to an auxiliary diffusion fitted to the first orbital frequency. Keeping the second frequency calls for a jump transfer or a singular limit of local transfers. The missing physical premise would have to relate the clock and its local kinetic coefficient to Newtonian motion independently of a phase constant. The calculation uses one orbit and supplies no four-dimensional gauge estimate.
