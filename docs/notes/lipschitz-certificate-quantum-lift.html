Lifting a Lipschitz Newton certificate to quantum mechanics

Markdown source · PDF

Result, 2026-10-27; written derivation, unrefereed. A classical kick–drift existence certificate for a one-dimensional conservative force with a global Lipschitz constant supplies the model-dependent estimates for a specified noncommutative quantum construction. For every supplied \(\hbar>0\) it gives the Schrödinger representation, an essentially self-adjoint Hamiltonian, a strongly continuous unitary group, and arbitrary-partition convergence of the quantum kick–drift products. On coherent preparations the same classical polygons and quantum products agree at their vertices to \(O(\sqrt\hbar)\), uniformly in the mesh on a fixed time interval. Together with the classical certificate this gives the requested joint observable limit.

Extension, 2026-11-18 (6.1 Sol Max). On any finite network of exchange-coupled classical spins, the same sequence of rotations that constructs the trajectory gives a quantum product for every supplied action \(h>0\). One estimate controls all simultaneous refinements of time and action on spin-coordinate readouts. Two irrationally related spin lengths admit this construction even though they admit no common action for an exact phase bundle on the given orbits (§6).

This is a bounded instance, not a proof of the universal constructive lifting conjecture formulated below. The input includes the conservative action and a globally Lipschitz force; the background analytic toolkit includes Nelson’s commutator theorem and the graph-domain propagation result cited below. No lower bound on \(V\) or derivatives beyond the Lipschitz force are assumed. This real-time theorem does not construct a Euclidean probability measure for a potential unbounded below.

The loss of the earlier \(O(\hbar)\) rate is necessary at this level of regularity: \(V(q)=q|q|/2\) has globally Lipschitz force, but a centered coherent packet has a momentum error of exact order \(\sqrt\hbar\) at small fixed positive times. No claim of literature priority is made.

Constructive lifting: the conjecture and its quantifiers

A proof may contain mathematical information that its author never expresses in quantum terminology. Under the conjecture, a construction of classical motion would also give enough control to construct quantum evolution from the same mechanical data. A direct classical proof that never mentions a Hilbert space therefore does not by itself refute this interpretation. One must determine whether its assumptions and estimates permit the quantum construction.

Literal textual inclusion would be a much stronger requirement on how people write proofs. Conversely, appending an unrelated quantum existence theorem to any classical proof would make containment uninformative. The intended requirement is an explicit construction whose convergence uses the classical data and estimates. Fix a named background analytic toolkit before the test. Every model-dependent hypothesis needed to apply those theorems must be supplied by the classical inputs or proved from them; adding a new dynamical restriction would weaken the containment claim.

The word consistency also needs an identified object. Logical consistency concerns the absence of contradictions in specified axioms. Existence of a mathematical model can establish relative consistency within the mathematical foundation used to construct it. Existence of motion, uniqueness from initial data, continuous dependence, and continuation for all time are different analytic properties. The present programme addresses these analytic constructions and their relation to quantum dynamics. It has not formulated a reduction between formal arithmetical consistency statements for two axiomatic theories.

A Hilbert-space representation alone would admit a classical example. For a complete Hamiltonian flow preserving phase volume, the operators \((U(t)f)(z)=f(\Phi_{-t}(z))\) form a strongly continuous unitary group on \(L^2\) of phase space. Stone’s theorem gives a self-adjoint generator; multiplying it by any \(h>0\) writes the same group as \(e^{-itH_h/h}\). Multiplication observables commute, and concentrating probability packets recover the classical trajectory. This Koopman representation would satisfy the weaker target without constructing the required noncommutative quantum observables.

Fix a class \(\mathcal A\) of mechanical actions, their state spaces, initial or boundary data, and finite refinement rules. A classical existence certificate means explicit estimates sufficient to construct the classical dynamics. For a polygon proof these may include a common bounded region on each finite time interval, a stability estimate and a force-balance residual tending to zero with the mesh. The admissible certificates must be specified before testing a proposed lift; defining them to include quantum convergence would assume the conclusion.

Constructive lifting conjecture. Fix the action class, certificate schema, quantum target and named background analytic toolkit. One construction procedure for that class takes any admissible mechanical data and classical certificate as input and constructs a compatible quantum family \(Q_\hbar\) for \(\hbar\in E\subset(0,\infty)\) with \(0\in\overline E\). Every model-dependent hypothesis used in the quantum construction must be supplied by those inputs or proved from them. The procedure must identify which certificate estimates control quantum refinement and the recovery of the classical dynamics on the stated preparations and observables. Additional dynamical restrictions cannot be introduced to complete the proof.

The proposed interpretation requires a single construction procedure for the chosen class, rather than a separate unexplained choice for each certificate. A certificate schema must not exclude a counterexample after the test begins. A theorem for one class would establish a bounded lifting result; extending it to every classical existence proof remains the universal conjecture.

Existence at one isolated \(\hbar>0\) cannot itself formulate an \(\hbar\to0\) limit. The lifting statement therefore requires a family indexed by positive values accumulating at zero. Requiring every sufficiently small positive \(\hbar\), or every \(\hbar>0\), strengthens that requirement. The current smooth-potential example constructs the quantum theory for every \(\hbar>0\). This family requirement makes the proposed lifting statement more specific than a bare assertion that some quantum theory exists.

The word compatible requires an observable statement. In the current example, fix \(z_0\), a finite \(T>0\), a time \(t\in[0,T]\) and \(a\in C_c^\infty(T^*\mathbb R)\). For the specified coherent preparations and partition vertices \(t_j\to t\), the required limit is \[ \left\langle\psi_{\hbar,\pi}(t_j), \operatorname{Op}_\hbar^W(a)\psi_{\hbar,\pi}(t_j)\right\rangle \longrightarrow a(\Phi_t(z_0)), \qquad \hbar,|\pi|\longrightarrow0. \tag{C} \] Here \(z_0\) is the initial canonical state, \(\Phi_t\) the constructed classical flow, \(\psi_{\hbar,\pi}(t_j)\) the state after the first \(j\) quantum cells, and \(\operatorname{Op}_\hbar^W\) Weyl quantization. Partitions containing \(t\) permit \(t_j=t\). The limit holds along every joint schedule with \(\hbar,|\pi|\to0\). A different action class may require different preparations, observables and topologies while retaining its specified noncommutative quantum target. Condition (C) does not require convergence of every quantum state to a single classical point.

The common-hypothesis implication \(\mathcal H\Rightarrow[C\ \mathrm{and}\ (\forall\hbar>0)Q_\hbar]\) is weaker than extracting the quantum construction from every certificate of \(C\). The theorem below covers the declared conservative \(C^{1,1}\) class. The smooth quantum construction separately supplies a Euclidean law and an \(O(\hbar)\) mean rate under stronger derivative and lower-bound assumptions; the present rate is sharp at the weaker regularity.

The 1998 stationary-phase test preserves the action differences, Hessian square-root weights and signatures lost by a classical critical-point distribution. Its fixed-partition remainder is not a uniform quantum refinement estimate. Classical completion at a cusp or wall also need not select a unique quantum preparation or boundary condition (cusp, wall). Neither the lifting conjecture nor this bounded theorem derives a universal positive physical action.

1. The input certificate and quantum target

Let \(m>0\), \(V\in C^1(\mathbb R;\mathbb R)\), and suppose \[|V'(x)-V'(y)|\le L|x-y|,\qquad L\ge0.\tag{1}\] Thus \(V\in C^{1,1}\) and \(V''\) exists almost everywhere with \(|V''|\le L\). The input is the mechanical action with kinetic term \(p^2/(2m)\) and potential \(V\), the initial point \(z_0=(q_0,p_0)\), and the classical kick–drift polygons \[ p_{j+1}^{\rm cl}=p_j^{\rm cl}-s_jV'(q_j^{\rm cl}),\qquad q_{j+1}^{\rm cl}=q_j^{\rm cl}+s_jp_{j+1}^{\rm cl}/m.\tag{2} \] Take their linear interpolation for a partition \(\pi\) of \([0,T]\). The usual Lipschitz certificate supplies a unique limit \(z(t)\) and \[\sup_{t\le T}\|z_\pi^{\rm cl}(t)-z(t)\|_\omega \le R_T^{\rm cl}|\pi|,\qquad \|(q,p)\|_\omega=|q|+\frac{|p|}{m\omega}.\tag{3}\] For example, the proof in the ODE lesson uses a common bounded region, force-balance residual and Gronwall stability. Here \(\omega>0\) is a fixed auxiliary unit; \(R_T^{\rm cl}\) depends only on the classical data and \(T\). No quantum convergence is included in the certificate.

For each \(\hbar>0\) set, on \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), \[ \begin{gathered} Qf(q)=qf(q),\quad P=-i\hbar\partial_q,\quad H_\hbar=P^2/(2m)+V(Q),\\ K_{\hbar,s}=e^{-isV(Q)/\hbar},\quad D_{\hbar,s}=e^{-isP^2/(2m\hbar)}. \end{gathered}\tag{4} \] The self-adjoint \(Q,P\) obey the Weyl relations and \([Q,P]=i\hbar I\) on Schwartz space. Unlike the Koopman construction, these observables are noncommutative.

2. The same force bound controls the operator domains

Fix \(\hbar>0\) and calibrated units and put \(N=1+Q^2+P^2\). The input (1) implies \[ |V'(q)|\le |V'(0)|+L|q|,\qquad |V(q)|\le |V(0)|+|V'(0)||q|+\tfrac L2q^2.\tag{5} \] For \(f\in\mathcal S\), integration by parts gives \[ 2\operatorname{Re}\langle Q^2f,P^2f\rangle =2\|QPf\|^2-2\hbar^2\|f\|^2.\tag{5a} \] Together with \(N\ge1\) and \(PQ=QP-i\hbar\), this gives the harmonic-oscillator graph bounds \[ \|Q^2f\|+\|P^2f\|+\|QPf\|+\|PQf\| \le C_\hbar\|Nf\|.\tag{5b} \] These estimates, (5), and the almost-everywhere bound on \(V''\) imply on \(\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\) \[ \|H_\hbar f\|\le C_\hbar\|Nf\|,\qquad \|[H_\hbar,N]f\|\le C_\hbar\|Nf\|.\tag{6} \] The commutator is a weak-derivative identity: \[ [H_\hbar,N] =-\frac{i\hbar}{m}(PQ+QP) +i\hbar\big(V'(Q)P+PV'(Q)\big).\tag{7} \] Products with \(V'\) have the weak derivative \(V''\); no third derivative is used. For \(f\in\mathcal S\), \(H_\hbar f\in D(N)\), so (6) gives \[ |\langle H_\hbar f,N^2f\rangle-\langle N^2f,H_\hbar f\rangle| \le2C_\hbar\langle f,N^2f\rangle.\tag{8} \] Schwartz space is a core for both \(N\) and \(N^2\). The first bound in (6), together with the closedness of the eventual closure, also gives \(D(N)\subset D(\overline H_\hbar)\). Apply Nelson’s commutator theorem with comparison operator \(N^2\): (6) gives its relative bound and (8) its form-commutator bound. It follows that \(H_\hbar\) is essentially self-adjoint on \(\mathcal S\).

Write \(c_\hbar=\inf\sigma(N^2)\) and \(G=N^2-c_\hbar\ge0\). Applying the same estimate to the generator \(H_\hbar/\hbar\) with \(E_0=c_\hbar\), using Energy-Limited Quantum Dynamics, Theorems 3.7–3.8 (statements and hypotheses read), shows that \(U_\hbar(t)=e^{-itH_\hbar/\hbar}\) preserves \(D(N)\) and is strongly continuous in its \(N\)-graph norm. Indeed \(D(\sqrt G)=D(N)\) and \(\|\sqrt G f\|^2+c_\hbar\|f\|^2=\|Nf\|^2\). The constants in (6)–(8) follow from \(m,\hbar,L,V(0),V'(0)\); they are not additional assumptions on the mechanical model.

3. A fixed-action quantum product estimate

The force certificate also controls the commutator of the two factors. Write \(T_\hbar=P^2/(2m)\) and, initially on Schwartz space, \[[T_\hbar,V(Q)] =-\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\big(PV'(Q)+V'(Q)P\big).\tag{9}\] It extends as a bounded map \(D(N)\to L^2\). To justify the product argument without assuming higher derivatives, mollify \(V\) with an even compactly supported unit-mass mollifier. The smooth \(V_n\) satisfy \(\|V_n''\|_\infty\le L\) and have uniformly linearly growing first derivatives. Moreover, Taylor’s inequality gives \[\|V_n-V\|_\infty \le \tfrac12L\,\varepsilon_n^2 \int y^2\rho(y)\,dy\longrightarrow0.\tag{10}\] This uniform bound holds even when \(V\) is unbounded below.

For these \(V_n\), define the finite constants \[ \eta_T=\hbar^{-1}\|[T_\hbar,N]N^{-1}\|,\quad \eta_V=\sup_n\hbar^{-1}\|[V_n(Q),N]N^{-1}\|,\quad B=\sup_n\|[T_\hbar,V_n(Q)]N^{-1}\|.\tag{11} \] Each commutator is initially computed on \(\mathcal S\); the products with \(N^{-1}\) mean their unique bounded extensions from that core. They are finite by (5) and the graph bounds above; their values can be bounded from the classical data \(m,L,V'(0)\) and the chosen \(\hbar\) and units. Use \(\|Nf\|\) as an equivalent \(N\)-graph norm. The induced graph norms of the kinetic, potential and full groups are bounded by \(e^{\eta_T|t|}\), \(e^{\eta_V|t|}\), and \(e^{(\eta_T+\eta_V)|t|}\), respectively.

For smooth \(V_n\), differentiation of \(U_n(s-r)D_rK_{n,r}\) and a second Duhamel integration give, on Schwartz vectors, \[ \begin{aligned} (D_sK_{n,s}-U_n(s))f =-\frac1{\hbar^2}\int_0^s\!dr\int_0^r\!du\, U_n(s-r)D_{r-u}[T_\hbar,V_n(Q)]D_uK_{n,r}f. \end{aligned}\tag{11a} \] For each \(n\), both finite factors preserve \(\mathcal S\), so the unbounded commutator here acts on a vector in its initial domain. Taking norms in (11a) yields \[ \|(D_sK_{n,s}-U_n(s))f\| \le\frac{Bs^2}{2\hbar^2} e^{(\eta_T+\eta_V)s}\|Nf\|.\tag{12} \] The left factors in the integral are unitary; the commutator (9) acts on \(D_uK_{n,r}f\). Density extends (12) to \(D(N)\). Telescoping along the exact \(U_n\) orbit and using the graph bound gives \[ \|U_{n,\pi}(T)f-U_n(T)f\| \le\frac{BT\,e^{(\eta_T+\eta_V)T}}{2\hbar^2} |\pi|\,\|Nf\|,\qquad f\in D(N).\tag{13} \] Equation (10) makes \(U_n(t)\to U_\hbar(t)\) and each finite product \(U_{n,\pi}(T)\to U_{\hbar,\pi}(T)\) in operator norm for fixed \(\hbar,T,\pi\), by the bounded-perturbation Duhamel estimate. The constants in (13) are uniform in \(n\), so (13) passes to \(V\). It proves arbitrary-partition strong convergence for \(f\in D(N)\); density and unitarity extend convergence to all \(L^2\) vectors. More explicitly, if \(C_{\hbar,T}\) is the coefficient of \(|\pi|\|Nf\|\) in (13), then for any \(\psi\in L^2\) \[ r_{\hbar,T,\psi}(\delta):= \inf_{f\in D(N)}\left[2\|\psi-f\| +C_{\hbar,T}\delta\|Nf\|\right] \longrightarrow0\quad(\delta\downarrow0).\tag{13a} \] Unitarity and (13) bound each product-to-unitary difference by \(r_{\hbar,T,\psi}(|\pi|)\); the triangle inequality gives the two-partition Cauchy criterion in the conjecture. The factor \(\hbar^{-2}B\) need not remain bounded as \(\hbar\downarrow0\).

4. The classical and quantum polygons

Use the coherent packet of the quantum-blocks note, equation (16), and let \((q_j,p_j)\) be the means after the first \(j\) quantum cells. The exact identities \[ K_s^*PK_s=P-sV'(Q),\qquad D_s^*QD_s=Q+sP/m\tag{14} \] hold on the propagated states; the \(C^{1,1}\) potential preserves the needed graph domain. In detail, \(D(N)=H^2(\mathbb R)\cap D(Q^2)\), and for \(f\in D(N)\), weak differentiation gives \[ \begin{aligned} (K_s f)''=K_s\bigg(f''-\frac{2is}{\hbar}V'f' -\frac{is}{\hbar}V''f-\frac{s^2}{\hbar^2}(V')^2f\bigg). \end{aligned}\tag{14a} \] Here \(V''\in L^\infty\), \(V'\) grows at most linearly, and the oscillator graph bound gives \(qf',q^2f\in L^2\). Thus \(K_s f\in H^2\) and \(Q^2K_s f=K_sQ^2f\in L^2\). The drift preserves \(D(N)\) by its conjugation of \(Q\) and the \(QP,PQ\) graph bounds. Hence all finite products preserve \(D(N)\). Define \[ W_j=\operatorname{sd}Q_j+\frac{\operatorname{sd}P_j}{m\omega}, \quad C=\sqrt{\frac2{m\omega}},\quad \beta=\omega+\frac{L}{m\omega}.\tag{15} \] As in the earlier note, (1) and (14) give \[W_j\le C\sqrt\hbar\,e^{\beta t_j}.\tag{16}\] No higher potential derivative or assumption of Gaussian shape after the first step is used.

Put \(e_j=\|(q_j,p_j)-(q_j^{\rm cl},p_j^{\rm cl})\|_\omega\). For each kick, \(|\langle V'(Q)\rangle-V'(q_j^{\rm cl})| \le L(\operatorname{sd}Q_j+|q_j-q_j^{\rm cl}|)\). The subsequent drift yields \[ e_{j+1}\le (1+s_j\omega)\!\left(1+\frac{s_jL}{m\omega}\right)e_j +\frac{s_jL}{m\omega}(1+s_j\omega)W_j.\tag{17} \] Since \(e_0=0\), (16) and \(1+x\le e^x\) imply the mesh-uniform bound \[ e_j\le \frac{LC}{m\omega}\,t_j e^{\beta t_j}\sqrt\hbar. \tag{18} \] Combining this with the classical certificate (3) gives \[ \|(q_j,p_j)-z(t_j)\|_\omega \le \frac{LC}{m\omega}T e^{\beta T}\sqrt\hbar +R_T^{\rm cl}|\pi|.\tag{19} \] For \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\), the Fourier/Weyl argument in the groupoid note, before its second-order refinement, bounds the expectation error by a symbol-dependent constant times the sum of \(W_j\) and (19). Therefore the observable limit required by the conjecture holds at all partition vertices and along every joint schedule \(\hbar,|\pi|\to0\).

The proof uses the classical input bound (1) for both the graph and packet estimates, while the certificate’s polygon error (3) controls recovery of the classical flow. The graph estimate (13) is a separate fixed-\(\hbar\) quantum estimate derived using the declared operator toolkit; it is not assumed in (3). The input includes the global action and its force bound, not merely the numerical convergence rate of one orbit. A single trajectory need not determine \(V\) elsewhere, so (13) is not claimed to follow from (3) alone.

5. Why the square-root rate cannot generally be improved

Set \(m=1\) and \[V(q)=\tfrac12q|q|,\qquad V'(q)=|q|.\tag{20}\] The force is globally Lipschitz, and the classical solution from \((0,0)\) is uniquely stationary. Define the unitary dilation \((D_\hbar f)(q)=\hbar^{-1/4}f(q/\sqrt\hbar)\). Homogeneity gives \[D_\hbar^{-1}H_\hbar D_\hbar=\hbar H_1,\qquad D_\hbar^{-1}P_\hbar D_\hbar=\sqrt\hbar\,P_1.\tag{21}\] For the centered Gaussian \(D_\hbar g_1\), the exact quantum momentum is therefore \(\langle P_\hbar\rangle_t=\sqrt\hbar\,f(t)\), where \(f(t)=\langle P_1\rangle_t\). Ehrenfest’s identity gives \[f'(0)=-\langle |Q|\rangle_{g_1}=-1/\sqrt\pi.\tag{22}\] Thus \(f(t)\ne0\) for every sufficiently small fixed \(t>0\). The mean error is exactly a nonzero multiple of \(\sqrt\hbar\) there. This counterexample does not contradict the \(O(\hbar)\) theorem for bounded \(V'''\): \(V'\) here has a corner at the origin.

6. A constructive lift for exchange-coupled spins

Fix a finite graph, one spin \(\mathbf z_i\) of length \(s_i>0\) at each vertex, constant fields \(\boldsymbol\omega_i\) and real symmetric edge couplings \(\lambda_{ik}\). Specify the initial directions and a fixed order of the edges. The mechanical action consists of the local spin actions on these spheres and the Hamiltonian \[ H_0=\sum_i\boldsymbol\omega_i\cdot\mathbf z_i,\qquad H_{ik}=\lambda_{ik}\mathbf z_i\cdot\mathbf z_k,\qquad H=H_0+\sum_{\{i,k\}}H_{ik}. \] Use \(\{z_i^a,z_k^b\}=\delta_{ik}\epsilon^{abc}z_i^c\), so the field at \(i\) generates \(\dot{\mathbf z}_i=\boldsymbol\omega_i\times\mathbf z_i\). The \(s_i\) and the trial reduced action \(h\) have action units, the fields have units of inverse time, and \(\lambda_{ik}\) has units of inverse action times inverse time. The Hamiltonian has energy units. Directions, graph and edge order are dimensionless.

Classical certificate. On each time interval of length \(\delta\), apply the field rotations for \(\delta\), then each edge flow for \(\delta\) in the specified order. For one edge the sum \(\mathbf z_i+\mathbf z_k\) is conserved. Both spins rotate with angular velocity \(\lambda_{ik}(\mathbf z_i+\mathbf z_k)\), including the identity rotation when the sum vanishes. This makes every cell explicit. Write \(s_*=\max_i s_i\), \(B=2s_*\), \(W=\max_{i,a}|\omega_i^a|\) and \(\Lambda=\sum_{\{i,k\}}|\lambda_{ik}|\). In the maximum coordinate norm the sums of the individual Lipschitz and speed bounds on the product of closed spin balls of radius \(B\) are bounded respectively by \[ L=2W+4B\Lambda,\qquad V=2BW+2B^2\Lambda . \] Each component of a cross product contains two products. For one edge, changing either factor gives the Lipschitz bound \(4B|\lambda_{ik}|\). All subflows and the exact flow preserve the individual lengths. During one cell every intermediate point is within \(V\delta\) of its starting point. Integrating each subfield against that displacement bounds its residual from the initial tangent, summed over the cell, by \(LV\delta^2\). The full flow has the same residual bound. Cell stability is at most \(e^{L\delta}\). Thus, at every vertex \(t_j\) of a partition \(\pi\) of \([0,T]\), \[ \|z_\pi(t_j)-z(t_j)\|_\infty \le 2LVT e^{LT}|\pi|. \tag{23} \]

Quantum construction. For each supplied \(h>0\) set \(j_i=\lceil s_i/h\rceil\) and take the tensor product of the ordinary spin-\(j_i\) representations. Use \(S_i^a=hJ_i^a\), where the \(J_i^a\) are dimensionless generators. At each site \([S_i^a,S_i^b]=ih\epsilon^{abc}S_i^c\); distinct sites commute. Every \(j_i\ge1\). Prepare the product \(\psi\) of highest-weight coherent states in the given directions. Define \(\widehat H_\ell\) by substituting \(\mathbf S\) in each field or edge Hamiltonian. In the classical cell order apply \(\exp(-i\delta\widehat H_\ell/h)\), writing \(U_{h,\pi}(t_j)\) for the accumulated product. All matrices are self-adjoint generators or unitary propagators as appropriate. At fixed \(h\) the first-order unitary product bound gives \[ \left\|U_{h,\pi}(t_j)-e^{-it_j\sum_\ell\widehat H_\ell/h}\right\| \le \frac{T|\pi|}{2h^2} \sum_{\ell<k}\|[\widehat H_\ell,\widehat H_k]\| . \tag{24} \] This follows cell by cell from the bounded-operator estimate of Childs et al., Proposition 15 (reading: passage). It gives norm convergence for every fixed \(h\). Its \(h\) dependence requires a separate estimate for the joint limit.

Joint observable estimate. Take \(0<h\le s_*\), so \(\|S_i^a\|\le hj_i\le B\). During each substep compare the Heisenberg coordinates \(S_i^a(u)=U(u)^*S_i^a U(u)\) with the corresponding classical subflow \(z_i^a(u)\), starting from the same preceding cell history. Put \[ D(u)=\max_{i,a}\|(S_i^a(u)-z_i^a(u))\psi\|,\qquad d_h=\max_i\sqrt{h^2j_i+(hj_i-s_i)^2}. \] The coherent-state mean \(hj_i\mathbf n_i\) and Casimir \(h^2j_i(j_i+1)\) give \(D(0)\le d_h\) by summing the three squared errors at a site. These are the usual rotated highest-weight states. See Arecchi et al. (reading: abstract, for the preparation).

The exact Heisenberg equations have the same coordinate expressions as the classical equations in every substep. For an edge this uses commutativity between distinct sites. In each product the discrepancy is \[ S_k^b(u)(S_i^c(u)-z_i^c(u)) +z_i^c(u)(S_k^b(u)-z_k^b(u)). \] Applied to \(\psi\) its norm is at most \(2B D(u)\). Integrating the two terms of each cross-product component therefore gives \(D(u)\le D(0)+4B|\lambda_{ik}|\int_0^u D(v)\,dv\) with time reset at the start of that substep. The field step gives the same inequality with \(2W\). Grönwall over the actual sequence gives \(D(t_j)\le e^{LT}d_h\) relative to the split classical trajectory, independently of how many cells the partition has. Combining with (23), any ordered word \(P\) of degree \(r\ge1\) in the spin coordinates satisfies \[ \left|\langle U_{h,\pi}(t_j)\psi,P(\mathbf S)U_{h,\pi}(t_j)\psi\rangle -P(z(t_j))\right| \le rB^{r-1} e^{LT}\bigl(d_h+2LVT|\pi|\bigr). \tag{25} \] Indeed, replace the factors from right to left, leaving bounded operators to the left of each difference and scalars to its right. This also covers polynomial readouts by linearity and their self-adjoint combinations. Since \(d_h^2\le h s_*+2h^2\), (25) holds along every joint schedule \(h,|\pi|\to0\) and at vertices tending to any specified time. The large-spin classical dynamics has established antecedents, including the Egorov theorem of Fröhlich, Knowles and Lenzmann (reading: abstract). Here (23) and (25) use the same subfields and stability constants for an arbitrary finite graph and unequal spin lengths. The estimates are finite-time, finite-network statements.

Which compatibility can the certificate supply? Fix two sites with \(s_2=\sqrt 2\,s_1\) and a nonzero exchange coupling. Equation (25) still applies for every small \(h\). The stronger target of exact \(U(1)\) phase transport with the original orbit curvature divided by \(h\) requires \(2s_i/h\in\mathbb Z\) at both sites. The two periods exclude every common \(h>0\), even a sparse sequence tending to zero. This is the two-sphere period test (reading: passage). The construction above instead realizes the mean lengths \(hj_i\) in its preparations, with \(0\le hj_i-s_i<h\). The readouts converge to those of the specified classical system. Exact holonomy at finite \(h\) would add information beyond that observable limit. Neither target selects a positive physical \(\hbar\).

7. Consequence for STATE

The bounded certificate-to-quantum test is affirmative for the specified one-dimensional conservative \(C^{1,1}\) action class. It constructs the noncommutative target and a quantum product estimate from the classical force data and the named analytic toolkit, and uses the classical polygon certificate in the observable limit. Finite exchange networks supply a second action class with a constructive lift, including irrationally related orbit lengths. Requiring exact orbit phases would exclude the same example. The universal observable-lifting conjecture, singular completion and the Newtonian necessity of a positive physical action remain open.
