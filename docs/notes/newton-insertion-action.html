Newton’s comparison: areas, action defects and controlled reunion

Markdown source · PDF

For horizontal speed \(v>0\) and downward force \(F>0\), Galileo’s inertial line and falling parabola enclose area \(A=vF\tau^3/(6m)\), with the exact conversion

\[\boxed{(3F/v)A=\tau\Delta E,\qquad\Delta E=F^2\tau^2/(2m).}\]

The matched-endpoint chord lens is half that area and has action defect \(F^2\tau^3/(24m)=\tau\Delta E/12\). A genuine receding-centre limit preserves this chord defect but cancels the apparent tangent-sector remainder. A specified \(F,-F,F\) force loop reunites both position and velocity and gives an exact scalar quantum phase for every common motional preparation, once quantum kinematics and coherent control are supplied. None of these geometric or phase statements establishes independent positive-action necessity.

For the short-time force measurement in Lemma X, two bounded, increasing forces can differ at release yet give the same final motion and even the same forced-particle propagator at the first reading. A bound on how fast the force changes restores uniform recovery with sufficient differential position accuracy. A separate test using three position probabilities gives a conditional lower bound on the trial action, with an explicit allowance for a recoiling source. For two trapped ions there is already a measured spin signal. Its attribution to a force transmitted through the ions’ motion admits a quantitative test using the optical wavelength and the trap clock. For a circular orbit, three direct angle readings on separately prepared pairs give a test that tolerates finite ruler and clock resolution. A classical impulse model reproduces the ideal mean fluorescence of a published rotor echo. For a single harmonically trapped ion, three published fluorescence contrasts give \(1.768\) against a classical ceiling of \(1.5\) for an additive position readout. A zero-mean probe impulse can reproduce the three contrasts. Direct position exposures would test the same inequality with an independent force calibration.

Four releases of a bound pair into different central forces can bound its initial energy spread and test Newtonian motion from final speeds alone. Two measured hydrogen fragment spectra already test how the capture process samples the orbit. Four central-force releases and hydrogen fragment counts. For an inverse-square attraction, a binding-energy tag and one radius count distinguish a factor of two in trial action. The measured bare lithium fraction after helium-6 decay tests how often another force must supply work when the central attraction increases.

This treatment integrates the constant-force derivation and its receding-centre correction with the controlled reunion. Written model/coordinator checks are internal; the mechanics and phase ingredients are established and no literature-priority claim is made.

The source geometry and three different areas

Take \(m,F,v,\tau>0\), downward \(y\), and

\[q_I(t)=(vt,0),\qquad q_F(t)=(vt,Ft^2/(2m)),\quad0\le t\le\tau.\]

The inertial–fall region is closed by the vertical segment at \(x=v\tau\). Direct integration gives \(A=vF\tau^3/(6m)\), sagitta \(s=F\tau^2/(2m)\) and deterministic transverse kinetic gain \(\Delta E=Fs=F^2\tau^2/(2m)\). Total mechanical energy is conserved; the potential loses that amount. This \(\Delta E\) is not a quantum energy standard deviation.

Lemma X and the measurement of initial force

In Book I, Lemma X, Newton changes vi regulari in the London 1687 edition to Vi finita in the Cambridge 1713 edition, specifying a force that is constant, continually increasing or continually decreasing. In the new Corollary 4 he infers force from initial displacement divided by time squared, as a proportion. Both proofs still represent velocities as ordinates, identify displacement with the area and invoke Lemma IX. The 1687 proof of IX already constructs a similar figure. The 1713 proof explicitly holds the length \(Ae\) fixed (manente longitudine Ae) while the original points approach \(A\). After X, the new Corollary 5 inverts the same proportion for time squared, and the ensuing Scholium explains what proportionality between quantities of different kinds means. The fixed segment belongs to the geometric construction. Its scale on a diagram supplies no physical resolution for a moving body. 1687, Lemmas IX–X and 1713, Lemmas IX–X, corollaries and Scholium (diplomatic transcriptions, passages).

Newton explains his decision to defer publication in the preface to the 1687 edition. Halley urged him to communicate his orbital proof. Newton says he postponed publication to investigate more subjects together, explicitly including the measurement of gravity and other forces. These passages connect force measurement to the delay and physical construction to the proof method. Two forces hidden before the first reading. Fix a force unit \(F_*>0\) and any interval of duration \(s>0\) between release and the first positive observation. Write \(u=t/s\) and define on \([0,1]\)

\[ \begin{gathered} H(u)=u^4(1-u)^6/24,\qquad h=H^{(4)},\\ \eta=\frac{1}{2(1+\|h\|_\infty+\|h'\|_\infty)},\qquad f_\pm(t)=F_*[1+u\pm\eta h(u)]. \end{gathered} \]

Both forces are positive and strictly increasing. Their slopes are at least \(F_*/(2s)\), and \(h(0)=1\), \(h(1)=h'(1)=0\). Continue both by \(F_*(3-\exp(1-u))\) for \(u\ge1\). They are continuously differentiable, bounded by \(3F_*\), and differ at release by \(2\eta F_*\) independently of \(s\). Four integrations by parts give \(\int_0^1u^j h(u)\,du=0\) for \(0\le j\le3\), since \(H\) and its first three derivatives vanish at both ends. The first two moments match the endpoint impulse and displacement for every common initial state. Within the window the position difference is \(2\eta F_*s^2 H''(u)/m\) and afterwards it is zero. Thus even a continuous position record with fixed positive error can be shared by choosing \(s\) small enough and taking the midpoint of the two records.

The pair also tests phase-sensitive readout. For the prescribed Hamiltonians \(p^2/(2m)-f_\pm(t)q\) with a common scalar-potential zero, the general force propagator identity (Theorem G7(a), passage) reduces endpoint evolution to two kicks and the scalar action \(\langle f,G_s f\rangle/(2m)\), where \(G_s\) is the Dirichlet inverse of \(-d^2/dt^2\) on \([0,s]\). The kicks agree by the first two moments. With \(b(t)=1+t/s\), the difference of the two scalar actions is \((2\eta F_*^2/m)\int_0^s h(t/s)(G_s b)(t)\,dt\). Since \(G_s b\) is a cubic polynomial in \(t\), the remaining moments make this integral zero. The two body propagators are identical, including their phase, for every supplied reduced action \(a>0\). This covers endpoint interference against a common reference. A record of the drive or an intervention within the interval is additional data. At each smaller first gate the construction chooses a different pair. It proves failure of a uniform force measurement over Newton’s finite monotone class and preserves the pointwise limit for each fixed force.

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-18. The decay of one-electron \(^{6}{\rm He}^{+}\) increases the nuclear charge from \(2e\) to \(3e\). It is an actual escape count in a system with a stronger final attraction. Their quantum calculation gives \(0.02322\) for their acceptance. The classical question is how much energy an escaping electron can receive from the emitted beta and other forces during the change. Couratin et al., Eqs. (1)–(5) (passages) and apparatus, cuts and energy calibration.

Let \(k=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)\), \(Z=2\), and \(B>0\) be the initial electron binding energy in \(-Zk/r\). Write \(\mu_i,\mu_f\) for the reduced electron masses and \(\boldsymbol p\) for the initial relative momentum. Work in the parent centre-of-mass frame. Write \(\boldsymbol q\) for the impulse from the nuclear decay and \(\boldsymbol V=\boldsymbol q/M_f\), where \(M_f\) is the daughter nuclear mass. If the spectator receives no impulse at the vertex, its final relative momentum is \(\boldsymbol p-\mu_f\boldsymbol V\). This follows by subtracting its share of the total recoil momentum. It accounts for the change of reduced mass even on an orbit with large initial speed. For a point charge switching instantaneously the new relative energy is

\[ \begin{aligned} E_f={}&-(1+1/Z)B+\frac{|\boldsymbol V|^2}{2}(\mu_f+d^{-1}) -\frac d2\left|\boldsymbol p+\frac{\boldsymbol V}{d}\right|^2,\\ d={}&(1/Z-g)/\mu_i,\qquad g=\mu_i/\mu_f-1=\mu_i(1/M_f-1/M_i). \end{aligned} \]

This follows by substituting \(Zk/r=B+|\boldsymbol p|^2/(2\mu_i)\) in the final energy. The daughter is lighter and \(\mu_i/M_f<10^{-4}\), so \(0\le g<10^{-4}\). A charge-state designation alone leaves excited parents possible.

Finite time to uncover the daughter charge. Allow the emitted beta charge to occupy an outgoing spherical shell of arbitrary increasing radius \(L(t)\), initially zero, around the coasting daughter. Its potential energy with the spectator is \(k/\max(r,L)\). The reference potential is therefore

\[ U(r,t)=-\frac{(Z+1)k}{r}+\frac{k}{\max(r,L(t))}, \qquad \partial_t U=-\frac{k\dot L}{L^2}\,{\bf1}_{r<L}\le0 . \]

\[ -B+E_R+gT_i+2\sqrt{(1+g)E_R T_i}+W . \]

This sign follows along the actual path and allows the electron to move throughout the field change. A measured outgoing beta direction defines an individual nonspherical force which belongs in \(W\). Averaging its source over directions before solving for a trajectory changes the escape problem.

The trajectory test. For any two real phases \(X,Y\) the real part of the three-point characteristic function obeys

\[ \cos X+\cos Y-\cos(X+Y)\le 3/2 . \tag{4} \]

This is the three-point Bochner positivity test, obtained by taking the squared modulus of \(1-e^{iX}-e^{-iY}\). Averaging retains the bound for every joint distribution. Even a common affine electronic readout \(c\mapsto g c+c_0\) preserves the ceiling when \(g\ge0\) and \(g+|c_0|\le1\), since \(W\mapsto gW+c_0\). Finite characteristic-function tests of Wigner positivity, including tests with conditional displacements and a qubit readout, are established. Here the question is whether the ion’s probe reads the same mechanical phases that these inequalities constrain. Narcowich and O’Connell, Mari et al. and Zaw (abstracts).

A force law gives (4) a direct mechanical interpretation. Choose one Cartesian coordinate of a central Hooke orbit with inertial mass \(m\) and angular frequency \(\omega\). Set the origin at the force centre. Expose one independently chosen terminal gate per release at \(t_0,t_0+\pi/(4\omega),t_0+\pi/(2\omega)\). Let the positions be \(x_-,x_m,x_+\). For every harmonic trajectory \(x_-+x_+=\sqrt{2}\,x_m\). Measure cosines of \(kx_-,kx_+\) and \(\sqrt{2}kx_m\) respectively, with \(k=2\pi/\Lambda\) set by a ruler pitch \(\Lambda\). Choosing the phase origin fixes which two quarter-period projections are the authors’ axes. The construction requires the same release law at all gates. It allows arbitrary initial position–velocity correlations. If the three gates have the same distribution of clock offsets independent of the release, their marginals can be coupled using one common offset. A centred Gaussian position error of the same width \(\ell\) at each gate preserves (4) for the raw cosines. Couple independent endpoint errors \(\xi,\zeta\) of width \(\ell\) to the middle error \((\xi+\zeta)/\sqrt{2}\), of the same width.

Newton’s force measurement sets a tolerance here. Suppose independent displacement measurements bound \(|\ddot x+\omega^2 x|\le b_F\) on the admitted trajectories. About the middle gate the sine response of the oscillator integrated on each side gives

\[ |x_-+x_+-\sqrt{2}x_m|\le (2-\sqrt{2})b_F/\omega^2 . \]

Thus the maximum excess attributable to the force error is \(|k|(2-\sqrt{2})b_F/\omega^2\). To compare canonical models on the same footing, let \(a>0\) be the trial reduced action and take a common midpoint state, a self-adjoint harmonic-plus-potential evolution and a bounded perturbation of slope at most \(mb_F\). The propagated harmonic endpoint mode translates the perturbation’s argument by a distance \(|ak\sin(\omega u)|/(m\omega)\) at lag \(u\). The commutator divided by \(a\) is bounded by \(|k|b_F|\sin(\omega u)|/\omega\). The two endpoint Duhamel integrals give the same combined tolerance. The Gaussian error only contracts each Fourier mode. Remaining timing, selection and readout errors need their own bounds. With direct Gaussian-blurred positions a verified excess beyond the errors would require \(a>2m\omega\ell^2\) in this family. Smoothing the midpoint Wigner distribution with widths \(\ell,m\omega\ell\) gives a positive joint law for every state when \(a\le2m\omega\ell^2\), by the Gaussian smoothing result used above. All these statements survive refinement between the physical gates.

A force transmitted between two ions

Historical experimental application before the 18 November scope reset.

In a modern test of the force measurement, give ion 2 a small transverse impulse \(J\) and measure ion 1’s displacement at time \(t\). For two equal inertial masses \(m\) in a harmonic trap the impulse response is

\[ \chi_{12}(t)=\frac{\delta x_1(t)}{J} =\frac{1}{2m}\left(\frac{\sin\omega_C t}{\omega_C} -\frac{\sin\omega_R t}{\omega_R}\right). \]

Here \(C,R\) denote the in-phase and relative modes. The displacements of the two modes initially cancel at ion 1. The first surviving term is \(-(\omega_C^2-\omega_R^2)t^3/(12m)\). The force gradient between the ions is measurable through this delayed response. Newton’s Lemma X concerns displacement of the body on which the force acts directly. This extension specifies where a second body can register the force. The impulse can be calibrated as \(m\) times an observed change in velocity. Alternatively, the two periods fix \(m\chi_{12}\). Reciprocal mechanical calibration (passage, mass normalization).

An observed spin readout. Wong-Campos et al. prepared both ion spins by optical pumping and a microwave rotation, applied ten optical kicks, and used microwave analysis and separate fluorescence detectors. They reported Bell fidelity \(F_{\rm obs}=0.76(1)\) and parity contrast \(0.69(1)\). The fidelity is half the sum of the even-spin population and the contrast. Separable spins have Bell fidelity at most \(1/2\) for trusted local spin analysis. The reported uncertainties are statistical. Wong-Campos et al., Figs. 1–2 and Eqs. For the norm estimate take the commutators bounded, and the real linear combinations and partial sums self-adjoint on closure from a common invariant core on which their unitary groups permit Duhamel differentiation. No scalar commutator is required.

Replace the ordered product on each branch by \(e^{i\sum_n B_n}\). With \(L_\mu=\kappa\sum_n s_n Q_\mu(t_n)/\sqrt2\), the replacement applies local spin rotations with angles given by the two commuting observables \(L_C+L_R\) and \(L_C-L_R\). Controlled reunion of the inertial and falling arms

Keep a reference arm at zero transverse force and apply to the signal arm

\[f(t)=\begin{cases}F,&0<t<\tau,\\-F,&\tau<t<3\tau,\\ F,&3\tau<t<4\tau.\end{cases}\]

Both begin at \(y=\dot y=0\). This is branch-controlled motion; gravity needs compensation on the reference and return stages. Define \(P(t)=\int_0^tf\), \(Y(t)=m^{-1}\int_0^tP\). Then \(P(4\tau)=Y(4\tau)=0\); \(P/F\) is respectively \(t\), \(2\tau-t\) and \(t-4\tau\). Direct integration gives

\[\int_0^{4\tau}P^2dt=\tfrac43F^2\tau^3,\qquad \int_0^{4\tau}Ydt=2F\tau^3/m,\qquad A_{\rm loop}=2vF\tau^3/m=12A_{\rm inertial,fall}.\]

\(Y\ge0\) peaks at \(F\tau^2/m\) at \(2\tau\). Using \(m\ddot Y=f\) and the vanishing boundary term \(mY\dot Y\),

\[\Delta S_{\rm loop}=-\frac1{2m}\int P^2dt =-\frac{2F^2\tau^3}{3m}=-\frac F{3v}A_{\rm loop} =-\frac43\tau\Delta E.\]

The sign and coefficient differ from the off-shell chord defect because return forces are part of this experiment. Its exact comparison with the original falling lens is \(|\Delta S_{\rm loop}|=4FA_{\rm inertial,fall}/v\).

\[[B(t),B(s)]=\frac{if(t)f(s)(t-s)}{m\hbar}.\]
