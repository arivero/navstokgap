Newton’s comparison: areas, action defects and controlled reunion

Markdown source · PDF

For horizontal speed \(v>0\) and downward force \(F>0\), Galileo’s inertial line and falling parabola enclose area \(A=vF\tau^3/(6m)\), with the exact conversion

\[\boxed{(3F/v)A=\tau\Delta E,\qquad\Delta E=F^2\tau^2/(2m).}\]

The matched-endpoint chord lens is half that area and has action defect \(F^2\tau^3/(24m)=\tau\Delta E/12\). A genuine receding-centre limit preserves this chord defect but cancels the apparent tangent-sector remainder. A specified \(F,-F,F\) force loop reunites both position and velocity and gives an exact scalar quantum phase for every common motional preparation, once quantum kinematics and coherent control are supplied. None of these geometric or phase statements establishes independent positive-action necessity.

For the short-time force measurement in Lemma X, two bounded, increasing forces can differ at release yet give the same final motion and even the same forced-particle propagator at the first reading. A bound on how fast the force changes restores uniform recovery with sufficient differential position accuracy. A separate test using three position probabilities gives a conditional lower bound on the trial action, with an explicit allowance for a recoiling source. For two trapped ions there is already a measured spin signal. Its attribution to a force transmitted through the ions’ motion admits a quantitative test using the optical wavelength and the trap clock.

This treatment integrates the constant-force derivation and its receding-centre correction with the controlled reunion. Written model/coordinator checks are internal; the mechanics and phase ingredients are established and no literature-priority claim is made.

The source geometry and three different areas

Take \(m,F,v,\tau>0\), downward \(y\), and

\[q_I(t)=(vt,0),\qquad q_F(t)=(vt,Ft^2/(2m)),\quad0\le t\le\tau.\]

The inertial–fall region is closed by the vertical segment at \(x=v\tau\). Direct integration gives \(A=vF\tau^3/(6m)\), sagitta \(s=F\tau^2/(2m)\) and deterministic transverse kinetic gain \(\Delta E=Fs=F^2\tau^2/(2m)\). Total mechanical energy is conserved; the potential loses that amount. This \(\Delta E\) is not a quantum energy standard deviation.

Region Positive ordinary area
Inertial horizontal line to falling parabola \(vF\tau^3/(6m)\)
Matched-endpoint chord to falling parabola \(vF\tau^3/(12m)\)
Inertial line to endpoint chord \(vF\tau^3/(4m)\)

The first is twice the second; the third is their sum. Kepler sectors about a force centre are another construction. A horizontal Galilean boost to speed \(v\) removes the plotted spatial area, while a matched-endpoint action difference remains: the common horizontal action cancels. The conversion therefore specifies a frame with \(v\ne0\).

Lemma X and the measurement of initial force

In Book I, Lemma X, Newton changes vi regulari in the London 1687 edition to Vi finita in the Cambridge 1713 edition, specifying a force that is constant, continually increasing or continually decreasing. In the new Corollary 4 he infers force from initial displacement divided by time squared, as a proportion. Both proofs still represent velocities as ordinates, identify displacement with the area and invoke Lemma IX. The 1687 proof of IX already constructs a similar figure. The 1713 proof explicitly holds the length \(Ae\) fixed (manente longitudine Ae) while the original points approach \(A\). After X, the new Corollary 5 inverts the same proportion for time squared, and the ensuing Scholium explains what proportionality between quantities of different kinds means. The fixed segment belongs to the geometric construction. Its scale on a diagram supplies no physical resolution for a moving body. 1687, Lemmas IX–X and 1713, Lemmas IX–X, corollaries and Scholium (diplomatic transcriptions, passages).

Newton explains his decision to defer publication in the preface to the 1687 edition. Halley urged him to communicate his orbital proof. Newton says he postponed publication to investigate more subjects together, explicitly including the measurement of gravity and other forces. Earlier in that preface he treats accurate construction of a line or circle as something geometry asks of mechanics. He attributes ordinary errors to the practitioner. These passages connect force measurement to the delay and physical construction to the proof method. Newton, 1687 author’s preface (diplomatic transcription, passages on practical mechanics and publication).

Newton’s retrospective notes on his correspondence with Cotes place the printing of the sheets through page 224 before 15 April 1710, Old Style. Lemma X is on pp. 29–30 of those sheets. The same draft describes a later objection by N. Bernoulli to a different problem in Book II. Newton thus dates the printing of the early sheets. What the marks have to resolve. Read positions \(y\) against an inertial ruler at the calibrated times \(0,\tau/2,\tau\). Let \(D_\tau y=y(0)-2y(\tau/2)+y(\tau)\). This cancels an unknown initial position and velocity, including a common affine ruler drift. For \(m q''=f(t)\) with continuous \(f\), the normalized second divided difference averages \(f\) with weight \(4\min(t,\tau-t)/\tau^2\) on the window. This weight integrates to one and has mean time \(\tau/2\). De Boor, §9, Eqs. (47)–(48) (passage, Peano kernel). Thus a single fixed force gives its initial value in the limit. For a class with an independently calibrated bound \(|f(t)-f(0)|\le Lt\) and differential record error \(|D_\tau y-D_\tau q|\le\rho_\tau\),

\[ \left|\frac{4m}{\tau^2}D_\tau y-f(0)\right| \le \frac{L\tau}{2}+\frac{4m\rho_\tau}{\tau^2}. \]

Here \(L\) has units force/time and \(\rho_\tau\) is a length. Calibration of the gate times or their contribution to \(\rho_\tau\) is part of the protocol. Uniform recovery follows if \(m\rho_\tau/\tau^2\to0\). For a specified force accuracy it suffices to keep the right side below that accuracy. The mass \(m\) is calibrated by inertia. To see the distinction from Lemma IX’s fixed comparison length, fix a clock interval \(T>0\) and magnify the recorded difference by \((T/\tau)^2\). For constant \(f\), its signal is now \(fT^2/(4m)\) and its input error bound is \((T/\tau)^2\rho_\tau\). Magnifying the diagram supplies no improvement in force accuracy. A transducer acting before the position error enters would be an additional physical operation. Classical mechanics allows arbitrarily accurate marks. For linear three-meter measurements of a free mass under a prescribed force, the same difference removes the initial test-body variables. Measuring a suitable combination of the middle pointer’s position and momentum also cancels its recoil from the output, at a cost in meter preparation. This prevents inferring an irreducible record error from the test mass’s initial spread alone. Braginsky et al., §II B, Eqs. (2.21)–(2.24), (2.29)–(2.33) (passage).

Two forces hidden before the first reading. Fix a force unit \(F_*>0\) and any interval of duration \(s>0\) between release and the first positive observation. Write \(u=t/s\) and define on \([0,1]\)

\[ \begin{gathered} H(u)=u^4(1-u)^6/24,\qquad h=H^{(4)},\\ \eta=\frac{1}{2(1+\|h\|_\infty+\|h'\|_\infty)},\qquad f_\pm(t)=F_*[1+u\pm\eta h(u)]. \end{gathered} \]

Both forces are positive and strictly increasing. Their slopes are at least \(F_*/(2s)\), and \(h(0)=1\), \(h(1)=h'(1)=0\). Continue both by \(F_*(3-\exp(1-u))\) for \(u\ge1\). They are continuously differentiable, bounded by \(3F_*\), and differ at release by \(2\eta F_*\) independently of \(s\). Four integrations by parts give \(\int_0^1u^j h(u)\,du=0\) for \(0\le j\le3\), since \(H\) and its first three derivatives vanish at both ends. The first two moments match the endpoint impulse and displacement for every common initial state. Within the window the position difference is \(2\eta F_*s^2 H''(u)/m\) and afterwards it is zero. Thus even a continuous position record with fixed positive error can be shared by choosing \(s\) small enough and taking the midpoint of the two records.

The pair also tests phase-sensitive readout. For the prescribed Hamiltonians \(p^2/(2m)-f_\pm(t)q\) with a common scalar-potential zero, the general force propagator identity (Theorem G7(a), passage) reduces endpoint evolution to two kicks and the scalar action \(\langle f,G_s f\rangle/(2m)\), where \(G_s\) is the Dirichlet inverse of \(-d^2/dt^2\) on \([0,s]\). The kicks agree by the first two moments. With \(b(t)=1+t/s\), the difference of the two scalar actions is \((2\eta F_*^2/m)\int_0^s h(t/s)(G_s b)(t)\,dt\). Since \(G_s b\) is a cubic polynomial in \(t\), the remaining moments make this integral zero. The two body propagators are identical, including their phase, for every supplied reduced action \(a>0\). This covers endpoint interference against a common reference. A record of the drive or an intervention within the interval is additional data. At each smaller first gate the construction chooses a different pair. It proves failure of a uniform force measurement over Newton’s finite monotone class and preserves the pointwise limit for each fixed force.

A reciprocal classical source. Give the source a finite mass \(M\), put \(\mu=mM/(m+M)\), and choose a common initial separation \(r_0\) and relative speed \(v>0\). Solve \(\mu\ddot r_\pm=f_\pm(t)\) from these data. Since \(\dot r_\pm\ge v\), define a time-independent pair potential along each path by \(V_\pm'(r_\pm(t))=-f_\pm(t)\). It exerts opposite forces on the two masses. At \(s\) the separations and relative speeds agree by the matched moments. Free centre-of-mass motion then gives identical endpoint positions and momenta of both masses. With equal initial potential zeros, the work identity \(V_\pm(r_0)-V_\pm(r_\pm(s))=\mu(\dot r_\pm(s)^2-v^2)/2\) also matches their terminal energies and allows a common continuation. During the window \(\dot r_\pm\le v+2F_*s/\mu\) and \(r_\pm(s)-r_0\le vs+F_*s^2/\mu\). The interaction changes over a shrinking length. Its force gradient at the join has magnitude \(F_*/(s\dot r_\pm(s))\). Fixed masses, initial speed and bounded exchanged work allow these potentials in classical mechanics. Their fabrication and independent inspection would require a source model of their own. The propagator equality above applies to the prescribed uniform fields. This reciprocal realization establishes classical endpoint equality for the stated initial preparation.

A three-time test with a calibrated position resolution

A candidate physical premise can be stated entirely in observed frequencies. Prepare the same ensemble of bodies and independently choose one final reading per run at \(t_j=0,\tau/2,\tau\), with no earlier measurement. For a spatially uniform calibrated acceleration \(g\), subtract \(g(t_j-\tau/2)^2/2\) from each position. Write \(p_j\) for the probability that this corrected position, after the calibrated blur below, falls in the same bounded interval \(I\) of the ruler. Count all preselected runs, with missing outcomes included in the error bounds. The proposed observation is a positive excess

\[V=p_0+p_2-p_1-1>0.\]

The corresponding classical bound \(V\le0\) is the particle reentry inequality of Paterek and Goussev, Eqs. (2) and (4) (passage), in the freely falling coordinates of the parabola. A classical trajectory is a straight line in these coordinates. Membership of both endpoints in \(I\) implies membership of the midpoint, for every initial place and velocity and hence for every positive distribution over them. This also allows arbitrary extra preparation labels when the choice of gate is independent and the calibrated detector reads the body’s position.

How a violation would exclude small actions. Fix a ruler width \(\ell>0\) and a calibration time \(T_c>0\) independently of the gate spacing. Set \(w=\ell/T_c\). Require that the total position response at each gate be a centred Gaussian of variance \(\sigma_j^2=\ell^2+w^2(t_j-\tau/2)^2\). This is a specification for the calibrated detector response including any blur deliberately applied to its records. It makes the three noise marginals exactly those of adding one random affine function \(X+U(t-\tau/2)\) with independent centred Gaussian offsets of standard deviations \(\ell,w\). Thus the blurred records still obey \(V\le0\) for every classical ensemble. Measurements on different runs require no joint draw of these offsets.

Compare also every canonical Schrödinger model with trial reduced action \(a>0\), the same mass \(m\), uniform force \(mg\), and Born position readout. Its Wigner distribution at the middle time propagates by the exact affine Newtonian flow, so blurring the three marginals is equivalent to convolving that distribution at the middle time with a Gaussian of covariance \(\operatorname{diag}(\ell^2,m^2w^2)\) in position and momentum. The Gaussian smoothing theorem gives a positive probability density for every state when \(\ell m w\ge a/2\). The classical interval argument then applies to that density. Quadratic transport, Theorem A(b) (passage) and Cartwright, Gaussian smoothing (abstract). If simultaneous error bounds \(e_j\) cover the three reported probabilities at the stated joint confidence level, then

\[ \widehat p_0+\widehat p_2-\widehat p_1-1>e_0+e_1+e_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a>2m\ell w=\frac{2m\ell^2}{T_c} \]

for every such candidate model. The observation also excludes the classical zero member under the stated trajectory and readout laws. The bound uses a mass, a length and a time supplied without quantum calibration. The other dimensional inputs are the gate times, the interval endpoints and the acceleration. For a spatially uniform force with acceleration miscalibration bounded by \(\delta g\), the residual shift at gate \(j\) is at most \(\delta g(t_j-\tau/2)^2/2\) relative to the middle-time tangent. A shift \(d\) changes the blurred interval probability by at most \(|d|/(\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_j)\), from the derivative of the Gaussian interval probability. Other errors in the force law, clock, detector or gate selection need their own probability bounds.

Allowing an unobserved source to recoil. Suppose the source remains coupled throughout the gate. In a canonical model, add a source Hamiltonian and a bounded self-adjoint potential \(W_t(Q)\) acting on the source at each body coordinate. Assume the source alone has a unitary propagator, the perturbation is norm-continuous in time, and a force calibration supplies \(\sup_q\|W_t(q+d)-W_t(q)\|\le m\delta g|d|\) for every \(d,t\). This includes bounded reciprocal pair potentials \(W(q-R)\) with the stated slope. For any joint state at the middle time \(t_m=\tau/2\), the error in gate probability caused by dropping this coupling is at most

\[b_j=\frac{\delta g(t_j-t_m)^2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_j}.\]

Indeed, under the reference uniform force, the terminal mode \(e^{ikQ}\) transported back to an intermediate time \(r\) translates the argument of \(W_r\) by a distance of magnitude \(a|k(t_j-r)|/m\). Its commutator with \(W_r\), divided by \(a\), has norm at most \(\delta g|k(t_j-r)|\). The bounded-perturbation Duhamel formula in either time direction gives a mode error at most \(\delta g|k|(t_j-t_m)^2/2\). For the Gaussian-blurred interval effect \(B_j\), \(|k\widehat B_j(k)|\le2\exp(-\sigma_j^2 k^2/2)\) in the Fourier convention with inverse factor \(1/(2\pi)\). Integrating the mode error gives \(b_j\). The deterministic correction for \(g\) translates the interval and leaves this bound unchanged. Starting both evolutions at the same middle-time joint state allows arbitrary body–source correlations. The comparison probabilities are those of one common reduced body state. For a classical source with \(|\ddot q-g|\le\delta g\) on each unobserved path, the tangent displacement bound above gives at most \(b_j/2\) per gate even if the residual depends on that path. Thus adding \(\sum_j b_j\) to the required excess covers both classes. The calibration has to bound the interaction on the admitted domain. A mean acceleration measurement alone supplies no such bound.

Subdividing the mathematical propagation between these fixed gates changes neither side of the test. Physically sending \(\tau\) to zero at fixed \(\ell,T_c\) and fixed release preparation is different. All three blurred probabilities then converge to the same \(p<1\) by continuity of the motion and readout, so \(V\to p-1<0\). The positive width and bounded \(I\) give the strict inequality. Conversely, a strict ideal reentry violation on a half-line persists for some finite interval and sufficiently small positive blur by continuity of the three marginal probabilities. Paterek and Goussev exhibit such states in the supplied quantum theory. The missing physical input is a violation in corrected gate counts with the response and errors independently calibrated. Brown et al., Fig. 3 and supplement IV B (passages) reconstruct a trapped atom’s state from quadratures by a quantum maximum-likelihood fit. Their reported Wigner negativity does not supply these three counts. A verified violation would constrain the calibrated motional models above. Extending that constraint to all preparations requires a further physical argument.

A force transmitted between two ions

In a modern test of the force measurement, give ion 2 a small transverse impulse \(J\) and measure ion 1’s displacement at time \(t\). For two equal inertial masses \(m\) in a harmonic trap the impulse response is

\[ \chi_{12}(t)=\frac{\delta x_1(t)}{J} =\frac{1}{2m}\left(\frac{\sin\omega_C t}{\omega_C} -\frac{\sin\omega_R t}{\omega_R}\right). \]

Here \(C,R\) denote the in-phase and relative modes. The displacements of the two modes initially cancel at ion 1. The first surviving term is \(-(\omega_C^2-\omega_R^2)t^3/(12m)\). The force gradient between the ions is measurable through this delayed response. Newton’s Lemma X concerns displacement of the body on which the force acts directly. This extension specifies where a second body can register the force. The impulse can be calibrated as \(m\) times an observed change in velocity. Alternatively, the two periods fix \(m\chi_{12}\). Reciprocal mechanical calibration (passage, mass normalization).

An observed spin readout. Wong-Campos et al. prepared both ion spins by optical pumping and a microwave rotation, applied ten optical kicks, and used microwave analysis and separate fluorescence detectors. They reported Bell fidelity \(F_{\rm obs}=0.76(1)\) and parity contrast \(0.69(1)\). The fidelity is half the sum of the even-spin population and the contrast. Separable spins have Bell fidelity at most \(1/2\) for trusted local spin analysis. The reported uncertainties are statistical. Wong-Campos et al., Figs. 1–2 and Eqs. (1)–(7) and White et al., Appendix B (passages).

What each optical kick fixes. Let \(\kappa\) be the difference of the two beam wave vectors projected on the motional axis. It is \(4\pi/\lambda\) for collinear counterpropagating beams of equal wavelength. A coherent spin flip at position \(x\) carries the measured optical phase \(\pm\kappa x\) up to a position-independent phase. This position-local pulse law is a physical assumption that can be tested by scanning spin phase against a ruler. The corresponding mechanical impulse is an output of the model. The calculation uses neither a photon momentum nor an oscillator ground-state width as a calibration. Clocked grating, preparation and readout (passage).

Prepare separable spins independently of the joint motional state. The two modes act on distinct factors and may be correlated in that state. In the interaction picture require the calibrated harmonic response for positions as observables,

\[ Q_\mu(t)=Q_\mu\cos(\omega_\mu t) +\frac{P_\mu}{m\omega_\mu}\sin(\omega_\mu t), \qquad x_{1,2}(t)=\frac{Q_C(t)\pm Q_R(t)}{\sqrt2}. \]

A centroid check on a single state is weaker than this dynamical premise. Remove the known local spin flips and spatially constant phases. For a spin branch \(z_1,z_2=\pm1\), kick \(n\) at \(t_n\) then acts by \(e^{iB_n}\) with \(B_n=s_n\kappa[z_1x_1(t_n)+z_2x_2(t_n)]\). The signs \(s_n=\pm1\) include the effect of all preceding spin flips. Allow a commutative motional algebra as well as regular operator realizations. Write \(K_\mu=\|[Q_\mu,P_\mu]\|\). For the norm estimate take the commutators bounded, and the real linear combinations and partial sums self-adjoint on closure from a common invariant core on which their unitary groups permit Duhamel differentiation. No scalar commutator is required.

Replace the ordered product on each branch by \(e^{i\sum_n B_n}\). With \(L_\mu=\kappa\sum_n s_n Q_\mu(t_n)/\sqrt2\), the replacement applies local spin rotations with angles given by the two commuting observables \(L_C+L_R\) and \(L_C-L_R\). Tracing motion averages product rotations over their joint spectral measure. Thus the replacement leaves the spins separable for any admitted motional preparation, including a trajectory that fails to return to its initial point.

On each branch the replacement has norm error at most \(\tfrac12\sum_{j<n}\|[B_n,B_j]\|\). This uses the unitary Lie product bound for each partial sum. Iserles and Kropielnicka, Theorem 2.1 and §3.1 (passage). Equal spin signs select \(C\), opposite signs select \(R\). In the selected mode \(B_n=\pm\sqrt2\,s_n\kappa Q_\mu(t_n)\), and \([Q_\mu(t_j),Q_\mu(t_n)]=[Q_\mu,P_\mu]\sin(\omega_\mu d_{nj})/(m\omega_\mu)\) with \(d_{nj}=t_n-t_j>0\). The norm error bounds the change in a Bell probability even after tracing the motion. Define

\[ D_\mu=\sum_{j<n}\frac{|\sin(\omega_\mu d_{nj})|}{\omega_\mu}, \qquad B_{\rm ion}=\frac{\kappa^2}{m}\max_{\mu=C,R} K_\mu D_\mu . \]

Let \(F_{\rm low}\) be a statistical lower limit, and \(e\) an independently justified upper bound on the upward shift of the Bell probability from preparation, readout and dynamics outside this model. Put \(\Delta=F_{\rm low}-1/2-e\). For a prescribed optical reference, \(\Delta\le B_{\rm ion}\). In particular, for \(N\ge2\) kicks,

\[ \boxed{\quad \Delta>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\max_\mu K_\mu}{m}\ \ge\ \frac{\omega_{\min}\Delta}{\kappa^2\binom{N}{2}}>0,\qquad \omega_{\min}=\min(\omega_C,\omega_R).\quad} \]

This bounds the norm of a mechanical commutator in units of action, uniformly over initial motional states. For regular canonical modes with common trial value \([Q_\mu,P_\mu]=iaI\) it bounds \(|a|\). The signed phase difference between equal- and opposite-spin branches at motional reunion is then \(\Phi=2a\kappa^2\sum_{j<n}s_js_n\chi_{12}(d_{nj})\), by the cited gate formula, Eq. (3). Write \(\Lambda=\Phi/a\). If \(\Lambda\ne0\), the two positive values \(a\) and \(a+2\pi/|\Lambda|\) give identical spin statistics for this one closed schedule and the same mechanical impulse response. Nevertheless \(\Phi\to0\) as \(a\to0\) at fixed wavelength. The controlled reunion below fixes a mechanical force and gives an inverse-action phase. At physically shortened delays the cubic impulse response suppresses the cross phase. Arbitrary mathematical cuts between fixed kicks preserve the propagator. Splitting a kick into pieces at its own time with the same sign and total gradient also preserves the estimate with the actual piece gradients in its pair sum.

Ruler, clock and source. In the reported gate \(\lambda=355\) nm, \(\omega_{C,R}/(2\pi)=1.267,1.170\) MHz, and the clock rate was \(81.42\) MHz. Alternating delays of 175 and 157 clock periods gave a gate of \(18.5\) microseconds. Each effective kick used a shaped train of about \(2.3\) ns. Treating it as instantaneous requires a pulse-error allowance. The coarse coefficient \(\omega_{\min}/(\kappa^2\binom{10}{2})\) is about \(1.3\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m}^2/{\rm s}\). This multiplies the certified \(\Delta\). The gate times, mode periods and projected wavelength are the dimensional inputs to the specific-action bound. An absolute action uses an independently calibrated mass \(m\). If \(\chi_{12}\) is measured directly, the additional ruler displacement and impulse enter through their ratio. For \(x=sX,\ t=rT\), the pulse gradient becomes \(s\kappa\), the frequencies \(r\omega_\mu\), the times \(t_n/r\) and the specific commutator norm \((r/s^2)K_\mu/m\). Spin preparation angles and the observed probabilities are dimensionless. The whole inequality is covariant and the fixed laboratory settings remove this dilation.

A physical reference may recoil. For example, put the pulse phase at \(\kappa(x_i-Y)\) with a common free reference \(Y(t)=Y+tP_s/M\) of independent mass \(M\). Let its algebra commute with both ion modes, allow any joint motional state, and write \(K_s=\|[Y,P_s]\|\) under the same regularity hypotheses. The summed reference generator again produces shared local rotations. The ordered factors add at most \(2\kappa^2(K_s/M)\sum_{j<n}d_{nj}\) to the norm bound, because the reference coefficient is \(-s_n\kappa(z_1+z_2)\). Consequently

\[ \Delta\le B_{\rm ion}+2\kappa^2(K_s/M)\sum_{j<n}d_{nj}. \]

A positive margin forces a positive commutator somewhere in this closed mechanical model. Attributing it to the ions alone requires an upper bound on the reference contribution. Other optical modes, a direct spin interaction or memory in the drive require separate control. Classical shared records and local operations cannot entangle initially separable spins, even with feedback. The classical-mediation theorem requires that all communication between the probes use that channel. Galley, Giacomini and Selby, Theorem 1 (theorem passage).

A direct-spin counterexample and a discriminating control. The measured excess is \(0.26\) at the central value. The paper studies gate errors but does not report an independent bound on spin entanglement through all paths outside the two modeled modes. The distinction can be tested explicitly. Keep the oscillators classical and let a direct \(e^{i\pi Z_1 Z_2/4}\) on two equatorial spins prepare a Bell state up to local rotations. Here \(Z_i\) is the spin Pauli matrix. Use the even Bell state with probability \(0.69\), an incoherent even pair with probability \(0.14\), and an incoherent odd pair with probability \(0.17\). The even population is \(0.83\), the parity contrast \(0.69\) and the Bell fidelity \(0.76\). This reproduces the two central readouts with commuting ion motion once a direct spin gate is admitted.

One further control cancels an arbitrary common diagonal spin gate in the prescribed-reference model. Prepare spin 1 on its equator and spin 2 in a fixed \(Z_2\) eigenstate. Reverse the optical gradient on ion 2 alone between runs while keeping the schedule, spin flips, spin 1 pulse and its Ramsey analyzer fixed. Denote the probabilities for one output of that analyzer by \(p_\pm\). In the summed-generator comparison the angle on spin 1 is \(L_C+L_R\) for both settings. The sign change on spin 2 drops out after tracing motion, even if the path does not close. A common diagonal spin gate supplies the same local phase in both runs. Therefore \(|p_+-p_-|\le 2B_{\rm ion}+e_{\rm diff}\), where \(e_{\rm diff}\) covers the setting-dependent error in the difference, including changes in the source. For a closed canonical gate the nonlocal phases reverse sign. The two Ramsey quadratures then give a separation \(2|\sin\Phi|\) between the ideal equatorial Bloch vectors, independent of the common diagonal phase. The published common-beam schedule scan does not implement this local reversal. A second interaction that also reverses with the gradient still requires an independent bound.

Matched endpoints, arbitrary partitions and action normalization

For \(L=m|\dot q|^2/2+Fy\), the same-endpoint chord has \(y_C(t)=F\tau t/(2m)\). If \(y=y_F+\eta\) and both endpoint variations vanish, integration by parts using \(m\ddot y_F=F\) gives

\[S[y_F+\eta]-S[y_F]=\frac m2\int_0^\tau\dot\eta^2dt.\]

For \(\eta=Ft(\tau-t)/(2m)\) this is

\[D_{C,F}=\frac{F^2\tau^3}{24m} =\frac{F}{2v}A_{\rm chord,fall} =\frac{F}{4v}A_{\rm inertial,fall}=\frac{\tau\Delta E}{12}.\]

A common ordinary point-function term \(dG(q,t)/dt\) added to \(L\) cancels because the endpoints and times agree. A velocity-dependent \(G\) changes the endpoint data required by that statement: adding \(d(\kappa\dot y)/dt\) changes this chord-minus-parabola action by \(-\kappa F\tau/m\), since their endpoint velocities differ. That is a higher-derivative Lagrangian, outside this point-mechanics convention. The bare inertial and falling paths have different vertical endpoints; an observable phase comparison needs endpoint/control phases.

For a partition \(0=t_0<\cdots<t_N=T\) with steps \(\tau_j\), the polygon interpolating exact trajectory values has

A genuine receding centre: subtract before taking the limit

For \(C_R=(0,R)\) define signed sector area

\[A_R[\gamma]=\frac12\int_\gamma[x\,dy-(y-R)\,dx] =\frac R2\Delta x+\frac12\int_\gamma(x\,dy-y\,dx).\]

The \(R\) term cancels exactly for paths sharing endpoints, giving their closed loop area. For a fixed limiting parabola its finite part is \(Fv\tau^3/(12m)\). A varying family must also retain its endpoint correction. Use the actual central-force solutions

Controlled reunion of the inertial and falling arms

Keep a reference arm at zero transverse force and apply to the signal arm

\[f(t)=\begin{cases}F,&0<t<\tau,\\-F,&\tau<t<3\tau,\\ F,&3\tau<t<4\tau.\end{cases}\]

\[\int_0^{4\tau}P^2dt=\tfrac43F^2\tau^3,\qquad \int_0^{4\tau}Ydt=2F\tau^3/m,\qquad A_{\rm loop}=2vF\tau^3/m=12A_{\rm inertial,fall}.\]

\(Y\ge0\) peaks at \(F\tau^2/m\) at \(2\tau\). Using \(m\ddot Y=f\) and the vanishing boundary term \(mY\dot Y\),
