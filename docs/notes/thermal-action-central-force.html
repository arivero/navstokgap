A thermal action scale from Newton’s central-force limit

Markdown source · PDF

6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-19. A central force proportional to radius admits a test of zero physical action in Newton’s own time. Fix a body in a plane with positive angular momentum. Prepare its radial degree of freedom in thermal equilibrium and allow changes of the strength of this one force. If a positive ensemble of Newtonian trajectories obeys the equilibrium work identity, doubling the orbital frequency costs exactly \(k_B\log 2\) in isothermal entropy loss at every temperature. Nernst’s heat theorem requires this loss to tend to zero. For the inverse-square force, two changes of strength separated by a short interval force the equilibrium radius law by the acceleration term in Lemma X. With both circular orbits inside fixed reflecting walls, the entropy loss tends to the logarithm of their radial frequency ratio in units of \(k_B\). Both exclusions include singular ensembles and survive arbitrary time subdivision.

The two-temperature work observable below defines a positive thermal action from the body’s equation of state, given the usual high-temperature correspondence and harmonic virial relation. In the regular planar Schrödinger family it determines the reduced action \(a\) uniquely, including a finite-observation lower bound. The independent physical premise concerns thermal equilibria of this rotating body. The value across different bodies, and an identification with quantum phase in other theories, remain open.

From the finite force to a thermodynamic question

In 1687 Newton specified a regular force in Lemma X. In 1713 he specified a finite force, allowed it to vary, and added Corollary 4 relating it to initial displacement divided by time squared. Proposition VI applies the construction to orbits. Proposition X finds the force toward the centre of an ellipse proportional to distance, with equal periods for all its ellipses at a common strength. These are edition passages. The thermal condition in this note is modern. Lemma X in 1687 and 1713 and Newton, 1713, Book I, Proposition X and its corollaries.

Write the acceleration as \(-\omega^2 {\bf q}\). At a point \({\bf q}_0\ne0\) the departure from its inertial tangent, divided by \(t^2/2\), tends to \(-\omega^2{\bf q}_0\). This fixes the frequency in mechanical time. The independently fixed sector is \(J=q_xp_y-q_yp_x>0\), with mass \(m>0\). A radial thermal contact can exchange energy while preserving \(J\). Write \(\theta=k_BT>0\) for temperature in energy units. At each fixed strength the initial law is stationary. During a prescribed strength change the body is isolated. For a Newtonian path the full source work on it is the change in

\[ H_\omega=K+V=\frac{|{\bf p}|^2}{2m} +\frac{m\omega^2|{\bf q}|^2}{2}. \]

Two equilibrium conditions have independent content.

  1. For an initial equilibrium and each smooth positive strength protocol from \(\omega\) to \(\omega_f\), inclusive work satisfies \(\langle e^{-W/\theta}\rangle=\exp[-(F(\theta,\omega_f)- F(\theta,\omega))/\theta]\), with finite Helmholtz free energies. The right side is common to all routes between the two strengths. This is Jarzynski’s identity for an initially thermal, subsequently isolated drive. It is used here as a constraint on any candidate law. Its usual forward derivation starts with a Gibbs state. The corresponding quantum work is the difference of the two endpoint energy readings. Jarzynski, equations (2) and (8) and Deffner and Lutz, equations (1)–(3) (passages).
  2. At fixed \(J\) assume differentiable equilibrium thermodynamics, \(S=-k_B\partial_\theta F\), and the Nernst heat theorem for the two strengths. In symbols \(S(\theta,\omega)-S(\theta,2\omega)\to0\) as \(\theta\downarrow0\). It concerns entropy differences within the same equilibrium sector. Its justification as a thermodynamic postulate is independent of an action constant. Applying it to this constrained radial system is a physical hypothesis. Independent copies give the same test per copy by entropy additivity. Nernst, Nobel lecture, pp. 358–359 and Masanes and Oppenheim, opening discussion of the heat theorem and its degeneracy condition (passages).

What a quarter-period change forces on every Newtonian law

Theorem. At fixed \(\theta,\omega,J>0\), condition 1 forces the Newtonian energy to have law \(E_0+X\), with \(E_0=\omega J\) and \(X\) exponential of mean \(\theta\). For every \(c>1\) it forces

\[ F(\theta,c\omega)-F(\theta,\omega)=(c-1)E_0+\theta\log c. \tag{1} \]

No density relative to phase volume is required. A hidden label may affect the initial law arbitrarily if the projected motion and work still have the specified mechanical values.

Proof. On a stationary orbit put \(E=K+V\) and \(D=V-K\). The pair \((D,\omega{\bf q}\cdot{\bf p})\) rotates at \(2\omega\) and has squared radius \(E^2-E_0^2\). This follows from the harmonic equations and the planar Gram determinant \(J^2\). Stationarity gives \(\mathbb E[D\mid E]=0\) and \(\mathbb E[D^2\mid E]=(E^2-E_0^2)/2\), including the circle. The orbit’s orientation need never be specified.

Between the initial and final strengths, use frequency \(\omega_p=\sqrt c\,\omega e^{s/2}\) for its quarter-period \(\pi/(2\omega_p)\). Its exact map is \(({\bf q},{\bf p})\mapsto({\bf p}/(m\omega_p),-m\omega_p{\bf q})\). The final energy at frequency \(c\omega\) is

\[ E_f=c(e^s V+e^{-s}K)=c(E\cosh s+D\sinh s). \tag{2} \]

These switches are legitimate limits of the smooth finite forces in condition 1. For \(|s|<\log c\) one has \(E_f\ge ce^{-|s|}E>E\). Smoothing each switch over a shrinking time changes the linear fundamental matrix continuously, so the same strict quadratic-form bound, with a smaller margin, holds for sufficiently narrow ramps. Dominated convergence applies to \(e^{-(E_f-E)/\theta}\), bounded by one. Inserting times into a fixed smooth motion composes its exact maps. Convergent Newton polygons give the same fundamental matrix at fixed duration. Finite-force polygon limit.

Condition 1 makes this work average independent of \(s\). Differentiate twice at \(s=0\). On any smaller neighborhood of zero the integrands and both derivatives are bounded by a polynomial in \(E\) times a decreasing exponential, even for an initial law with heavy tails. With \(t=(c-1)/\theta>0\) and \(L(t)=\mathbb E[e^{-tE}]\), the conditional moments above give

\[ L''(t)+\frac{2}{t+1/\theta}L'(t)-E_0^2 L(t)=0. \tag{3} \]

Indeed the averaged second derivative has the two factors \(-cE/\theta\) and \(c^2(E^2-E_0^2)/(2\theta^2)\). Set \(u=t+1/\theta\). Then \((uL)''=E_0^2(uL)\). The bound \(0<L(t)\le e^{-tE_0}\) removes the growing solution since \(J>0\). Normalization at zero gives

\[ L(t)=\frac{e^{-tE_0}}{1+\theta t}. \]

Uniqueness of the Laplace transform gives the stated law. At \(s=0\) we have \(W=(c-1)E\), and the work identity gives (1). Differentiating (1) in temperature gives \((S(\theta,\omega)-S(\theta,c\omega))/k_B=\log c\). Taking \(c=2\) contradicts condition 2. The familiar classical oscillator entropy already has this dependence. The converse above is what prevents a different positive distribution of Newtonian moving orbits from repairing it while keeping the source work. Boyer, oscillator thermodynamics, §§III and VI (passages).

An action scale from two finite work averages

Set \(G(\theta,\omega)=F(\theta,2\omega)-F(\theta,\omega)\). Compare the same compression at two positive temperatures using

\[ B(\theta,\omega)= \frac{G(\theta,\omega)-G(\theta/2,\omega)}{\theta/2}. \tag{4} \]

This is the average of the isothermal entropy loss in units of \(k_B\) over the interval \([\theta/2,\theta]\) (or its value at an intermediate temperature by the mean value theorem). It uses only two finite work averages. An additive temperature-independent endpoint energy cancels. For Newtonian motion (1) gives \(B=\log 2\). Nernst gives \(B\to0\), so there is already a strict separation at a fixed positive temperature. Fix this temperature before refining the mechanical time mesh or comparing trial actions.

To define an action directly from thermodynamics, keep the usual mechanical source conjugacy \(F_\omega=m\omega\langle r^2\rangle=U/\omega\) in equilibrium, with \(U=F-\theta F_\theta\). The last mechanical equality is the harmonic virial law at fixed \(J\). Under precisely this work law oscillator entropy has Wien’s scaling form, a function of \(\theta/\omega\) at fixed body and sector. Thus (4) is a continuous function of \(x=\theta/(2\omega)\), which has action units. Boyer, equations (1), (8) and (13) (passages). Assume classical thermal correspondence \(B(x)\to\log 2\) at large \(x\). For existence of the scale it suffices that \(B\) exceed \(\log(3/2)\) at one finite \(x\). Then

\[ A_*=\inf\{x>0:B(x)\ge\log(3/2)\},\qquad 0<A_*<\infty . \tag{5} \]

Indeed there is an entire neighborhood of zero below this threshold, and the defining set is nonempty. Continuity gives a first crossing even if the response elsewhere is nonmonotone. The choice \(3/2\) is the midpoint of the two endpoint ratios \(e^B=1,2\). Other intermediate thresholds define other finite positive scales. No action-valued cell size or absolute entropy enters (4) or (5).

The reduced action in the regular canonical theory

For a spinless regular planar oscillator with trial reduced action \(a>0\) and \(J=ka\), \(k\) a positive integer, the exact radial energies are \(E_n=\omega[J+(2n+1)a]\), \(n\ge0\), each simple at fixed \(k\). The Helmholtz difference follows from this equally spaced spectrum. Mazhar et al., equation (56) (passage) and radial period and action. Writing \(z=2a\omega/\theta=a/x\) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} G_a(\theta,\omega)&=\omega(J+a)+\theta\log(1+e^{-z}),\\ e^{B_a}&=\frac{(1+e^{-z})^2}{1+e^{-2z}}=1+\operatorname{sech}z . \end{aligned} \tag{6} \]

Thus any observed \(B\le b<\log 2\), \(0<b\), implies

\[ a\ \ge\ \frac{\theta}{2\omega} \operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac1{e^b-1}\right)>0. \tag{7} \]

Equality in the measured response determines one trial action through (6), subject to \(J/a\) integral. In this theory \(a=A_*\operatorname{arcosh}2\). The vanishing-temperature and vanishing-action limits in (6) give different answers in opposite orders. Each positive \(a=J/k\) separately obeys Nernst. What excludes an entire sequence \(a\downarrow0\) for one preparation is the shared finite-temperature value (4).

For example take \(\theta=J\omega/\log 2\). At \(a=J\) and \(a=J/2\), respectively, \(B=\log(25/17)\) and \(\log(9/5)\). Both trials use the same angular momentum, two source strengths, two temperatures and work rule. Even a single-temperature bounded reading separates them. For the \(s=0,c=2\) quarter-period change, \(W=E\) also holds for the two quantum energy readings. The harmonic evolution interchanges position and momentum as in (2), giving \(U_p^\dagger H_{2\omega}U_p=2H_\omega\). On the initial thermal state the mean of \(e^{-(W-E_0)/\theta}\) is \(1/[2\cosh(a\omega/\theta)]\). The two means are \(2/5\) and \(\sqrt2/3\), compared with \(1/2\) for the Newtonian work law. Rounding \(W-E_0\) down in bins of width \(\theta/256\) and giving the reading zero at energies above \(8\theta\) changes each mean by at most \(e^{1/256}-1+e^{-8}<1/128\). All three intervals are disjoint.

An exactly smooth compression with the same endpoint relation is also available. Take a smooth scale factor \(b(t)\) constant at 1 initially and \(1/\sqrt2\) finally. Use the standard harmonic dilation with \(\Omega^2=\omega^2/b^4-\ddot b/b\). A sufficiently long finite duration keeps \(\Omega^2>0\). The construction acts identically on both Cartesian coordinates and preserves \(J\). The endpoint energies scale by two. The same profile works for both trial actions. Initial and final energy readings can lie anywhere in fixed time windows on the constant plateaus. Chen et al., Ermakov equation and endpoint conditions (passage, reversed compression).

The dimensional inputs here are \(m\), the prepared angular momentum \(J\), the source frequencies and their durations in physical time, and the thermal energies \(\theta,\theta/2\). \(k_B\) converts temperature to energy. One may equivalently fix independent length and time units \(L,\tau\) and set \(J=mL^2/\tau,\ \omega=1/\tau\). The finite energy bins and plateau windows can then be fixed in those same units before choosing \(a\). Both \(B\) and the threshold in (5) are dimensionless. With the mechanical reference \(S_0=J\), the dimensionless trial is \(\epsilon=a/J\). The physical trial \(a\) tends to zero with \(\epsilon\) at fixed \(J\). The time mesh is a separate regulator. In the canonical identification \(a=\hbar\) the ordinary Planck action is \(h_P=2\pi a\).

A radius reading with a fixed ruler and clock. Take those same independent \(L,\tau\) and the same thermal preparation at \(\theta=J\omega/\log2\). Sample its initial stationary radius at a uniform time in \([\tau,2\tau]\). Use bin width \(\rho=L/1024\) and report \(f(r)=\min\{64L,\rho\lfloor r/\rho\rfloor\}^2\). The equilibrium virial relation gives \(\langle r^2\rangle=U/(m\omega^2)\). The two geometric level populations give \(U/(J\omega)=8/3,5/2\) at \(a=J,J/2\), respectively. Their energy second moments in units of \((J\omega)^2\) are \(80/9,33/4\).

For each regular oscillator eigenfunction \(\psi\), integration by parts gives \(2\operatorname{Re}\langle K\psi,V\psi\rangle \ge -a^2\omega^2\|\psi\|^2\), since \(\Delta V=2m\omega^2\) in the plane and the remaining integral is \((a^2/m)\int V|\nabla\psi|^2\). Thus \(\langle V^2\rangle\le\langle H_\omega^2\rangle+a^2\omega^2\) also in the thermal mixtures. Both have \(\langle r^4\rangle\le40L^4\) and \(\langle r\rangle<2L\). The loss from rounding and saturation is at most \(2\rho\langle r\rangle+\langle r^4\rangle/(64L)^2<L^2/64\). The intervals for \(\langle f\rangle/L^2\) therefore lie within \([8/3-1/64,8/3]\) and \([5/2-1/64,5/2]\), respectively. The action changes the thermal populations and their position laws. The same finite spatial bins and the same time window act on both.

Why positive angular momentum matters

A classical exception at zero angular momentum. In the invariant diameter of the same central force take the common reference measure \(dq\,dp/(2\pi b_0)+\delta_{(0,0)}\), where \(b_0>0\) has action units. Every change of spring strength preserves area and fixes the origin. The Gibbs weights on this measure therefore obey the work identity by change of variables, for the full mechanical work. Its partition function is \(1+\theta/(b_0\omega)\). Its entropy tends to the same value at both strengths as \(\theta\downarrow0\). Its high-temperature loss tends to \(k_B\log2\). It has continuous Newtonian trajectories and no quantum phase. The extra weight gives a thermal scale \(b_0\). Here (3) admits a constant term in \(L\), the mass at the fixed point. The strictly rotating sector removed exactly that possibility. Also, an entropy difference is insensitive to temperature-independent shifts of level energies. An absolute zero-point energy requires further input.

Two short force changes on a Kepler orbit

Take two Kepler strengths \(\mu_2>\mu_1>0\). With \(v=\dot r\), write \(H_\mu=mv^2/2+U_\mu(r)\), where

\[ V(r)=-m/r,\qquad U_\mu(r)=\frac{J^2}{2mr^2}+\mu V(r),\qquad R_\mu=\frac{J^2}{m^2\mu},\qquad \nu_\mu=\frac{m^3\mu^2}{J^3}. \]

\(R_\mu,\nu_\mu\) are the circular radius and its radial frequency. The unconfined Kepler canonical partition function diverges at large radius. Use the same two fixed elastic walls \(0<r_-<r_+\) for all strengths in an open interval containing \(\mu_1,\mu_2\), with every \(R_\mu\) strictly inside. The walls perform zero work. The effective radial force points into the annulus at each wall, so a stationary Newtonian law spends zero probability at the endpoints, including turning points with zero speed. Assume finite mean kinetic energy. Kepler actions and circular limit and elastic and Dirichlet walls.

Theorem. For this source, condition 1 and stationarity determine both the radial probability \(P_\mu\) and the conditional mean square radial speed.

\[ P_\mu(dr)=\frac{e^{-U_\mu(r)/\theta}}{I_\mu(\theta)}\,dr,\qquad \mathbb E_\mu[v^2\mid r]=\theta/m,\qquad I_\mu(\theta)=\int_{r_-}^{r_+} e^{-U_\mu(r)/\theta}\,dr . \tag{8} \]

Only the scalar source work and the motion enter the proof. In particular it also applies after marginalizing any hidden state that leaves this source work and equation of motion intact. The established work-weighted endpoint formula starts with a Gibbs ensemble. Here two strength changes and their short-time motion determine the radius marginal of an initially unspecified stationary law. Hummer and Szabo, equation (4) (passage, forward identity).

Proof. Change \(\mu\) to \(\lambda=\mu+\delta\), let the orbit move for a time \(t\), then change to \(\alpha\). Its work is \(\delta V(r_0)+(\alpha-\lambda)V(r_t)\). Comparing this route with \(t=0\) in condition 1 shows that under the tilted initial measure \(h_\delta(r_0)d\rho_\mu\), where \(h_\delta=e^{-\delta V/\theta}\) and \(\rho_\mu\) is the whole stationary law, \(V(r_t)\) and \(V(r_0)\) have identical Laplace transforms as \(\alpha\) ranges near \(\lambda\). These variables are bounded. Equality on an interval determines their laws, and \(V\) is one-to-one. Thus the radius marginal of the tilted measure is stationary under evolution at \(\lambda\).

The switches can be obtained with smooth ramps. The work throughout is bounded by the total variation of the strength times \(\sup|V|\). For an initial point in the interior the intermediate energy equals the potential at either wall for at most two values of \(\lambda\), since \(V(r_0)\ne V(r_\pm)\). By Fubini, for almost every intermediate strength these grazing energies have zero initial probability. For those strengths the limiting position flow is continuous through transverse wall impacts. Bounded convergence therefore gives the stated work comparison. Two distinct nonzero shifts from this full-measure set suffice below.

Let \(\Pi(dr)=\mathbb E_\mu[v^2{\bf1}_{\{r\in dr\}}]\), a finite measure. For a smooth test \(f\) supported in the interior, stationarity of \(vf(r)\) at the original strength gives \(\int f'\,d\Pi=m^{-1}\int f U_\mu'\,dP_\mu\). For the tilted measure differentiate its constant mean of \(f(r_t)\) twice at zero, using Newton’s short-time acceleration at strength \(\lambda\). This gives

\[ 0=\int h_\delta f''\,d\Pi -\frac1m\int h_\delta f'(U_\mu'+\delta V')\,dP_\mu. \tag{9} \]

The compact support removes the wall impulse from both identities. The second derivative along the flow is bounded by a constant times \(1+v_0^2\), since the potentials are bounded on the annulus. Subtract the original identity tested with \(h_\delta f'\). It follows that \(h_\delta V'(\Pi-\theta P_\mu/m)\) has zero distributional derivative and hence is constant times Lebesgue measure on the connected interior. Two distinct shifts have a nonconstant ratio of tilts. Both constants must vanish. Since \(V'>0\), \(\Pi=\theta P_\mu/m\). The original identity now reads \(P_\mu'=-(U_\mu'/\theta)P_\mu\). Multiplication by \(e^{U_\mu/\theta}\) and normalization give (8).

An immediate strength change then gives \(G_K(\theta)=F(\theta,\mu_2)-F(\theta,\mu_1) =-\theta\log(I_{\mu_2}/I_{\mu_1})\). Near each circle \(U_{\mu_i}\) has a unique nondegenerate minimum \(E_{c,i}\) of curvature \(m\nu_i^2\), where \(\nu_i=\nu_{\mu_i}\). The leading Laplace formula gives \(I_{\mu_i}\sim e^{-E_{c,i}/\theta}\sqrt{2\pi\theta/m}/\nu_i\). In consequence,

\[ G_K(\theta)=E_{c,2}-E_{c,1}+\theta\Lambda+o(\theta),\qquad B_K(\theta):=\frac{G_K(\theta)-G_K(\theta/2)}{\theta/2} \longrightarrow \Lambda=\log(\nu_2/\nu_1)>0 . \tag{10} \]

The ordinary entropy loss has the same limit in units of \(k_B\) because the weighted Laplace asymptotic gives \(\langle U_{\mu_i}-E_{c,i}\rangle/\theta\to1/2\) at both minima. No wall length or phase-volume normalization enters \(\Lambda\). Condition 2 for these two strengths requires \(B_K\to0\). The conflict comes from the local acceleration in (9) and the source work. DLMF §2.3(iii), applied on each side of the minimum (passage).

A finite Kepler lower bound

For explicit dimensional choices take the earlier \(m,L,\tau\) and \(J=mL^2/\tau\). Use \(\mu_1=L^3/\tau^2,\ \mu_2=\sqrt2\,\mu_1\), \(r_-=L/2\) and \(r_+=2L\). Then \(\nu_1=1/\tau\), \(\nu_2=2/\tau\) and \(\Lambda=\log2\). Fix a positive \(\theta\) low enough that the Newtonian value in (10) exceeds \(3\Lambda/4\) and the physical preparation obeying Nernst has \(|B_K|<\Lambda/4\). Existence follows from the two limits. A regular canonical realization at each fixed admissible \(a>0\) is consistent with Nernst. The Dirichlet radial operator on this finite interval has a simple ground and positive excitation gap in the fixed angular sector. The selected temperature belongs to the preparation.

Here is a computable lower bound at that fixed \(\theta\). In flat radial measure, at \(a=J/k\) the two Dirichlet Hamiltonians have kinetic part \(-a^2\partial_r^2/(2m)\) and potentials \(U_{\mu_i}-a^2/(8mr^2)\). Put \(M=\max_i\sup|U_{\mu_i}'|\) on the annulus, \(\eta=\min\{\Lambda/12,1/2\}\). For the comparison take \(N=\max\{1,\lceil 6M(r_+-r_-)/(\eta\theta)\rceil\}\) and \(d=(r_+-r_-)/N\le\eta\theta/(6M)\). Every matching canonical trial must satisfy

\[ a>a_0,\qquad a_0=\min\left\{ r_-\sqrt{\frac{4\eta m\theta}{3}}, \ \frac{\eta d}{6}\sqrt{\frac{m\theta}{4\pi}}\right\}>0 . \tag{11} \]

To see this, denote the canonical partition function by \(Z_{a,i}(u)\) at thermal energy \(u\). Write \(\ell_u=a\sqrt{2\pi/(m\,u)}\) and \(Q=a^2/(8mr_-^2)\). Dirichlet–Neumann bracketing on cells of length \(d\), with the extrema of the potential on each cell, gives

\[ e^{-Md/u}(1-\ell_u/d) \ \le\ \frac{\ell_u Z_{a,i}(u)}{I_{\mu_i}(u)} \ \le\ e^{(Md+Q)/u}(1+\ell_u/d). \]

Indeed the constant-potential cell kinetic sums are bounded below by \(d/\ell_u-1\) for Dirichlet and above by \(d/\ell_u+1\) for Neumann, by the integral bounds for the decreasing Gaussian. If \(a\le a_0\), at both \(u=\theta,\theta/2\) the logarithm of the middle ratio is bounded in magnitude by \((Md+Q)/u+2\ell_u/d\le\eta\). The two strengths give error at most \(2u\eta\) in their free-energy difference. The two temperatures give error at most \(6\eta\le\Lambda/2\) in \(B_K\). This is too small to span the stipulated strict separation. The partition here bounds a trace. Its cell length \(d\) can be chosen once and is independent of the time subdivision in Newton’s construction. Frank, Laptev and Weidl, Dirichlet–Neumann bracketing (chapter summary) and regular planar radial operator.

Consequence for STATE

The heat theorem and the finite-time work identity exclude the zero-action Newtonian trajectory description for a thermal rotating body in Proposition X’s central force. The thermal action (5) is positive under virial scaling and thermal correspondence. On a confined Kepler orbit (9) gives the exclusion directly from the short-time acceleration. Inequalities (7) and (11) bound the reduced action of a regular canonical realization at fixed physical inputs. Two uncoupled bodies can have different positive trial actions and satisfy all these tests jointly. Their work averages multiply, free energies add and both entropy differences vanish on cooling. A common phase scale for interacting bodies and an exclusion from the bare trajectory limit remain open.
