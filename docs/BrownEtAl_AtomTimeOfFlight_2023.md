# Brown et al. — time-of-flight photographs of one atom

M. O. Brown, S. R. Muleady, W. J. Dworschack, R. J. Lewis-Swan,
A. M. Rey, O. Romero-Isart and C. A. Regal photographed the expansion
of a single rubidium-87 atom released from an optical tweezer. They also
varied the time spent in the trap before release and reconstructed the
motional state from the resulting position distributions. The camera
magnification used in their analysis came from the measured fall of
the atom under gravity. [*Nature Physics* **19**, 569–573 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-022-01890-8)
and [authors' preprint, version 2, 7 April 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03053v2).

## Reading coverage

Checked the authors' article and supplement text, source captions and
the published data statement on 18 November 2026. The following figure
and section numbers refer to the preprint, *Time-of-Flight Quantum
Tomography of Single Atom Motion*. PDF text extraction and the authors'
TeX source distinguish the raw image averages, processed projections
and fitted quantum state.

- **One release time.** After loading and cooling, the group transferred
  population into the excited level of a second well. The wells were
  separated and the first trap extinguished before imaging. Figure 2(e)
  averages 48,309 images at zero extra hold, 0.5 ms flight and 10 µs of
  fluorescence. Runs with no initial atom supplied the background.
  Atoms lost later produce no fluorescence signal. [Fig. 2 and supplement II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03053v2#page=3).
- **Ruler.** The sensor pitch was 16 µm. Separate drop images and
  $g=9.8\ {\rm m/s^2}$ gave magnification $64(1)$. The authors used
  this value throughout. They also compared it with an estimate from
  the fitted pattern of an excited state. Their in-trap point-spread
  function had an asymmetric tail. [Supplement II, Table I and the magnification and point-spread passages](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03053v2#page=10).
- **Multiple holds.** In Fig. 3(d) the group doubled the trap depth
  before release. They recorded 64 projections, with a median of
  6,191 images per setting. The upper plot shows the measured
  profiles after Richardson–Lucy deconvolution and integration over the
  vertical axis. The lower plot is a model prediction. The Wigner
  minimum $-0.060(6)$ comes from the density-matrix reconstruction
  and its bootstrap. A fit to centre-of-mass oscillations constrained
  the trap anharmonicity. [Fig. 3, caption and supplement IV B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03053v2#page=4).

The published statement offers the underlying data on reasonable
request. It does not specify a per-run record of later losses. The
public figures do not give a calibrated mean of the bounded position weight in
the [central-force test](../notes/newton-insertion-action.md#two-action-scales-in-the-same-atom-photographs).
