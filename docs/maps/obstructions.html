Pointwise coherent records require nonzero frequency sums for non-convergence. Equal-action cross terms can cancel, and a derivative sign change may produce only two illuminated branches. The Kepler angle map also needs its signed image width: compression cannot cause pre-fold wrapping. These exceptions restrict crossing-based conclusions, while the incoherent classical-limit countermodel survives.

A classical transverse-area restriction bounds energy sublevel sets, not quantum levels; its pointwise admitted energies still approach zero. The inverse-square comparison also requires a specified domain and stability: self-adjointness alone does not select a stable positive scale.

All-coupling T2′ belongs to a separate optional strengthening: the continuum problem requires an appropriate weak-coupling scaling trajectory, construction and a surviving positive physical edge. See the correction. None of these closures is a reason to abandon either main research goal.
