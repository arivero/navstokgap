Tutorial · what survives refinement

A minimum action, and a mass gap

Two limits that refine a picture until nothing is left to see: Newton shrinking a cell of motion to a point, and a lattice gauge theory halving its spacing toward the continuum. This tutorial walks through what each keeps.

Use the arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, or scroll. Every claim links to the note that holds its proof and states its status.

1 · Newton's comparison

A force read off a vanishing sagitta

Galileo compares a body moving freely with the same body under a constant force F. Over a cell of duration τ the two paths separate by the sagitta Fτ²/2m, and Newton's Lemma X makes the force the limit of that separation as the cell shrinks.

The cell carries an action, the Galileo area in Newton's quantities:

Kτ = F²τ³ / (24 m)

Newton takes τ → 0, and Kτ goes to zero with it.

2 · The cut measure

Every cut spends a fixed share

Cut a cell at the fraction s of its duration. The cut spends exactly 3s(1−s)Kτ of the cell action, and the shares add over any sequence of cuts. Halving spends ¾; n halvings spend 1−4−n.

The stick of Zhuangzi 33, halved every day from one end and "not exhausted in ten thousand generations", spends only 6/7: the pieces it takes away are never cut again.

Cut-measure note, Theorems 1–2 (refereed).

3 · The cost of a record

A mark that locates also kicks

To read the comparison, the body must be marked. Each mark locates it within δ and delivers an impulse spread Δ, with the trade-off

δ Δ ≥ κ

Marks placed at the cuts of the cell distinguish the force with squared statistical distance equal to the action those cuts spend, divided by κ. The cut measure and the record measure are one object.

Proposition 7 (refereed); the dense limit is Theorem 2 of the Planck paper.

4 · Leibniz's test, 1687

Continuity of the best verdict

The best error in deciding between free motion and a force F is

P*(F) = Φ(−½√(Kτ/κ))

With a floor κ > 0 it rises smoothly to ½ as F → 0. With κ = 0 perfect marks decide any force at once, and the verdict jumps.

"Lorsque la difference de deux cas peut estre diminuée au dessous de toute grandeur donnée in datis … il faut qu'elle se puisse trouver aussi diminuée … in quaesitis" — Leibniz, July 1687

Continuity note: read on records, Leibniz's law holds exactly when κ > 0 (refereed; the reading is the premise).

5 · Voices on division

The scholium Newton did not write

Book I of the Principia sets limits against indivisibles. The dispute about motion and its records runs through older and contemporary texts, each quoted in the notes from a named edition.

Zeno, Hui Shi, the Mohists

The arrow at rest in every now; the stick halved without end; halving that stops at a point. Paradoxes about division that set the problem.

Galileo, 1638

A weight dropped from the thickness of a sheet of paper makes an impression "del tutto impercettibile": the beginning of fall argued from its record.

Leibniz, 1676–1704

Leaps are pushed to ever smaller bodies unless atoms stop them; nature makes no leaps; a hundred thousand nothings cannot make something.

Newton, Rule III (1713)

Parts can be distinguished by reason, "ex mathematica certum est"; whether nature can divide them "incertum est", and one experiment would decide.

Newton, Query 31

Hard particles that never wear, so that natures stay the same in all ages: a stability premise that asks for a scale.

What the theorem adds

A floor on action fixes no length; it survives the similarity x → λx, t → λ²t, the scaling of quantum paths.

6 · Halving a lattice

Series moves and parallel moves

A lattice gauge theory refines by halving its spacing. Halving one direction inserts a new layer of links. Moves along a line compose exactly; the new mid-plane, coupled to its neighbours through the plaquettes, carries the whole defect.

The atlas of halving records what survives in each group and dimension: free fields, U(1), SU(2), SU(3), in 1+1, 1+2 and 1+3 dimensions.

7 · One step, uniformly in the plane

The mid-plane against a covariant Gaussian

For SU(2) in 1+2 dimensions, one halving step is compared with a Gaussian built in the background connection. The comparison holds with error of relative order tα per face, uniformly in the size of the plane, and the dependence between distant links decays exponentially.

|Kab| ≤ C tα′ e−γ d(a,b)

Small-field note, §§13–25, rounds refereed one by one.

8 · Small fields and large fields

Rare clusters of large fields

Most links stay close to their classical values. A connected set H of links outside the small-field chart is rare jointly, with probability at most

p|H|/16,   p = 2(7/16)kt

The barrier is never differentiated, and uniform convexity alone gives the joint bound. Two distant clusters decorrelate exponentially in their distance.

9 · From SU(2) to SU(3)

The group enters through one invariant

Every one-loop coefficient of the mid-plane step is quadratic in the background and enters through the Killing form. For SU(N) the coefficients are those of SU(2) times N/2: times 3/2 for SU(3).

Twisted boundary conditions carry the centre Z3. The clock and shift matrices give eight adjoint phase pairs, none of them trivial, so the twisted sector has no zero mode.

Order-t note, §7b; small-field note, §16.

10 · Ultraviolet halving, infrared gap

Why halving toward the ultraviolet bears on confinement

Halving the lattice walks toward short distances, while confinement and the mass gap live at long distances. The steps connect through the running coupling: each halving shifts the coupling by an amount fixed by b0 = 11N/(48π²) in four dimensions, and after many steps the lattice spacing, measured in physical units, is set by a scale that no step contains. Dimensional transmutation supplies that scale; whether the spectrum above the vacuum starts at a positive multiple of it is the gap question.

Didactic note; in Newton's problem the analogous scale is an action, and ℏ stays explicit throughout.
