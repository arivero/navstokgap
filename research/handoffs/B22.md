## Audit of proposed formulas

For `Q=lambda flip_s + epsilon flip_r`, the four basis functions
`1,s,r,sr` have `-Q` eigenvalues `0,2lambda,2epsilon,2(lambda+epsilon)`.
With `v=us`, only the `s` mode is visible, so `H=mu^2/lambda` and the full
gap is `2 min(lambda,epsilon)`. With `f_1=us`, `f_2=u delta r`,
`f_3=u delta sr`, the total susceptibility is
`u^2[1/(2lambda)+delta^2/(2epsilon)+delta^2/(2(lambda+epsilon))]`.
Setting `epsilon=lambda delta^2` keeps this bounded while the frame value
`alpha=u^2 delta^2` and full gap close. These are direct finite-state
calculations, not source quotations.

The injective choice `v_delta=u(s+delta r)/sqrt(2)`, `0<delta<=1/4`, has four
distinct velocities, remains below `c`, and at `epsilon=lambda delta^2` has
exactly `H=mu^2/lambda` while gap `2lambda delta^2` closes. Reconstructing `ur`
from `v` requires Lipschitz gain at least `sqrt(2)/delta` (same `s`, flipped
`r`), so injectivity alone does not provide uniform observability.

For independent factors, additive generators give additive mixed-sector rates;
local centered observables miss `H_0,1 tensor H_0,2`, whose dimension is
`(n_1-1)(n_2-1)`. Thus local frame/susceptibility bounds can imply a full-gap
bound only through the explicit independent-product premise. Couplings require
a new estimate. Fixed per-factor rates preserve the `2lambda` gap for N
identical two-state factors; discrete random scan with total rate fixed instead
introduces the `1/N` factor.
