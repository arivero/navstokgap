## Handoff findings

The strongest primary evidence is Xylander’s 1570 Basel Latin *Moralia*, printed
pp. 823–824. Its OCR explicitly contains the Democritus cone-section dilemma and
Chrysippus’s “neque aequales… neque inaequales” answer. This proves pre-1700 print
availability. Holland’s 1603 complete English Moralia is catalogued in EEBO/TCP,
but EEBO returned HTTP 403 for passage extraction, so no Holland wording/page is
claimed.

Auffret pp. 271–276 (PDF pp. 42–47) is the key reception check. He quotes the
cone passage and interprets it as an ancient limit/convergence analogue, but his
Leibniz evidence is *Theodicy* §§332–336, GP VI, 312–314: a separate rolling
cylinder/cone free-will analogy. No direct Leibniz quotation or influence claim
for the section paradox is supported. Bounded searches found no verified direct
invocation in Wallis, Barrow, Cavalieri or Gassendi. The Gassendi/Charleton lead
uses conical atom shape, not the Plutarch dilemma.
