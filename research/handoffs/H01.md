## Findings

1. `#par489-#par490` is the strongest limits cluster. It describes fluxions as
   velocities, moments as generated parts, distinguishes investigative
   approximations from exact geometrical demonstration, and ultimately locates
   the method in first/last ratios rather than nascent/evanescent quantities.
   The diplomatic/TEI shows that consequential qualifications are additions,
   including the contrast between first moments and ratios of nascent moments.

2. The dossier preserves multiple explanatory registers. `#par9`,
   `#par484-#par485`, and `#par796` also speak of an infinitely small `o` or of
   infinitely short/narrow generated parts. These should not be flattened into a
   single modern ontology.

3. `#par520-#par522` and parallel passages say *Principia* propositions were
   found analytically/with fluxions but demonstrated by synthesis to be admitted
   into geometry. This supports a retrospective claim about discovery and
   presentation, not a chronology of publication delay.

4. `#par558`, `#par564`, and `#par793` mention writing the book in 1684/1685 and
   publication in 1687. They do not mention limit doubts or a delay.

5. `#par686` answers why Newton did not publish his methods sooner, refers to a
   treatise communicated in 1669, and attributes discouragement to contentions.
   It is heavily revised and grammatically awkward in the TEI; it does not state
   that the *Principia* was delayed or identify shrinking areas/limits.

6. `#par701` says Newton omitted asserting priority in the published
   *Principia* to avoid controversy. This concerns polemical presentation, not
   publication timing. A related claim that he thereafter declined contention
   is an addition.

7. `#par1093` attributes nonpublication of a 1671 methods/series treatise to the
   sake of quiet. That object is not the 1687 *Principia*.

8. The sole normalized `delay*` occurrence, `#par975`, concerns Leibniz's alleged
   suppression of an invention. The `doubt*` occurrences concern receipt of
   letters or documentary sincerity. No `classical` occurrence was found.

9. The selected text therefore does **not establish** that Newton's doubts about
   shrinking areas delayed the 1687 edition. This passage-level negative result
   is not a universal refutation; hidden deletions, unsearched wording, other
   manuscripts, correspondence, publication records, and historical scholarship
   remain outside H01.

10. NATP00385 is not the six Book III Classical Scholia corpus. Its repeated
    scholium reference is principally Book II, Lemma II in the fluxions-priority
    dispute. H02/H03 must locate, concord, and read the Classical Scholia
    separately.

## Extraction defects and rights caveats

- Normalized HTML hides 7,123 deletions, silently includes 4,904 additions, and
  applies 1,744 regularizations according to the site.
- XML mechanical counts confirmed 7,123 `<del>` and 4,904 addition-like starts
  (4,886 `<add>` and 18 `<addSpan>`). Python's standard XML parser found the file
  well formed; no schema validation was run.
- `lynx` text dumps flatten MathML, layout, hands, and nested editorial markup.
  They were used for search only; substantive wording was checked against HTML
  and XML.
- The XML has 285 page-break elements, but rendered HTML has 195 standalone
  page-number spans; page breaks inside paragraphs complicate folio coverage.
- No facsimile/formula verification was performed. Uncertain readings remain
  editorial, not independently checked.
- TEI availability is marked “restricted” and links CC BY-NC-ND 3.0. Local
  copies are for source verification; redistribution was not cleared.
