\hypertarget{newtons-record-as-a-parallel-move-what-forgetting-a-record-leaves}{%
\section{Newton's record as a parallel move: what forgetting a record
leaves}\label{newtons-record-as-a-parallel-move-what-forgetting-a-record-leaves}}

\textbf{Result, 2026-09-27 (atlas of halving, open cell 6).} Inserting
an unobserved time into Newton's cell is a series move and closes
exactly (\href{refinement-composition-and-limit.md}{refinement note},
§2). Inserting a \emph{record} at that time attaches a new variable, the
pointer, to the body; forgetting it is the Newtonian counterpart of the
parallel move of the
\href{series-parallel-gauge-refinement.md}{series/parallel note}. For a
position record with Gaussian pointer of width \(\sigma\) this move is
exact (Proposition 1): it leaves the body's position distribution
unchanged and convolves its momentum distribution with a Gaussian of
variance

\[v=\frac{\hbar^2}{4\sigma^2}.\]

Three consequences fill the Newton row of the
\href{halving-atlas.md}{atlas}.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Records at one instant combine in parallel:} precisions add,
  \(\sigma^{-2}=\sigma_1^{-2}+\sigma_2^{-2}\), and so do the momentum
  variances, as the heat times of parallel faces combine by conductances
  in the gauge case.
\item
  \textbf{Refining with a record at every inserted time converges iff}
  \(\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}<\infty\): the total momentum diffusion is
  \(\frac{\hbar^2}4\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}\), and the same sum measures the
  information gathered about the place. The continuous-measurement limit
  of Caves and Milburn is the case \(\sigma_j^{-2}=\kappa\tau_j\)
  (Corollary 2).
\item
  \textbf{Information and disturbance are locked at \(\hbar^2/4\) per
  unit:} \(\sigma^2v=\hbar^2/4\) for every Gaussian record and every
  mesh (for other pointers the product is bounded below by the Planck
  paper's Theorem 4). For \(\hbar=0\) the move is the identity for every
  \(\sigma\) on the classical phase-space distribution, since averaging
  the posterior over a forgotten outcome returns the prior (the factor
  \(e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}\) contains no \(\hbar\); its effect on \(W\) is
  a momentum convolution of variance proportional to \(\hbar^2\)):
  records are series-exact, the Newtonian counterpart of a theory with
  no parallel planes, and exactly the joint determinacy of the
  \href{principia-fifth-postulate.md}{fifth-postulate note}.
\end{itemize}

The Gaussian record channel and its continuous limit are standard
(\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.36.5543}{Caves and Milburn
1987}, metadata, cited in the Planck paper); the reading as the parallel
move of the atlas is this note's. For non-Gaussian records the general
floor is Theorem 6 of the \href{planck-gap-paper.md}{Planck paper}.

\hypertarget{the-record-channel}{%
\subsection{1. The record channel}\label{the-record-channel}}

A position record with Gaussian pointer of width \(\sigma\) and outcome
\(y\) has Kraus operator \(M_y\) acting on wavefunctions by
multiplication with \((2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-(x-y)^2/(4\sigma^2)}\);
forgetting the outcome gives the channel
\(\rho\mapsto\int M_y\rho M_y^\dagger\,dy\). Write \(W(q,p)\) for the
Wigner function of \(\rho\).

\textbf{Proposition 1.} The forgotten record acts as
\(\rho(x,x')\mapsto\rho(x,x')\,e^{-(x-x')^2/(8\sigma^2)}\), that is,

\[W(q,p)\ \mapsto\ \int W(q,p-p')\,\frac{e^{-p'^2/(2v)}}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}\,dp',
\qquad v=\frac{\hbar^2}{4\sigma^2}.\]

The position marginal is unchanged; the momentum marginal is convolved
with a centred Gaussian of variance \(v\).

\emph{Proof.}
\(\int M_y(x)M_y(x')\,dy=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\int e^{-[(x-y)^2+(x'-y)^2]/(4\sigma^2)}dy=e^{-(x-x')^2/(8\sigma^2)}\).
With \(s=x-x'\) and
\(W(q,p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-1}\int\rho(q+\frac s2,q-\frac s2)e^{-ips/\hbar}ds\),
multiplication by \(e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}\) is convolution in \(p\) with
the Gaussian whose characteristic function in \(s/\hbar\) is
\(e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}\), of variance \(v\) with
\(v/(2\hbar^2)=1/(8\sigma^2)\). The factor equals one on the diagonal
\(s=0\), so the position marginal is unchanged. \(\square\)

The record's error is \(\sigma\) and its delivered impulse has spread
\(\sqrt v\); their product is \(\hbar/2\), the saturated mark cost of
the Planck paper's Theorem 4.

\hypertarget{composition}{%
\subsection{2. Composition}\label{composition}}

\textbf{Parallel.} Two forgotten records at one instant multiply the
density matrix by
\(e^{-s^2/(8\sigma_1^2)}e^{-s^2/(8\sigma_2^2)}=e^{-s^2(\sigma_1^{-2}+\sigma_2^{-2})/8}\):
one record with \(\sigma^{-2}=\sigma_1^{-2}+\sigma_2^{-2}\). Precisions
add like conductances, and the momentum variances add. This is the
Newtonian counterpart of two parallel faces at heat time \(2t\)
combining into one at \(t\) (the series/parallel note, §3).

\textbf{Series.} Between records at different times the body evolves
freely or under constant force, an affine symplectic map on \(W\); a
momentum convolution at one time becomes a sheared convolution in
\((q,p)\) at a later time. The channels compose exactly, because
Gaussian convolutions and affine symplectic maps form a closed family.

\textbf{Corollary 2 (refinement with records).} Put a record of width
\(\sigma_j\) at each vertex of a partition of \([0,T]\). The forgotten
records convolve the final momentum distribution with a Gaussian of
variance \(\frac{\hbar^2}4\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}\); the shear adds the
position spread \(\sum_jv_j(T-t_j)^2/M^2\le(T/M)^2\sum_jv_j\). The noise
covariance of the forgotten-record channel converges along the
refinement iff \(\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}\) converges; for a divergent sum
the momentum marginal flattens and the channel has no limit. The
records' Fisher information about the initial place, for free motion
with known momentum, is \(\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}\) and converges on the
same condition. With \(\sigma_j^{-2}=\kappa\tau_j\) the sum is
\(\kappa T\): individual records become coarse as the mesh shrinks, and
the rate \(\kappa\) and the momentum diffusion rate \(\hbar^2\kappa/4\)
survive the limit (the Lindblad term \(-\frac\kappa8[x,[x,\rho]]\)).
This is a continuous limit of the kind Caves and Milburn construct; the
Gaussian-pointer parametrization is stated here, their abstract being
the part checked.

\emph{Proof.} The multiplicative factors of Proposition 1 at one instant
multiply; free evolution between vertices is an affine symplectic map,
which carries Gaussian convolutions to Gaussian convolutions without
changing the momentum variance of a convolution applied before it. The
momentum variances therefore add (a harmonic force would rotate the
kicks, which is why the corollary is stated for free and constant-force
motion). \(\square\)

\hypertarget{what-the-newton-row-of-the-atlas-now-says}{%
\subsection{3. What the Newton row of the atlas now
says}\label{what-the-newton-row-of-the-atlas-now-says}}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Series move} (unobserved insertion): exact after one scalar
  counterterm, at every \(\hbar\).
\item
  \textbf{Parallel move} (forgotten record): a momentum convolution of
  variance \(\hbar^2/(4\sigma^2)\). It is the identity for \(\hbar=0\),
  as a gauge theory with no transverse planes has no parallel defect.
\item
  \textbf{What survives refinement with records:} the information rate
  \(\kappa\) and the diffusion rate \(\hbar^2\kappa/4\), with fixed
  product per record. This is the Newtonian form of the atlas's error
  budget: summable records, like summable parallel defects, give a
  limit, and the ratio that survives is set by the one action constant.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{4. Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

Atlas cell 6 is filled for Gaussian records: forgetting a record is an
exact parallel move whose defect is proportional to \(\hbar^2\), which
makes joint determinacy the statement that Newton's refinement has no
parallel defect. The general, non-Gaussian record is covered by the
Planck paper's Theorem 6 and by Theorem E of the fifth-postulate note.
