\section{SU(3) constants and conditional spectral
comparisons}\label{su3-constants-and-conditional-spectral-comparisons}}

With the goal narrowed to \(SU(3)\), each bound proved in these notes
becomes a number. The group data are \[N=3,\quad \dim G=8,\quad
C_2(R_{\rm f})=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}=\frac43,\quad
C_2(\text{adj})=N=3,\quad
Z(G)=\mathbb Z_3,\] in the normalization
\(\operatorname{tr}T^aT^b=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}\), and the one- and
two-loop coefficients are
\[b_0=\frac{11N}{48\pi^2}=\frac{11}{16\pi^2}=0.06966,\qquad
\frac{b_1}{2b_0^2}=\frac{51}{121}=0.4215 .\] The tables specialize group
factors, running coefficients and stated conditional bounds. The
Kogut--Susskind stability threshold remains existential. One-loop
running to order-one coupling is an extrapolation. A \(SU(3)\) field gap
is not obtained by substituting group constants into the proved
\(SU(2)\) matrix theorem. Locality upper bounds and observed-channel
estimates retain their separate scope.

\hypertarget{the-proved-bounds-evaluated}{%
\subsection{2. The proved bounds,
evaluated}\label{the-proved-bounds-evaluated}}

\textbf{T1.} Unchanged in form: unique positive physical ground state
and \(\delta(g;N_s,SU(3))>0\) at every finite lattice and coupling.

\textbf{T2} (\href{strong-coupling-uniform-gap.md}{T2 note}). The
perturbation parameter is
\[\beta=\frac{48N^2}{g^4(N^2-1)}=\frac{48\cdot9}{8\,g^4}=\frac{54}{g^4},\]
so the criterion \(\beta\le\beta_*(3,\{0,1\}^3)\) becomes
\[g\ \ge\ g_0=\Big(\frac{54}{\beta_*}\Big)^{1/4},\] and the gap is
\[\Delta_{a,L}\ \ge\ \gamma\,\frac{g^2}{2}\,\frac43\,\frac{\hbar c}{a}
=\frac{2\gamma}{3}\,g^2\,\frac{\hbar c}{a}.\] A single plaquette
flux-loop excitation in the fundamental, costs
\(2C_2g^2\hbar c/a=\tfrac83g^2\hbar c/a\).

\textbf{Feynman--Bijl, strong coupling}
(\href{lattice-gap-upper-bounds.md}{upper-bound note} Corollary 3). At
\(g=\infty\) the Haar variance is
\(\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p)=\tfrac12\)
for \(N\ge3\), so the two-sided statement reads
\[\frac{2\gamma}{3}g^2\,\frac{\hbar c}{a}\ \le\ \Delta^{\rm phys}_{a,L}\ \le\ 12\,g^2\,\frac{\hbar c}{a}\]
for \(g\) beyond both thresholds, a ratio of \(18/\gamma\) between the
two sides.

\textbf{Lieb--Robinson} (\href{lieb-robinson-kogut-susskind.md}{note}).
Four plaquettes per link and \(\|w_p\|\le12\hbar c/(ag^2)\) give
\(J\le48\hbar c/(ag^2)\) and \(v_{\rm LR}\le48C_{\rm LR}c/g^2\). The
former \(96e\,c/g^2\simeq261c/g^2\) uses the unverified
\(C_{\rm LR}=2e\) normalization. It implies no actual superluminal
speed. The gap gives
\(\hbar v_{\rm LR}/\Delta\le72C_{\rm LR}a/(\gamma g^4)\); a clustering
length also retains microscopic range.

\textbf{Exact identities} (\href{magnetic-energy-identities.md}{note}).
With \(C_2=4/3\) and \(N=3\),
\[\Delta V=\frac{16}{3}\big(3|\mathcal P|-V\big),\qquad
|\nabla V|^2\le16NV=48\,V,\] and the ground-state sum rule of that note
holds with \(2C_2=8/3\).

\textbf{Blocking} (\href{blocking-criterion-monotone.md}{criterion
note}). The block criterion is
\(\beta_{\rm block}=72M^2/(g^2\delta(g;M))\) and the direct one is
\(\beta_{\rm direct}=72/g^4\), from \(32N/C_2=72\), so the ratio at
strong coupling is \(3M^2/8\) as before, blocking losing by \(3/2\)
already at \(M=2\).

\textbf{Small volume.} A spatially constant \(SU(3)\) sector has three
vectors in \(\mathbb R^8\) with the \(SU(3)\) structure constants.
Homogeneity gives the candidate energy scale \(g^{2/3}\hbar c/L\) if
that matrix Hamiltonian has a positive first gap. The
\href{low-dimensional-mass-gap.md}{explicit matrix proof} is for
\(SU(2)\). The \(SU(3)\) spectral input and the full field theory's
nonzero-mode comparison are additional obligations, not proved group
substitutions.

\hypertarget{two-features-specific-to-su3}{%
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{4. Two features specific to
\(SU(3)\)}{4. Two features specific to SU(3)}}\label{two-features-specific-to-su3}}

\textbf{The defining representation is complex.} For \(SU(2)\),
\(\operatorname{tr}U_p\) is real, and the plaquette term is the full
trace; for \(SU(3)\) it is complex and the Wilson term keeps
\(\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p\). Consequences: the bound
\(\int(\operatorname{tr}U)^2dU=0\) used in
\href{lattice-gap-upper-bounds.md}{the upper-bound note} Corollary 3
holds for \(N\ge3\) and gives Haar variance \(\tfrac12\) rather than
\(1\); and charge conjugation is a nontrivial symmetry, so the spectrum
splits into \(C\)-even and \(C\)-odd sectors, and a \(C\)-even glueball
insertion probes its own channel. This does not prove that the channel
contains the global lowest excitation.

\textbf{The centre is \(\mathbb Z_3\).} The valley minima of
\href{torus-valley-potential.md}{the valley note} §4 lie at holonomies
that are central, so there are three per direction and \(27\)
electric-flux sectors on a three-torus, against \(8\) for \(SU(2)\).
These labels and central holonomy minima do not determine spectral
weights or prove a physical gap. The quoted \(O(\hbar c/L)\) barrier is
the scale of the specified one-loop valley calculation.

\hypertarget{scope-and-consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{5. Scope and consequence for
STATE}\label{scope-and-consequence-for-state}}

The \href{mass-gap-obligations-lattice.md}{spectral obligations}
establish neither an exhaustive no-go classification of Hamiltonian
methods nor an equivalence between all-coupling positivity and absence
of every phase transition. The
\href{finiteness-half-flowed-susceptibility.md}{susceptibility/moment
argument} needs a nonzero admissible Hamiltonian insertion and infrared
moments. Four-dimensional flow finiteness alone supplies neither.

In the specified free Gaussian example the channel estimate
\(4.26\,\hbar c/\sqrt{8t}\) is unchanged when eight colour species
multiply numerator and denominator. It is not an interacting physical
gap bound without the observable, domain and limit premises.

The group constants survive. Kogut--Susskind and Wilson thresholds,
formal running counts, matrix truncations, observed channels and
physical continuum limits retain their individual hypotheses.
