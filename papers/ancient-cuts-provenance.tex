\hypertarget{ancient-cuts-and-modern-readjustment-provenance}{%
\section{Ancient cuts and modern readjustment:
provenance}\label{ancient-cuts-and-modern-readjustment-provenance}}

The ancient anchor is Aristotle's discussion of possible and jointly
realised divisions in \emph{On Generation and Corruption} I.2,
especially around 317a. The statement that inserting a time node can
make neighbouring trajectory vertices readjust is our modern extension.
This distinction was clarified in the conversation on 2026-09-10 and is
part of the restart context.

\hypertarget{what-the-sources-supply}{%
\subsection{1. What the sources supply}\label{what-the-sources-supply}}

Aristotle challenges the inference from divisibility at any chosen point
to simultaneous division everywhere, using the absence of immediately
consecutive points. Earlier he treats a contact as a relation of two
things. These are the direct passage anchors in the
\href{../docs/classics/Aristotle_GC_I2_Joachim_cut_passages.md}{Joachim
companion}.

Plutarch's
\href{../docs/classics/Plutarch_DeCommunibusNotitiis_39_Goodwin1874_perseus.md}{cone
report, section 39}, 1079e--1080a, is a companion question about cutting
surfaces and neighbouring sections. It reports Democritus's dilemma and
Chrysippus's response through Plutarch's critical account. The
anywhere-versus-all-together route in I005 came principally from
Aristotle, rather than from a statement in the cone passage that
inserting a section moves the surrounding sections.

\hypertarget{what-we-borrowed-and-extended}{%
\subsection{2. What we borrowed and
extended}\label{what-we-borrowed-and-extended}}

The source-inspired question is whether individually possible cuts can
be combined while preserving the compatibility of the whole
construction.

In a modern time mesh, inserting \(y\) between \(x\) and \(z\) replaces
segment \(xz\) by \(xy\) and \(yz\). Both adjacent velocity assignments
change when \(y\) varies. If the old vertices are unknowns in a newly
discretised boundary-value or variational problem, their matching
equations can change and their solved positions can readjust. This is a
modern mechanical interpretation, not an ancient theorem or evidence
that Aristotle anticipated path integrals.

Keep three operations distinct:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Sampling a fixed trajectory adds a record and leaves old positions
  intact.
\item
  Refining an approximate dynamical construction and solving again can
  change its old-time vertices. Their readjustment compares mathematical
  solutions; it does not describe changing the physical past.
\item
  Inserting a variable into an exact consistent path description
  preserves the old marginal after integration. Conditioning on its
  observed value can change predictions for correlated variables. Exact
  classical segment-action composition likewise differs from changing an
  approximate discretisation.
\end{itemize}

The
\href{quantum-blocks-construct-newton.md\#projective-gaussian-preparation-and-raw-kinetic-action}{one-cut
calculation}, keeps the two surrounding endpoints fixed. It isolates the
local action cost; it does not establish how all free vertices respond
to a new global discretisation.

\hypertarget{modern-discrete-state-and-operator-comparisons}{%
\subsection{Modern discrete-state and operator
comparisons}\label{modern-discrete-state-and-operator-comparisons}}

The earlier conversation's list of discrete-substrate authors was
unverified recall, not source evidence for a scientific conclusion. Its
useful question is whether loss of classical state information supplies
an action restriction. The relevant named source leads are 't Hooft's
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01011560}{1988 equivalence relations},
\href{https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/16/10/316}{1999 dissipative
system} and \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-41285-6}{2016 book}
(metadata only). No universal time-grain or information-loss necessity
is established from those unread arguments. A finite reversible
permutation already has a unitary realization with a self-adjoint
logarithm whose spectrum can be bounded below; the phase eigenvalues
leave logarithm branches and physical energy normalization undetermined.
The former blanket assertion that a reversible automaton has no ground
state was too strong.

A discrete tick and a force-dependent record resolution make different
claims. The conditional \href{mark-cost-and-statistical-floor.md}{mark
bounds} neither require a universal time atom nor rule out an
independently supplied smaller one. The
\href{necessity-unit-and-indeterminacy.md\#thermodynamic-information-costs-an-inequality-not-an-optimum}{thermodynamic
inequality} prices a stipulated information/work trade-off without
deriving an absolute floor. The
\href{superdeterminism-floor.md}{superdeterminism model} has its own
setting-device mixing and resolution assumptions; it is not a theorem
about every deterministic substrate.

Dedekind's cut specifies a real through two rational sets; a finite
recorded interval is a different object. The
\href{cut-measure-newton.md}{cut-energy proof} counts cuts only after a
positive record-share floor is supplied. Similarly, writing a
spectral-triple differential as \([D,f]\) does not remove points:
commutative function algebras still have evaluation states. A distance
formula using \(\|[D,f]\|\le1\) requires its spectral-triple
assumptions. Setting \(p=\hbar D\) supplies an action conversion rather
than deriving one. These are modern mathematical comparisons, not
ancient transmission claims.

The \href{groupoid-quantum-newton-states.md}{groupoid state theorem}
separates geometric \(\epsilon=\hbar/S_0\) from duration and mesh. A
mark decision window cannot be declared a distance from its classical
fiber by identifying all three parameters. The normalized free blocking
test admits both zero and positive action coefficients; it supplies a
concrete countertest to automatic scale selection.

\hypertarget{consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

Aristotle's anywhere-versus-simultaneous division question, exact
sampling, re-solving a discretized boundary-value problem and
marginalizing an inserted variable remain distinct. Modern
discrete-state and noncommutative definitions add no historical
influence or physical action-necessity theorem by analogy. The proofs
and measured-source passages, rather than conversation chronology,
govern the comparison.
