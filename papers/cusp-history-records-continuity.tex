\hypertarget{classical-histories-complete-records-and-the-cusp-limit}{%
\section{Classical histories, complete records and the cusp
limit}\label{classical-histories-complete-records-and-the-cusp-limit}}

\textbf{Result, 2026-10-28; written derivations, unrefereed.} For the
bounded Newton cusp already used in the
\href{newton-cusp-preparation-floor.md}{preparation-floor note}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  There is a classical, deterministic, time-reversal-symmetric group on
  the space of complete solutions. Its instantaneous \((q,p)\)
  projection follows Newton's equation, and it admits exact states and
  ordinary selective records with zero covariance action. The group
  cannot descend to an evolution on instantaneous phase-space
  probabilities.
\item
  For Gaussian classical preparations whose dimensionless width is
  \(\epsilon\), the initial covariance action is \(S_0\epsilon^2\). All
  their initial laws tend to the same point as \(\epsilon\to0\), while
  the future law can tend to any prescribed mixture of the two departing
  trajectories. At each fixed \(\epsilon>0\) even complete-trajectory
  statistics depend continuously on the Gaussian center; the modulus is
  not uniform in \(\epsilon\).
\item
  Closure under arbitrarily fine, nondisturbing joint \((q,p)\) records
  forces the covariance-action infimum to zero, without any weak-closure
  or canonical-covariance assumption. Thus this record postulate cannot
  be added to the three hypotheses of the cusp preparation-floor
  theorem.
\item
  A separate continuity-and-copying theorem forces a positive covariance
  action without assuming reversibility, provided evolution actually
  transports the admitted Gaussian states by the cusp law. A classical
  entropy-restricted model meets those hypotheses with a supplied scale.
\end{enumerate}

This is a test of the missing premise, not a derivation of \(\hbar>0\).
The trajectory model has extra, generally future-selecting classical
state information and no constructed symplectic or local apparatus
structure on that enlarged space. A probability of selecting a branch is
not quantum coherence. The Gaussian width \(\epsilon\) is a
dimensionless \emph{classical preparation parameter}, not Planck's
constant. The quantum comparison in section 6 retains the physical
\(\hbar\) explicitly.

The route has substantial prior art.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0396(73)90075-2}{Sell 1973}
(publisher abstract read) treats dynamical systems for nonunique ODEs;
the solution-space group below is given its own proof. The branching of
vanishing classical uncertainty is an instance of the established
subject of spontaneous stochasticity: see
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.04941}{Eyink and Drivas 2015} (author
abstract read) for the related rough-potential quantum and classical
context, and \href{https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0063388}{Vanpoucke and
Wenmackers 2021} (author abstract read) for probability assignments in
the same power-law non-Lipschitz mechanical setting. No literature
priority is claimed for the construction or the Gaussian weights here.
The repository's
\href{necessity-unit-and-indeterminacy.md\#3-theorem-i-the-two-pointer-construction}{two-pointer
theorem} already shows operational failure of a prior density ceiling
under specific Hamiltonian instruments; section 5 gives a more general,
explicitly conditional version at the probability-law level.

\hypertarget{force-and-notation}{%
\subsection{1. Force and notation}\label{force-and-notation}}

Fix \(m,\gamma,R>0\), \(0<\alpha<1\), and a smooth even cutoff \(\chi\)
with \(0\le\chi\le1\), \(\chi=1\) on \([-1,1]\) and \(\chi=0\) outside
\([-2,2]\). Consider

\[
 V(q)=-\frac{\gamma}{1+\alpha}|q|^{1+\alpha}\chi(q/R),\qquad
 \dot q=p/m,\qquad \dot p=-V'(q). \tag{1}
\]

The force is continuous and bounded, although not Lipschitz at the
origin. For every initial \((q,p)\) there is a global solution; the
proof and the uniform bounds used below are in the cusp
preparation-floor note, §1. Put

\[
 k=1+\alpha,\quad \lambda=\frac{\gamma}{1+\alpha},\quad
 \beta=\frac{2}{1-\alpha},\quad
 B=\left[\frac{\gamma(1-\alpha)^2}{2m(1+\alpha)}\right]^{1/(1-\alpha)}.
 \tag{2}
\]

For \(0\le t\le T\) with \(BT^\beta<R\), the two rest-departure
solutions are

\[
 \Gamma_\pm(t)=\bigl(\pm Bt^\beta,\ \pm m\beta B t^{\beta-1}\bigr).
 \tag{3}
\]

Let \(L_0,P_0>0\) be arbitrary calibration units, \(S_0=L_0P_0\), and
write \(z=(q/L_0,p/P_0)\). For a finite-covariance law \(\nu\) put
\(\kappa(\nu)=\sqrt{\det\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(q,p)}\). The calibrations
do not introduce a physical lower bound.

\hypertarget{the-trajectory-state-completion}{%
\subsection{2. The trajectory-state
completion}\label{the-trajectory-state-completion}}

Let \(\Omega\) be the set of all globally defined solutions of (1),
viewed as functions \(\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2\) with the topology
of uniform convergence on compact intervals. Write
\(e_t(\gamma)=\gamma(t)\) and

\[
 (T_s\gamma)(t)=\gamma(t+s),\qquad
 (\Theta\gamma)(t)=\bigl(q(-t),-p(-t)\bigr). \tag{4}
\]

\textbf{Theorem 1.} \(\Omega\) is a nonempty Polish space. The maps
\(T_s\) form a jointly continuous group of homeomorphisms and \(\Theta\)
is a continuous involution satisfying \(\Theta T_s\Theta=T_{-s}\). Every
\(e_0\)-fiber is nonempty and compact. The state class
\(\mathcal P(\Omega)\) of all Borel probabilities has weakly continuous
reversible evolution \(\mu\mapsto(T_s)_*\mu\), contains point states,
and supports ordinary classical conditioning on any Borel trajectory
record of positive probability. Its instantaneous phase laws comprise
all Borel probabilities on \(\mathbb R^2\); in particular their
covariance-action infimum is zero.

\emph{Proof.} The integral form of (1) is preserved by local-uniform
limits: on each compact time interval the convergent paths have a common
compact range, and the continuous vector field is uniformly continuous
there. Thus \(\Omega\) is closed in the Polish compact-open space
\(C(\mathbb R,\mathbb R^2)\). Time translations and time reversal
preserve the autonomous equation and have the asserted algebra and
continuity.

Given a compact set of initial points, the bounded-force estimates in
the cusp note give locally uniform bounds on \(q,p\) and their
derivatives. Arzelà--Ascoli, a diagonal subsequence and closedness give
compactness of \(e_0^{-1}(K)\). Global existence makes every fiber
nonempty. Pushforward by the continuous time shifts gives the group on
\(\mathcal P(\Omega)\); for each bounded continuous test function,
dominated convergence proves weak continuity in time. Ordinary
restriction and normalization give the stated conditioning rule.

For the marginal claim, the proper continuous surjection \(e_0\) has a
Borel section \(s:\mathbb R^2\to\Omega\). One justification is the
Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski measurable-selection theorem
(\href{https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/30345D24D3DFFAAF9CE805C30EE959C6/S0008414X00041699a.pdf/some_selection_theorems_for_measurable_functions.pdf}{statement},
theorem statement read): the compact-valued fiber map is
Borel-measurable, since the image under a proper map of a closed set is
closed and an open set in a metric space is a countable union of closed
sets. Hence \(s_*\nu\) is a history preparation with instantaneous
marginal \(\nu\) for any \(\nu\in\mathcal P(\mathbb R^2)\). A Dirac
history has a Dirac instantaneous marginal, with \(\kappa=0\).
\(\square\)

The theorem constructs reversible \emph{history} evolution, not a
reversible map on laws of \((q,p)\) alone. Take two histories that
arrive at the cusp from opposite sides. At their common arrival time
their instantaneous marginals are both \(\delta_{(0,0)}\), while the
full states and their pasts remain distinct. The resting history and a
departing history have the same instantaneous marginal at time zero but
different future marginals, so no single map on phase laws can represent
the shift for all history states. Moreover, the resting solution and the
two solutions which leave after any prescribed delay \(a\ge0\) have the
same entire \((q,p)\) past up to time zero but different futures. Thus a
deterministic completion representing all these possibilities must carry
information beyond that past. In (4) the chosen future is part of the
state. The construction does not show that this information has a
finite-dimensional, local Hamiltonian realization or is accessible to a
physical observer.

Because the phase marginal class is all of \(\mathcal P(\mathbb R^2)\),
it is weakly closed, affine-canonically invariant, and closed under
classical product preparations, canonical mixing and marginalization
\textbf{as a class of single-time laws}. The Borel section is a
state-preparation selection; it is not a proof of a reversible canonical
mixer acting on the enlarged history states. Nor does the instantaneous
marginal determine its later law. The preparation-floor theorem
explicitly assumes that a reversible evolution acts on those marginals
themselves; Theorem 1 does not satisfy that premise, so the two results
are consistent.

\hypertarget{a-continuous-classical-family-with-nonuniform-zero-width-limit}{%
\subsection{3. A continuous classical family with nonuniform zero-width
limit}\label{a-continuous-classical-family-with-nonuniform-zero-width-limit}}

Let \(X,Y\) be independent standard normal variables. For fixed
\(u\in\mathbb R\) and \(\epsilon>0\), prepare

\[
 q_0=\epsilon L_0(u+X),\qquad p_0=\epsilon P_0Y. \tag{5}
\]

The energy-zero set is Lebesgue-null, so (5) selects unique global
trajectories almost surely. Equivalently, lift the law by the section
\(s\): different choices of \(s\) agree almost surely here. Let \(\Phi\)
denote the standard normal distribution function.

\textbf{Theorem 2.} Let \(0<T<(R/B)^{1/\beta}\). As \(\epsilon\to0\),
the initial law of (5) converges weakly to \(\delta_{(0,0)}\) for every
fixed \(u\), and

\[
 \kappa_\epsilon=S_0\epsilon^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{Law}\bigl((q(t),p(t))_{0\le t\le T}\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow
 \Phi(u)\,\delta_{\Gamma_+}+\Phi(-u)\,\delta_{\Gamma_-}
 \quad\text{in }C([0,T],\mathbb R^2). \tag{6}
\]

Every mixture weight strictly between zero and one is obtained by a
fixed \(u\). For either pure branch one may instead take
\(u=u_\epsilon\to\pm\infty\) with \(\epsilon|u_\epsilon|\to0\). The
distributions are classical ensembles of Newton trajectories, with no
supplied \(\hbar\).

\emph{Proof.} The first limit and covariance are immediate. Fix a
realization with \(u+X\ne0\) and finite \(Y\). Its initial energy is,
for small \(\epsilon\),

\[
 E_\epsilon=\frac{\epsilon^2P_0^2Y^2}{2m}
            -\lambda\,|\epsilon L_0(u+X)|^k<0. \tag{7}
\]

Indeed \(k<2\). Thus it cannot reach \(q=0\), so its sign remains that
of \(u+X\). Its turning distance
\(a_\epsilon=(-E_\epsilon/\lambda)^{1/k}\) satisfies
\(0<a_\epsilon\le|q_0|\), and tends to zero. Inside \(|q|<R\),
separation of the energy equation gives the time from the turning point
to \(r\ge a\):

\[
 I_a(r)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\lambda}}\int_a^r
              \frac{dv}{\sqrt{v^k-a^k}}. \tag{8}
\]

For fixed \(0<r<R\), \(I_a(r)\to I_0(r)\) as \(a\downarrow0\), where

\[
 I_0(r)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\lambda}}\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
                    r^{(1-\alpha)/2},\qquad I_0^{-1}(t)=Bt^\beta.
 \tag{9}
\]

For the convergence, the part from \(a\) to \(2a\) is
\(O(a^{(1-\alpha)/2})\); on \(v\ge2a\) the integrand is dominated by a
constant times \(v^{-k/2}\), which is integrable at zero. Moreover,

\[
 I_a(r)\le \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\lambda}}\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
                 (r-a)^{(1-\alpha)/2}, \tag{10}
\]

since \(v^k-a^k\ge(v-a)^k\) for \(k>1\). Hence the time from the initial
point to its turning point is \(I_{a_\epsilon}(|q_0|)\to0\). The outward
time to any fixed \(r\) is \(I_{a_\epsilon}(r)\) plus or minus this
vanishing initial term. Bracketing fixed times by the hitting times of
two nearby radii proves \(q(t)\to\operatorname{sgn}(u+X)Bt^\beta\) for
each \(t>0\). The energy equation gives the corresponding momentum
limit, with outward sign. For \(T\) as stated the limiting path remains
inside \(|q|<R\); the same hitting-time argument keeps the approximating
paths there for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\).

The bounded-force estimates give equicontinuity of both coordinates on
\([0,T]\), so the pointwise convergence is uniform there. It holds for
almost every \((X,Y)\), and bounded convergence of continuous path
functionals gives (6). If \(u_\epsilon\to\pm\infty\) with
\(\epsilon|u_\epsilon|\to0\), the same argument has eventual fixed sign
for each \((X,Y)\) and gives the corresponding pure branch. \(\square\)

\textbf{Fixed-width continuity.} Let
\(\mu_{z,\epsilon}=s_*\mathcal N(z,\epsilon^2I)\) on \(\Omega\), with
\(z\) dimensionless. For all centers \(z,w\) and every \(t\),

\[
 \bigl\|(T_t)_*\mu_{z,\epsilon}-(T_t)_*\mu_{w,\epsilon}\bigr\|_{\rm TV}
 \le \frac{|z-w|}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\epsilon}. \tag{11}
\]

Here total variation is the supremum over measurable events. Pushforward
cannot increase it. For the Gaussian bound, interpolate its center along
the line from \(z\) to \(w\) and integrate the absolute directional
derivative: half its integral is \(1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\epsilon)\) per unit
center distance. Equation (11) applies to any subsequent fixed
measurable record, including a complete history record. Its constant
diverges as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

The divergence cannot be removed uniformly. Fix \(0<t<T\) and a bounded
continuous \(f\) with \(f(Bt^\beta)=1\) and \(f(-Bt^\beta)=0\); a
clipped linear function suffices. For the two centers
\(z_\pm=(\pm\epsilon,0)\), Theorem 2 gives

\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
 \left[\mathbb E_{z_+,\epsilon}f(q(t))-
       \mathbb E_{z_-,\epsilon}f(q(t))\right]
 =2\Phi(1)-1>0. \tag{12}
\]

The two initial centers are only \(2\epsilon\) apart. Thus no common
modulus of continuity tending to zero can hold for these responses as
\(\epsilon\) varies. A uniform response modulus would exclude the
zero-width sequence; it is an extra physical premise, not a consequence
of continuity at each fixed width. More concretely, if the absolute
response difference is bounded by \(L|z-w|\) for some \(L>0\), uniformly
over all allowed widths, and both translated preparations at each such
width are admitted, (12) implies an \(\epsilon_0>0\) such that widths
below \(\min\{\epsilon_0,[2\Phi(1)-1]/(4L)\}\) are excluded for this
family. This conditional bound does not identify \(S_0\epsilon^2\) with
\(\hbar\) and does not establish the response premise for physical
records.

\textbf{Corollary.} No evolution on phase-space probability laws which
agrees with the actual Newton transport of all the Gaussian laws (5) can
be weakly continuous at \(\delta_{(0,0)}\) at any fixed \(t\in(0,T)\).
Indeed all the inputs have that same weak limit while (6) gives distinct
output limits for different \(u\). This obstruction does not require
injectivity or a rule for the exact point at the origin. It does require
the indicated Gaussian preparations and their actual cusp transport.

\hypertarget{a-continuity-selector-without-reversibility}{%
\subsection{4. A continuity selector without
reversibility}\label{a-continuity-selector-without-reversibility}}

The preceding corollary yields a different conditional action theorem.
It uses actual Newton transport of nonpoint preparations, rather than
compatibility only for point preparations if any exist.

\textbf{Theorem 3.} Use the \(W_2\) metric in the calibrated
coordinates, and let \(\mathcal S\subset\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^2)\)
satisfy:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(\mathcal S\) contains a state with positive-definite covariance and
  is invariant under affine canonical transformations.
\item
  \(\mathcal S\) is closed under \(W_2\) convergence. Independent finite
  products of members, followed by copy-index orthogonal canonical
  mixing and retention of one output pair, produce members of
  \(\mathcal S\), as specified in §1 of the
  \href{central-limit-canonical-gaussian-sector.md}{central-limit note}.
\item
  At one fixed \(t\in(0,(R/B)^{1/\beta})\), there is a map
  \(\mathcal E_t:\mathcal S\to\mathcal P(\mathbb R^2)\) which is
  continuous from \(W_2\) convergence of inputs to weak convergence of
  outputs. For every nondegenerate Gaussian member of \(\mathcal S\),
  its output is the actual almost-everywhere Newton transport under (1).
\end{enumerate}

Then the infimum of \(\kappa\) on \(\mathcal S\) is strictly positive.
The map \(\mathcal E_t\) need not be invertible, form a semigroup, or
map back into \(\mathcal S\) for this conclusion.

\emph{Proof.} Work in calibrated coordinates. Suppose the infimum is
zero and choose states with covariance matrices \(C_n\) and
\(\det C_n\to0\). Translate their means to zero. Choose \(\eta_n>0\)
such that \(D_n=C_n+\eta_n I\) has \(\det D_n\to0\), and put
\(A_n=(\det D_n)^{1/4}D_n^{-1/2}\). In one pair \(\det A_n=1\), and

\[
 A_nC_nA_n^{\mathsf T}\le\sqrt{\det D_n}\,I. \tag{12a}
\]

The transformed admissible states have second moment tending to zero, so
they converge to \(\delta_0\) in \(W_2\); closure admits \(\delta_0\).

By the central-limit result, a positive-definite covariance member
yields an admissible Gaussian with the same covariance. Translation and
a determinant-one transformation give an admissible
\(G=\mathcal N(0,\zeta I)\) for some \(\zeta>0\). For each \(n\), take
one independent copy of \(G\) and \(n-1\) copies of \(\delta_0\), then
use an orthogonal copy-index mixer whose first row is
\((1,\ldots,1)/\sqrt n\). The retained pair has law
\(\mathcal N(0,(\zeta/n)I)\). Translations therefore admit both Gaussian
inputs (5) with \(\epsilon_n=\sqrt{\zeta/n}\) and \(u=\pm1\).

Both input sequences converge to \(\delta_0\) in \(W_2\). The continuity
assumption forces their output sequences to have the same weak limit
\(\mathcal E_t\delta_0\). Actual Newton transport and Theorem 2 give two
different branch mixtures instead. This is a contradiction. \(\square\)

The assumptions are consistent in a classical model. The entropy-floor
class \(\mathcal E_\zeta\) of §6 of the central-limit note has a
\textbf{supplied} \(\zeta>0\), is \(W_2\)-closed, has the required
canonical copy-index mixers, and is transported by the actual cusp flow.
Its members are absolutely continuous. The classical flow is continuous
at every initial state off the energy-zero set: bounded-force
compactness and uniqueness there give continuous dependence. The
exceptional set has Lebesgue measure zero, so the extended
continuous-mapping theorem gives weak continuity of the transport on
\(\mathcal E_\zeta\) under \(W_2\)-convergent inputs. This provides a
nonvacuous classical witness for the full continuity premise; its
positive action is inherited from its imposed entropy bound.

The theorem therefore identifies a possible independently motivated
stability requirement and makes its consequence explicit. It does not
establish that all physical preparations obey the copy, closure and
continuity rules, or that the selected floor is universal or quantum.

\hypertarget{fine-records-and-the-action-floor}{%
\subsection{5. Fine records and the action
floor}\label{fine-records-and-the-action-floor}}

\textbf{Theorem 4.} Let \(\mathcal S\) be a nonempty class of Borel
probabilities on the classical phase plane. Suppose that, for every
\(\nu\in\mathcal S\) and every bounded open ball \(D\) in the calibrated
\((q/L_0,p/P_0)\) plane with \(\nu(D)>0\), the ordinary conditional law
\(\nu(\cdot\mid D)\) is again in \(\mathcal S\). Then

\[
 \inf_{\nu\in\mathcal S,\ \operatorname{Cov}\nu<\infty}
 \sqrt{\det\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(q,p)}=0. \tag{13}
\]

If \(\mathcal S\) is also weakly closed, it contains a Dirac measure.

\emph{Proof.} Every probability has a point \(z_*\) in its support. The
balls \(D_r\) of radius \(r>0\) centered there have positive
probability. For the calibrated conditional covariance \(C_r\), bounded
support and centering at the conditional mean give
\(\operatorname{tr} C_r\le r^2\). Therefore

\[
 \kappa\bigl(\nu(\cdot\mid D_r)\bigr)
 =S_0\sqrt{\det C_r}\le\tfrac12 S_0\operatorname{tr}C_r
 \le\tfrac12S_0r^2. \tag{14}
\]

These conditional laws have finite covariance and converge weakly to
\(\delta_{z_*}\) as \(r\downarrow0\). This proves both statements.
\(\square\)

The premise is an ideal, nondisturbing, arbitrarily fine \emph{joint}
record of classical position and momentum, with each
positive-probability outcome admitted as a new preparation. It is not a
theorem that every physical instrument has this property. Theorem I of
the necessity note constructs a more specific two-pointer Hamiltonian
protocol even with a prior density ceiling, but has its own explicit
access and storage assumptions. In particular a prior cap alone does not
supply the postulate needed to preserve a floor after records.

Combining (13) with the cusp preparation-floor theorem shows that its
nonempty, finite-covariance preparation class cannot also satisfy the
fine-record postulate. Equivalently, with weak closure and affine
translations, the records would admit the two point preparations that
merge, contradicting its reversible phase-state evolution. This is an
incompatibility among assumptions, not a proof that any one of them is
physically mandatory.

\hypertarget{what-separates-this-from-the-quantum-completion}{%
\subsection{6. What separates this from the quantum
completion?}\label{what-separates-this-from-the-quantum-completion}}

For every supplied physical \(\hbar>0\), the cusp note's self-adjoint
Hamiltonian gives a global quantum unitary group. For a quantum state
with finite position and momentum covariances, the
Robertson--Schrödinger inequality gives

\[
 \sqrt{\det\operatorname{Cov}(Q,P)}\ge\hbar/2. \tag{15}
\]

This remains true after any selective instrument whose conditional
output is a quantum state with finite covariance. There is nevertheless
an exact initial-state comparison: set

\[
 \epsilon_\hbar=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2S_0}},\qquad
 \sigma_q=L_0\epsilon_\hbar,\quad
 \sigma_p=P_0\epsilon_\hbar. \tag{15a}
\]

The Gaussian in (5) then has \(\kappa=\hbar/2\) and is exactly the
Wigner function of the normalized quantum wavefunction

\[
 \psi_{\hbar,u}(q)=(2\pi\sigma_q^2)^{-1/4}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(q-u\sigma_q)^2}{4\sigma_q^2}\right]. \tag{15b}
\]

Thus covariance saturation and even equality of these \emph{initial}
positive phase-space functions do not by themselves select the
subsequent quantum propagation or an interference law. The classical
limit in Theorem 2 then corresponds to a formal \(\hbar\to0\) family at
fixed \(S_0\); no equality of the quantum and classical evolved states
is asserted. At fixed physical \(\hbar\), a small ratio \(\hbar/S_0\)
describes a large-characteristic-action regime only when \(L_0,P_0\)
specify actual scales of a family of preparations, not merely a change
of units.

Equation (15) does not arise by conditioning a positive joint
sharp-position--sharp-momentum law: the canonical Weyl operators obey

\[
 e^{-iaP/\hbar}e^{ibQ/\hbar}
   =e^{-iab/\hbar}e^{ibQ/\hbar}e^{-iaP/\hbar}, \tag{16}
\]

and are noncommuting for general \(a,b\). An approximate joint
measurement can still have two classical outcomes; those outcomes are
not the nondisturbing sharp joint record assumed in Theorem 4. The
positive quantum constant in (15)--(16) is an input. Conversely, the
classical history model has no noncommuting observables or demonstrated
interference law, and its apparent probabilistic branch splitting in (6)
is not a coherent superposition.

There is an independently motivated empirical separator, with additional
composite-system hypotheses. Under setting independence and conditional
locality, an arbitrary classical hidden state including trajectory
memory satisfies \(|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2\); the short proof and its precise
assumptions are in the \href{shared-readiness-chsh.md}{shared-readiness
note}. A Bell test with spacelike-separated electron-spin measurements
reported \(S_{\rm CHSH}=2.42\pm0.20\) in 245 trials
(\href{https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15759}{Hensen et al.~2015}, primary
abstract read). Its setting-independence and locality interpretation is
an additional physical premise, motivated by the relativistic causal
setup; the recorded statistical uncertainty matters. The normalized
inequality is dimensionless, so this observation does not determine an
action scale or select quantum theory uniquely. Nonlocal or
setting-dependent classical completions are not excluded by this bound.

\hypertarget{consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{7. Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

The cusp now distinguishes three logically different requirements:
reversibility on complete classical states, weak continuity of evolution
on instantaneous phase laws, and preservation of an action floor under
selective records. History dynamics satisfies the first with zero
action; Theorem 2 obstructs the second at the exact cusp for actual
Liouville transport; Theorem 4 shows that unrestricted sharp classical
records contradict the third. Theorem 3 derives a conditional positive
floor from the actual transport, stability and copying without
reversibility, but its classical entropy-floor witness leaves quantum
identification open. A physically justified response modulus or
record-disturbance law might remove the classical zero-width family, but
neither is established here. The strong claim that Newtonian mechanics
requires nonzero \(\hbar\) remains open.
