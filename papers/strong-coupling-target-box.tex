\section{The target box: the strong-coupling gap survives local
perturbations with an existential tolerance; its explicit location is
open}\label{the-target-box-the-strong-coupling-gap-survives-local-perturbations-with-an-existential-tolerance-its-explicit-location-is-open}

correction of \href{kogut-susskind-strong-coupling-explicit.md}{the
Kogut--Susskind note}, which withdrew that note's volume-uniform
thresholds. The changes here:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  The former Proposition, ``for \(\lambda=(1-\theta)\varepsilon'\),
  \(u'\le\frac12\) and \(4Fu'\le\lambda\), every

  the corrected overlap multiplicity adds a condition that binds for

\end{enumerate}

The strong-coupling gap of the \(SU(3)\) Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian
survives bounded, gauge-invariant, translation-invariant few-link
perturbations and a link-local deformation of the electric term,
uniformly in the volume. Precisely (§1), let
\(\mathcal C_{\rm TI}(g,\eta,\eta_E,q)\) be the class of Hamiltonians
\[H=H_E'-\frac1{g^2}\sum_pw_p-\sum_jd_j\] on a periodic cubic lattice of
side \(N_s\ge2q+1\), in units \(\hbar c/a\). Here \(H_E'\) is link-local
and diagonal in the Peter--Weyl basis, and every nontrivial link
representation costs at least \((1-\eta_E)\varepsilon\) with
\(\varepsilon=2g^2/3\) and \(0\le\eta_E<1\). Each \(d_j\) is bounded,
self-adjoint, gauge invariant and supported on a connected set of at
most \(q\) links, the family \(\{d_j\}\) is translation covariant, and
\(\sum_{j\ni\ell}\|d_j\|\le\eta\) for every link \(\ell\). If
\[\eta\ \le\ \frac{2(1-\eta_E)\beta^*_q}{9}\,g^2-\frac6{g^2},\] then
\(H\) has a non-degenerate, gauge-invariant ground state, and its full
and physical gaps are at least
\[\gamma_q\,(1-\eta_E)\,\frac{2g^2}3\,\frac{\hbar c}a\] for every such
\(N_s\). The constants \(\beta^*_q,\gamma_q>0\) are those of Yarotsky's
Theorem 1 for the range \(\{-q,\dots,q\}^3\); they exist and are not
known as numbers. The tolerance grows linearly in \(g^2\) with an
existential slope, and the box opens at
\(g^4\ge27/[(1-\eta_E)\beta^*_q]\).

That turns the strong-coupling result into a \textbf{target}: a
renormalization scheme that is an exact low-energy reduction and steers
the effective Hamiltonian into \(\mathcal C_{\rm TI}\) inside this
region gives a volume-uniform lattice gap of the original Hamiltonian,
with the bound of §4. On the lattice, a volume-uniform gap along a
weak-coupling trajectory therefore follows once a scheme carries the
flow into the box; the continuum statements T3 and T4 of
\href{mass-gap-obligations-lattice.md}{the obligations map} are
separate.

Explicit numbers come only from the continuous-time count of the
Kogut--Susskind note restricted to adjacent growth (§§2--3). Corrected,
that count gives formal values such as \(\eta\le0.104\) for
plaquette-like terms (\(q=4\)) and \(\eta\le0.00655\) for two-plaquette
terms (\(q=8\)) at \(g^2=100\), linear in \(g^2\) and decreasing in
\(q\) like \(2^{-q}/\max(4,q)\). They inherit the missing root estimate,
the missing general count and the missing true-vacuum correlation bound
of that note's correction, so \textbf{no gap follows from them}.
Constants explicit; reading labels at each use.

\subsection{1. The existential box: a hypothesis check of Yarotsky's
theorem}\label{the-existential-box-a-hypothesis-check-of-yarotskys-theorem}

Notation as in \href{strong-coupling-uniform-gap.md}{the T2 note} §1:
the periodic lattice \(\Lambda=(\mathbb Z/N_s\mathbb Z)^3\), each link
based at its lower endpoint, \(\mathcal H_x=L^2(SU(3))^{\otimes3}\) for
the three links based at \(x\), \(\Omega_x\) the constant function, and
units \(\hbar c/a=1\) until restored. Set
\(\varepsilon=\frac{g^2}2\cdot\frac43=\frac{2g^2}3\),
\(\varepsilon'=(1-\eta_E)\varepsilon\),
\(w_p=\operatorname{tr}U_p+\operatorname{tr}U_p^\dagger\), so
\(\|w_p\|\le6\). Write \(\Lambda_q=\{-q,\dots,q\}^3\).

Hypotheses of the class \(\mathcal C_{\rm TI}(g,\eta,\eta_E,q)\),
\(q\ge1\):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{(E)} \(H_E'=\sum_\ell\sum_Re'(R)P^\ell_R\), where \(P^\ell_R\)
  is the Peter--Weyl isotypic projection of link \(\ell\),
  \(e'({\rm triv})=0\) and \(e'(R)\ge\varepsilon'\) for every nontrivial
  \(R\). The choice \(e'(R)=(1-\eta_E)\frac{g^2}2C_2(R)\) is an example.
\item
  \textbf{(D)} \(D=\sum_jd_j\) with each \(d_j\) bounded, self-adjoint,
  gauge invariant, and acting on a connected set \({\rm supp}\,d_j\) of
  at most \(q\) links; the family is invariant under lattice
  translations; and \(\sum_{j\ni\ell}\|d_j\|\le\eta\) for every link
  \(\ell\). The family may depend on \(N_s\).
\item
  \textbf{(V)} \(N_s\ge2q+1\), so every translate \(x+\Lambda_q\) embeds
  in the torus.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Grouping into site terms.} Let \(n_x(j)\) be the number of links
of \({\rm supp}\,d_j\) based at \(x\), and put
\[\delta_x=\sum_j\frac{n_x(j)}{|{\rm supp}\,d_j|}\,d_j.\] Then
\(\sum_x\delta_x=D\), because \(\sum_xn_x(j)=|{\rm supp}\,d_j|\). The
\(\delta_x\) are translates of \(\delta_0\), because the family and the
weights are translation covariant. Each \(j\) with \(n_x(j)>0\) contains
a link based at \(x\), so
\[\|\delta_x\|\le\sum_{i=1}^3\ \sum_{j\ni(x,x+e_i)}\|d_j\|\le3\eta .\] A
connected set of at most \(q\) links containing a link based at \(x\)
has all its vertices within graph distance \(q\) of \(x\), hence in
\(x+\Lambda_q\); so \(\delta_x\) acts on \(\mathcal H_{x+\Lambda_q}\).
Under (V), \(\delta_0\) lifts to a local term on \(\mathbb Z^3\) and the
interaction is the periodic restriction of a translation-invariant
interaction of range \(\Lambda_q\). Assign each plaquette \(p(x,i,j)\),
\(i<j\), to its corner \(x\), as in the T2 note; its links are based in
\(x+\{0,1\}^3\subseteq x+\Lambda_q\).

\emph{Normalized form.} Dividing by \(\varepsilon'\),
\[\frac H{\varepsilon'}=\sum_xh'_x+\sum_x\phi_x,\qquad
h'_x=\frac1{\varepsilon'}\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_Re'(R)P_R^{(x,x+e_i)},\qquad
\phi_x=-\frac1{\varepsilon'g^2}\sum_{i<j}w_{p(x,i,j)}-\frac{\delta_x}{\varepsilon'} .\]
Every link is based at exactly one site, every plaquette has one
assigned corner, and every \(d_j\) is shared out with total weight one,
so the sums reproduce \(H/\varepsilon'\). The hypotheses of Yarotsky's
Theorem 1, as recorded in the T2 note §2 (repository hypothesis check
read; Yarotsky, Commun. Math. Phys. 261, 799, Section 1 and Theorem 1 at
passage level recorded there), are met:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(h'_x\) is a function of the product Peter--Weyl partition of unity
  on \(\mathcal H_x\), which contains the projection onto \(\Omega_x\);
  \(h'_x\Omega_x=0\) since \(e'({\rm triv})=0\); on the orthogonal
  complement of \(\Omega_x\) some link carries a nontrivial
  representation, and all terms are nonnegative, so
  \(h'_x\ge\varepsilon'/\varepsilon'=1\).
\item
  \(\phi_x\) is a bounded symmetric form on \(\mathcal H_{x+\Lambda_q}\)
  with \(|\phi_x(v,v)|\le\beta\|v\|^2\) and \(\alpha=0\), where
  \[\beta=\frac{3\cdot6/g^2+3\eta}{\varepsilon'}
  =\frac1{1-\eta_E}\left(\frac{27}{g^4}+\frac{9\eta}{2g^2}\right).\]
\item
  Translation invariance, finite range \(\Lambda_q\) and the periodic
  cubic volume are as in item 5 of the T2 note.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proposition (existential target box).} Let
\(\beta^*_q=\beta_*(3,\Lambda_q)\) and \(\gamma_q=\gamma(3,\Lambda_q)\)
be the constants of Yarotsky's Theorem 1 for \(\nu=3\) and range
\(\Lambda_q\). If \(H\in\mathcal C_{\rm TI}(g,\eta,\eta_E,q)\) and
\[\frac{27}{g^4}+\frac{9\eta}{2g^2}\ \le\ (1-\eta_E)\,\beta^*_q,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\eta\ \le\ \frac{2(1-\eta_E)\beta^*_q}9\,g^2-\frac6{g^2},\] then for
every \(N_s\ge2q+1\) the operator \(H\) on \(L^2(SU(3)^{\mathcal E})\)
has a non-degenerate ground state \(\psi_0\), \(\psi_0\) is gauge
invariant, and
\[\Delta^{\rm phys}_{a,L}\ \ge\ \Delta_{a,L}\ \ge\ \gamma_q\,(1-\eta_E)\,\frac{2g^2}3\,\frac{\hbar c}a,\]
with \(\eta\) in units \(\hbar c/a\). The bound is uniform in \(N_s\).

\emph{Proof.} By items 1--3, Theorem 1 applies to \(H/\varepsilon'\)
with \(\alpha=0\) and \(\beta\le\beta^*_q\), volume by volume with the
same constants: it gives a non-degenerate ground state and a gap at
least \(\gamma_q\) for \(H/\varepsilon'\), hence at least
\(\gamma_q\varepsilon'\) for \(H\). Gauge transformations
\(\mathcal V_\gamma\), \(\gamma\in SU(3)^\Lambda\), commute with \(H\):
\(P_R^\ell\) commutes with left and right translations of \(U_\ell\),
\(w_p\) is the trace of a closed holonomy, and each \(d_j\) is gauge
invariant by (D). Thus \(\mathcal V_\gamma\psi_0=c(\gamma)\psi_0\) with
\(c\) a continuous homomorphism \(SU(3)^\Lambda\to U(1)\). Its
differential is a Lie-algebra homomorphism into the abelian
\(i\mathbb R\), so it vanishes on commutators; \(\mathfrak{su}(3)\) is
semisimple and equals its commutator algebra, so the differential is
zero, and \(c\equiv1\) on the connected group. The Haar average
\(P=\int\mathcal V_\gamma\,d\gamma\) projects onto the physical subspace
and commutes with the resolvent of \(H\), so that subspace reduces
\(H\), contains \(\psi_0\), and carries a spectrum contained in that of
\(H\). Hence \(\Delta^{\rm phys}_{a,L}\ge\Delta_{a,L}\). Restoring units
gives the display. \(\square\)

The same theorem gives a thermodynamic limit of the ground states with
exponentially decaying correlations of local observables (T2 note §2,
passage level). The positivity argument of T1 is not used, since a
general \(d_j\) need not preserve positivity.

\emph{Scope.} The Proposition covers the qualitative content of the
original class \(\mathcal C(g,\eta,\eta_E,q)\) under three added
hypotheses: translation covariance, connected supports (or any support
in a fixed translate of a finite set), and a link-local electric term. A
general diagonal \(E'(\sigma)\ge\varepsilon'|S(\sigma)|\), or
perturbations without translation invariance, would need either a
version of Yarotsky's theorem for such interactions or the explicit
expansion of §2. The dependence of \(\beta^*_q\) and \(\gamma_q\) on
\(q\) is not recorded in the statement; a decrease with the range is a
heuristic expectation, and the \(2^{-q}\) law of §3 is a property of the
formal count only.

\subsection{2. The perturbed continuous-time expansion
(formal)}\label{the-perturbed-continuous-time-expansion-formal}

This section keeps the original class \(\mathcal C(g,\eta,\eta_E,q)\):
units \(\hbar c/a=1\), physical sector, \(N_s\ge4\); \(H_E'\) gauge
invariant, diagonal in a Peter--Weyl basis of the physical subspace,
commuting with every support projection \(P_S\), with
\(E'(\emptyset)=0\) and \(E'(\sigma)\ge\varepsilon'|S(\sigma)|\); each
\(d_j\) bounded, self-adjoint, gauge invariant, on at most \(q\) links,
with local norm \(\sum_{j\ni\ell}\|d_j\|\le\eta\). Assume each \(d_j\)
centered, \(\langle\Omega_0,d_j\Omega_0\rangle=0\). Subtracting these
constants shifts \(H\) by a scalar and at most doubles \(\eta\). Without
it, a term acting away from the excited set contributes an insertion
with no energy damping.

Insert the projections \(P_S\) of the Kogut--Susskind note §1 between
the factors of the Duhamel series for \(e^{-t(H_E'-W-D)}\). A history is
a sequence of supports \(S_0=\emptyset,S_1,\dots,S_n=\emptyset\) with
insertions \(X_k\), each a plaquette term \(w_p/g^2\) or a term \(d_j\).
Since \(H_E'\ge\varepsilon'|S|\) on the range of \(P_S\) and
\(\|P_{S'}XP_S\|\le\|X\|\),
\[|w(\gamma)|\le\prod_{k=1}^n\|X_k\|\;e^{-\varepsilon'A(\gamma)},\qquad
A(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|S_k|x_k .\] Each \(P_S\) contains every
representation label with support \(S\), so the labels are summed inside
the operator norm and need no separate count. Gauge invariance of every
insertion keeps the history in the physical sector, so \(|S_k|\ge4\) for
\(0<k<n\) (Kogut--Susskind note, §1 correction, \(N_s\ge4\)). A
\(d\)-insertion changes the support only on its own links, so given
\(S_{k-1}\) there are at most \(2^q\) outcomes \(S_k\). By centering, a
\(d\)-insertion either touches \(S_{k-1}\) or excites a link of its
support. The hard-core polymer organization of these histories is
assumed as in that note §1; it is proposed there and not established.

\emph{Restricted count.} Root at a link \(\ell_0\), and keep the
histories whose first insertion contains \(\ell_0\) and whose later
insertions touch the current excited set. Write \(h=6e/g^2\), where the
factor \(e\) per insertion comes from the term \(e^{n}\) in the
Kotecký--Preiss weight \(e^{a(\gamma)}\), and
\(\delta'=\varepsilon'-\lambda=\theta\varepsilon'\) with
\(\lambda=(1-\theta)\varepsilon'\). A later step has at most
\(4|S_{k-1}|\) plaquettes touching \(S_{k-1}\), each with \(16\)
outcomes, and the \(d\)-terms containing a link of \(S_{k-1}\), with
weighted sum at most \(|S_{k-1}|\eta\) and \(2^q\) outcomes each.
Repeated use of the same plaquette or term is included in these sums.
The preceding interval integral is \(1/(\delta'|S_{k-1}|)\), so the
per-step factor is
\[u'=\frac{64h+e\,\eta\,2^q}{\delta'}=\frac{384e/g^2+e\,\eta\,2^q}{\delta'} .\]
The first step has the four plaquettes containing \(\ell_0\), for which
\(S_1\) is forced, and the \(d\)-terms containing \(\ell_0\), each with
up to \(2^q\) outcomes:
\[F'=4h+e\,\eta\,2^q=\frac{24e}{g^2}+e\,\eta\,2^q .\] With \(n\)
insertions the restricted sum is at most \(F'u'^{\,n-1}\), so the
majorant with marked-interval multiplier \(n-1\) obeys
\[B'_\lambda\ \le\ F'\sum_{m\ge1}m\,u'^{\,m}=\frac{F'u'}{(1-u')^2}\ \le\ 4F'u'
\qquad(u'\le\tfrac12).\]

\emph{Overlap conditions.} In the Kogut--Susskind note §2, each maximal
excitation interval begins at an insertion. A \(d\)-insertion touches up
to \(q\) links, so \(\sum_\ell r_\ell\le\max(4,q)\,n\), and the overlap
bound becomes \(Q'A(\gamma)+\frac{Q'}{4\delta'}\max(4,q)\,n(\gamma)\).
Comparing with \(a(\gamma)=n+\lambda A\), the sufficient conditions read
\[Q'\le\lambda,\qquad Q'\le\frac{4\delta'}{\max(4,q)},\] where \(Q'\) is
the all-roots majorant of that section with \(d\)-insertions included.

\textbf{Correction (2026-10-02), status of this section.} \(B'_\lambda\)
bounds only the restricted class. \(Q'\) also contains histories that
reach \(\ell_0\) after their first insertion (the missing roots of the
Kogut--Susskind note §3), and histories in which a plaquette or a
\(d\)-term starts a disjoint piece that is later bridged; the marked
general count for these is withdrawn there. Even with \(Q'\) controlled,
the spectral lemma of that note §4 needs a normalized true-vacuum
correlation bound for every bounded gauge-invariant observable, and none
is supplied. The former sentence ``then the gap is at least \(4\lambda\)
on the cyclic subspace of local observables'' is withdrawn: the
restricted count proves neither decay on that subspace nor a spectral
gap. The rate \(4\lambda=\frac83g^2(1-\eta_E)(1-\theta)\) is a candidate
rate.

\subsection{4. What the box asks of the intermediate
region}\label{what-the-box-asks-of-the-intermediate-region}

A renormalization step at scale \(a\) produces an effective Hamiltonian
at \(2a\). Suppose a scheme exists such that

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  each step is an exact low-energy reduction: \(E_0(a)<E_c(a)\), and
  below \(E_c(a)\) the spectrum of \(H(a)\), with multiplicities and
  after a common constant shift, coincides with that of
  \(H_{\rm eff}(2a)\);
\item
  the effective Hamiltonian stays in the class
  \(\mathcal C_{\rm TI}(g(a),\eta(a),\eta_E(a),q)\) with \(q\) fixed;
\item
  the coupling runs, \(g(2a)>g(a)\), and after finitely many steps, at a
  scale \(a_s\), reaches the region of the Proposition:
  \(27/g_s^4+9\eta_s/(2g_s^2)\le(1-\eta_{E,s})\beta^*_q\).
\end{enumerate}

Here \(\Delta\) is the gap in the sector on which the reduction acts
(full or physical; the Proposition bounds both). Item 1 gives
\(\Delta(H(a))\ge\min\{\Delta(H_{\rm eff}(2a)),\,E_c(a)-E_0(a)\}\): if
\(E_1(a)<E_c(a)\) the two lowest levels are shared and the gaps agree;
otherwise the gap is at least \(E_c(a)-E_0(a)\). Iterating from
\(a_0=a_{\rm UV}\) through \(a_k=2^ka_0\),
\[\Delta(H(a_{\rm UV}))\ \ge\ \min\Big\{\gamma_q(1-\eta_{E,s})\frac{2g_s^2}3\frac{\hbar c}{a_s},\
\min_{k<s}\big(E_c(a_k)-E_0(a_k)\big)\Big\},\] uniformly in the volume
over lattices whose final side is at least \(2q+1\). This is positive in
physical units when, as \(a_{\rm UV}\to0\), \(a_s\) stays bounded above
and the margins \(E_c-E_0\) stay bounded below in physical units; that
requirement is part of T3. Under items 1--3 at every scale of a
trajectory, the bare couplings on it have positive volume-uniform gaps,
the content of T2\('\) there. Items 1--3 in the weak region have
analogues in the constructive programme (Balaban's small-field effective
actions are the Euclidean analogue of item 2); in the intermediate
region they are the open problem.

The box makes the finish line definite and existential: the flow has to
arrive at couplings and correction sizes satisfying item 3. The formal
count of §3 suggests, without proof, \(g^2\sim10^2\) with corrections of
local norm about \(10^{-1}\,\hbar c/a\) for single-plaquette terms and
below \(10^{-2}\,\hbar c/a\) for two-plaquette terms. The repository's
only extracted value of Yarotsky's constant, \(\beta_*\simeq e^{-465}\)
