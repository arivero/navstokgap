\section{Ground-state measure transfer: the slice identity is exact, the
large-field estimate remains
open}\label{ground-state-measure-transfer-the-slice-identity-is-exact-the-large-field-estimate-remains-open}

Later corrections: \href{large-field-operator-inequality.md}{the
operator-inequality note} separates measure from operator control;
\href{flow-jacobian-truncation-error.md}{the corrected Jacobian note}
and \href{flow-instability-large-field.md}{instability note} withdraw
the Hamiltonian truncation and sharp-crossover claims;
\href{lattice-truncation-uniform.md}{the lattice note} treats a
different, single-step regime (relevant passages read).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Correction (2026-10-02).} The original title and lead claimed
that isotropic Wilson-measure estimates transfer verbatim to the
Kogut--Susskind ground state and that a normalized large-field bound
with running coupling is thereby available. Those claims are
\textbf{withdrawn}. The fixed-temporal-spacing slice identity is proved
below; its Wilson-to-Hamiltonian limit is conditional on ground-state
projector convergence, with no interchange of limits asserted. Section 3
retains a continuum action lower bound, not a probability bound. The
asserted universal constant \(c=8\pi^2\), the sharp crossover
\(\eta_*=2g^2/c\), and the claim that the good region is almost
everything at that crossover are withdrawn. The stray \(\cdot2/1\) in
the action display is replaced by its explicit coefficient \(2/g^2\).
Section 4 also incorporates the later corrections to the flow
normalization and to the distinction between kernel tails and
Hamiltonian errors.
\end{quote}

On a \textbf{fixed finite spatial lattice}, the square of the normalized
Perron vector of a positive transfer matrix is its infinite-time
Euclidean slice marginal. For the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian the
analogous identity holds directly for its heat semigroup. Identifying
these two marginals as the temporal spacing tends to zero requires a
separate convergence result. Neither identity transfers an estimate
proved only for an isotropic Wilson measure without control of that
limit. Neither identity proves a large-field estimate.

The available calculation here is instead the following: with the
continuum normalization \(S_E/\hbar=(4g^2)^{-1}\int|F|^2\), a
four-dimensional block of side \(\ell\) with root-mean-square field at
least \(\eta/\ell^2\) forces action at least \(\eta^2/(4g^2)\). This
scale cancellation does not control a normalized partition-function
ratio. The proposed suppression \(C\exp[-c\eta^2/g(\ell)^2]\) remains a
\textbf{heuristic target} for the interacting theory. No local
large-field closure, Hamiltonian decimation bound, or continuum mass gap
is proved.

\subsection{1. The transfer matrix and the two
limits}\label{the-transfer-matrix-and-the-two-limits}

Fix \(G=SU(N)\), \(g>0\), spatial spacing \(a>0\), and a finite periodic
spatial box. Let \(d\mu\) be normalized product Haar measure on its
spatial links. Write \(a_t\) for the temporal spacing \textbf{in length
units} (the physical time step is \(a_t/c\)), and \(\epsilon=a_t/a\). In
the convention of \href{mass-gap-obligations-lattice.md}{the obligations
map}, §1 (passage read), the anisotropic Wilson action is
\[\frac{S_E}{\hbar}=\frac{2}{g^2}\left[
\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{P\ \mathrm{temporal}}
\big(N-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_P\big)
+\epsilon\sum_{P\ \mathrm{spatial}}
\big(N-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_P\big)\right].\] The target
Hamiltonian, on the gauge-invariant subspace, is
\[H_{\rm KS}=\frac{\hbar c}{a}\left[
\frac{g^2}{2}\sum_e(-\Delta_e)
+\frac{2}{g^2}\sum_p\big(N-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p\big)
\right].\] The transfer-matrix references are Lüscher, \emph{Commun.
Math. Phys.} \textbf{54} (1977) 283--292, and Creutz, \emph{Phys.
Rev.~D} \textbf{15} (1977) 1128--1136 (both metadata level; not used
here as a checked proof of temporal-limit uniformity). A
character-expansion motivation of this Hamiltonian limit is not a
ground-state convergence theorem. The former formula
\(\mathcal T=\exp[-a_tH_{\rm KS}/\hbar]\big(1+O(a_t)\big)\) is
withdrawn: it omitted \(c\) with the length convention, specified no
operator topology, and supplied no spectral convergence control.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (fixed temporal spacing).} Fix \(a_t>0\). Assume
the physical transfer matrix \(\mathcal T_{a_t}\) is compact,
self-adjoint, nonnegative, and has a simple largest eigenvalue
\(\lambda_{a_t}>0\) with normalized positive eigenvector
\(\Omega_{a_t}\). These are the transfer-matrix hypotheses used below;
they are distinct from T1 for \(H_{\rm KS}\). For bounded multiplication
\(F\) and any boundary vector \(\chi\) with
\(\langle\Omega_{a_t},\chi\rangle\ne0\),
\[\langle\Omega_{a_t},F\Omega_{a_t}\rangle
=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\langle\mathcal T_{a_t}^{n}\chi,
F\mathcal T_{a_t}^{n}\chi\rangle}
{\|\mathcal T_{a_t}^{n}\chi\|^2}.\] For nonnegative \(\chi\) and the
Wilson transfer-kernel representation, the ratio is the expectation in
the anisotropic Euclidean slab of \(2n\) steps, with boundary weights
\(\chi\) and \(F\) inserted at the middle slice. Thus its limit is the
marginal \(|\Omega_{a_t}|^2d\mu\). For a general signed or complex
\(\chi\) the spectral formula still holds, but its boundary functional
need not be a positive Euclidean probability measure.

\emph{Proof.} Let \(P_{a_t}\) project onto \(\Omega_{a_t}\). Compactness
and simplicity give
\[r_{a_t}=\|\mathcal T_{a_t}(1-P_{a_t})\|/\lambda_{a_t}<1,
\qquad
\lambda_{a_t}^{-n}\mathcal T_{a_t}^n\chi
=P_{a_t}\chi+O(r_{a_t}^n\|\chi\|)\] in Hilbert norm. Since
\(P_{a_t}\chi\ne0\), the normalized ratio converges to the displayed
expectation. Integrating successive transfer kernels is the slab
functional by its construction. \(\square\)

\textbf{Hamiltonian identity and conditional Wilson limit.} The
finite-lattice \(H_{\rm KS}\) has a unique positive ground state
\(\Omega\) and a gap by T1 of \href{mass-gap-obligations-lattice.md}{the
obligations map}, §2 (passage read). Spectral projection therefore
gives, directly, \[\langle\Omega,F\Omega\rangle
=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}
\frac{\langle e^{-\tau H_{\rm KS}/\hbar}\chi,
F e^{-\tau H_{\rm KS}/\hbar}\chi\rangle}
{\|e^{-\tau H_{\rm KS}/\hbar}\chi\|^2},\] where \(\tau\) is physical
time and \(\langle\Omega,\chi\rangle\ne0\). This is an exact semigroup
identity, without invoking a Wilson limit.

To identify it with the preceding anisotropic Wilson marginal, a
sufficient additional hypothesis is
\[\|P_{a_t}-P_0\|\longrightarrow0\quad(a_t\downarrow0),
\qquad P_0=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|.\] Under this hypothesis the
positive normalized vectors converge in \(L^2(d\mu)\), and their
densities converge in \(L^1(d\mu)\), since
\[\big\||\Omega_{a_t}|^2-|\Omega|^2\big\|_1
\le2\|\Omega_{a_t}-\Omega\|_2.\] Consequently the \textbf{iterated}
limit, first \(n\to\infty\) at fixed \(a_t\), then \(a_t\downarrow0\),
equals \(\langle\Omega,F\Omega\rangle\) for every fixed bounded \(F\).
Projector convergence is not proved here. No interchange with the
reverse order, no joint limit, and no estimate uniform in spatial volume
or spacing is asserted.

\subsection{2. What transfers, and what does
not}\label{what-transfers-and-what-does-not}

At fixed \(a_t\), a probability bound for a property of a single slice
under the infinite-time \textbf{anisotropic} Wilson measure is exactly a
bound under \(|\Omega_{a_t}|^2d\mu\). Under the additional projector
convergence hypothesis, any bound
\[\int_A|\Omega_{a_t}|^2d\mu\le p\qquad(0<a_t<a_{t,0})\] for a fixed
measurable slice event \(A\), with the same \(p\), passes to
\(\int_A|\Omega|^2d\mu\le p\). One may also start with slab bounds
uniform in \(n\) and \(a_t\) and then take the indicated iterated
limits. If the event or the coupling varies in the limit, its
convergence must be controlled separately.

An isotropic estimate has \(a_t=a\), whereas the Hamiltonian limit takes
\(a_t/a\to0\) at fixed \(a\). It is not a bound for that family. Nor is
a four-dimensional block event automatically a property of its middle
slice: temporal coherence and a spatial-slice field constraint are
different events. A comparison of ensembles and observables is required.

Plaquette factorization of the unnormalized Wilson weight does not make
plaquettes independent: they share links, and normalization involves all
links. It does not supply local probability control. The
\href{magnetic-energy-identities.md}{identities note}, §§4--5 (passages
read), shows why one particular eigenvalue-equation comparison loses
local control through an extensive energy threshold. The present
identity provides another representation; it \textbf{does not close}
that missing local estimate or prove that Hamiltonian methods cannot
obtain one.

\subsection{3. Action cost at a block scale, not a probability
theorem}\label{action-cost-at-a-block-scale-not-a-probability-theorem}

\textbf{Proved continuum action bound.} For a smooth field with finite
Euclidean action in the stated normalization, let \(B\) be a
four-dimensional cube of volume \(\ell^4\) and define the
gauge-invariant root-mean-square field
\[\mathcal F_B=\left(\ell^{-4}\int_B|F|^2d^4x\right)^{1/2}.\] On
\(\mathcal F_B\ge\eta/\ell^2\), positivity gives
\[\frac{S_E}{\hbar}=\frac1{4g^2}\int|F|^2d^4x
\ge\frac1{4g^2}\int_B|F|^2d^4x
\ge\frac{\eta^2}{4g^2}.\] The norm sums the colour and ordered spacetime
indices in the action. The same argument applies to a flowed block
\(L^2\) constraint if the flow exists and decreases this action. It is
the lower-bound argument of \href{large-field-action-lower-bound.md}{the
action note}, §2 (passage read); its further sharpness and probability
claims are not used. There is no \(\ell\) in this bound. There is also
no lattice-spacing parameter in this continuum calculation: an exact
Wilson-lattice bound requires an explicit lattice field definition and
its action comparison. No supported constant-field sharpness or uniform
lattice result is inferred from the continuum display.

\textbf{Missing normalization.} For any finite regulated probability
measure \(d\nu\) and dimensionless action \(s\), the interacting law is
\(d\mathbb P=Z^{-1}e^{-s}d\nu\). An action bound \(s\ge q\) on \(A\)
gives only \[\mathbb P(A)=\frac{\int_Ae^{-s}d\nu}{Z}
\le\frac{e^{-q}\nu(A)}{Z}.\] A useful local estimate needs a lower bound
on the denominator relative to the constrained numerator, with
controlled boundary, entropy, volume and cutoff dependence. The action
bound alone supplies none. Accordingly
\[\mathbb P\left(\mathcal F_B\ge\frac{\eta}{\ell^2}\right)
\le C\exp\left[-\frac{c\eta^2}{g(\ell)^2}\right]\] is a
\textbf{heuristic target, not an available theorem in this note}.
Replacing bare \(g\) by running \(g(\ell)\) requires a renormalized
measure and observable comparison as well; it is not licensed by scale
cancellation. No citation checked here establishes this interacting
bound with the needed constants and limit uniformity.

\textbf{Free-field comparison, with its own observable.} Gaussian tails
do have quadratic exponents, but their constants depend on the
covariance, smearing and threshold. For a scalar Gaussian \(X\) of
variance \(\sigma^2\), \(\mathbb P(|X|\ge u)\le2e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}\)
by the exponential-moment bound. For the three-component, single-colour
abelian magnetic field with Gaussian heat smearing used in
\href{flowed-bound-free-field.md}{the free-field note}, §§1--3 (passages
read), \[\widetilde G_{ij}(k)=\frac{g^2|k|}{2}e^{-2t|k|^2}
(\delta_{ij}-\hat k_i\hat k_j).\] Taking its trace and radial integral
gives \[\langle|b_t|^2\rangle
=\frac{g^2}{2\pi^2}\int_0^\infty k^3e^{-2tk^2}dk
=\frac{g^2}{16\pi^2t^2}.\] Rotational invariance makes each component's
variance \(\sigma^2=g^2/(48\pi^2t^2)\), one third of this trace. Their
joint Gaussian law is \(\sigma^2 I_3\). Its radial density is
proportional to \(r^2e^{-r^2/(2\sigma^2)}\), so in the large-threshold
regime \[\log\mathbb P\left(|b_t|>\frac{\eta_t}{t}\right)
=-\frac{24\pi^2\eta_t^2}{g^2}
+O\big(\log(\eta_t/g)\big),\qquad \eta_t/g\to\infty.\] The polynomial
radial prefactor is not a constant uniform in threshold. The former
\(8\pi^2\) comparison from \href{typical-field-strength-window.md}{the
typical-field note}, §3 (passage read), treated the trace variance as a
scalar variance for the vector norm and cannot fix a constant here.
Moreover, with \(\ell^2=8t\), a threshold \(\eta_\ell/\ell^2\) means
\(\eta_t=\eta_\ell/8\); even this particular free-field logarithmic
exponent becomes \(-3\pi^2\eta_\ell^2/(8g^2)\). Neither value is a
sharp-block or an interacting constant. A sharp average uses a different
covariance; nonabelian field averaging also requires a gauge convention
or an invariant observable such as \(\mathcal F_B\).

At the cutoff scale compact Wilson plaquettes have bounded action. That
fact gives no useful normalized block-coherence estimate, and
\(\ell\gg a\) alone does not prove one either.

\subsection{4. Where this leaves the
route}\label{where-this-leaves-the-route}

\textbf{Correction to the former competition.} Neither Proposition 1 nor
Section 3 controls the decimation error outside a proved rare set. The
assertion that a crossover \(\eta_*=2g^2/c\) makes the good region
almost everything is withdrawn. Even assuming the old bound with unit
prefactor, its value there was \(e^{-4g^2/c}\to1\): an upper bound
tending to one establishes no rarity. It also does not prove that the
actual bad-set probability tends to one.

The corrected \href{flow-jacobian-truncation-error.md}{Jacobian note},
§§2--3 (full-read), gives an \textbf{absolute kernel-tail bound for a
specified auxiliary covariant linear evolution}, under smoothness and
bounded curvature hypotheses. With its full curvature block norm
\(\Gamma(s)\), its budget is \[A_t=2\int_0^t\Gamma(s)ds,\qquad
\delta(R,t)\le e^{A_t}\left[
\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt2\kappa)
+\frac{2\sqrt2\kappa}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-2\kappa^2}\right],
\qquad R=\kappa\sqrt{8t}.\] If \(\Gamma(s)\le\eta/\ell^2\) throughout
\(0\le s\le t\) and \(\ell^2=8t\), then \(A_t\le\eta/4\), not \(2\eta\).
A lower bound on a block RMS field does not give this curvature supremum
upper bound. Even a proved probability bound for \(\mathcal F_B\)
therefore would not supply this kernel budget without an additional
comparison.

Under the further hypothetical bound \(\mathbb P(A_\eta)\le
C e^{-c\eta^2/g^2}\) and a controlled corresponding growth factor
\(e^{\eta/4}\), the product has schematic exponent
\(-c\eta^2/g^2+\eta/4\), whose algebraic zero is \(g^2/(4c)\). This is
bookkeeping of additional assumptions, \textbf{not a sharp crossover or
a probability-weighted Hamiltonian error theorem}. A tail bound for
\(\mathcal F_B\) alone does not justify that product, particularly when
curvature is unbounded on the event.

For finite \(A_t\), increasing the range \(\kappa\) controls the
displayed kernel majorant. This is the surviving range observation
behind \href{large-field-operator-inequality.md}{the operator-inequality
note}, §1 (passage read), which supersedes the fixed-range competition.
It does not prove a relative form bound for a conjugated Hamiltonian.
Measure rarity also does not alone control a perturbation of
unrestricted sign: that note's min-max and localized trial-state
arguments, §3 (full-read), distinguish positive errors from negative
wells. The later \href{flow-instability-large-field.md}{instability
correction}, §§2 and 4 (passages read), supplies no sharp lower bound on
truncation tails. The separate single-lattice-step discussion in
\href{lattice-truncation-uniform.md}{the lattice note}, §§1--2 (passages
read), must not be read as a fixed-physical-scale continuum estimate or
as a repair of these missing operator comparisons.

\subsection{5. Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}

The usable result is the fixed-temporal-spacing spectral slice identity
and its exact Hamiltonian-semigroup counterpart. Passing from the
anisotropic Wilson family to the Kogut--Susskind marginal remains
conditional here on projector convergence. Transferring a local bound
then requires that bound for the same slice observable, uniformly in
that family and in every further limit claimed.

The scale-invariant continuum action lower bound survives. The
normalized interacting large-field estimate with running coupling, the
import of isotropic estimates verbatim, and the claimed Hamiltonian
decimation closure are withdrawn; the probability estimate is retained
only as a heuristic target. The next required estimates are the
normalized anisotropic slice bound with controlled limits and, for a
Hamiltonian comparison, a signed operator or relative form bound. No
advance on the continuum existence or physical mass gap follows from the
marginal identity alone.
