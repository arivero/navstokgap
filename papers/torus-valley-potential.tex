\hypertarget{one-loop-torus-valleys-local-confinement-periodicity-and-spectral-limits}{%
\section{One-loop torus valleys: local confinement, periodicity and
spectral
limits}\label{one-loop-torus-valleys-local-confinement-periodicity-and-spectral-limits}}

\textbf{Sector-gap result.} For the explicitly specified flat-holonomy
benchmark \(H_{\rm hol}=(\hbar c/L)[-(g^2/2)\Delta_b+2\Phi(b)/\pi^2]\)
on the \(4\pi\) torus, retaining centre transformations as physical
symmetries, a centre-odd Weyl-even trial state gives
\[\Delta_{\rm hol}\le\frac{\hbar c}{L}
\frac{32C_\psi}{\pi^2v_0}\,g^{8/3}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\pi\sqrt{v_0}}{2g}\right]\] for all sufficiently
small \(g>0\). The fixed positive barrier \(v_0\) and trial constant
\(C_\psi\) are defined in Section 4. This rules out a uniform positive
\(g^{2/3}\hbar c/L\) lower bound for that benchmark's full Weyl-even
spectrum. It does not identify the benchmark with the compressed
field-theory Hamiltonian or prove its physical gap.

For a constant abelian \(SU(2)\) background on \(T^3_L\), the one-loop
zero-point energy defined by discarding the formal \(m=0\) Fourier term
in the Poisson-summed expression (distinct from the physical \(k=0\)
oscillator) is \[U(a)=\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\Phi(La),\qquad
\Phi(b)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{1-\cos(m\cdot b)}{|m|^4}.\] Here \([a]=L^{-1}\). This nonnegative
bounded periodic function has the local expansion
\[U(a)=2\hbar c|a|+\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\hbar cL|a|^2
+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3).\] The first term is the four transverse zero-mode
oscillators' energy; the quadratic correction is useful input to a
small-volume comparison. The formula is exact for this specified
one-loop prescription, not for the interacting ground-state energy. A
local Taylor expansion supplies neither the global operator bound nor
the nonzero-mode estimates in
\href{weak-coupling-feshbach-reduction.md}{the Feshbach reduction}.

The derivation below retains the frequency count, Fourier normalization
and symmetry argument. Weiss (\emph{Phys. Rev.~D} \textbf{24} (1981)
475, metadata) and Lüscher (1983, abstract) are historical source leads,
not independent checks of this calculation. No novelty or full
field-theory gap is claimed.

\hypertarget{frequencies-in-a-constant-abelian-background}{%
\subsection{1. Frequencies in a constant abelian
background}\label{frequencies-in-a-constant-abelian-background}}

Let \(a_i=\alpha_iT^3\), \(i=1,2,3\), with \(\vec\alpha\in\mathbb R^3\),
and write \(a\) for \(\vec\alpha\) when no confusion arises. Decompose
the adjoint fluctuation
\(\tilde A_i=\tilde A_i^3T^3+\tilde A_i^+T^++\tilde A_i^-T^-\). The
covariant derivative \(D_j=\partial_j+[a_j,\cdot]\) acts on the neutral
component as \(\partial_j\) and on the charged components as
\(\partial_j\pm i\alpha_j\), so on the Fourier mode \(e^{ik\cdot x}\),
\(k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3\), the charged components have covariant
momentum \(k\pm a\). The quadratic form
\(\frac1{2g^2}\int|D_i\tilde A_j-D_j\tilde A_i|^2\) is, for a constant
abelian background, the free transverse form with \(k\) replaced by
\(k\pm a\) on the charged components, so after the usual transverse
gauge choice the frequencies are
\[\hbar\omega^{(0)}_k=\hbar c|k|\quad(\text{neutral, two polarizations}),\]
\[\hbar\omega^{(\pm)}_k=\hbar c|k\pm a|\quad(\text{charged, two polarizations each}),\]
away from shifted zero frequencies. At \(k=0\) and \(a\ne0\), the
background-covariant transverse condition \(a\cdot\tilde A^{\pm}=0\)
leaves two charged spatial polarizations; the neutral components are the
valley directions themselves: with \(a=\alpha_1T^3\) along \(x_1\) only,
these are the charged parts of \(\tilde A_2,\tilde A_3\), four real
oscillators of angular frequency \(c|a|\), while the charged parts of
\(\tilde A_1\) are gauge directions removed by Gauss's law. Enhanced
zero modes at \(a=0\) or shifted momenta require their own treatment;
this local oscillator count is not a uniform projection across them.

\textbf{The \(k=0\) term is C133.} The SU(2) matrix model of C133 with
\(\vec x_1=\lambda\hat n\), \(\vec x_2=\vec x_3=0\) has potential
\(\tfrac12g^2(|\vec x_1\times\vec x_2|^2+|\vec x_1\times\vec x_3|^2+\cdots)\),
whose transverse Hessian at that point gives the components of
\(\vec x_2,\vec x_3\) orthogonal to \(\hat n\), four oscillators of
frequency \(g|\lambda|/\sqrt m\); in the field-theory variables of
\href{low-dimensional-mass-gap.md}{G07} Proposition 7
(\(m=\hbar L^3/(g^2c)\), \(g_B^2=\hbar cL^3/g^2\)) this frequency is
\(c|a|\). The zero-point energy \(4\cdot\tfrac12\hbar c|a|=2\hbar c|a|\)
is the confining term of G07 Theorem 6(1) and the floor of G08 Theorem 2
along the valley.

\hypertarget{poisson-summation}{%
\subsection{2. Poisson summation}\label{poisson-summation}}

The zero-point energy of the fluctuations relative to \(a=0\), summing
two polarizations and both charges, is
\[U(a)=\frac{\hbar c}2\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}\sum_{\rm pol=1,2}\big(|k+a|+|k-a|-2|k|\big) =\hbar c\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}\big(|k+a|+|k-a|-2|k|\big),\]
where the \(k=0\) term is \(2\hbar c|a|\) and the neutral modes cancel.
The raw summands are nonnegative and of order \(|a|^2/|k|\); their
unregularized sum diverges. Periodicity of a regulated sum alone does
not justify exchanging this sum and subtraction. We use the specific
Fourier finite-part prescription that discards the \(m=0\) term below.
It restores periodicity by momentum reindexing and fixes the displayed
one-loop potential; a different prescription needs its own matching. For
\(f(k)=|k|\) the distributional Fourier transform is
\(\hat f(x)=\int|k|e^{-ik\cdot x}d^3k=-8\pi/|x|^4\) for \(x\ne0\) (the
formula
\(\widehat{|k|^\alpha}=2^{\alpha+3}\pi^{3/2}\frac{\Gamma((3+\alpha)/2)}{\Gamma(-\alpha/2)}|x|^{-3-\alpha}\)
at \(\alpha=1\), with \(\Gamma(-\tfrac12)=-2\sqrt\pi\)), and
\[\sum_{k\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3}f(k+a)=\Big(\frac L{2\pi}\Big)^3\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^3}\hat f(mL)\,e^{im\cdot La}\]
with the \(m=0\) symbol a prescription-dependent subtraction, not a
separately evaluable distributional Fourier value. Hence
\[U(a)=\hbar c\Big(\frac L{2\pi}\Big)^3\sum_{m\ne0}\Big(-\frac{8\pi}{L^4|m|^4}\Big)\big(e^{im\cdot La}+e^{-im\cdot La}-2\big) =\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\sum_{m\ne0}\frac{1-\cos(m\cdot La)}{|m|^4}=\frac{2\hbar c}{\pi^2L}\Phi(La).\]
Matching a lattice dispersion to this continuum one-loop expression
requires a separate cutoff estimate. No uniform relative
\(O(a_{\rm lat}^2/L^2)\) error follows merely from the nonzero Fourier
indices.

Properties of \(\Phi\): it is nonnegative, periodic with period \(2\pi\)
in each component, invariant under the cubic group, bounded by
\(2\sum'|m|^{-4}\), and zero exactly on \(2\pi\mathbb Z^3\).

\hypertarget{expansion-at-the-origin-and-the-sign-of-the-correction}{%
\subsection{3. Expansion at the origin and the sign of the
correction}\label{expansion-at-the-origin-and-the-sign-of-the-correction}}

\(\Phi\) is not smooth at \(0\). Its Laplacian is the periodic Green's
function \(G(b)=\sum'_me^{im\cdot b}|m|^{-2}\), which has the Coulomb
singularity \(2\pi^2/|b|\) at the origin (from
\(\int d^3m\,e^{im\cdot b}|m|^{-2}\)) and a finite remainder:
\(G(b)=2\pi^2/|b|+C_M+O(|b|^2)\), where \(C_M\) is the constant term of
the periodic Coulomb Green's function of the cubic lattice, the
analytically continued Epstein sum \(\sum'_m|m|^{-2}\). Since
\(\Delta(\pi^2|b|)=2\pi^2/|b|\), the function \(\Phi-\pi^2|b|\) has
Laplacian \(C_M+O(|b|^2)\) near \(0\), and the only quadratic form with
cubic symmetry and Laplacian \(C_M\) is \(\tfrac16C_M|b|^2\); so
\[\Phi(b)=\pi^2|b|+\tfrac16C_M|b|^2+O(|b|^3),\qquad
U(a)=2\hbar c|a|+\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\,\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3).\]
The linear term \(2\hbar c|a|\) is the \(k=0\) contribution of Section
1, so the nonzero modes contribute
\[U_\perp(a)=U(a)-2\hbar c|a|=\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3),\]
with \(C_M<0\) for the cubic lattice (lattice-sum tables; the sign is
not re-derived here and affects only the direction of a subleading
correction). In the rescaled variables \(a=g^{2/3}\xi/L\) this is
\(\frac{C_M}{3\pi^2}g^{4/3}|\xi|^2\,\hbar c/L\), of relative order
\(g^{2/3}\) to the zero-mode Hamiltonian \(g^{2/3}h_3\,\hbar c/L\): the
size that (H3) of the formal local power counting suggests for the
Feshbach reduction. The coefficient is identified for this one-loop
prescription, not for the full compressed-operator gap hypothesis (H3).

\hypertarget{a.-general-constant-backgrounds-at-quadratic-order}{%
\subsection{3a. General constant backgrounds at quadratic
order}\label{a.-general-constant-backgrounds-at-quadratic-order}}

The following extension is conditional: suppose a specified gauge-fixed
nonzero-mode determinant, including its longitudinal and ghost
contributions, defines a smooth even gauge-invariant one-loop function
near the trivial background and is matched to the same Fourier
prescription as the abelian calculation. An unfixed Hessian at a general
noncommuting off-shell background does not automatically have these
properties. Under that assumption its quadratic term is constrained by
symmetry. It is invariant under the constant gauge transformations,
which rotate the three colour vectors \(\vec a_i\) simultaneously, under
the cubic group acting on the spatial index, and under \(a\to-a\). The
quadratic invariants of \((\vec a_1,\vec a_2,\vec a_3)\) under
\(SO(3)_{\rm colour}\) are the bilinears \(\vec a_i\cdot\vec a_j\), and
the only cubic-invariant symmetric form in the spatial indices is
\(\delta_{ij}\), so
\[U_\perp(a)=c\,\hbar cL\sum_{i=1}^3|\vec a_i|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3),\]
with a single pure number \(c\), which the abelian valley
(\(\vec a_i=\alpha_i\hat e_3\), \(\sum_i|\vec a_i|^2=|\alpha|^2\))
determines: \(c=C_M/(3\pi^2)\). The Landau-level structure of
non-commuting backgrounds cannot change this quadratic invariant within
the same smooth one-loop prescription. The remainder is at least cubic
in this local estimate. This identifies a candidate coefficient for
(H3); symmetry does not establish a cutoff-uniform expansion, a global
relative bound or the gap of the compressed operator.

\hypertarget{b.-the-abelian-theory-in-the-same-expansion}{%
\subsection{3b. The abelian theory in the same
expansion}\label{b.-the-abelian-theory-in-the-same-expansion}}

For compact \(U(1)\) the constant modes commute, the quartic potential
vanishes, and the photons are neutral, so the one-loop potential of the
holonomy is identically zero: the constant-mode Hamiltonian is the free
Laplacian on the torus of holonomies,
\(H_0=\frac{e^2c}{2\hbar L^3}|p|^2\) with \(a\) periodic of period
\(2\pi/L\), spectrum \(e^2\hbar c|n|^2/(2L)\), \(n\in\mathbb Z^3\), gap
\(e^2\hbar c/(2L)\). The abelian small-volume gap is linear in \(e^2\),
not \(e^{2/3}\), because there is no commutator to confine the holonomy
and no charged mode to give it a potential; with \(e\) fixed by the
absence of running, the \(1/L\) decay is uncompensated and the
infinite-volume theory is gapless, in agreement with the Coulomb phase
of Guth and Fröhlich--Spencer. In the nonabelian matrix model, the
\(g^{2/3}\) scale comes from the kinetic/quartic commutator balance.
Running of the field-theory coupling requires a general-background
renormalization and matching calculation; it cannot be read from the
commuting-valley function \(U\) alone.

\hypertarget{saturation-and-central-holonomies}{%
\subsection{4. Saturation and central
holonomies}\label{saturation-and-central-holonomies}}

The full one-loop potential satisfies
\(0\le U\le2\hbar c\max\Phi/(\pi^2L)=O(\hbar c/L)\), and its minima are
at \(La\in2\pi\mathbb Z^3\). For \(SU(2)\) with adjoint charges
\(\pm1\), \(La=2\pi e_i\) gives fundamental holonomy
\(\exp(2\pi iT^3)=-1\). With strictly periodic gauge transformations
these are distinct central holonomy configurations. Strictly periodic
\(SU(2)\) gauge transformations identify covering shifts by \(4\pi/L\)
(and Weyl reflection), so the infinitely many covering minima represent
\(2^3\) central holonomy triples. If transformations periodic only up to
the centre are instead gauged, the identification and allowed sectors
change. A configuration is not itself a quantum state.

When centre transformations are global symmetries, their characters on
three torus cycles label electric-flux sectors: \(\mathbb Z_N^3\) gives
\(8\) labels for \(SU(2)\) and \(27\) for \(SU(3)\). The latter has
three central holonomies per direction. These labels and the one-loop
barrier scale \(O(\hbar c/L)\) determine neither spectral splittings nor
spectral weights. Tunnelling and electric-flux interpretations require
the corresponding Hamiltonian, boundary conventions and estimates; they
are not derived by counting minima.

In the isolated matrix model, balancing kinetic and quartic terms gives
\(La\sim g^{2/3}\) and the energy scale \(g^{2/3}\hbar c/L\). Its linear
valley term is the local limit of \(U\), whereas \(U\) itself saturates
and is periodic. Crucially, \(U_\perp=U-2\hbar c|a|\) is \textbf{not}
bounded or periodic on the covering space: the subtracted linear term
grows. The boundedness of \(U\) therefore cannot establish a global
perturbation bound for \(U_\perp\) relative to the confining matrix
operator. Linear confinement may control \(|a|\) and allow some form
bound; the small relative bound needed for a gap comparison has not been
supplied. Nor does a local quadratic estimate control arbitrarily large
\(|\xi|\) after \(a=g^{2/3}\xi/L\).

Localization near one minimum and an expansion of the full field-theory
gap require the Feshbach hypotheses and a global treatment of the
holonomy domain. The informal comparison \(g(L)^{2/3}\sim1\) marks the
loss of a small parameter; it proves no delocalization, sector
coalescence or crossover at a specified value of
\(z=\Delta L/(\hbar c)\).

\hypertarget{a-centre-odd-variational-countertest}{%
\subsubsection{A centre-odd variational
countertest}\label{a-centre-odd-variational-countertest}}

The distinction can be proved without calculating a tunnelling
asymptotic. Take the declared scalar operator
\[h_g=-\frac{g^2}{2}\Delta_b+W(b),\qquad
W(b)=\frac{2}{\pi^2}\Phi(b),\qquad
b\in\mathbb T_{4\pi}^3=(\mathbb R/4\pi\mathbb Z)^3,\] on flat
\(L^2(d^3b)\) with its periodic \(H^2\) domain, and set
\(H_{\rm hol}=(\hbar c/L)h_g\). Its Weyl-even subspace consists of
\(f(-b)=f(b)\). Centre translations are \((T_if)(b)=f(b+2\pi e_i)\), not
gauge identifications in this model. These commuting involutions commute
with \(h_g\) and with Weyl reflection. The nonnegative bounded
continuous \(W\) has exactly eight zeros
\(Z=(2\pi\mathbb Z)^3\bmod4\pi\), and \(W(b)\le C_W|b|\) near zero. No
nonzero-mode, orbit-measure or Berry term is included in this benchmark;
matching those terms is a separate field-theory obligation. Rayleigh
quotients below use the closed quadratic form
\(q_g[v]=(g^2/2)\int|\nabla v|^2+\int W|v|^2\), with domain \(H^1\); the
Lipschitz cutoffs need not belong to the operator's \(H^2\) domain.

Let \(e_0(g)\) and \(\Omega_g>0\) be the normalized ground eigenpair.
Compactness and positivity improvement give a simple ground state, fixed
by every \(T_i\) and by Weyl reflection. Its energy is also the lowest
energy in the Weyl-even subspace. Fix a normalized
\(\psi\in C_c^\infty(B_1(0))\), and put
\[C_\psi=\frac12\int|\nabla\psi|^2d^3z
+C_W\int|z|\,|\psi(z)|^2d^3z.\] For \(s=g^{2/3}\) small enough that
\(W(b)\le C_W|b|\) on \(B_s\), the normalized trial
\(s^{-3/2}\psi(b/s)\) gives \[0\le e_0(g)\le C_\psi g^{2/3}. \tag{V1}\]
Indeed its kinetic energy is \(g^2s^{-2}\int|\nabla\psi|^2/2\) and its
potential energy is at most \(C_Ws\int|z||\psi|^2\). This is a
ground-energy upper bound, not an excitation-gap estimate.

Use the flat torus distance, let \(A\) be the union of the eight closed
balls of radius \(r=\pi/4\) about \(Z\), and define the fixed constant
\[v_0=\min_{\operatorname{dist}(b,Z)\ge\pi/4}W(b)>0.\] Assume \(g\) is
small enough for (V1) and \(e_0\le v_0/4\). The Lipschitz weight
\[\rho(b)=\frac{\sqrt{v_0}}2\operatorname{dist}(b,A)\] vanishes on
\(A\). The
\href{ground-state-measure-transfer.md\#weighted-estimates-and-global-magnetic-tails}{weighted
eigenfunction identity}, with kinetic coefficient \(g^2/2\) and weight
\(\rho/g\), reads \[\frac{g^2}{2}\int|\nabla(e^{\rho/g}\Omega_g)|^2
+\int\left(W-e_0-\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{2}\right)
 e^{2\rho/g}\Omega_g^2=0.\] On \(A^c\), its second coefficient is at
least \(v_0-v_0/4-v_0/8\ge v_0/2\); on \(A\) it is at least \(-e_0\).
Consequently
\[\int_{A^c}e^{2\rho/g}\Omega_g^2\le\frac{2e_0}{v_0}. \tag{V2}\] All
constants here are fixed by this compact benchmark, not uniform
field-theory or continuum estimates.

Choose a real periodic Lipschitz \(f=f(b_1)\) with plateaus \(+1\) about
\(b_1=0\) and \(-1\) about \(b_1=2\pi\). Interpolate linearly across the
two strips of half-width \(\delta=\pi/4\) centered at \(\pi,3\pi\). Then
\(f(b_1+2\pi)=-f(b_1)\), \(f(-b_1)=f(b_1)\), \(|f'|\le4/\pi\), and
\(f^2=1\) off their union \(B\). Every point of \(B\) has distance at
least \(\pi/2\) from \(A\), so (V2) gives
\[p_B:=\int_B\Omega_g^2\le\frac{2e_0}{v_0}
 e^{-\pi\sqrt{v_0}/(2g)}\le\frac12.\]

The vector \(f\Omega_g\) is Weyl-even and has centre characters
\((-,+,+)\). It is orthogonal to \(\Omega_g\) by the \(T_1\) symmetry,
and the exact ground-state transform gives
\[\frac{q_g[f\Omega_g]-e_0\|f\Omega_g\|^2}
 {\|f\Omega_g\|^2}
=\frac{g^2}{2}\frac{\int|\nabla f|^2\Omega_g^2}{\int f^2\Omega_g^2}
\le\frac{8g^2}{\pi^2}\frac{p_B}{1-p_B}
\le\frac{32g^2e_0}{\pi^2v_0}
 e^{-\pi\sqrt{v_0}/(2g)}.\] Rayleigh--Ritz and (V1) prove the displayed
physical bound for the gap above the vacuum in the full Weyl-even
benchmark. They also upper-bound the lowest \((-,+,+)\) sector energy
above \(E_0\). In particular
\[\frac{L\Delta_{\rm hol}}{\hbar c\,g^{2/3}}
\le\frac{32C_\psi}{\pi^2v_0}g^2e^{-\pi\sqrt{v_0}/(2g)}\longrightarrow0.\]
This is an upper bound, not a matching tunnelling formula. \(\square\)

Even cubic symmetry alone does not remove this benchmark trial. The
three vectors \(f(b_i)\Omega_g\) have different centre characters and
are mutually orthogonal, also for \(q_g-e_0\). Cubic symmetry gives them
equal norms and form energies. Their sum is invariant under coordinate
permutations and sign changes and has the same Rayleigh quotient. Thus
the bound also holds in the cubic-invariant subspace when all centre
characters are admitted. This sum has no trivial-centre component,
although it has no single charged character. The calculation concerns
the declared scalar symmetry action, not an imported classification of
the full gauge-theory levels.

The trial is excluded if centre translations are gauged or the Hilbert
space is restricted to the trivial centre character. That exclusion
alone proves no \(g^{2/3}\) lower bound in the remaining space. Thus H3
must name its sector, global holonomy domain, measure and projection; a
local matrix spacing cannot stand for every centre sector. Nor does this
finite-volume benchmark settle any infinite-volume gap: \(g(L)\), the
full operator and the order of limits must first be identified. The
vacuum subspace cyclic for a centre-neutral local observable algebra
must also be distinguished from the full finite-box Gauss-invariant
space; the charged trial is not produced by a neutral multiplication
observable.

\textbf{Source boundary.} The separation of channel energies and
electric-flux splittings is established literature, not a novelty claim.
Lüscher--Münster's 1984 publisher abstract defines its \(z\) using the
\(J^p=0^+\) sector. Weisz--Ziemann, \emph{Nucl. Phys. B} \textbf{284}
(1987) 157--170,
\href{https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1986/desy86-109.pdf}{printed
p.~168}, passage-read, discusses electric-flux sectors, perturbative
degeneracy and tunnelling. The Koller--van Baal calculation cited there
has not been read here. The bound above is a written derivation for the
declared scalar benchmark, not a verification of that field-theory
calculation.

\hypertarget{consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{5. Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

The explicit one-loop input is a periodic bounded \(U\) with central
minima and a local correction
\(U_\perp=(C_M/(3\pi^2))\hbar cL|a|^2+O(\hbar cL^2|a|^3)\). The symmetry
argument identifies its quadratic form under the stated
smooth-prescription assumption. Global (H3), nonzero-mode comparison,
\(SU(3)\) matrix control, cutoff removal and physical spectral transport
remain separate. The centre-odd variational bound additionally shows why
the global benchmark gap differs from a local matrix spacing; H3 must
specify its centre sector. The abelian comparison alone proves no
nonabelian continuum gap.
