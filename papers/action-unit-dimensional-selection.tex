\hypertarget{classical-action-selection-units-symmetries-and-orbital-thresholds}{%
\section{Classical action selection: units, symmetries and orbital
thresholds}\label{classical-action-selection-units-symmetries-and-orbital-thresholds}}

Classical mechanics admits positive actions accumulating at zero even in
one fixed smooth, confining, force-bounded Hamiltonian. Positive orbital
thresholds survive under additional excitation or singular-core
assumptions: a regular closed orbit with speed at least \(v_*>0\) and
force at most \(F_{\max}\) has the sharp bound
\(J\ge m^2v_*^3/F_{\max}\); the relativistic Coulomb model has
\(|L|>k/c\) precisely on its complete collision-free bound domain. These
are conditional classical bounds. Neither identifies a universal scale
with physical \(\hbar\) or constructs quantum states, dynamics or
instruments.

The logical order is to specify the observable and admitted class,
determine its fixed dimensional constants, test its scaling symmetries,
and only then ask which dynamical restriction excludes zero. This
treatment incorporates C052--C061 and Q14. The written proofs and
historical model/coordinator checks are internal checks; the dimensional
and orbital ingredients are established mathematics, and no literature
novelty is claimed.

\hypertarget{dimensional-necessity-and-a-fixed-coulomb-unit}{%
\subsection{Dimensional necessity and a fixed Coulomb
unit}\label{dimensional-necessity-and-a-fixed-coulomb-unit}}

Specify positive fixed constants \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\), admitted masses,
preparations and solutions. Let \(d_i\in\mathbb R^3\) be their
mass--length--time dimension vectors, \(d_A=(1,2,-1)\) the action
vector, and \(\mathcal A(c)\) the set of attained nonnegative actions.
Define

\[g(c)=\inf\{A\in\mathcal A(c):A>0\},\]

with \(g=\infty\) if no positive action is attained. Assume dimensional
homogeneity and that the infimum ranges over every non-fixed mass and
preparation parameter. For a change of units \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)^3\),

\[\mathcal A(\lambda^{d_1}c_1,\ldots,\lambda^{d_r}c_r)
 =\lambda^{d_A}\mathcal A(c).\]

\textbf{Dimensional theorem.} If \(d_A\) is outside the span of the
\(d_i\), then \(g\in\{0,\infty\}\). Otherwise, for
\(d_A=\sum_i a_i d_i\),

\[g(c)=\left(\prod_i c_i^{a_i}\right)F(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_s),\]

where the \(\pi_j\) are a basis of dimensionless products and \(F\)
takes values in \([0,\infty]\). If the \(d_i\) are independent, \(F\) is
a constant.

\emph{Proof.} Write \(\lambda=e^\ell\). In the first case choose
\(\ell\) orthogonal to every \(d_i\) but with \(\ell\cdot d_A\ne0\).
Constants remain fixed while \(g=e^{t\ell\cdot d_A}g\) for all real
\(t\), forcing zero or infinity. In the second case divide \(g\) by the
displayed action product; the quotient is invariant under unit changes
and hence depends only on the dimensionless products. This is the
Buckingham reduction. \(\square\)

Replacing \(d_A\) by any observable dimension \(d_X\) gives the general
floor/unit theorem C131 with the same universal-infimum hypotheses and
proof. The similarity argument below likewise applies to that
observable.

For the specified constant set \(k_e=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)\), \(c\) and
\(G\), the dimension vectors are \((1,3,-2)\), \((0,1,-1)\) and
\((-1,3,-2)\). Solving

\[a(1,3,-2)+b(0,1,-1)+\gamma(-1,3,-2)=(1,2,-1)\]

gives uniquely \(a=1\), \(b=-1\), \(\gamma=0\). Thus \(k_e/c\) is the
unique monomial action unit for this constant set; \(G,c\) alone supply
none. Including a fixed mass adds \(Gm^2/c\). A fixed length, mass and
\(c\) supply \(m_0c\ell_0\). These statements depend on the declared
constants, not on an exhaustive list of what classical physics could
contain.

For a chosen elementary charge, \(k_e/c=\alpha\hbar\), with
\(\alpha=e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c)\). This is a comparison of units
with physical \(\hbar\). Fixing a nonzero charge product supplies
\(k/c\) whether or not a theory quantizes charge. Charge quantization is
an additional physical statement and does not follow from this
dimensional argument. The normalized radiation unit is constructed
separately in \href{necessity-unit-and-indeterminacy.md}{unit and
indeterminacy}.

\hypertarget{a-supplied-least-length-with-gravity}{%
\subsubsection{A supplied least length with
gravity}\label{a-supplied-least-length-with-gravity}}

The declared constants \((G,c,\ell)\) admit the action monomial
\(c^3\ell^2/G\). A least-length premise therefore supplies an action
unit; it does not prove a positive infimum of any specified action
observable. Setting it equal to physical \(\hbar\) is equivalent to
supplying \(\ell=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}\). Newton's optical fit interval,
approximately \(1/89000\) inch, would instead give order \(10^{22}\) J s
in this formula, far from the quantum value. This dimensional comparison
neither derives the least length nor makes the historical interval a
universal cutoff. The Book I scholium's divisibility claim and its
limits are treated with the \href{planck-gap-paper.md}{held sources}.

\hypertarget{admitted-similarities-and-mechanical-dilation}{%
\subsection{Admitted similarities and mechanical
dilation}\label{admitted-similarities-and-mechanical-dilation}}

\textbf{Similarity theorem.} At fixed constants, suppose the admitted
class contains \(T_a s\) for arbitrarily small scale factors giving
\(A(T_a s)=a^\delta A(s)\to0\), with \(0<A(s)<\infty\) and
\(\delta\ne0\). Then its positive-action infimum is zero. If every
\(a>0\) is admitted, the positive values are also unbounded above. A
finite common value invariant under any admitted nontrivial rescaling
must be zero.

\emph{Proof.} Apply the displayed scaling to that one finite positive
action. \(\square\)

The map must preserve the admitted class. Changing units in the
dimensional theorem and changing a physical model by a similarity are
different operations. The following exact mechanical map makes that
distinction explicit. For \(m>0\), use \(T(p)=|p|^2/(2m)\) or
\(T(p)=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2|p|^2}\), \(c>0\), and \(V\in C^2(D)\). Put

\[D_a=aD,\quad V_a(Q)=V(Q/a),\quad
q_a(t)=a q(t/a),\quad p_a(t)=p(t/a),\qquad a>0.\]

Then \(\dot q_a=\nabla T(p_a)\) and
\(\dot p_a=-a^{-1}\nabla V(q_a/a)=-\nabla V_a(q_a)\). Solution intervals
scale by \(a\); completeness, boundedness of an orbit and smoothness at
each positive \(a\) survive. Mass, speeds and energy values are
unchanged, while force and acceleration scale by \(a^{-1}\). This is a
map between potentials, not generally a symmetry of a single fixed
Hamiltonian. Its phase map \(F_a(q,p)=(aq,p)\) is conformally
symplectic:

\[F_a^*\sum_i dQ_i\wedge dP_i=a\sum_i dq_i\wedge dp_i.\]

For corresponding closed orbits and finite segments,

\[J_a=aJ,\qquad
\int_0^{aT}(p_a\cdot\dot q_a-H_a)dt
 =a\int_0^T(p\cdot\dot q-H)dt.\]

Push an invariant preparation forward by \(F_a\). The identity
\(\phi_a^tF_a=F_a\phi^{t/a}\) proves invariance, and for a selected
plane \(Z=(x,p_x)\) with finite covariance,

\[\Sigma_a=\operatorname{diag}(a,1)\Sigma\operatorname{diag}(a,1),\qquad
2\sqrt{\det\Sigma_a}=a\,2\sqrt{\det\Sigma}.\]

Uniform circular phase, subsystem independence and qualitative
correlation decay survive. For stationary position increments,

\[\mathsf h_a(\Delta)=a\mathsf h(\Delta/a).\]

Corresponding windows \(\Delta_a=a\Delta\) therefore scale by \(a\); a
finite long-window limit does too when the window limit is taken at
fixed \(a\) first. No uniform fixed-window limit in \(a\) is asserted. A
prescribed dimensional correlation time need not survive.

The admissibility tests are concrete: \(V=-k/r\) maps \(k\mapsto ak\);
\(V=-k/\sqrt{r^2+b^2}\) maps \((k,b)\mapsto(ak,ab)\); a harmonic
stiffness maps \(k_s\mapsto k_s/a^2\). A fixed force ceiling excludes
contraction of a nontrivial orbit. Fixed energy and speed ceilings do
not. Excluding this map alone does not prove a floor, as the
fixed-potential family below shows. Source/proof context:
\href{../references/batches/B30.md}{B30}.

\hypertarget{a-prepared-orbit-covariance-and-transport-response}{%
\subsection{A prepared orbit: covariance and transport
response}\label{a-prepared-orbit-covariance-and-transport-response}}

For any circular planar orbit of radius \(R>0\), momentum magnitude
\(P>0\) and frequency \(\omega>0\), prepare uniform phase \(\Phi\):

\[q=R(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\quad
p=P(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\quad \theta=\omega t+\Phi.\]

Relativistic velocity gives \(P=\gamma mR\omega\),
\(\gamma=(1-R^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\); Newtonian motion has
\(\gamma=1\). The orbital action is \(\ell=RP\). With
\(z=\omega\Delta\),

\[\mathbb E[x(t)x(t+\Delta)]=\frac{R^2}2\cos z,\qquad
\mathsf h_x(\Delta)=\frac{\ell}{\gamma}\frac{1-\cos z}{z}.\]

Given \(x\in(-R,R)\) the next positions are
\(x\cos z\pm\sqrt{R^2-x^2}\sin z\), equally weighted, so

\[\operatorname{Var}(x(t+\Delta)\mid x(t)=x)=(R^2-x^2)\sin^2z,\qquad
\overline{\mathsf h}_{\rm cond}=\frac{\ell\sin^2z}{2\gamma z}.\]

Conditioning on the full canonical state gives a deterministic future.
Both window coefficients vanish at zero and infinite window. Since
\(1-\cos z\le\min(z^2/2,2)\), a fixed fraction
\(\mathsf h_x\ge\eta\ell\), \(\eta>0\), requires
\(2\gamma\eta\le z\le2/(\gamma\eta)\): its endpoint ratio is at most
\(1/(\gamma^2\eta^2)\). A broad plateau requires additional time scales.

For \(Z=(x,p_x)\), uniform phase gives \(\operatorname{Var}x=R^2/2\),
\(\operatorname{Var}p_x=P^2/2\) and zero cross covariance. Hence
\(2\sqrt{\det\Sigma}=RP=\ell\). Affine canonical changes within this
plane preserve it, because \(\Sigma\mapsto S\Sigma S^T\) and
\(\det S=1\). On this orbit the flow is

\[S_t=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t&(R/P)\sin\omega t\\
-(P/R)\sin\omega t&\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}.\]

It preserves the determinant for every phase law, but a point-phase law
has zero determinant despite \(\ell>0\). Equality with orbit action
depends on preparation; nonlinear canonical invariance is not claimed.
This is the rms-emittance construction with the stated normalization.
Source/proof context: \href{../references/batches/B29.md}{B29}.

\hypertarget{one-smooth-force-bounded-hamiltonian-has-zero-orbital-infimum}{%
\subsection{One smooth force-bounded Hamiltonian has zero orbital
infimum}\label{one-smooth-force-bounded-hamiltonian-has-zero-orbital-infimum}}

Fix \(m,c,K,b>0\) and

\[H=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2|p|^2}+V(|q|),\qquad
V(r)=Kb^2(\sqrt{1+r^2/b^2}-1).\]

This Cartesian-smooth confining potential has inward force
\(f(r)=Kr/\sqrt{1+r^2/b^2}<Kb\). Finite energy bounds position and
momentum, so trajectories are complete. Choosing \(Kb\le F_{\max}\)
meets a fixed force ceiling without changing the Hamiltonian across the
family. No propagating mediator is constructed by this
external-potential model.

At each \(R>0\), set \(s=Rf(R)\) and

\[\gamma_R=\frac{s/(mc^2)+\sqrt{[s/(mc^2)]^2+4}}2,\quad
v_R=c\sqrt{1-\gamma_R^{-2}},\quad P_R=m\gamma_Rv_R,\quad \ell_R=RP_R.\]

The identity \(mc^2(\gamma_R-\gamma_R^{-1})=s\) is exactly the circular
balance \(P_Rv_R/R=f(R)\). At fixed \(\ell_R\), the effective radial
energy \(U_\ell(r)=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2\ell^2/r^2}+V(r)\) satisfies

\[U_\ell''(R)=f'(R)+(3-v_R^2/c^2)f(R)/R>0,\qquad
f'(R)=K(1+R^2/b^2)^{-3/2}.\]

Indeed the kinetic contribution is
\(P_R^2/(m\gamma_R^3R^2)+2P_Rv_R/R^2\). Thus each circle is radially
stable at fixed angular momentum, without Hamiltonian attraction. At
\(R\downarrow0\),

\[s=KR^2+O(R^4),\quad v_R\sim\sqrt{K/m}\,R,\quad
\ell_R\sim\sqrt{mK}\,R^2,\quad \omega_R\to\sqrt{K/m}.\]

Both kinetic energy above rest and \(V(R)\) are asymptotic to
\(KR^2/2\); force and acceleration tend to zero. Every strictly positive
speed or acceleration upper ceiling holds eventually, and the period
stays finite. Every member is regular, nonstationary and has positive
uniform-phase covariance action, while the infimum is zero at this fixed
Hamiltonian. Source/proof context:
\href{../references/batches/B31.md}{B31}.

\hypertarget{a-sharp-excitation-and-force-bound-for-all-closed-regular-orbits}{%
\subsection{A sharp excitation-and-force bound for all closed regular
orbits}\label{a-sharp-excitation-and-force-bound-for-all-closed-regular-orbits}}

Let \(q:\mathbb R/T\mathbb Z\to\mathbb R^d\), \(d\ge2\), be \(C^2\),
with matching endpoint tangents and \(v=|\dot q|\ge v_*>0\). Set
\(p=P(v)n\), \(n=\dot q/v\), where \(P(v)=mv\) or \(m\gamma v\); in the
latter case assume \(v<c\) and \(v_*<c\). Suppose
\(|\dot p|\le F_{\max}\) in this inertial frame. For scalar-potential
mechanics this mechanical momentum is canonical. Vector-potential
couplings require separate treatment.

Fenchel's total-turning theorem gives
\(\mathcal K=\int_0^T|\dot n|dt\ge2\pi\), including self-intersecting
regular closed curves. Orthogonality gives

\[|\dot p|^2=\dot P^2+P^2|\dot n|^2,\qquad
|\dot n|\le F_{\max}/P_*,\quad P_*=P(v_*).\]

Integrating and using monotonicity of \(P(v)v\) proves

\[T\ge\frac{P_*\mathcal K}{F_{\max}},\qquad
J=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^T P(v)v\,dt
 \ge\frac{P_*^2v_*}{F_{\max}}\frac{\mathcal K}{2\pi}
 \ge\frac{P_*^2v_*}{F_{\max}}.\]

The Newtonian value is \(m^2v_*^3/F_{\max}\); the relativistic value is
\(m^2v_*^3/[F_{\max}(1-v_*^2/c^2)]\). A circle of radius
\(R_*=P_*v_*/F_{\max}\) at speed \(v_*\) attains equality. It is
realized by a globally smooth force-bounded confining potential: choose
a smooth nondecreasing \(\chi\) equal to zero below \(1/4\) and one
above \(1/2\), and set

\[V(r)=F_{\max}\int_0^r\chi(s/R_*)ds.\]

This is constant near the origin and linear at large radius, and has the
required force at \(R_*\). Sharpness is over the admitted potential
class, not every preassigned potential. The positive speed floor is
essential; letting \(v_*\downarrow0\) closes the bound. Oscillations
with turning points fall outside its hypotheses. The distinct
finite-duration reversal problem, with a sharp \(1/24\) mesh defect,
remains in \href{bounded-acceleration-return.md}{bounded acceleration}.
Source reading: \href{../references/batches/B32.md}{B32}, Milnor Theorem
3.4 and its smooth-curve total-curvature identification (passages).

\hypertarget{singular-and-softened-relativistic-coulomb-cores}{%
\subsection{Singular and softened relativistic Coulomb
cores}\label{singular-and-softened-relativistic-coulomb-cores}}

Fix \(m,c,k>0\), \(r>0\), and planar canonical Hamiltonian

\[H=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2(p_r^2+L^2/r^2)}+V(r).\]

A regular bound trajectory is complete and stays in a compact radial
subset of \((0,\infty)\). Its angular cycle action is \(\ell=|L|\); the
spatial rosette need not close. Recoil, radiation and a dynamical second
body are excluded. For \(V=-k/r\), write \(x=1/r\) and

\[F_\ell(x)=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2\ell^2x^2}-kx.\]

For \(\ell>0\), \(F_\ell\) is strictly convex, starts with derivative
\(-k\) and has derivative limit \(c\ell-k\). A finite minimum therefore
exists exactly for \(\ell>k/c\), at

\[E_{\min}=mc^2\sqrt{1-k^2/(c^2\ell^2)},\qquad
r_0=\frac{\ell\sqrt{c^2\ell^2-k^2}}{mkc}.\]

The full bound domain is \(E_{\min}\le E<mc^2\). Equality gives the
circle; otherwise the two radial turning points bound a compact allowed
component. Energy bounds momentum there and the smooth vector field
extends for all time. At \(E\ge mc^2\) the outer radius is unbounded;
below the minimum no state exists. At \(\ell=k/c\), \(F_\ell\) decreases
to zero without attaining it; for \(\ell<k/c\) it decreases to minus
infinity, including \(\ell=0\). For the allowed plunging trajectories,

\[c^2p_r^2=E^2-m^2c^4+2Ek/r+(k^2-c^2\ell^2)/r^2,\qquad
\dot r=c^2p_r/(E+k/r).\]

If \(\ell<k/c\), inward \(|\dot r|\to c\sqrt{k^2-c^2\ell^2}/k>0\). At
equality, allowed \(E>0\) gives \(|\dot r|\sim c\sqrt{2E/k}\sqrt r\).
Both reach the excluded centre in finite time. Thus the angular-action
infimum is exactly \(k/c\), excluded, with every larger value admitted
continuously. It closes as \(c\to\infty\) or \(k\downarrow0\); at
\(k=0\) there are no nonstationary bound trajectories. At fixed
\(\ell,k,m\), the Newtonian limits are \(r_0\to\ell^2/(mk)\) and
\(E_{\min}-mc^2\to-mk^2/(2\ell^2)\).

Now fix a softening length \(a>0\) and \(V_a=-k/\sqrt{r^2+a^2}\). For
every \(\ell>0\) the radial energy

\[U_{a,\ell}(r)=\sqrt{m^2c^4+c^2\ell^2/r^2}-k/\sqrt{r^2+a^2}\]

tends to infinity at zero and has expansion
\(mc^2-k/r+\ell^2/(2mr^2)+O(r^{-3})\) at infinity. It therefore attains
a minimum below \(mc^2\) at finite positive radius. This gives a
complete circle; energies between the minimum and \(mc^2\) have compact
allowed radial components. The positive angular-action infimum is zero
at every fixed \(a,m,k,c>0\).

For fixed \(0<\ell<k/c\), minimizing radii tend to zero as
\(a\downarrow0\). Choose a fixed large \(y\) with
\(c\ell/y<k/\sqrt{1+y^2}\). Then \(aU_{a,\ell}(ay)\) tends to a negative
constant, so the minimum tends to minus infinity. On \(r\ge\delta>0\)
the energy is bounded below by \(mc^2-k/\delta\), forcing its minimizer
below every \(\delta\). The softened circles leave the singular model's
regular domain: the two infima do not commute with removal of the core.

For elementary charges the singular threshold is \(Z\alpha\hbar\). The
Sommerfeld--Dirac circular-energy correspondence substitutes
\(\ell=\hbar|\kappa_D|\) into the displayed \(E_{\min}\); radial quantum
indicial coefficients similarly contain \(c^2\ell^2-k^2\). This
correspondence uses a supplied angular quantum unit. Essential
self-adjointness, distinguished extensions and endpoint dynamics have
different thresholds; the precise Dirac and Klein--Gordon domains are in
\href{self-sufficiency-contrast.md}{self-sufficiency}, not inferred from
the classical inequality. Boyer's equations (19)--(20), (37)--(42) were
read in \href{../references/batches/B27.md}{B27}.

\hypertarget{sommerfeld-energies-and-quantum-coulomb-thresholds-c130}{%
\subsubsection{Sommerfeld energies and quantum Coulomb thresholds
(C130)}\label{sommerfeld-energies-and-quantum-coulomb-thresholds-c130}}

For \(0<E<mc^2\) and \(\ell>k/c\), the classical radial identity is

\[c^2p_r^2=E^2-m^2c^4+\frac{2Ek}{r}
 +\frac{k^2-c^2\ell^2}{r^2}.\]

It supplies both the collision-free bound domain above and the radial
action. Put \(A=m^2c^4-E^2\), \(B=Ek\), \(C=c^2\ell^2-k^2\), with
turning radii \(r_1<r_2\) satisfying \(r_1+r_2=2B/A\), \(r_1r_2=C/A\).
Then

\[\begin{aligned}
J_r&=\frac1{2\pi}\oint p_r\,dr
 =\frac1{\pi c}\int_{r_1}^{r_2}
       \frac{\sqrt{A(r_2-r)(r-r_1)}}r\,dr\\
 &=\frac1c\left[\frac{Ek}{\sqrt{m^2c^4-E^2}}
                 -\sqrt{c^2\ell^2-k^2}\right].
\end{aligned}\]

The evaluation uses
\(\int_a^b\sqrt{(b-r)(r-a)}\,dr/r =\pi(a+b)/2-\pi\sqrt{ab}\) for
\(0<a<b\), obtained by \(r=\tfrac12[(a+b)+(b-a)\cos\theta]\) or the
cited generic integral. The circular orbit has \(J_r=0\). The additional
old-quantum rules \(J_r=n_r\hbar\), \(\ell=n_\varphi\hbar\), with
integer \(n_r\ge0\), positive integer \(n_\varphi\), and
\(n_\varphi>k/(\hbar c)\), give Sommerfeld's energy formula

\[E=mc^2\left[1+
 \frac{\nu^2}{(n_r+\sqrt{n_\varphi^2-\nu^2})^2}\right]^{-1/2},
\qquad \nu=\frac{k}{\hbar c}.\]

For the ordinary subcritical Dirac--Coulomb spectrum this numerical
formula agrees with the energies after setting
\(n_\varphi=|\kappa_D|=j+1/2\) and supplying the allowed Dirac quantum
numbers. In particular, its allowed \(n_r=0\) levels equal the classical
circular energy at \(\ell=\hbar|\kappa_D|\). The comparison reconstructs
neither spin states nor degeneracies, and does not derive either
quantization rule or physical \(\hbar\).

The scalar Klein--Gordon equation provides a related, different check.
For \(\psi=u(r)Y_{lm}/r\) in the minimally coupled point potential,

\[\hbar^2c^2u''+\left[E^2-m^2c^4+\frac{2Ek}{r}
 +\frac{k^2-\hbar^2c^2l(l+1)}{r^2}\right]u=0.\]

Its origin exponents obey \((\sigma-1/2)^2=(l+1/2)^2-\nu^2\). The
reality threshold therefore has the classical form with
\(\ell=\hbar(l+1/2)\), whereas the regular bound-state energies contain
the different radial shift:

\[E_{n_r,l}=mc^2\left[1+
 \frac{\nu^2}{(n_r+1/2+\sqrt{(l+1/2)^2-\nu^2})^2}\right]^{-1/2}.\]

For the Dirac radial system the corresponding exponent is
\(s=\sqrt{\kappa_D^2-\nu^2}\). These discriminants identify formal
point-Coulomb threshold expressions. They do not give the essential
self-adjointness threshold, choose a boundary condition when several
extensions exist, or imply that a self-adjoint energy becomes complex.
Those domain questions, including critical equality, belong to
\href{self-sufficiency-contrast.md}{the Coulomb completion proof}.

\subsection{Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

Dimensional homogeneity limits the form of a possible universal floor;
admitted similarities and the fixed smooth small-circle family defeat
positivity under upper ceilings alone. The excitation bound and singular
Coulomb threshold retain the exact extra premises that make them
positive. A radiation preparation, pumped attractor, topological sector
or record-risk constraint must be tested in its own admitted class. None
of these classical results establishes quantum kinematics, universality
of its scale, or the necessity of physical \(\hbar>0\).
