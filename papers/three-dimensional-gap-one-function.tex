\hypertarget{three-dimensional-yangmills-conditional-scaling-and-the-physical-gap-limit}{%
\section{Three-dimensional Yang--Mills: conditional scaling and the
physical gap
limit}\label{three-dimensional-yangmills-conditional-scaling-and-the-physical-gap-limit}}

For a \textbf{constructed physical} pure Yang--Mills theory in three
spacetime (two space) dimensions, with no additional parameters and a
fixed square-torus shape, dimensional covariance gives

\[\Delta(L)=E_3 f(x),\qquad E_3=\hbar^2c g_{\rm cl}^2,
\qquad x=\hbar g_{\rm cl}^2L.\]

The positive finite infinite-volume gap would require
\(\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=C\in(0,\infty)\), together with physical
spectral convergence and reconstruction. Dimensional analysis alone
proves none of these existence or positivity statements. The
constant-mode matrix model and a volume-uniform strong-coupling lattice
estimate control different regimes; neither transports itself to that
limit. The current-variable kinetic scale \(N/(2\pi)\) of Karabali--Nair
is not an established full physical gap constant. New research remains
suspended; this note maintains the separate continuum obligation and the
exact normalization tests.

\hypertarget{physical-units-and-the-one-variable-form}{%
\subsection{Physical units and the one-variable
form}\label{physical-units-and-the-one-variable-form}}

Use a connection-valued field with
\(S=(4g_{\rm cl}^2)^{-1}\int F^a_{\mu\nu}F^a_{\mu\nu}\,d^3x\) and weight
\(e^{-S_E/\hbar}\). Then
\([1/g_{\rm cl}^2]=\mathrm{action}\,\mathrm{length}\). Put
\(\lambda_3=\hbar g_{\rm cl}^2=1/\ell_g\), which is an inverse length,
and \(E_3=\hbar c\lambda_3\). The classical coupling by itself is not a
mass when physical \(\hbar,c\) remain explicit. The mass unit is
\(\mu=E_3/c^2=\hbar^2g_{\rm cl}^2/c\).

On \(T_L^2\times\mathbb R\), if the gap depends only on those constants
and \(L\), the \href{action-unit-dimensional-selection.md}{dimensional
theorem} makes it \(E_3\) times a function of \(x=L/\ell_g\).
Vacuum-energy shifts leave a defined excitation gap unchanged; that
observation does not establish ultraviolet construction or spectral
convergence. The \href{low-dimensional-mass-gap.md}{low-dimensional
treatment} owns the field normalization and solved matrix/circle
comparisons.

\hypertarget{constant-modes-an-exact-truncation-and-a-conditional-small-volume-use}{%
\subsection{Constant modes: an exact truncation and a conditional
small-volume
use}\label{constant-modes-an-exact-truncation-and-a-conditional-small-volume-use}}

For \(SU(2)\) in temporal gauge, take constant spatial components
\(\vec a_1,\vec a_2\). With \(F_{0i}=c^{-1}\dot a_i\) and
\(F_{12}=\vec a_1\times\vec a_2\), their Lagrangian and Hamiltonian are

\[L_0=\frac{L^2}{2g_{\rm cl}^2c}|\dot{\vec a}|^2
 -\frac{L^2c}{2g_{\rm cl}^2}|\vec a_1\times\vec a_2|^2,\]

\[H_0=\frac{g_{\rm cl}^2c}{2L^2}\sum_{i=1}^2|\vec p_i|^2
 +\frac{L^2c}{2g_{\rm cl}^2}|\vec a_1\times\vec a_2|^2,
\qquad[a_i^a,p_j^b]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\delta^{ab}.\]

This is C133 with \(D=2\), \(m_{\rm eff}=L^2/(g_{\rm cl}^2c)\) and
\(g_B^2=L^2c/g_{\rm cl}^2\). Dilation gives the energy unit

\[\hbar^{4/3}g_B^{2/3}m_{\rm eff}^{-2/3}
 =\hbar^{4/3}c(g_{\rm cl}^2)^{1/3}L^{-2/3}
 =\frac{\hbar c}{L}x^{1/3}=E_3x^{-2/3}.\]

The \textbf{truncated} Gauss-invariant gap is therefore
\(\Delta_{0,\rm inv}(L)=\delta_{\rm inv}^{(2)}E_3x^{-2/3}\), with
\(\delta_{\rm inv}^{(2)}\ge\delta_1^{(2)}>0\) from the invariant-sector
theorem. The unrestricted scalar or matrix first gap cannot silently
replace this physical-sector coefficient. This cross-product proof is
\(SU(2)\); general \(SU(N)\) constant-mode conclusions need their
corresponding matrix theorem.

Transport to a full physical finite-volume theory would give
\(f(x)=\delta_{\rm inv}^{(2)}x^{-2/3}[1+o(1)]\) \textbf{if} the
nonzero-mode coupling, renormalization and sector/domain controls make
the relative error vanish. The earlier wording treated that error as
already controlled merely by analogy with Lüscher's four-dimensional
small-volume method. It is not proved here. The natural scaling
parameter \(x^{1/3}\) is dimensional bookkeeping, not a remainder
estimate. The \href{weak-coupling-feshbach-reduction.md}{Feshbach
argument} states the distinct complement, Schur and
effective-Hamiltonian obligations; a finite-matrix gap alone does not
satisfy them.

\hypertarget{the-lattice-route-and-its-strong-coupling-end}{%
\subsection{The lattice route and its strong-coupling
end}\label{the-lattice-route-and-its-strong-coupling-end}}

On the periodic square lattice with \(N_s\) sites per side, use

\[H_{a,L}=\frac{\hbar c}{a}\left[
 \frac{g_{\rm lat}^2}{2}\sum_\ell(-\Delta_\ell)
 +\frac{2}{g_{\rm lat}^2}\sum_p
 (N-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p)\right],
\qquad g_{\rm lat}^2=\lambda_3a.\]

Here \(N\) is colour rank, \(N_s=L/a\) is lattice size and
\(g_{\rm lat}\) is dimensionless. The
\href{mass-gap-obligations-lattice.md}{finite-lattice theorem} gives a
unique physical ground state and
\(\Delta_{a,L}=(\hbar c/a)\delta(g_{\rm lat};N_s,G)>0\). The
\href{strong-coupling-uniform-gap.md}{strong-coupling theorem} with
spatial \(\nu=2\), \(\Lambda_0=\{0,1\}^2\), two links and one plaquette
per site gives

\[\beta=\frac{8N}{g_{\rm lat}^4C_2}
 =\frac{16N^2}{g_{\rm lat}^4(N^2-1)},\qquad
 g_0^{(2)}(N)=\left[
 \frac{16N^2}{(N^2-1)\beta_*(2,\{0,1\}^2)}\right]^{1/4},\]

\[\Delta_{a,L}\ge\gamma_2\frac{g_{\rm lat}^2}{2}C_2\frac{\hbar c}{a},
\qquad g_{\rm lat}\ge g_0^{(2)}(N),\]

uniformly in \(N_s\). The unspecified theorem constants
\(\beta_*,\gamma_2\) retain their source hypotheses; they are not
numerical continuum estimates.

At fixed \(\lambda_3,L\), taking \(a\to0\) sends \(g_{\rm lat}\to0\)
with \(x=g_{\rm lat}^2N_s\) fixed and
\(\Delta_{a,L}/E_3=\delta(g_{\rm lat};N_s,G)/g_{\rm lat}^2\). A
finite-volume continuum spectral theorem would need

\[\frac{\delta(g_{\rm lat};x/g_{\rm lat}^2,G)}{g_{\rm lat}^2}\longrightarrow f(x).\]

For the volume-first lattice route define
\(\delta_\infty(g_{\rm lat})=\liminf_{N_s\to\infty}\delta(g_{\rm lat};N_s,G)\)
and \(c_3(g_{\rm lat})=\delta_\infty(g_{\rm lat})/g_{\rm lat}^2\). At
strong coupling \(c_3\ge\gamma_2C_2/2\). A finite positive limit
\(c_3\to C\) near zero, plus construction, matching of limit orders and
surviving physical spectral weight, is the continuum target. Positivity
and a common lower bound at \textbf{every} intermediate bare coupling
form a sufficient optional strengthening, not a necessary all-coupling
theorem for the continuum problem. The strong-coupling lower bound does
not control the required weak-coupling end.

For compact \(U(1)\) the all-coupling Villain gap can coexist with a
massless fixed-\(\lambda_3\) continuum limit. The
\href{villain-monopole-refinement.md}{Villain proof} retains normalized
measure bounds, its essential monopole suppression and the distinct
massive Gaussian scaling trajectory. The former Debye prefactor with
dimensionless \(\beta/a^3\) is not a mass-squared formula in the present
physical units and is withdrawn here. These Euclidean source results are
not automatically a theorem for the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian just
defined.

In four dimensions the usual scaling target instead has a transmutation
factor: up to its specified higher-order running factors,
\(\delta_\infty(g)/g^2\) must vanish like \(g^{-2}e^{-1/(2b_0g^2)}\).
This is the required trajectory shape if a physical gap survives, not an
estimate proved from the strong-coupling end. The three-dimensional
energy unit is simpler, but a nonzero spectral coefficient is still an
independent dynamical question.

\hypertarget{construction-boundary-and-consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{Construction boundary and consequence for
STATE}\label{construction-boundary-and-consequence-for-state}}

Chandra--Chevyrev--Hairer--Shen construct a renormalized local evolution
in \textbf{auxiliary stochastic time}, with possible blow-up; the cited
result supplies neither global survival, an invariant measure nor OS
reconstruction (\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03487}{2024
introduction}, passage reading recorded in
\href{refinement-composition-and-limit.md}{refinement}).
Karabali--Nair's \(N/(2\pi)\) is a current-variable kinetic scale
(\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9602155}{1996 equations (28)--(29)},
passage), not a proved full gap. The cited 2026 outline likewise leaves
that proof open; its source boundary is kept in the refinement
treatment.

Conditional on the physical construction and limit, the question is
whether \(f(x)\) has a positive finite limit. A lower bound
\(f(x)\ge f_->0\) for \(x\ge x_0\) would supply the positive side only
after that construction; one still needs finiteness and an appropriate
spectral limit. The matrix proof, conditional small-volume transport,
strong lattice estimate and continuum limit remain separate. This note
stays distinct from the solved low-dimensional models because it owns
the explicit \(\nu=2\) lattice thresholds, normalization and unresolved
field-theoretic limit, not another matrix-gap proof.
