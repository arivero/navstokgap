\section{Quantum preparations select continuations at a bounded Newton
cusp}\label{quantum-preparations-select-continuations-at-a-bounded-newton-cusp}

\textbf{Result, 2026-10-27; written derivation, unrefereed.} For the
bounded \(C^1\) cusp potential already used in the
\href{newton-cusp-preparation-floor.md}{preparation-floor note}, there
are two explicit coherent-state families with the \textbf{same position
density} at \(t=0\), the same limiting phase-space point \((0,0)\), and
the same limiting incoming trajectory for \(t<0\). Their unique
Schrödinger evolutions have different outgoing semiclassical limits for
\(t>0\): one rebounds on the positive side and the other crosses to the
negative side. The preparations differ by a momentum shift tending to
zero. Their normalized superpositions yield any prescribed mixture of
the two outgoing point measures.

The result is local in time and uses the specified logarithmically
shrinking preparation. It neither classifies every possible preparation
nor selects a waiting-time distribution. It shows explicitly that the
limiting incoming phase-space history alone does not determine the
outgoing limit of these quantum families. The positive value of
\(\hbar\) is supplied, not derived.

For three finite central forces that agree on a moving circle, the
terminal radii impose a common-preparation inequality. A positive
classical distribution with the stated spread in initial conditions
gives a mean score at most \(2/3\). One squeezed radial state of the
canonical theory gives about \(0.687\) in the same limit. The
three-quadrature inequality and its violating state are established
results. The calculation below realizes the three sign choices as inward
and outward departures under autonomous central forces.

This phenomenon has substantial prior art.
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.1651}{Athanassoulis--Paul, Theorem 2.1
and Lemma 5.3} (theorem statements and relevant passages read) treat
splitting and one-dimensional cutoff \(-|q|^{1+\theta}\) potentials,
including \(\theta=1/2\), with detailed assumptions on phase-space
preparations. \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03716}{Chabu, Theorem
1.12 and Proposition 3.1} (statements and surrounding discussion read)
exhibit the same incoming semiclassical history with distinct outgoing
histories, and mixtures, for the conical potential \(-|q|\). In Theorem
1.12 the two initial profiles can be chosen equal, so identical initial
position densities also occur in that conical example. The present
calculation uses the repository's smoother \(C^1\) cusp and explicit
Gaussian preparations; exact coverage by the cited rough-potential
theorems is not settled and no literature priority is claimed.

\subsection{1. Potential, initial data and target
trajectories}\label{potential-initial-data-and-target-trajectories}

Use dimensionless calibrated units, \(m=1\), and choose \(R>0\) and a
smooth even cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one on \([-R,R]\) and zero outside
\([-2R,2R]\). Put \[
 V(q)=-\frac23|q|^{3/2}\chi(q),\qquad
 F(q)=-V'(q),\qquad
 H_\hbar=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\partial_q^2+V(Q).\tag{1}
\] \(V\) and \(F\) are bounded, \(V\in C^1\), and on \(|q|\le R\),
\(F(q)=\operatorname{sgn}(q)\sqrt{|q|}\). The bounded-perturbation
theorem gives a self-adjoint \(H_\hbar\) on \(H^2(\mathbb R)\) for every
\(\hbar>0\). Write \(F_*=\|F\|_\infty\), and fix \(T>0\) so small that
\(F_*T^2/2<R/4\). All mean trajectories considered below remain in
\(|q|<R/2\) for small \(\hbar\).

There are constants \(C_0,C_1>0\), depending on the fixed cutoff, such
that for all \(x\) and all \(q\ne0\), \[
 |F(x)|\le C_0\sqrt{|x|},\qquad
 |F(x)-F(q)|
   \le C_1(1+|q|^{-1/2})\,|x-q|.\tag{2}
\] For \(|x-q|\le |q|/2\), the second bound follows from the derivative
of the square-root force and the smooth cutoff. Otherwise use the first
bound and \(|x-q|\ge |q|/2\). This is a Lipschitz bound anchored at
\(q\ne0\), \textbf{not} a global Lipschitz bound at the cusp.

For \(0<\hbar<e^{-1}\) set \[
 a_\hbar=\frac1{\log(1/\hbar)},\qquad
 g_{\hbar,k}(q)=(\pi\hbar)^{-1/4}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(q-a_\hbar)^2}{2\hbar}
     -\frac{i k a_\hbar^{3/4}(q-a_\hbar)}{\hbar}\right].\tag{3}
\] Choose a fixed \(K>0\) with
\[K^2>\max\{16\sqrt2\,C_0,\;16/3\}.\tag{4}\] The two preparations are
\(g_{\hbar,0}\) and \(g_{\hbar,K}\). Both have exactly the same Gaussian
position density and
\(\operatorname{sd}Q=\operatorname{sd}P=\sqrt{\hbar/2}\). Their means
are \[
 (q,p)=(a_\hbar,0),\qquad
 (q,p)=(a_\hbar,-K a_\hbar^{3/4}),\tag{5}
\] both converging to \((0,0)\). The classical energies of these
\textbf{centres} are respectively \(-\frac23a_\hbar^{3/2}\) and
\((K^2/2-2/3)a_\hbar^{3/2}\): both vanish, but their signs differ.

Let \(z_+(t)=(t^4/144,t^3/36)\) for \(-T\le t\le T\), and let \[
 z_\times(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  (t^4/144,t^3/36),&t\le0,\\
  (-t^4/144,-t^3/36),&t\ge0.
 \end{cases}\tag{6}
\] The first trajectory returns to the positive side, while the second
crosses to the negative side. Both follow the same incoming path, arrive
at \((0,0)\) at \(t=0\), and satisfy the cusp Newton equation.

\textbf{Theorem.} Let
\(\psi_{\hbar,k}(t)=e^{-itH_\hbar/\hbar}g_{\hbar,k}\). For \(k=0,K\),
the packet means and the position and momentum standard deviations
converge uniformly on \([-T,T]\): \[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\langle Q\rangle_{\hbar,0},\langle P\rangle_{\hbar,0})
       &\longrightarrow z_+(t),\\
 (\langle Q\rangle_{\hbar,K},\langle P\rangle_{\hbar,K})
       &\longrightarrow z_\times(t),\\
 \operatorname{sd}Q_{\hbar,k}(t)+\operatorname{sd}P_{\hbar,k}(t)
       &\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}\tag{7}
\] In particular, the Weyl expectations of every
\(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\) converge uniformly to \(b(z_+(t))\) and
\(b(z_\times(t))\), respectively.

\subsection{2. A mean and spread estimate with an anchored
force}\label{a-mean-and-spread-estimate-with-an-anchored-force}

Suppress the subscripts, let \(q(t)=\langle Q\rangle\),
\(p(t)=\langle P\rangle\), and write
\(W(t)=\operatorname{sd}Q(t)+\operatorname{sd}P(t)\). The standard
Ehrenfest identities and Cauchy--Schwarz give \[
 \dot q=p,\qquad \dot p=\langle F(Q)\rangle,\qquad
 D^+W\le\operatorname{sd}P+\operatorname{sd}F(Q).\tag{8}
\] These identities hold as quadratic-form identities for the present
\(H_\hbar\): \(V\in W^{1,\infty}\), the Gaussian lies in
\(D(Q)\cap H^2\), and the required weighted and \(H^1\) domains are
preserved by the bounded-force evolution. They can be obtained by
regularizing \(Q,P\) and removing the cutoffs.

For \(q(t)\ne0\), (2) gives \[
 \operatorname{sd}F(Q)
 \le\|[F(Q)-F(q)]\psi\|
 \le L(q)\operatorname{sd}Q,\quad
 L(q):=C_1(1+|q|^{-1/2}),\tag{9}
\] and also \(|\langle F(Q)\rangle-F(q)|\le L(q)W\). Consequently \[
 D^+W\le[1+L(q)]W,\qquad W(0)=\sqrt{2\hbar}.\tag{10}
\] On an interval reached from the initial time without a cusp crossing,
if \(|q|\ge a_\hbar/2\) and its length is at most \(2T\), (10) gives \[
 W(t)\le\sqrt{2\hbar}\,e^{C_T/\sqrt{a_\hbar}},\qquad
 \int L(q(t))W(t)\,dt
    \le C_Ta_\hbar^{-1/2}\sqrt\hbar\,
                  e^{C_T/\sqrt{a_\hbar}}
    =o(a_\hbar^N)\tag{11}
\] for every fixed \(N>0\). Here and below \(C_T\) may change from line
to line but is independent of \(\hbar\). This super-polynomial smallness
in \(a_\hbar\) is why the logarithmic separation in (3) is convenient.
After the controlled passage in §3, its additional factor
\(e^{Ca_\hbar^{-1/4}}\) can be absorbed into \(e^{C_T/\sqrt{a_\hbar}}\),
so the same form of bound applies on the outgoing interval.

On the positive side, \(F(q)\ge0\) within the chosen core. For
\(g_{\hbar,0}\) at positive times, or either preparation at negative
times, (11) and (8) bootstrap \(q(t)\ge a_\hbar/2\): the total
force-bias error is \(o(a_\hbar)\), whereas the force itself pushes away
from the cusp in the appropriate time direction. On those intervals
compare \((q,p)\) with the classical solution from (5). The field is
Lipschitz on \(q\ge a_\hbar/2\) with constant \(O(a_\hbar^{-1/2})\).
Gronwall and (11) show that their phase-space distance is
\[O\!\left(a_\hbar^{-1/2}\sqrt\hbar\,
              e^{C_T/\sqrt{a_\hbar}}\right)=o(a_\hbar^N)
 \quad\text{for every fixed }N.\tag{12}\] The displayed constant \(C_T\)
absorbs the additional Gronwall factor.

\subsection{3. The transmitted packet crosses the
cusp}\label{the-transmitted-packet-crosses-the-cusp}

It remains to control the \(k=K\) preparation while its mean passes
through \(q=0\), where (11) cannot be used with a fixed cutoff. Start at
\(q=a_\hbar\) and \(p=-Ka_\hbar^{3/4}\). Bootstrap, until the first
arrival at \(q=-a_\hbar\), the two inequalities
\[W\le a_\hbar,\qquad p\le-\frac K2a_\hbar^{3/4}.\tag{13}\] While
\(|q|\le a_\hbar\), the first inequality and (2) imply \[
 |\langle F(Q)\rangle|\le C_0\sqrt{\mathbb E|Q|}
 \le C_0\sqrt{2a_\hbar}.\tag{14}
\] The second inequality bounds the passage time by
\(4a_\hbar^{1/4}/K\). In that time the total change in \(p\) is at most
\((4\sqrt2 C_0/K)a_\hbar^{3/4}\), which is strictly below
\((K/2)a_\hbar^{3/4}\) by (4).

The same velocity bound makes the singular coefficient integrable: \[
 \int_{\rm passage}|q(t)|^{-1/2}\,dt
 \le \frac{2}{K a_\hbar^{3/4}}
        \int_{-a_\hbar}^{a_\hbar}|q|^{-1/2}\,dq
 =\frac8K a_\hbar^{-1/4}.\tag{15}
\] The mean crosses zero at most once, so (10) holds away from that
single time; apply it on the two sides and use continuity at the
crossing. It follows that
\[W\le\sqrt{2\hbar}\,e^{C a_\hbar^{-1/4}}=o(a_\hbar).\tag{16}\] The
inequalities (13) therefore close by continuity. The exit time
\(\tau_\hbar\) satisfies \(\tau_\hbar=O(a_\hbar^{1/4})\), and at exit
\[q(\tau_\hbar)=-a_\hbar,\qquad
 -\tfrac{3K}{2}a_\hbar^{3/4}
 \le p(\tau_\hbar)\le-\tfrac K2a_\hbar^{3/4}.\tag{17}\]

After this exit the force is negative in the chosen core. The same
bootstrap as in §2 keeps \(q\le-a_\hbar/2\) and makes (11)--(12) valid
against the classical solution started at the \textbf{actual} exit mean
(17). No self-adjoint Hamiltonian with a regularized potential is
substituted for (1).

\subsection{4. The classical limits and the
mixtures}\label{the-classical-limits-and-the-mixtures}

The zero-energy positive-side arrival solves
\(\dot q=-\sqrt{\frac43q^{3/2}}\). Integration gives \[
 |t|=\int_0^{q(t)}
 \frac{ds}{\sqrt{\frac43s^{3/2}}}
 =2\sqrt3\,q(t)^{1/4},\qquad
 q(t)=\frac{t^4}{144}.\tag{18}
\] For the \(k=0\) centre, energy conservation gives on either side of
\(t=0\) \[
 |t|=\int_{a_\hbar}^{q_a(t)}
 \frac{ds}{\sqrt{\frac43(s^{3/2}-a_\hbar^{3/2})}}.\tag{19}
\] The part from \(a_\hbar\) to \(2a_\hbar\) is \(O(a_\hbar^{1/4})\); on
the remainder the integrand is bounded by \(Cs^{-3/4}\) and tends to the
integrand in (18). Thus (19) tends to (18).

For \(k=K\) at negative times, and for its outgoing classical comparison
after (17), the analogous integral has denominator \[
 \sqrt{u_\hbar^2+\frac43(s^{3/2}-a_\hbar^{3/2})},
 \qquad u_\hbar\asymp a_\hbar^{3/4}.\tag{20}
\] Condition (4) ensures \(u_\hbar^2\ge(4/3)a_\hbar^{3/2}\) in the
outgoing case, so the integrand is dominated by \(Cs^{-3/4}\); it tends
to the one in (18). At the entrance for negative times, \(u_\hbar\) is
exactly \(Ka_\hbar^{3/4}\) and the same argument applies. The exit-time
shift tends to zero. Energy conservation gives the corresponding
momentum limits, and the bounds in §§2--3 supply (7), including a
uniform estimate on the shrinking passage interval.

The overlap of the two initial Gaussians is
\[\langle g_{\hbar,0},g_{\hbar,K}\rangle
 =\exp\!\left[-\frac{K^2a_\hbar^{3/2}}{4\hbar}\right]
 \longrightarrow0.\tag{21}\] It remains unchanged by unitary evolution.
For \(0\le\lambda\le1\) and fixed \(\theta\), normalize \[
 \Psi_{\hbar,\lambda,\theta}(0)
 \propto\sqrt\lambda\,g_{\hbar,0}
       +e^{i\theta}\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,g_{\hbar,K}.\tag{22}
\] For any \(b\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\), the Fourier representation
of Weyl quantization and \(\|(e^{iA}-1)\psi\|\le\|A\psi\|\) show that \[
 \|[\operatorname{Op}^W_\hbar(b)-b(z_k(t))]
                         \psi_{\hbar,k}(t)\|\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{uniformly for }|t|\le T,\tag{23}
\] using (7). Thus the cross term in a Weyl expectation equals
\(b(z_k(t))\langle\psi_{\hbar,0},\psi_{\hbar,K}\rangle+o(1)\) and
vanishes uniformly. The pure states (22) have the semiclassical limit \[
 \lambda\delta_{z_+(t)}+(1-\lambda)\delta_{z_\times(t)}.\tag{24}
\] For \(t\le0\) this is a single point on the common incoming path; for
\(t>0\) it has the prescribed two branch weights. The result does not
assert that the finite-\(\hbar\) Wigner function is nonnegative.

\subsection{Three central forces on the same moving
circle}\label{three-central-forces-on-the-same-moving-circle}

\textbf{6.1 Sol Max, 2026-11-19.} Use the mass \(m\), length \(R\),
inverse time \(\omega\) and reference action \(S_0=mR^2\omega\) of the
\href{balaban-ode-lesson.md\#tangent-orbits-under-the-two-impulse-orders}{moving-circle
construction}. Use dimensionless radii \(r\) and time \(t\). Write
\(y=r-1\), \(b=e^{-20}\), \(L(y)=\log(1/|y|)\) and
\(\alpha(y)=y(L(y)^4-2L(y)^3)\), with \(\alpha(0)=0\). Consider the
three finite inward accelerations of magnitude

\[
 g_\kappa(r)=r^{-3}-\kappa^2\alpha(y),\quad |y|\le b,
 \qquad \kappa\in\{1/2,1,3/2\}.                                  \tag{25}
\]

Continue each by the linear core and inverse-square exterior of (C1),
matching the values at the ends of the annulus. All three are positive
on the annulus and have bounded \(C^1\) potentials \(U_\kappa\) with
\(U_\kappa'=g_\kappa\). Each gives the same circular orbit with unit
speed and unit normal acceleration. Thus the tangent displacement in
Lemma X on that orbit is identical for the three laws. As in (C1) each
is a reciprocal two-body force. Their Poisson densities can be signed.

Fix \(0<T<1/40\). The two immediate radial departures at \(T\) are
\(1\pm e^{-1/(\kappa T)}\). Fix one smooth \(0\le f\le1\) equal to zero
for \(r\le1-e^{-2/T}/2\) and one for \(r\ge1+e^{-2/T}/2\). Write
\(p_\kappa\) for the expectation of \(f\) at \(T\) after choosing that
force. Count an outward departure for the two extreme forces and an
inward departure for the middle force. The observable is

\[
 \mathcal P=\tfrac13(p_{1/2}+1-p_1+p_{3/2}).                      \tag{26}
\]

\subsubsection{What a common Newtonian preparation
permits}\label{what-a-common-newtonian-preparation-permits}

Let \(d\downarrow0\), \(\ell=\log(1/d)\) and \(X=(r_0-1)/d\),
\(V=p_{r,0}/(d\ell^2)\) in dimensionless variables. Admit any common
positive initial law with \((X,V)\) tight and
\((\gamma-1)/(d\ell^4)\to0\) in probability, where \(\gamma\) is squared
specific angular momentum. Require that no subsequential limiting law
charges \((X,V)=(0,0)\). An initial scaled position marginal with an
atomless limit already ensures this. Dependence between \(X\) and \(V\),
and any choice of an admissible continuation at a nonunique state, are
allowed. Then

\[
                  \tfrac13\le\liminf\mathcal P
                 \le\limsup\mathcal P\le\tfrac23 .                \tag{27}
\]

Indeed in time \(u=t\ell^2\) the radial equation
\(r''=\kappa^2\alpha(y)+(\gamma-1)/r^3\) gives
\(y/d\to X\cosh(\kappa u)+(V/\kappa)\sinh(\kappa u)\) on bounded \(u\)
intervals for convergent initial data. This follows from the uniform
scaled force estimate in
\href{balaban-ode-lesson.md\#which-part-of-the-initial-preparation-leaves-on-each-side}{(C8)--(C9)}.
Where \(V+\kappa X\ne0\) the ray leaves immediately on its sign. After a
fixed inner time it has signed displacement \(\ge c d\) and positive
signed velocity. The signed acceleration is then at least
\(\kappa^2\alpha(|y|)/2\), including a perturbation
\(|\gamma-1|=o(d\ell^4)\). The level estimate (C4) excludes waiting.
Compactness and the limiting energy equation \(|y'|=\kappa|y|L(y)^2\)
give the stated terminal radius.

For \((X,V)\ne(0,0)\) the three numbers \(V+\kappa X\) are ordered in
\(\kappa\), and at most one vanishes. The middle lies between the
extremes. The sum in (26) is between one and two pointwise, even
assigning any score in \([0,1]\) to a vanishing number. For a sequence
of laws take a joint weak limit of the scaled inputs and three bounded
scores along a subsequence. The scores may be coupled conditional on the
common initial data. The sign conclusion holds uniformly on compact sets
away from each dividing line, and the common origin has zero limiting
mass. Integrating proves (27).

\subsubsection{A squeezed radial preparation in continuum quantum
dynamics}\label{a-squeezed-radial-preparation-in-continuum-quantum-dynamics}

Let \(\epsilon=a/S_0=d^2\ell^2\) and let \(k\) be the integer nearest
\(1/\epsilon\). Use the same normalized angular mode
\(e^{ik\theta}/\sqrt{2\pi}\) in all three evolutions. Its squared
centrifugal coefficient in radial \(L^2(dr)\) is
\(\gamma=\epsilon^2(k^2-1/4)=1+O(\epsilon)\). In that space use initial
radial amplitude proportional to \(d^{-1/2}\chi(r-1)\phi((r-1)/d)\),
where \(\|\phi\|_2=1\), \(\phi\) is Schwartz, and \(\chi\) is a smooth
cutoff equal to one on \(|y|\le b/2\) and zero on \(|y|\ge3b/4\). The
corresponding planar amplitude includes \(r^{-1/2}\). The angle is
uniform. The incoming scaled radial position has limiting density
\(|\phi|^2\), and the radial velocity scale is \(d\ell^2\). The product
of the position and momentum units is the trial action. The actual
standard deviations also depend on \(\phi\). For all three \textbf{exact
continuum} Schrödinger evolutions,

\[
  p_\kappa\longrightarrow
  \int_0^\infty\left|\widehat{\exp(i\kappa X^2/2)\phi}(\xi)\right|^2d\xi
  =\langle\phi,\mathbf1_{(0,\infty)}(\Pi+\kappa Q)\phi\rangle ,
  \quad Q=X,\quad\Pi=-i\partial_X .                              \tag{28}
\]

Here the Fourier transform is unitary with kernel
\(e^{-i\xi X}/\sqrt{2\pi}\). Equation (28) is the step transferring a
quadrature to a fixed macroscopic position score.

\textbf{Exit from the inner region.} On the annulus the effective
potential \(W=U_\kappa+\gamma/(2r^2)\) satisfies

\[
 W(1+y)-W(1)=\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\big((1+y)^{-2}-1\big)
              -\tfrac{\kappa^2}{2}y^2L(y)^4 .                    \tag{29}
\]

In \(X=y/d\), \(u=t\ell^2\), remove the constant phase. The kinetic
operator is \(\Pi^2/2\) and the potential is (29) divided by
\(d^2\ell^4\). On the cutoff support its difference from
\(-\kappa^2X^2/2\) is bounded by \(C(1+|X|^3)/\ell+Cd|X|/\ell^2\). For
\(|X|\le1\) use boundedness of \(|X|^2|\log |X||^j\), \(1\le j\le4\).
For \(1\le|X|\le b/d\) use \(0\le\log|X|/\ell\le1\). Let \(G_\kappa(u)\)
be the inverted oscillator group and put \(u_*=(\log\ell)/20\). The
exact linear Heisenberg evolution bounds its third position moment in
norm by \(C_\phi e^{3\kappa u}\) and its momentum norm by
\(C_\phi e^{\kappa u}\). Applying Duhamel to \(\chi(dX)G_\kappa(u)\phi\)
for \(0\le u\le u_*\) costs \(o(1)\) because
\(u_* e^{3\kappa u_*}/\ell\to0\) and the cutoff commutator costs
\(O(u_*(d e^{\kappa u_*}+d^2))\). The cutoff keeps the comparison in the
domain of the radial half-line operator. The removed norm tends to zero
by the same position bound.

At \(u_*\) put \(B=\sinh(\kappa u_*)/\kappa\) and
\(A=\cosh(\kappa u_*)\). The exact quadratic propagator gives, up to a
constant phase,

\[
 G_\kappa(u_*)\phi(X)= B^{-1/2} e^{i A X^2/(2B)}
       \widehat{e^{i A Q^2/(2B)}\phi}(X/B).
\]

This is the standard metaplectic kernel for the linear Hamiltonian flow
with upper row \((A,B)\).
\href{https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/72e5ea46-6226-42f1-b494-80940b4b02b8/NT_CMP20_rev.pdf}{Nicola
and Trapasso, Theorem 2.5} (quadratic Fourier kernel) and
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.4056}{Schubert, Vallejos and Toscano,
Appendix A.3.1} (quadratic propagation). In the coordinate \(\xi=y/D\),
\(D=dB\), the envelope converges in Schwartz space to
\(\widehat{\exp(i\kappa Q^2/2)\phi}\). The radial phase has slope
\(p_r=\ell^2(A/B)y\). Thus on each side of the circle it points away
from the circle.

\textbf{Transport to the terminal radius.} Localize that envelope
smoothly to \(\eta\le|\xi|\le M\) on either side. The exact classical
rays for \(W\) start there at time \(u_*/\ell^2=o(1)\) with the
displayed phase slope. They stay on that side by the acceleration bound
used in (27). Their terminal radii converge uniformly to the
corresponding \(1\pm e^{-1/(\kappa T)}\). To control diffraction, use
the
\href{balaban-ode-lesson.md\#quantum-transport-through-the-circle}{Jacobi
and transported-amplitude estimates (C11)--(C12)} with width \(D\). The
following rates account for the larger action
\(\epsilon/D^2=\ell^2/B^2\) in these estimates.

Measure time from the start of this transport and let
\(t_1=4000\log\ell/\ell^2\), \(t_2=2t_1\). Along the support,
\(J(0)=1\), \(J'(0)=\ell^2 A/B\) for the derivative of position with
respect to its initial radius. The initial second and third derivatives
of the velocity vanish. Write \(F=-W'\) there. Then \(J''=F'J\) with
\(F'=\kappa^2\alpha'-3(\gamma-1)/r^4>0\). Up to \(t_2\),
\(|y|\le CD\exp(C\ell^2t_2)<\sqrt d\) since \(|y|\ge\eta D\) and
\(F=O(\ell^4 |y|)\). In this interval \(F'\ge\ell^4/200\) and the signed
acceleration is at least \(\ell^4|y|/200\) for large \(\ell\). The same
block summation, or integration over blocks of length \(100/\ell^2\),
gives \(|y|\ge D\ell^8\) after \(t_1\) and \(J(v)/J(t)=O(\ell^{-8})\)
for \(v\le t_1,\ t\ge t_2\). Both \(J\) and \(|y|\) are increasing.

In (C12) the time measure here is ordinary time. The scaled higher
derivative bounds \(D|F''|+D^2|F'''|\) are \(O(\ell^3)\) at first since
\(|y|\ge\eta D\). They are \(O(D+\ell^{-5})\) after \(t_1\) by (25).
Consequently \(E_2+E_3=O(1)\) up to \(t_2\) and \(O(\ell^{-5})\) after
\(t_2\), by splitting the two integrals in (C12) at \(t_1\). The
envelope on each cutoff has bounded derivatives in \(\xi\) through order
two. The transported half-density estimate thus bounds its total WKB
error in norm by

\[
 C\,\frac{\ell^2}{B^2}\big(t_2+T\ell^{-5}\big)=o(1),
\]

since \(B\) grows as a positive power of \(\ell\). The two propagated
parts are supported in disjoint terminal plateaus, with their respective
initial cutoff masses. Sending \(\eta\) to zero and \(M\) to infinity
proves the integral in (28). Conjugating momentum by the displayed
quadratic phase gives its spectral form.

One can use a single moving point as the limiting launch. Replace the
constant angular amplitude by \(d^{-1/4}g(\theta/\sqrt d)\), with a
fixed unit-norm smooth \(g\) supported in \((-1,1)\), and retain the
factor \(e^{ik\theta}\). Position and velocity now converge to \((1,0)\)
and \((0,1)\), respectively. Indeed the radial derivative in the
momentum norm costs \(O(\epsilon/d)=O(d\ell^2)\), and the angular
envelope costs \(O(\epsilon/\sqrt d)\) in angular momentum. In the
Fourier sum the probability of \(|n-k|>M/\sqrt d\) is at most
\(\|g'\|^2/M^2\). The remaining sectors have the same radial initial
profile and \(\gamma-1=O_M(\epsilon/\sqrt d)\). In the inner potential
comparison this replaces \(Cd|X|/\ell^2\) by \(C_M\sqrt d\,|X|/\ell^2\),
still giving \(o(1)\) in norm by \(u_*\). The outer force and Jacobi
estimates are uniform for these sectors since
\(\epsilon/\sqrt d=o(D\ell^4)\). Orthogonality for the radial readout
and then \(M\to\infty\) prove (28) for this localized launch.

\subsubsection{A common preparation with score above two
thirds}\label{a-common-preparation-with-score-above-two-thirds}

Write \(L_\kappa=\Pi+\kappa Q\) and \(s=(\sqrt3/2)^{1/2}\). The pair
\(Q'=sL_{3/2}\) and \(P'=2s(L_{1/2}+L_{3/2}/2)/\sqrt3\) has
\([Q',P']=i\). Its three quadratures at angles \(0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3\) are
\(sL_{3/2},sL_{1/2},-2sL_1\). Thus (26) is exactly Tsirelson's
three-quadrature score in this limit. Take \(\phi\) in the oscillator
number basis of \((Q',P')\) to be

\[
 \phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\frac4{\sqrt{21}}|0\rangle
          -|3\rangle+\sqrt{\frac5{21}}|6\rangle\right).
\]

This is Schwartz by metaplectic covariance. The published score of this
specific state is approximately \(0.687>2/3\).
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0611147}{Tsirelson, §1} (original
oscillator sign problem) and \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06017}{Zaw
and Scarani, Eq. (3) and Fig. 2} (state and score, passage). Equations
(25)--(29) give the same strict excess for the limiting central-force
radii. The gap persists for all sufficiently small \(d\) in this
specified quantum family. At each fixed \(d>0\) its score also survives
arbitrarily fine time subdivision by the bounded-potential product
formula.

All three number states in \(\phi\) have the same phase under rotation
by \(2\pi/3\). Thus if \(P_*\) denotes the cited score, the three
outward probabilities in increasing \(\kappa\) order are
\((P_*,1-P_*,P_*)\). If only the limiting launch point and final radii
are specified, a classical construction can reproduce this triple.
Prepare the circular point and a single uniform variable \(z\in[0,1]\)
before choosing the force. For each law take the immediate outward
solution when \(z\) is below its indicated probability and the immediate
inward solution otherwise. Both are exact central-force trajectories.
This preparation has \(X=V=0\) with certainty. The scaled initial
position law in (27) and (28) distinguishes it.

The dimensional inputs to this comparison are \(m,R,\omega\), the trial
\(a=mR^2\omega\,d^2\log^2(1/d)\), the normal length \(Rd\), the radial
momentum unit \(mR\omega\,d\ell^2\), the fixed time \(T/\omega\) and the
common readout width \(R e^{-2/T}\). The force and potential units are
\(mR\omega^2\) and \(mR^2\omega^2\). The three \(\kappa\), the cutoff
ratios and the profiles are dimensionless. The angular momentum is
\(k a\to S_0\) in the single mode. In the localized version the
tangential length is \(R\sqrt d\) and the angular momentum spread is
\(O(a/\sqrt d)\). This comparison fixes the same preparation before the
force is chosen. Its violation excludes the positive classical
preparation class in (27). Every trial action in the construction can be
made arbitrarily small.

\subsubsection{A quantitative bound with a common radial velocity
spread}\label{a-quantitative-bound-with-a-common-radial-velocity-spread}

A finite common velocity spread gives a state-independent bound on the
trial action ratio. Set \(\tau=a/(S_0d^2\ell^2)>0\) and take the radial
refinement at each \textbf{fixed} \(\tau\). In (28) the result is then
the positive spectral probability for
\(L_{\kappa,\tau}=\Pi_\tau+\kappa Q\), \(\Pi_\tau=-i\tau\partial_X\).
Indeed the inner equation has \(i\tau\partial_u\) and kinetic energy
\(\Pi_\tau^2/2\). The same quadratic kernel uses the Fourier transform
with action \(\tau\) and phase \(\kappa Q^2/(2\tau)\). Duhamel's inner
error gains a fixed factor \(1/\tau\) and the outer transport error a
fixed factor \(\tau\). The same convergence applies to a fixed mixed
radial state by finite Schwartz approximation in trace norm, since the
scores are bounded.

Give the common preparation a signed radial momentum increment
\(mR\omega\,d\ell^2 z\) in physical units. Draw \(z\) uniformly on
\([-\eta,\eta]\), \(\eta>0\) fixed, independently of the initial state
and the subsequent force choice. In the canonical model the multiplier
on the radial state is \(e^{izX/\tau}\). In either model this replaces
the positive-sign indicator by \(F_\eta(v)=\Pr(v+z>0)\), a ramp with
Lipschitz constant \(1/(2\eta)\) and \(F_\eta(-v)=1-F_\eta(v)\). The
classical bound persists by conditioning on the common \(z\). In
particular the continuous increment removes an atom at the common origin
for tight classical initial data. Even the circular-point construction
above must obey (27) when this boost is implemented as an actual change
of radial velocity before the force is chosen.

For any canonical state on the scaled radial line, its Husimi
distribution is a common positive law on \((X,V)\). Its marginal along
\(V+\kappa X\) equals the spectral law of \(L_{\kappa,\tau}\) convolved
with a centered Gaussian of variance \(\tau(1+\kappa^2)/2\). This uses
the isotropic coherent-state seed with component variances \(\tau/2\),
the phase-space marginal formula, and rotation of that seed.
\href{https://www.rintonpress.com/xqic4/qic-4-67/546-562.pdf}{Werner,
(26)--(27)} (covariant marginals and convolution error, passage).
Replacing a spectral marginal by this positive-law marginal changes its
ramp expectation by at most \(\sqrt{\tau(1+\kappa^2)/\pi}/(2\eta)\). The
three positive-law expectations obey the same pointwise order bound with
the common increment. Thus for the limiting boosted score
\(\mathcal P_\eta\), any excess \(\Delta=\mathcal P_\eta-2/3>0\)
requires

\[
 \frac{a}{mR^2\omega\,d^2\log^2(1/d)}
 =\tau\ \ge\
 \frac{36\pi\,\eta^2\Delta^2}
 {(\sqrt{5/4}+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{13/4})^2}.                         \tag{30}
\]

The explicit preparation above gives a positive excess even after a
nonzero boost spread. At \(\tau=1\) its three limiting quadrature
densities are bounded by \(M_\phi=\|\phi\|_1^2/(2\pi)\) by (28).
Averaging the momentum increment changes each probability by at most
\(M_\phi\eta/2\). For example, taking \(\eta=(P_*-2/3)/M_\phi\) leaves
an excess of at least \((P_*-2/3)/2\). Dephasing the same three
number-state populations gives a parity-invariant mixture. All three
linear quadratures then have symmetric distributions and the boosted
limiting score is \(1/2\).

Equation (30) bounds the action relative to the declared preparation
area from its spread and a dimensionless probability. It compares the
limits at fixed trial ratios. A remainder uniform in a trial ratio
tending to zero together with \(d\) is open. The dimensional reference
area itself tends to zero in this family. Newton's common circular
trajectory supplies the three force coefficients at launch. It does not
supply a premise requiring a positive boosted excess.

\subsection{5. Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}

The cusp's unique quantum evolution need not choose one classical
continuation from the limiting incoming phase-space history. Even
identical initial position densities admit distinct continuations
through vanishing momentum offsets, and coherent superpositions can
produce any mixture of the two demonstrated branches. These explicit
preparations supplement the earlier point-merger obstruction and align
with established rough-potential selection results; they do not derive a
physical preparation rule, a waiting-time selection, or a necessary
positive action constant.

Three forces sharing the same Lemma X displacement now give a
radial-position inequality for a common classical preparation. The
canonical dynamics supplies an explicit violating preparation and (30)
converts a surviving excess into a bound relative to that preparation.
Whether Newton's trajectory argument independently requires admission of
those three responses remains open.
