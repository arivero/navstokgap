\hypertarget{radiation-reservoirs-and-the-action-scale-question}{%
\section{Radiation, reservoirs and the action-scale
question}\label{radiation-reservoirs-and-the-action-scale-question}}

A finite universal equilibrium radiation spectrum with Wien scaling and
the Rayleigh--Jeans low-frequency normalization defines a positive
action \textbf{unit}. It does not impose a minimum body action or a
recording restriction. Linear radiation balance retains a freely
supplied field amplitude; a nonlinear pumped oscillator has a genuine
action attractor but its value depends on supplied power and frequency.
Classical two-pointer records defeat a prior-only density ceiling when
the specified instruments are available. A stipulated information-- work
inequality prices resolution without selecting a positive action floor.

These are separate results under separate physical assumptions. Physical
\(\hbar\) remains explicit: identifying a unit or reservoir parameter
with it is an additional claim. This treatment integrates the radiation
and energy-depot tests with the unit/indeterminacy distinction. Written
model/coordinator reviews were internal, not independent peer review;
the ingredients have the cited prior art and no novelty claim is made
for their assembly.

\hypertarget{a-normalized-radiation-action-unit}{%
\subsection{A normalized radiation action
unit}\label{a-normalized-radiation-action-unit}}

Let \(u(\nu,T)\ge0\) be a measurable equilibrium spectral energy
density, \(T>0\), and assume:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{(\Alph{enumi})}
  \setcounter{enumi}{10}
  \tightlist
  \item
    the same spectrum for every material enclosure;
  \end{enumerate}
\item
  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{(\Alph{enumi})}
  \setcounter{enumi}{22}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Wien scaling \(u(\nu,T)=\nu^3 f(\nu/T)\);
  \end{enumerate}
\item
  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{(\Alph{enumi})}
  \setcounter{enumi}{18}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \(0<\int_0^\infty u(\nu,T)d\nu<\infty\);
  \end{enumerate}
\item
  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumi{(\Alph{enumi})}
  \setcounter{enumi}{17}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \(u(\nu,T)/[(8\pi\nu^2/c^3)k_BT]\to1\) as \(\nu/T\to0\), with fixed
    \(c,k_B>0\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Theorem U.} Put \(x=\nu/T\) and define

\[r(x)=\frac{c^3xf(x)}{8\pi k_B},\quad
I=\int_0^\infty x^2r(x)dx,\quad
\beta=I^{-1/3},\quad h_{\rm rad}=k_B\beta.\]

Then \(0<I<\infty\), \([\beta]={\rm K\,s}\) and \(h_{\rm rad}>0\) is an
enclosure-independent action unit. With \(\varphi(y)=r(y/\beta)\),

\[u(\nu,T)=\frac{8\pi k_BT\nu^2}{c^3}\varphi(\beta\nu/T),\quad
\varphi(0+)=1,\quad\int_0^\infty y^2\varphi(y)dy=1.\]

If \(\int u\,d\nu=\sigma'T^4\), then

\[h_{\rm rad}=\left(\frac{8\pi k_B^4}{c^3\sigma'}\right)^{1/3}.\]

\emph{Proof.} Substitution gives \(\int u\,d\nu=(8\pi k_B/c^3)T^4I\).
Nonnegativity and (S) make \(I\) finite; (R) makes it positive.
Universality of \(f\) makes \(I\) independent of enclosure. Its units
are \(({\rm K\,s})^{-3}\). Changing variable \(y=\beta x\) proves the
remaining identities. \(\square\)

The normalization is conventional; fixed positive numerical multiples
are equally universal units. Enclosure independence permits dependence
on other universal constants, including microscopic masses or charges.
It is not independence from every such constant. No finite-monomial
hypothesis is used. If \(y^4\varphi(y)\) has a specified finite positive
maximizer \(y_*\), the wavelength peak \(\lambda_{\max}T=b\) satisfies
\(h_{\rm rad}=y_*k_Bb/c\). Integrability alone permits narrow
high-frequency spikes and does not imply that peak or its uniqueness.

For the Planck shape \(\varphi_P(y)=y/(e^y-1)\) in its usual
normalization, \(\int y^2\varphi_Pdy=\pi^4/15\). Thus

\[h_P=(\pi^4/15)^{1/3}h_{\rm rad},\qquad h_P=2\pi\hbar\]

when that measured shape is identified with physical Planck radiation.
Its wavelength maximum has \(y_{*,P}\simeq4.965\). Sources:
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-663-13885-3_12}{Wien 1893 reprint},
metadata; \href{https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19013090310}{Planck 1901},
metadata. The earlier Wien-law entropy paper is not the source for the
Planck distribution.

A second available unit is \(k_e/c=\alpha\hbar\) for a fixed elementary
Coulomb coupling.
\href{action-unit-dimensional-selection.md}{Dimensional and orbital
selection} proves its exact scope. With both units available a proposed
floor may depend on their dimensionless ratio. Neutral composite probes
may still be polarizable; they do not remove charge dependence. Neither
an exponential spectral tail nor a choice of unit derives
\(p=h_P/\lambda\) as an individual corpuscle law.

\hypertarget{extending-a-radiation-unit-to-other-fields}{%
\subsection{Extending a radiation unit to other
fields}\label{extending-a-radiation-unit-to-other-fields}}

The normalized radiation theorem above does not establish that every
well-posed classical wave equation requires a universal quantum action.
A proposed thermal extension would need a specified mode density,
equilibrium state, actual equipartition and coupling to matter:
infinitely many modes with a nonsummable \(k_BT\) contribution preclude
finite equilibrium energy within that model. A cutoff, changed
occupation law or different thermal premise changes the conclusion.
Finite energy alone does not identify a unique occupation law or its
constant across distinct fields.

Cross-field universality under mutual equilibrium remains a conjectural
physical step. Einstein's solid-vibration use of Planck's constant
\href{https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19063270110}{1907} (metadata) is a
historical application, not a proof for every wave field. Deterministic
PDE existence, a thermal state and a quantum continuum limit are
different constructions. Superrenormalizable power counting alone is
likewise not a convergence proof; four-dimensional running and the
positivity of an infrared spectral coefficient require the separate
\href{halving-atlas.md}{gauge estimates}.

\hypertarget{a-prior-density-ceiling-does-not-close-under-classical-records}{%
\subsection{A prior density ceiling does not close under classical
records}\label{a-prior-density-ceiling-does-not-close-under-classical-records}}

\textbf{Theorem I.} Suppose a classical instrument class permits
Hamiltonian body/pointer dynamics, the product rectangular preparations
below, Bayesian conditioning on finite-resolution coordinate records,
and the two specified impulsive couplings with storage of the first
reading. There are no additional limits on squeezing, energy, gain or
readout precision. For every \(X,\eta>0\) it contains a finite protocol
whose final posterior support widths obey \(\Delta q\,\Delta p<\eta\),
although every initial canonical pair has density at most \(1/X\).

\emph{Proof.} Prepare the body uniformly on \([0,a]\times[0,X/a]\),
pointer one \((y,p_y)\) on \([0,\varepsilon]\times[0,X/\varepsilon]\),
and pointer two \((z,p_z)\) independently on
\([0,\varepsilon_2]\times[0,X/\varepsilon_2]\). A pulse \(gqp_y\),
\(g>0\), gives \(y\mapsto y+gq\), \(p\mapsto p-gp_y\). A stored \(y\)
bin of width \(\varepsilon'\) confines \(q\) to width
\((\varepsilon+\varepsilon')/g\). A second pulse \(g_2p_yp_z\),
\(g_2>0\), sends \(z\mapsto z+g_2p_y\), \(y\mapsto y+g_2p_z\), leaving
\(q,p,p_y\) fixed. Retain the earlier reading and read \(z\) to bin
width \(\varepsilon_2'\). Then

\[\Delta q\,\Delta p\le
 \frac{X(\varepsilon+\varepsilon')}{ag}
 +\frac{(\varepsilon+\varepsilon')(\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_2')}{g_2}.\]

Small positive \(\varepsilon,\varepsilon'\) suffice at fixed finite
other parameters. Both maps preserve phase volume, and each prior
rectangle has area \(X\). Unread conjugate ranges grow as coordinate
widths shrink. \(\square\)

This is a four-part instrument/preparation counterexample, not a claim
that abstract Hamiltonian dynamics guarantees the required apparatus. A
universal posterior restriction must exclude at least one of its
assumptions. The \href{sed-closure-under-recording.md}{regulated
Gaussian bath treatment} extends the two-pointer escape to every finite
joint Gaussian covariance, including body--pointer correlations, and
finite pulse durations at fixed cutoff.

Posterior area and the \href{record-distance-path-length.md}{quantum
disturbance bound} are different: its variances concern specific hybrid
states, not later conditional inference of a realized classical impulse.
A posterior ceiling alone does not supply that statistical-speed
theorem. Quantum kinematics, a restriction on admissible
readout/composition, and a correlated preparation class are different
possible inputs, each needing its own physical premise. The
covariance-restricted Gaussian theory of Bartlett--Rudolph--Spekkens
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5057}{2012}, author abstract) supplies
more than a pointwise prior density ceiling, including instrument
restrictions.

Nor does a bare Coulomb impulse supply indeterminacy. Integrating the
force along an externally prescribed straight path \((ut,b)\) gives
\(|\Delta p_\perp|b=2k/u\). It is a small-deflection approximation for
scattering. Exact repulsive Rutherford scattering instead has

\[b|\Delta p|=\frac{2k/u}{\sqrt{1+[k/(\mu u^2b)]^2}}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(b\downarrow0),\]

with reduced mass \(\mu\). An infinitely heavy repulsive centre and
\(u\ll c\) give a nonrelativistic counterexample; finite particle size
adds a new cutoff. A calculable impulse magnitude is not an unknown
stochastic spread.

\hypertarget{stable-regulated-radiation-balance-shape-and-free-amplitude}{%
\subsection{Stable regulated radiation balance: shape and free
amplitude}\label{stable-regulated-radiation-balance-shape-and-free-amplitude}}

In SI units supply a centred stationary \textbf{Gaussian} field, a
laboratory cutoff \(\Lambda>\omega_0>0\) and the stable weak-damping
dipole approximation

\[m\ddot x+m\gamma\dot x+m\omega_0^2x=qE(t),\qquad
\gamma=\frac{q^2\omega_0^2}{6\pi\epsilon_0mc^3}>0,\]
\[\langle E(t)E(0)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}
 S_E(\omega)e^{-i\omega t},\qquad
S_E(\omega)=\frac{K}{3\pi\epsilon_0c^3}|\omega|^3
 1_{|\omega|\le\Lambda},\quad K\ge0.\]

The damping is the cycle-averaged Larmor loss matched near resonance,
\(\gamma/\omega_0\ll1\); it is not a high-frequency radiation-reaction
law. The cutoff selects a frame. The field, trap and dipole
approximation are premises; Gaussian tails impose no hard speed ceiling.
The ensemble action is
\(J=m\langle\dot x^2+\omega_0^2x^2\rangle/(2\omega_0)\). At fixed
positive damping and finite cutoff, the homogeneous solution decays and
Fourier response gives exactly in this effective model

\[J_* =\frac{q^2}{2m\omega_0}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}
 \frac{(\omega^2+\omega_0^2)S_E(\omega)}
 { (\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2},\]
\[\frac{J_*}{K}=\frac{\gamma}{\pi\omega_0^3}\int_0^\Lambda
 \frac{\omega^3(\omega^2+\omega_0^2)}
 { (\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2}d\omega\quad(K>0).\]

Reach stationarity first, then take \(\gamma/\omega_0\to0\) at fixed
finite \(\Lambda>\omega_0\). Near \(\omega_0\), writing
\(u=\omega-\omega_0\), the integrand has numerator \(2\omega_0^5\) and
denominator \(\omega_0^2(4u^2+\gamma^2)\), and
\(\int du/(4u^2+\gamma^2)=\pi/(2\gamma)\). Away from a fixed resonance
neighbourhood the integral is bounded and its prefactor vanishes.
Shrinking that neighbourhood afterwards proves \(J_*\to K\). For any
fixed even finite-cutoff spectrum continuous at resonance the same
calculation gives

\[J_*\longrightarrow\frac{3\pi\epsilon_0c^3S_E(\omega_0)}{\omega_0^3}.\]

Thus equal limiting actions over a frequency band require a cubic
spectral shape on that band. The amplitude remains free. Convergence is
pointwise, with resonance inside the cutoff; dipole/nonrelativistic
validity also needs small \(K\omega_0/(mc^2)\). Taking \(q\to0\) at
finite time removes both forcing and damping and retains initial energy,
because relaxation time has diverged. It does not give the
stationary-first limit.

Amplitude scaling \(E\mapsto aE\) sends \((K,J_*)\mapsto(a^2K,a^2J_*)\)
at fixed damping; \(K=0\) admits rest. At fixed damping, cutoff removal
instead has

\[J_*/K\sim\gamma\Lambda^2/(2\pi\omega_0^3).\]

Position variance diverges logarithmically and velocity supplies the
quadratic energy divergence. A joint limit needs ultraviolet estimates
not supplied by narrow-resonance cancellation. This is a failure of the
chosen viscous model and spectrum together, not a theorem about every
causal extended-charge model. Source context:
\href{../docs/batches/B23/harmonic-receiver-source-companion.md}{B23},
Zwanzig equations (16)--(17), (23), (25)--(30), passages distinguishing
friction, forcing and bath preparation;
\href{../reviews/radiation-noise-Q02.md}{Q02 internal review}.

\hypertarget{lorentz-invariant-spectral-form-and-conditional-planck-calibration}{%
\subsection{Lorentz-invariant spectral form and conditional Planck
calibration}\label{lorentz-invariant-spectral-form-and-conditional-planck-calibration}}

A homogeneous isotropic stationary radiation background with
Lorentz-invariant second-order spectrum has mode energy
\(\kappa\omega\), leaving the action amplitude \(\kappa\) free.
Gaussianity is additional when Gaussian oscillator statistics are
claimed. Sources: \href{https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1963.0220}{Marshall
1963}, metadata; \href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.182.1374}{Boyer
1969}, abstract. A nonzero background supplies positivity; invariance
also permits zero.

In the formal third-order dipole response
\(M\ddot x=-M\omega_0^2x+M\tau_{\rm rad}\dddot x+eE\), with SI
\(\tau_{\rm rad}=e^2/(6\pi\epsilon_0Mc^3)\), a specified stationary
Fourier response has the resonant asymptotics

\[\langle x^2\rangle_{\rm res}\sim\frac\kappa{M\omega_0},\quad
\langle p^2\rangle_{\rm res}\sim\kappa M\omega_0,\quad
\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm res}(x,p)=0,\quad
\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{\rm res}}\sim\kappa.\]

The window must contain asymptotically many linewidths while remaining
narrow relative to \(\omega_0\); a fixed few linewidths capture only a
fraction. Linear Gaussian response is Gaussian. The squared inverse
susceptibility near resonance is
\(4\omega_0^2(\omega-\omega_0)^2+ \tau_{\rm rad}^2\omega_0^6\), with
integral \(\pi/(2\tau_{\rm rad}\omega_0^4)\), yielding cancellation of
charge and mass and equal kinetic/potential resonant energies. This is a
band-filtered stationary-response statement, not a causal well-posedness
theorem for the bare third-order equation.

Its off-resonant momentum contribution for
\(\omega_0\ll\omega\ll1/\tau_{\rm rad}\) is relatively of order
\(1/(\pi\tau_{\rm rad}\omega_0)\); above \(1/\tau_{\rm rad}\) it
diverges logarithmically. The position correction is of relative order
\(\tau_{\rm rad}\omega_0\log(1/(\tau_{\rm rad}\omega_0))\). A cutoff
\(\Omega_c\ll\sqrt{\pi\omega_0/\tau_{\rm rad}}\) can retain resonance
dominance while breaking the assumed Lorentz invariance. These
ultraviolet statements concern a different response model from the
stable viscous approximation above, whose energy divergence is
quadratic. \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00728140}{Goedecke 1983},
metadata, and \href{https://doi.org/10.3390/atoms7020059}{Nieuwenhuizen
2019}, abstract, record the oscillator regularization issue. Access to
filtered variables still needs an instrument prescription.

\textbf{Conditional calibration.} If an SED derivation gives the Planck
thermal spectrum together with zero-point mode energy \(\kappa\omega\),
matching its thermal quantum gives \(h_P=4\pi\kappa\). For the further
state identification \(\zeta=\kappa\), this yields

\[2\zeta=\frac{h_P}{2\pi}
 =\frac{(\pi^4/15)^{1/3}}{2\pi}h_{\rm rad}\simeq0.297h_{\rm rad}.\]

At the experimental quantum identification this is \(\kappa=\hbar/2\).
The SED Planck derivation is disputed: Jiménez--de la Peña--Brody
\href{https://doi.org/10.1119/1.12221}{1980}, metadata; Senatchin
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0105054}{2001}, passage reported in
the original internal review, challenges the wall-damping argument;
Boyer's \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04179}{2017 review}, passage as
reported there, maintains a modified construction. The formula is
conditional on that thermal-spectrum premise and the resonant/state
identification. The radiation unit theorem itself assumes none of this
SED derivation.

\hypertarget{nonlinear-feedback-a-real-attractor-with-a-supplied-power-scale}{%
\subsection{Nonlinear feedback: a real attractor with a supplied power
scale}\label{nonlinear-feedback-a-real-attractor-with-a-supplied-power-scale}}

An effective oscillator of frequency \(\omega>0\), action \(J\ge0\) and
depot energy \(e\ge0\) has the supplied laws

\[\dot\theta=\omega,\quad\dot J=2(ge-\gamma)J,\quad
\dot e=P-\nu e-2ge\omega J,\]

with losses \(\gamma,\nu>0\), gain \(g>0\) of units
\((\mathrm{energy}\,\mathrm{time})^{-1}\) and power \(P\ge0\). Internal
conversion cancels in total energy \(E=\omega J+e\):

\[\dot E=P-2\gamma\omega J-\nu e\le P-aE,
\qquad a=\min(2\gamma,\nu)>0.\]

The positive quadrant is invariant and this proves global boundedness.
At finite fuel \(P=0\), \(E(t)\le E(0)e^{-at}\) and \(J\to0\), even
after initial amplification. The quadrature realization

\[J=(Q^2+R^2)/2,\quad
\dot Q=(ge-\gamma)Q-\omega R,\quad
\dot R=\omega Q+(ge-\gamma)R\]

removes the angle singularity. With \(x=Q/\sqrt{m\omega}\),
\(p=\sqrt{m\omega}R\), its energy is \(\omega J\); gain acts on both
quadratures, so this is an effective amplitude model, not unmodified
Newtonian \(\dot x=p/m\) or a closed Hamiltonian field.

For \(P>P_c=\nu\gamma/g\), a positive equilibrium exists:

\[e_* =\gamma/g,\qquad J_*=(P-P_c)/(2\gamma\omega).\]

It attracts every positive seed. The nonnegative Lyapunov function

\[V=\omega[J-J_*-J_*\log(J/J_*)]+e-e_*-e_*\log(e/e_*)\]

has compact sublevel sets inside the positive quadrant. Writing
deviations \(\delta J,\delta e\), the oscillator term contributes
\(2g\omega\delta J\delta e\), while
\(\dot e=-(\nu+2g\omega J_*)\delta e-2g\omega e\delta J\). Multiplying
by \(1-e_*/e\) cancels the cross term, giving

\[\dot V=-(\nu+2g\omega J_*)\frac{(e-e_*)^2}{e}\le0.\]

The only invariant set with \(\dot V=0\) is the equilibrium, proving
convergence. An empty depot becomes positive immediately at \(P>0\); an
exactly zero oscillator seed remains zero even above threshold. The
quiet equilibrium has action growth rate \(2(gP/\nu-\gamma)\).

At the same supplied power, frequencies acquire the same energy, not the
same action. A common positive \(K\) requires
\(P(\omega)=P_c+2\gamma\omega K\), inserting that action into the
source. Also \(J_*\downarrow0\) continuously as \(P\downarrow P_c\).
Attraction does not give universality or exclusion of exact rest.
Mechanism source: Schweitzer--Ebeling--Tilch
\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.80.5044}{1998}, publisher
abstract; the specific law and Lyapunov proof above are the written
model calculation, without a full-text equation match or priority
assessment.

\hypertarget{thermodynamic-information-costs-an-inequality-not-an-optimum}{%
\subsection{Thermodynamic information costs: an inequality, not an
optimum}\label{thermodynamic-information-costs-an-inequality-not-an-optimum}}

Assume a specified measurement/reset thermodynamic account requires mean
work \(W\ge k_BT\,I\) for gained mutual information \(I\) in nats. The
conditions of that cycle, system free-energy changes and memory
preparation are part of this premise; it is not asserted for every
possible measurement/erasure process. The cited ingredients are Landauer
\href{https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183}{1961}, Bennett
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02084158}{1982} and Sagawa--Ueda
\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.102.250602}{2009}, metadata.

For a uniform prior on area \(A_0\), if almost every posterior is
supported on area at most \(\eta\), its entropy is at most \(\log\eta\)
while the prior entropy is \(\log A_0\). Hence \(I\ge\log(A_0/\eta)\)
and

\[W\ge k_BT\log(A_0/\eta),\qquad
\eta\ge A_0e^{-W/(k_BT)}.\]

The area unit cancels. This is a necessary bound, not a proof that the
minimal attainable area equals its right side. Finite-bit storage of a
finite-precision reading does not bound every preparation, control and
reset cost, nor establish equality in the stipulated work law. Theorem I
supplies arbitrarily fine finite classical protocols under its idealized
resource access; the inequality by itself adds no universal positive
resolution floor when the work budget is unrestricted. Identifying
\(k_BT\tau\) with an action unit would additionally fix a time scale
that this information bound never chooses.

\hypertarget{consequence-for-state}{%
\subsection{Consequence for STATE}\label{consequence-for-state}}

The radiation integral fixes an action unit under its stated equilibrium
assumptions. Resonant response, a pumped attractor, classical posterior
inference and thermodynamic accounting impose different conditional
laws. None bridges all four obligations: classical dynamics, positive
record floor, quantum states/instruments, and a universal scale
identified with physical \(\hbar\). The exact zero-field, finite-fuel,
squeezing and complete-record countermodels remain admitted wherever
their stated operations are allowed.
