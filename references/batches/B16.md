# B16 composition universality audit

**Result (bounded audit, 2026-09-08):** No exact prior-art theorem was found for the proposed all-positive-mass rule: a nonnegative coefficient assigned to a whole composite equals the independent centre-of-mass coefficient, forcing mass independence. The argument in C035 is an elementary monotone-Cauchy consequence once that physical premise is imposed. No source found derives a universal positive coefficient or identifies it with ℏ.

## Coverage

Two searches targeted mass-additive diffusion/Green–Kubo composition. The strongest lead was the ACS article on mutual-diffusion Green–Kubo formulas (JPC, DOI 10.1021/jp9533739), but its page was inaccessible (403) and its snippet concerns multicomponent cross-correlations, not this theorem. One primary opening was attempted, failed at the publisher. Cached passages read: Pavliotis (2010), printed p.5, Proposition 2.1 and (2.3)–(2.5), for Green–Kubo/Poisson representation; Pitman–Yor (2018), printed p.6 (PDF page 7), equations (4)–(5), for centered Gaussian covariance/bridge. These are passage-level, not full reads.

## Prior-art classification

Established ingredients: integrated velocity correlation and Poisson-equation representation (Pavliotis); Gaussian covariance and conditioning (Pitman–Yor); standard variance addition and reversible product-chain algebra. Derived consequence: if f(m)=mκ(m) is additive and nonnegative on all m>0, monotonicity gives f(m)=Km. The project's interpretation is coefficient closure under independent composition as a physical universality premise, combined with one finite-rate nonzero-speed telegraph reference to obtain K>0. Exact prior art for this combined statement remains unmatched under the bounded search; novelty is unassessed.

## Limits / defects

The product state is Markov; the projected velocity need not be, so C035 must retain the product label. Independence is essential: cross covariance adds a term. The theorem is per preparation class; environmental labels can change K. The positive reference uses classical probability, u>0 and 0<λ<∞, and proves positivity only in that class. Product closure with rates λ_m=mu²/K is a consistency family, not a derivation; K→0 remains possible across families. No quantum derivation claim is supported.
