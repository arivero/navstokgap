## Findings

### C030: equal-mass elastic midpoint pair

**Status: derived consequence, proof-audited; source ingredient established.**
Barkai p. 3 gives the one-dimensional elastic collision premise and p. 4 gives
the affine tracer map, explicitly attributing it to momentum and energy
conservation. Setting both masses equal and using the direct two-body exchange
map gives the construction in the note. The initial separation `sigma*u*Delta`
and opposite velocities make the meeting time exactly `Delta/2`; after exchange
the labelled tracer returns to zero. Direct algebra gives zero pair momentum,
pair kinetic energy `m*u^2`, midpoint variance `u^2 Delta^2/4`, tracer action
`m*u^2 Delta/2`, and `k_mid=m*u^2 Delta`. Thus the midpoint normalization and
the fixed-`kappa` energy scaling are elementary consequences, not formulas
quoted from Barkai. The construction changes the prepared receiver separation
when `Delta` changes; it is not a phase-space-invisible cut intervention.

### C031: sharp bounded-speed midpoint support and action relation

**Status: elementary derived theorem, proof-audited; no exact prior-art match
claimed.** For `|dot X| <= u`, each half-interval puts the midpoint in two
intervals whose intersection has centre `(x+z)/2` and radius
`Delta*(u-|v|)/2`, `v=(z-x)/Delta`. Bounded support implies the stated sharp
variance bound and hence `k_mid <= m*Delta*(u-|v|)^2`. The two-segment action
identity is exact: excess over the straight chord is `2*m*zeta^2/Delta`.
For a biased law this is `2*m*(Var(zeta)+E[zeta]^2)/Delta`, so the centred
action relation requires zero midpoint bias. Equal endpoint masses at the two
extremes attain the variance bound. At `|v|=u` the support is a singleton.
The Gaussian comparison correctly fails strict speed for every positive
variance. These statements are proved from interval geometry and variance
definitions; neither source asserts them.

### C032: ballistic-support convolution semigroup

**Status: elementary derived theorem under a deliberately narrow hypothesis;
proof-audited.** If `mu_0=delta_0`, `mu_(s+t)=mu_s*mu_t`, and
`support(mu_t) subset [-u*t,u*t]`, then finite variance and convolution give
`Var(mu_T) = n Var(mu_(T/n)) <= u^2 T^2/n`, hence zero variance. The measures
are point masses `delta_(a(t))`; additivity plus `|a(t)|<=u*t` gives continuity
at zero and `a(t)=b*t`, `|b|<=u`. The scope is only translation-invariant,
position-only, stationary independent increments. It does not cover the
telegraph process, general state-dependent Markov motion, or phase-space laws.
Cinque pp. 1--2 independently supports the comparison: finite velocities,
endpoint atoms, and the fact that `(T,V)` is Markov while `T` alone is not.
