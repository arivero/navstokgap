## Claim matching and limits

For R10, write `v=p/m`. The record difference estimator has error
`|v_hat-v| <= (eps1+eps2)/delta + (F/m)delta/2`; multiplying by `m` gives
the note's `B`. For equal errors, balancing the two terms gives
`delta*=2 sqrt(m eps/F)` and `B*=2 sqrt(m F eps)`. This is the same
noise-versus-curvature square-root scaling discussed by Seeber–Haimovich, but
their continuous/sampled differentiator class uses all available histories and
their noise bound is a signal-noise amplitude, not two separately bounded
position records.

The source's indistinguishable sampled parabola construction is a useful
template for R11: a common record history can support two admissible motions
with separated terminal derivatives, forcing any causal estimator to incur at
least half their separation. R10's exact `B` is an upper bound, and its stated
simultaneous attainability proves the worst case for that estimator; it is an
upper bound on the minimax risk unless the admissible-record pair and estimator
class establish matching lower separation. Known `(q0,p0)` further restricts
the minimax class relative to source definitions that vary initial data.

## R11 idea

Construct two measurable forces with common fixed initial `(q0,p0)` whose
position graphs remain within the two record error strips at `t1,t2` (or yield
the same record values), while their terminal momenta differ. Use opposite
bounded-force arcs on the inter-record interval and compensate with admissible
arcs before/after it. Any estimator receiving the identical pair `(y1,y2)`
then has worst-case terminal-momentum error at least half the separation.
Compare that separation with `2B` and state explicitly whether the records,
initial state, and force history available to the estimator are fixed or
unknown. This tests minimax information loss rather than the chosen difference
quotient.
