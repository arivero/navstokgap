**Task:** B01 (bounded bibliography batch), Luna bibliography role

**Scope:** at most eight primary leads; this batch stops at six. Three query families were used: (i) operational/axiomatic quantum reconstruction, (ii) primary action/path-phase formulations, and (iii) operational finite-resource state distinguishability. Search snippets were treated only as leads; metadata and the stated abstract/passages were checked at the linked arXiv, APS, or APS-archive pages.

## Ranked reading queue

### 1. Hardy (2001), reconstruction from five axioms

- **Verified metadata:** Lucien Hardy, “Quantum Theory From Five Reasonable Axioms,” arXiv:quant-ph/0101012v4 (last revised 25 September 2001), 34 pages. arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101012>; versioned identifier: <https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101012v4>. The record has no journal DOI beyond the arXiv-issued DOI 10.48550/arXiv.quant-ph/0101012.
- **Actual reading depth:** `passage` — arXiv abstract, introduction, the stated continuity consequence, and conclusion were read in the HTML rendering (record lines 8–30 and paper lines 7–10, 51, 885 in the experimental HTML). The proof was not audited end-to-end.
- **What it contributes:** A reconstruction in a generalized probabilistic/state-space setting. The abstract explicitly says five axioms are used, and that removing the continuity wording from Axiom 5 leaves classical probability theory. This is a useful control for the project’s Branch B: a classical countermodel survives the other premises, so the premise excluding it must be named rather than smuggled in as “quantum.”
- **What it cannot establish:** It does not derive a dimensionful nonzero $\hbar$ or $h$, an action phase $e^{iS/\hbar}$, a Hamiltonian, a path integral, or any lower bound on classical action differences. Its finite-dimensional formalism and axiom choices do not by themselves establish that Newtonian mechanics must become quantum.
- **Open route:** [arXiv abstract/version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101012v4); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/Hardy2001.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/Hardy2001.md).

### 2. Chiribella, D’Ariano & Perinotti (2011), informational reconstruction

- **Verified metadata:** Giulio Chiribella, Giacomo Mauro D’Ariano, and Paolo Perinotti, “Informational derivation of quantum theory,” *Physical Review A* **84**, 012311 (published 11 July 2011), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.84.012311. Open preprint: arXiv:1011.6451v3, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6451>; APS record: <https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.84.012311>.
- **Actual reading depth:** `abstract` plus `passage` — arXiv/APS abstracts and the arXiv record’s version/journal metadata were read; the HTML source states that mixed-state structure is derived without assuming Hilbert space. The full 47-page proof was not audited.
- **What it contributes:** A distinct operational-probabilistic route. The abstract lists causality, perfect distinguishability, ideal compression, local distinguishability, pure conditioning, and a purification postulate; purification singles out quantum theory within the broader class. This gives the coordinator a second axiom dependency graph rather than treating Hardy’s continuity premise as unique.
- **What it cannot establish:** It does not derive the numerical value or units of \hbar, a path-integral weight, a mechanical action gap, or a finite-resource bound for the project’s chord/curve pair. “Perfect distinguishability” and purification are operational premises, not consequences of Newtonian trajectories.
- **Access caveat:** APS full article text indicates authorization is required in this route; the open arXiv abstract/preprint route is lawful and sufficient for this batch’s metadata/abstract status.
- **Open routes:** [arXiv record/version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6451v3), [APS metadata/abstract](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.84.012311); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/ChiribellaDArianoPerinotti2011.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/ChiribellaDArianoPerinotti2011.md).

### 3. Masanes & Müller (2011), physical requirements and generalized probabilistic theories

- **Verified metadata:** Lluis Masanes and Markus P. Mueller, “A derivation of quantum theory from physical requirements,” *New Journal of Physics* **13**, 063001 (2011), DOI 10.1088/1367-2630/13/6/063001. arXiv:1004.1483v4 (last revised 16 May 2011), <https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1483>; DOI route: <https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/13/6/063001>.
- **Actual reading depth:** `passage` — arXiv abstract and selected HTML passages on generalized probabilistic state spaces, capacity/dimension, symmetry, and the derivation’s two-bit/Bloch-ball discussion were read; not a proof audit of the 16-page paper.
- **What it contributes:** A reconstruction using five physical requirements about preparations, transformations, and measurements, with an explicit comparison to classical alternatives and possible consistent modifications. It is useful for identifying where continuity/symmetry/composition assumptions enter and for avoiding the claim that “operational” automatically means uniquely complex quantum.
- **What it cannot establish:** It does not produce a universal action scale, derive the action phase, prove a lower bound on ΔS, or connect a reconstructed state-space dimension to the numerical Planck constant. The requirements are additional physical premises and the scope is the reconstructed finite-dimensional formalism.
- **Open route:** [arXiv versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1483v4); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/MasanesMueller2011.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/MasanesMueller2011.md).

### 4. Feynman (1948), primary action-phase/path-integral statement

- **Verified metadata:** Richard P. Feynman, “Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum Mechanics,” *Reviews of Modern Physics* **20**, 367–387 (published 1 April 1948), DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367. APS record: <https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367>.
- **Actual reading depth:** `abstract` — the APS abstract was read. It states that a path contribution is postulated to be an exponential whose imaginary phase is the classical action in units of \hbar, and that summing paths yields the wavefunction/Schrödinger equation. The APS full-text route is access-limited here; no full-paper proof or normalization audit was attempted.
- **What it contributes:** The clean primary citation for the distinction between a classical action functional and the quantum phase rule. It directly supports wording that $e^{iS/\hbar}$ is a quantum postulate/formulation input in this context, not a consequence of the Newtonian limiting geometry.
- **What it cannot establish:** The source does not imply that equal phases force a minimum nonzero action difference, that all paths with $S=nh$ are selected, or that the project’s ΔS family has a positive infimum. It also does not supply the project’s finite-resource measurement protocol.
- **Access caveat:** APS shows an authorization-required article route; only the public abstract and DOI metadata were used.
- **Open route:** [APS metadata/abstract](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.20.367); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/Feynman1948.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/Feynman1948.md).

### 5. Wootters (1981), statistical distance from finite samples

- **Verified metadata:** W. K. Wootters, “Statistical distance and Hilbert space,” *Physical Review D* **23**(2), 357–362 (published 15 January 1981), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.23.357. Open APS archive/full text: <https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.357/fulltext>; issue metadata: <https://journals.aps.org/prd/issues/23/2>.
- **Actual reading depth:** `passage` — the six-page open archive PDF was read through the introduction and finite-$n$ photon-polarization setup (PDF pp. 357–358; archive text lines 6–43 and 67–88). The later derivation was not independently proof-audited.
- **What it contributes:** An operational notion of statistical distance tied to fluctuations and the number $n$ of available identically prepared systems. The opening passage explicitly contrasts indistinguishability at finite trials with Hilbert-ray geometry. This is a good template for making the project’s Q3 resource budget and error target explicit.
- **What it cannot establish:** It presupposes quantum preparations/Hilbert-space states and does not turn a geometric trajectory area or action difference into a state-discrimination distance. It gives no universal action lower bound, no \hbar derivation, and no claim that finite-sample indistinguishability discretizes classical paths.
- **Open route:** [APS archive full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.357/fulltext); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/Wootters1981.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/Wootters1981.md).

### 6. Audenaert et al. (2007), quantum Chernoff bound

- **Verified metadata:** K. M. R. Audenaert, J. Calsamiglia, Ll. Masanes, R. Muñoz-Tapia, A. Acín, E. Bagan, and F. Verstraete, “The Quantum Chernoff Bound,” *Physical Review Letters* **98**, 160501 (2007), arXiv:quant-ph/0610027v1 (submitted 4 October 2006), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.160501. Open record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610027>; DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.160501>.
- **Actual reading depth:** `abstract` — arXiv abstract, author list, version, journal reference, and DOI were checked. The optimization proof and finite-copy prefactors were not audited.
- **What it contributes:** A rigorous primary result for asymptotically many-copy binary quantum-state discrimination: the optimal exponential error rate (quantum Chernoff bound), reducing to the classical Chernoff bound for commuting states. It is a candidate quantitative bridge only after the project defines states, copies/resources, priors and error criterion.
- **What it cannot establish:** “Asymptotically many copies” is not the same as a fixed finite resource budget. The result assumes quantum density operators and does not identify a trajectory action difference with a state overlap or Chernoff exponent, nor derive \hbar or an action gap.
- **Open route:** [arXiv versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610027v1); source-facing note: [`docs/batches/B01/Audenaert2007.md`](../../docs/batches/B01/Audenaert2007.md).

## Cross-source dependency notes for task Q01 / programme question Q3

1. Hardy, Masanes–Müller, and Chiribella–D’Ariano–Perinotti are alternative reconstruction starting points. Their premises differ; none should be merged into a single “minimal axiom set” without a separate comparison.
2. Feynman’s source places $\hbar$ in the phase rule. Thus a derivation that starts from $e^{iS/\hbar}$ is Branch A (quantum input admitted), not a derivation of the existence or nonzero value of $\hbar$ from Newtonian mechanics.
3. Wootters supplies a finite-sample operational intuition; Audenaert et al. supply an asymptotic optimal error exponent. Neither source fixes the project’s protocol, path encoding, or resource-to-action map.
4. The next technical step is a dependency audit: choose one reconstruction branch, list the classical models satisfying subsets of its premises, and separately define the finite-resource distinguishability protocol before relating it (if possible) to the exact ΔS calculation.
