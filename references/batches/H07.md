## Findings that touch the programme

- Newton's Rule III is the classical template for the A08 universality
  premise: a property found in all bodies within reach of experiment is
  esteemed universal until an exception appears.
- Lagrange calls the least-action principle a simple and general result of the
  laws of mechanics rather than a metaphysical principle, and derives it in
  Part II, Section III, §VI at fixed vis viva.
- Helmholtz's 1886 paper is the nineteenth-century claim that one action
  principle covers all reversible physics; his 1887 history paper documents
  the Leibniz–Maupertuis priority dispute.
- Hertz's second image adopts Hamilton's principle and his third image uses
  concealed masses, a precedent for the hidden slow-mode test in G01.
- Planck's paper gives the first numerical value of h; its talk date is
  December 1900 and its printed date January 1901, which the companion states.

## Addendum: Thomson and Tait, and two access failures

Thomson and Tait, *Treatise on Natural Philosophy* Part I, §§318–330 (archive
`treatiseonnatura01kelv_0`, OCR lines 25284–29734) joins the folder with the
textbook statement of least action. Leibniz's 1686 and 1695 dynamics papers
were sought in Gerhardt's *Mathematische Schriften*; the only combined
archive.org item with text is a French-language selection, and volume VI was
not found as a separate text. Boltzmann's 1866 paper in *Wissenschaftliche
Abhandlungen* volume 1 is available only through a lending-restricted 1968
reprint. Both remain for a later batch.

## Access limits

The Crelle journal copy of Helmholtz is paywalled and the Göttingen manifest
route failed; the 1895 collected reprint was used. The December 1900
Verhandlungen text of Planck is outside Wikisource. Wikisource's Hertz stops
after Book I. The Lagrange volume date in the Wikisource header needs
verification against the Œuvres title page.
