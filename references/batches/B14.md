# B14: bounded audit of the telegraph return bridge

Date: 2026-09-08. Scope: `notes/telegraph-return-bridge.md`, C033 (the
conditional count/simplex bridge, midpoint atom and velocity convention) and
C034 (restriction consistency and the polygon-action error bound). The audit
used the cached Cinque source through pp. 1--4, with a rendered check of pp.
3--4, and two targeted web queries for telegraph bridges/occupation atoms.
No broad review was attempted.

## Result

Cinque's finite-velocity model and its alternating switch-time representation
are an exact prior-art match for the ingredients used here (Cinque (1.1)--
(1.3), pp. 1--2, and Remark 2.1 (2.4), p. 3). Theorem 2.1 (2.6), p. 4,
supplies the conditional occupation-time density; the equal-rate symmetric
specialization at occupation time `T/2`, together with elementary Poisson and
simplex algebra, gives the draft's weights
`w_k=(z/2)^(2k)/(k!^2 I_0(z))`. The midpoint atom, exact cut restriction,
and action estimate are not stated in the source and are derived consequences
of the explicitly chosen disintegration and path version.

## Claim audit

### C033 -- conditional bridge and midpoint law

**Status: mixed, with a version/protocol qualification.** The source supports
the two-speed integrated Poisson process, the Markov pair `(T,V)`, and the
alternating-time formula (Cinque (1.1)--(1.2), p. 1; (2.4), p. 3). The draft's
simplex density for the positive occupation time is the symmetric beta density
obtained from uniform ordered switch spacings; multiplying by the Poisson count
mass and the position Jacobian gives the displayed joint density and, after
summing, the normalized `I_0` weights. This is an elementary specialization,
not a quoted theorem. The rendered p. 4 formula (2.6) agrees with the cited
occupation-time input.

The phrase “conditioning on `X_T=0`” must remain explicitly a density
disintegration/version choice: the event has probability zero. It is not an
ordinary event conditional probability, and the source does not select this
regular conditional version. The draft does state this at lines 20--24, but
the claim label should preserve the qualification wherever C033 is cited.
The terminal velocity removes even switch counts, while the zero-switch atom
at `(uT,+u)` is correctly excluded.

The atom `P(Y=uT/2)=1/I_0(z)` follows from the `k=0` component and the
simplex-boundary argument for `k>=1`; it was not located as a prior-art
statement. The assertion that the remaining component is absolutely continuous
is valid on each finite `k` region, but the audit did not independently prove
all pushforward regularity details beyond the affine-simplex argument in the
note.

The right-continuous value at the unique jump is a legitimate path-version
convention. The endpoint-window comparison is a separate observation protocol:
on the one-switch component it gives a symmetric switch-time window and hence
the 1/2--1/2 midpoint velocity mixture in the limit. It must not be presented
as the regular conditional value of the exact bridge. Targeted searches found
telegraph bridge/occupation-time literature and atoms in related occupation
observables, but no exact prior-art match for this velocity-resolved return
bridge, its midpoint atom, or the proposed window comparison.

### C034 -- consistency and action estimate

**Status: derived consequence under the fixed-preparation model.** Evaluating
all cuts on one count/simplex probability space gives pointwise deletion of
fine coordinates and therefore exact joint-law restriction consistency. This
is stronger and cleaner than multiplying singular transition densities; it is
not a theorem imported from Cinque. The midpoint-reset countertest correctly
uses the speed ceiling and the atom event, but it is conditional on that reset
rule introducing nonzero fresh variance.

The square-completion identity for polygonal averages proves
`0 <= S[X]-S_pi <= (m u^2/2) N_T |pi|`. The count is finite almost surely and
the displayed Bessel-series derivative gives
`E[N_T|bridge]=1+z I_1(z)/I_0(z)`, so the almost-sure and `L^1` convergence
claims follow for deterministic shrinking meshes. These are elementary
consequences, not source matches. The bound uses positional polygon samples;
it does not preserve endpoint velocities, and the chord comparison is likewise
positional. The distinction should remain attached to any action-gap claim.

The coefficient `m u^2 T/2` is a finite-duration return-path cost and is not
the long-observation coefficient `m u^2/lambda`; the draft correctly separates
these limits. The midpoint variance bound and the replacement by a second
moment for a biased midpoint are inherited from the A05 context and were not
re-audited here.

## Search coverage and next obligation

Queries were limited to (i) telegraph process bridges conditioned on endpoint
position/velocity and midpoint atoms and (ii) return bridges with Bessel/I0
weights. Results located the Cinque article and related occupation-time/bridge
literature, but no exact match for the full C033 construction. One additional
primary-source route, *Occupation time distributions for the telegraph
process* (Bogachev and Ratanov; White Rose copy), was opened only for metadata and
discovery; no result from it is used in this audit. A future librarian task
should read its stated occupation atom theorem if the midpoint-atom novelty
claim is material.
