## Result-by-result classification and assumptions

### 1. Normalized free heat/Gaussian convolution

**Status: established standard formula; exact blocking is a direct derivation.** Pitman–Yor eq. (25) gives the unit-mass Brownian transition density with variance \(t\), and eqs. (23)–(24) give its convolution/semigroup law. The manuscript’s
\[
G_t^\kappa(x)=\sqrt{m/(2\pi\kappa t)}e^{-mx^2/(2\kappa t)}
\]
is exactly the same density under variance rescaling \(t\mapsto \kappa t/m\). The displayed completion-of-the-square proof in Proposition 1 is therefore a correct elementary derivation of the normalization and blocking identity, not a new theorem.

**Assumptions to retain:** line state space \(\mathbb R\), Lebesgue reference measure, \(m>0\), \(\kappa>0\), positive real kernel, and finite partitions only. No continuum path measure is used. Calling the exponent an action divided by \(\kappa\) is a model interpretation, not supplied by Pitman–Yor.

### 2. Centered Gaussian convolution semigroup

**Status: derived consequence of elementary characteristic-function algebra; no exact primary theorem needed for this restricted statement.** For centered Gaussian \(\mu_t\), \(\widehat\mu_t(k)=e^{-w(t)k^2/2}\). Convolution gives \(w(s+t)=w(s)+w(t)\); weak continuity makes \(w\) continuous, hence \(w(t)=at\), \(a\ge0\). The converse is immediate. Pitman–Yor’s eqs. (23)–(26) provide the broader Brownian Gaussian semigroup context, but do not state this exact one-dimensional classification as a named theorem.

**Assumptions to retain:** every member is centered Gaussian (not merely infinitely divisible); weak continuity includes \(t=0\) with \(\mu_0=\delta_0\); variance may be zero. The \(a=0\) family is \(\mu_t=\delta_0\) for every \(t\), not a positive-width density. At fixed mass, \(\kappa=ma\) has action units; composition alone does not select \(a>0\) or a positive lower bound.

### 3. Brownian bridge and concentration

**Status: established bridge representation plus derived scaled consequence.** Pitman–Yor §3.4 (printed p. 10) gives
\[
B_u^{x,y,T}=x+B_u-(u/T)B_T+(u/T)(y-x),
\]
and printed p. 5, eqs. (4)–(5), gives the centered Gaussian bridge covariance. Thus
\[
\mathbb E B_u^{x,y,T}=x+u(y-x)/T,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}B_u^{x,y,T}=u(T-u)/T.
\]
Scaling Brownian variance to \(\kappa/m\) yields the manuscript’s \(\kappa u(T-u)/(mT)\). The finite-dimensional bridge density is obtained by the Markov product formula (printed pp. 12–13, eqs. (20)–(23)) and endpoint normalization; the manuscript’s one-time density is correct. For fixed finitely many times and tolerances, Chebyshev at each time plus a union bound proves concentration as \(\kappa\downarrow0\).

**Assumptions to retain:** fixed \(0<s<T\), fixed endpoints \(x,z\), finite set of internal times, positive Gaussian bridge law for \(\kappa>0\), and tolerances independent of \(\kappa\). This is finite-dimensional concentration only; it does not establish uniform path-space convergence or a continuum path measure. The linear mean is the free Newtonian path only after choosing the free Lagrangian and fixed-endpoint comparison.

### 4. Free Fourier multiplier and Schrödinger group

**Status: established standard operator result; manuscript is a rescaled restatement.** Teschl §7.3 (existing local companion, printed pp. 167–169, eqs. (7.20)–(7.30)) defines free evolution by the Fourier multiplier and gives the oscillatory propagator. Restoring \(m\) and \(\kappa\) gives multiplier \(e^{-i\kappa t k^2/(2m)}\), Hamiltonian \(-\kappa^2(2m)^{-1}d^2/dx^2\), and domain \(H^2(\mathbb R)\). Modulus-one multiplication proves unitarity; dominated convergence proves strong continuity. The kernel must be understood as a tempered-distribution/Fourier-operator kernel, not as an ordinary absolutely convergent convolution integral.

**Assumptions to retain:** \(\kappa>0\), real time (the group extends to all real \(t\), while the displayed kernel uses \(t>0\)), Hilbert space \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), Fourier convention stated or inherited from Teschl, and domain \(H^2\). The continuation from positive Gaussian to oscillatory kernel is an explicit analytic-continuation/model choice; it is not forced by positive-kernel composition.
