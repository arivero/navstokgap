# B09: bounded audit of A01 classical action field

Date: 2026-09-07. Scope: papers/classical-action-field.tex and
scripts/classical_action_field_checks.py; claims C018--C021. Four primary
sources, at most twelve selected pages, and three query families were allowed.

## Result

A01 establishes a positive model-dependent transport coefficient under its
stated assumptions. The literature directly matches the
Green--Kubo/Poisson representation and the finite-velocity telegraph model;
the finite-state spectral bounds, strict duration monotonicity, and the exact
noncommuting *mass-weighted, duration-normalized second-moment* limits are elementary consequences
of the displayed formulas, not found as exact prior-art statements in this
bounded reading.

## Claim classifications

### C018 -- bounded-speed microscopic limit

**Status: elementary consequence; prior-art coverage bounded.** If paths are
absolutely continuous and `|dot X| <= u`, then
`Var[X(t+Delta)-X(t)] <= u^2 Delta^2`, so `h_Delta <= m u^2 Delta -> 0`.
Cinque pp. 1--2 independently supplies the finite-velocity telegraph setting
and endpoint atoms, but does not state this normalized observable or inequality.
For each fixed rate the conclusion also holds under `u_lambda^2=a lambda`.
That family's speed bound grows with the rate, so the short-duration estimate
is not uniform in the rate and permits the different iterated limits of C021.

### C019 -- covariance, Green--Kubo, reversible positivity and spectral bounds

**Status: mixed: exact prior-art match for Green--Kubo/Poisson; elementary
consequence for the finite-state theorem; no exact match located for the full
package.** The covariance identity and `H_* = 2m integral C` are the standard
Green--Kubo relation. Pavliotis Proposition 2.1 and (2.3)--(2.8), pp. 4--6,
give the centered ergodic-Markov Poisson representation and nonnegativity;
reversibility gives symmetry. For a finite irreducible reversible chain, the
paper's eigen-expansion, positivity, bounds
`2m sigma_v^2/gamma_max <= H_* <= 2m sigma_v^2/gamma_min`, and the error bound
`1-H_Delta/H_* <= 1/(gamma_min Delta)` follow directly by diagonalizing `-Q`.
The scalar `1-(1-exp(-y))/y` derivative proves strict monotonicity. These are
proved consequences, not an imported theorem. A uniform positive constant
still needs rate and variance controls; replacing `Q` by `rQ` changes the
plateau by `1/r`.

### C020 -- two-state telegraph formula, PDE, rate and mass dependence

**Status: exact prior-art model/PDE match plus elementary consequences; rate-law
interpretation unmatched in this audit.** Cinque (1.1)--(1.3), pp. 1--2,
defines the alternating finite-velocity motion, identifies the Markov pair and
states the telegraph PDE, including the symmetric reduction and Kac scaling.
For `Q=[[-lambda,lambda],[lambda,-lambda]]`, direct Poisson counting gives
`C(s)=u^2 exp(-2 lambda s)` and
`h_Delta=(m u^2/lambda)[1-(1-exp(-2 lambda Delta))/(2 lambda Delta)]`;
the script's correlation, variance, PDE-moment and plateau residuals check this.
The no-reversal endpoint atoms are also consistent with Cinque p. 2. The
mass-dependent universality condition `lambda_m=m u_m^2/H_*` is A01's algebraic
diagnostic, not a source result. Reversal jumps are an external impulsive
mechanism, not a smooth Newtonian force law.

### C021 -- noncommuting second-moment limits

**Status: elementary consequence of C020; exact prior-art match not found in
the bounded pages.** With `u_lambda^2=a lambda`, fixed `Delta>0` gives
`h_lambda -> ma`, while fixed finite `u` and `lambda` gives the microscopic
limit zero. Hence
`lim_{Delta->0} lim_{lambda->infty} h_lambda=ma` but
`lim_{lambda->infty} lim_{Delta->0} h_lambda=0`.
Cinque's Kac paragraph supports the related process/PDE diffusion scaling, but
not this order-sensitive mass-weighted second moment. The scaling also sends
`u_lambda` beyond any fixed physical speed ceiling, so it cannot be combined
with C018 without changing the model class.

## Where action coefficients enter reconstructions

Nelson's official APS abstract assumes diffusion `hbar/(2m)` as a premise; it
does not derive the coefficient from finite-speed classical dynamics. Hall--
Reginatto pp. 5 and 8 add an inverse-position momentum-fluctuation premise and
obtain a positive universal `C`, then set `C=(hbar/2)^2` to recover the
Schrodinger equation. A01's `m Var(Delta X)/Delta` has action units and a
positive long-time value, but neither source identifies it with `hbar`; its
value changes with mass, speed variance and reversal rate unless a new
invariant rate law is supplied.

## Next bounded model test

Construct a closed momentum-exchange realization of the two-state process and
test whether it enforces the mass-weighted correlation invariant
`2m integral_0^infty C_m(s) ds = H_*` across masses and preparations. Hall--
Reginatto suggests testing whether an inverse-position fluctuation constraint
can force such a law without inserting `hbar`; Nelson supplies the comparison
mass scaling. A valid zero countermodel remains: fix `u` and let
`lambda -> infinity`, or fix `lambda` and let `u -> 0`; then `H_* = m u^2/lambda`
can tend to zero. Any claimed universal positive plateau must exclude these
families by an independently stated physical premise.
