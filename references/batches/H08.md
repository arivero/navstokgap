The decisive early modern document is Cavalieri's letter of 12 September 1634
(Opere XVI, archive lines 8836–9311), which restates Galileo's objection from
the bowl and the cone and answers that the method never composes the continuum
from indivisibles but compares aggregates. The 28 June 1639 letter records
Cavalieri's reception of the Discorsi's “ocean of indivisibles, vacua and
infinites”.

## Access failures and exclusions

- Perseus returned HTTP 503 for Aristotle's Greek on every attempt; Physics
  VI.9 was taken from the 1879 Teubner OCR. De generatione et corruptione I.2,
  Democritus's division-everywhere argument at 316a, is recorded here as
  wanted: Prantl's 1881 Greek OCR is unusable and Joachim's 1922 volume was
  OCR'd as Greek only.
- Simplicius on Physics VI (CAG X, 1895) was downloaded but its OCR carries no
  searchable Greek; not stored.
- Guldin's Centrobaryca was not located on archive.org.
- The Arabic text of al-Shahrastānī (Cureton 1846) exists on archive.org but its
  Arabic-script OCR was not assessed; the German translation was stored.
- No pre-1901 English exists for the Mohist Canons or for Liu Hui; the Chinese
  stands alone with the anchors quoted in the companions.
- Torricelli's own letters are in his 1919 Opere and were left out.
