# B19: forced multiscale blowup and the price of a refinement limit

The fluid papers released on 2026-09-08 carry out, in a setting with its own
proofs, the construction this programme keeps testing: infinitely many scales
placed inside a finite parameter interval, each level small in the quantity that
must stay bounded and large in the quantity that is allowed to diverge, with the
whole scheme admissible exactly when a residual stays controlled across all
levels. Their contribution here is a proof obligation and a construction to
imitate, rather than a claim or a citation for any existing result.

## What the construction does

Take the Boussinesq layer lemma, the cleanest of the three. On an affine background
$u_{\rm old}=Dx$, $\theta_{\rm old}=G\cdot x$ with $\operatorname{tr}D=0$, add a
temperature wave $\vartheta=\Theta F(s)$ and a vorticity wave $\varpi=\Omega F'(s)$
sharing the phase $s=\lambda\zeta\cdot x$. The residual increments vanish exactly when

$$\dot\zeta=-D^{T}\zeta,\qquad
\dot\Theta=-\frac{J\zeta\cdot G}{\lambda|\zeta|^{2}}\,\Omega,\qquad
\dot\Omega=\lambda\zeta_1\Theta .$$

The wave velocity is perpendicular to $\zeta$ while both wave gradients are parallel
to it, so the wave does not advect itself. The amplitude $\Theta$ and the gradient
scale $\lambda\Theta$ are separated by the free frequency $\lambda$: the layer stays
small in $L^\infty$ while its gradient grows. The amplified gradient becomes the
background $G$ for the next layer, introduced at a finer scale, and infinitely many
such stages are placed inside $[0,T_*)$.

Three structural devices carry the scheme, and each names a decision this programme
has faced:

1. **The residual is the force.** A localized oscillation fails to be an exact
   unforced steady state, and the nonlinear remainder is declared to be the applied
   force ([IPM](../../docs/batches/B19/AlpogeBuckmasterCoiculescu_IPM_2026.md), p. 4).
   All remainders are estimated and none are dropped.
2. **The parent is a coarse-grained child.** The background for level $q+1$ is the
   complete preceding field after a smoothing Fourier cutoff,
   $P_{q+1}=S_{\mu_{q+1}}\rho^{(q)}$, so each level sees a band-limited version of
   everything already built.
3. **Corrections replace consistency.** Adding a fine layer perturbs the coarse
   levels already in place. Boussinesq §6 and Euler §9 restore the equations with
   explicit finite corrections, and the admissibility requirement is that the
   accumulated correction be summable in every mixed space–time derivative, so that
   the total force is $C^\infty$ up to and including the blowup time.

## Correspondence with the refinement results

The scheme has the same shape as [`regulator-limits`](../../papers/regulator-limits.tex),
Proposition on the exact defect law. There, $d=N-1$ cuts each carry action scale
$\kappa_N$; the path converges uniformly almost surely to the classical path while the
action defect converges in $L^2$ to $\ell/2$, with the three regimes set by
$d\kappa_N\to0$, $\to\ell$, $\to\infty$. Here, layers each carry amplitude $\Theta_q$;
the field stays bounded in $L^\infty$ while its gradient diverges, and admissibility is
set by the summability of the force across levels. Both fix a zeroth-order object,
let a first-order object run, and decide the outcome by the product of level count and
per-level size. The units differ and no theorem transfers; the shared object is the
bookkeeping.

The sharper contact is with [`cut-point-consistency`](../../notes/cut-point-consistency.md).
C027–C029 record that at fixed Gaussian preparation each inserted node adds expected
kinetic action $\kappa/2$, and that keeping the total defect finite by shrinking
$\kappa$ **changes the marginals of the nodes already placed**. That is the same
collision between refinement and consistency that device 3 above meets, and the fluid
programme answers it differently: it abandons exact consistency of the earlier levels
and requires instead that the correction restoring them be summable at every derivative
order. This programme has not tried that route.

## Source-generated next obligation

State the cut-refinement scheme in the corrected form and find what the correction
costs. Concretely: replace the demand that inserted nodes preserve old node marginals
exactly by a demand that the per-level correction be summable, then ask whether the
surviving action parameter $\ell$ is still positive, and whether positivity now depends
on the summability exponent rather than on the preparation. A08's positive-reference
theorem and A09a's common-finite-window obstruction are the two places where a
corrected scheme would give a different answer, since both currently turn on exact
conditions across all masses or windows.

[I003](../../ideas/I003-cut-paradox-arrow-obstruction.md) asks whether an obstruction
forbids mapping time to positions indefinitely as a double limit, and whether that
obstruction forces a minimum action. These constructions are a double limit of exactly
that kind, spatial scale shrinking as $t\uparrow T_*$, and they go through. What they
pay is a force acting at every scale, smooth but nonzero. The reading for I003: the
double limit is available at a price, and the quantity to identify is the price, rather
than a barrier.

## Status of the Navier–Stokes claims

Nothing here proves anything about the Millennium problem. The forced alternatives C and
D of [Fefferman's statement](../../notes/millennium-problem-definitions.md) permit
forcing, and admissible forcing there must satisfy the decay bounds
$|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^mf|\le C_{\alpha mK}(1+|x|+t)^{-K}$; these results are for
Euler, Boussinesq and IPM, and none of them is Navier–Stokes. The claimed
hypodissipative Navier–Stokes result is unreleased, and the reported internal OpenAI
forced-Navier–Stokes proof has no public artifact, no stated hypotheses and no
confirmation of content. Both stay outside the citable record.

The relation to [Tao's averaged equation](../../docs/Tao_AveragedNS_1402.0290v3.md) is
worth keeping straight, since the companion there already records the distinction: Tao
modifies the nonlinearity and keeps $f=0$; this programme keeps the nonlinearity and
pays a force. They are two different ways of buying a singularity, and the Millennium
statement excludes the first while permitting the second under decay conditions.
