# B15 bounded checkerboard source batch

**Finding:** B15’s remembered sources are substantially correct, but their
roles differ. Feynman–Hibbs Problem 2-6 (1965; pp. 34–36 reported in secondary leads) is a
book problem on one-dimensional light-speed zig-zag paths. Gaveau et al.
(PRL 53, 419–422, 1984, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.53.419) explicitly describe
real Poisson processes leading to the telegrapher equation and analytic
continuation to Dirac. Jacobson–Schulman (J. Phys. A 17, 375–383, 1984, DOI
10.1088/0305-4470/17/2/023) describe the relativistic-to-nonrelativistic
stochastic scaling and the Compton transition scale.

## Critical distinction

The real Poisson reversal rate \(\lambda\) belongs to a normalized positive
telegraph process. The checkerboard corner factor belongs to a complex path
amplitude and is introduced/used with analytic continuation. A real Kac-style
diffusion/telegraph coefficient is therefore not automatically a quantum
action coefficient, and neither paper’s abstract establishes universality,
mass-additivity, or a positive lower bound on action. These are separate model
questions.
