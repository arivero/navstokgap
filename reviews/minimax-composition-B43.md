## Mathematical review

The scalar theorem requires nonempty compact convex inputs symmetric about
zero, continuous linear target/record maps, and nonempty closed convex error
sets symmetric about zero. Closedness makes the central fibre compact. The
half-difference of any compatible pair remains in it, and sign-selected product
extremizers attain the sum of radii. Separate coordinate midpoint estimators
can be collected into a single phase estimate without claiming simultaneous
pointwise error lower bounds. Written checks cover negative target weights,
the reduced-mass relative momentum, the one-constituent identity, and zero radii.

For the mechanical specialization, every constituent has its own hidden-pair
preparation duration and the same terminal time. The maximum-duration hypothesis
permits all pairs at once. R11's known initial states are retained. No speed
ceiling or stochastic independence is introduced. The n-copy formula follows
by direct substitution and has action units.

For aggregate records, both inclusion directions were checked: weighted errors
lie in the aggregate band, and proportional splitting of arbitrary aggregate
force and error lifts every aggregate experiment back to the original class.
The lift preserves each initial state and does not require its unobserved
individual records to be zero. The aggregate preparation ratio is a positive
force-weighted average of constituent ratios, so its horizon requirement is
satisfied. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the displayed inequality and its exact equality
condition; unequal force ratios in the two-body example give strict loss.

The radius-closure conclusion follows by applying nonnegative additivity to
mQ(m) and P(m) separately, with all positive real masses and one fixed
information/preparation class. It does not infer the two closure premises
from closure of their product. Identical-copy product invariance already
excludes a positive invariant product. The fixed-acceleration realization
and its common horizon verify consistency of the extensive alternative.
At fixed copy count, precision going to zero closes the product.
