# B76 review: publication position of the classical-spin example

## Editorial decision

Position P06 as a **conceptual and pedagogical worked example**, not as a
general mathematical novelty claim. The local sphere-to-Bloch-ball quotient is
an instance of the established classical-extension construction. Instability
of preparation equivalence when the transformation set is enlarged also has a
close operational precedent. What remains distinctive in the manuscript is
the compact combination of those ideas with one solvable, complete classical
Hamiltonian and a proof that no finite list of expectation coordinates repairs
exact evolution for all admitted preparations.

The four-query, two-paper, eight-page audit found no source with that complete
combination, but its coverage cannot certify priority. A stronger novelty
claim would require a broader audit of classical extensions under dynamics,
moment-closure no-go results and Koopman invariant-subspace examples.

## Recommended manuscript adjustment

Add Cavalcanti--Menicucci--Pienaar as a conceptual citation near the opening
definition of dynamical consistency. Say that preparation equivalence is always
relative to an admitted family of transformations and measurements. Then call
the two atomic preparations “equivalent for the retained terminal tests before
the Hamiltonian is added,” or “equal in the stipulated finite quotient.” Once
the Hamiltonian and its precomposed terminal tests are admitted, they are not
equivalent in the full operational theory; the result is precisely that the old
quotient is not dynamically stable.

A suitable contribution sentence is:

> We give a solvable classical Hamiltonian example in which a qubit-shaped
> preparation quotient is not dynamically stable, and prove that no exact
> finite enlargement by expectation coordinates restores stability for all
> probability preparations.

The title could retain its current form, but “Classical spins and the dynamical
stability of an operational quotient” states the literature boundary more
precisely. Avoid “first,” “new general obstruction,” and any suggestion that
the sphere-to-ball construction itself is contributed here.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

This review reuses B69/B70/B72/B74 for the minimal composite, reversible-
interaction theorem, classical-spin bracket and Koopman framework. B76 adds
only result-specific positioning. It did not audit the 1995 original
Beltrametti--Bugajski paper because the discovered public mirror was
inaccessible, and it did not search citation graphs or broader moment-closure
literature after the hard query/page stop. Those are publication-stage options,
not a selected research continuation.
